Card 1

Rotational Motion में Moment of Inertia की परिभाषा और SI इकाई लिखें।

Moment of Inertia (जड़त्व आघूर्ण, I) किसी rigid body को its rotational axis के सापेक्ष angular acceleration देने के लिए आवश्यक torque की विरोधात्मक मात्रा है। I = Σ mᵢ rᵢ² ( discrete particles ) I = ∫ r² dm ( continuous body ) SI इकाई: kg·m² Dimensions: [M¹ L² T⁰] ध्यान दें: I का मान द्रव्यमान के अलावा rotation axis की orientation और position पर निर्भर करता है।

Card 2

Parallel Axis Theorem और Perpendicular Axis Theorem लिखें — दोनों के प्रयोग की शर्तें बताएं।

Parallel Axis Theorem: I = I_cm + M d² यहाँ I_cm दो parallel axes में से किसी एक (COM के पास) का moment of inertia है, M कुल द्रव्यमान है, और d दोनों axes के बीच लंबवत दूरी है। शर्त: axes प्लेनर (समतलीय) body के लिए ही लागू होता है। Perpendicular Axis Theorem: I_z = I_x + I_y यह केवल planar (समतलीय) bodies के लिए लागू है, जहाँ z-axis x और y दोनों के लंबवत हो और तीनों मिलकर एक orthogonal set बनाएँ। ⚠️ cylindrical/spherical bodies के लिए perpendicular theorem सीधे लागू नहीं होता जब तक कि हम appropriate cross-section लें।

Card 3

Common rigid bodies के moment of inertia (axis के साथ) याद करें: (1) Thin rod (center), (2) Thin rod (end), (3) Circular ring (about diameter), (4) Solid disc (about diameter), (5) Solid sphere (about diameter), (6) Hollow sphere (about diameter), (7) Solid cylinder/disc about central axis, (8) Hollow cylinder about central axis, (9) Hollow cone about its axis, (10) Solid cone about its axis।

Rod (length L, mass M): • About COM (perpendicular): I = ML²/12 • About one end (perpendicular): I = ML²/3 Ring (radius R, mass M): • About central axis (perp. to plane): I = MR² • About any diameter: I = MR²/2 Solid Disc (radius R, mass M): • About central axis: I = MR²/2 • About any diameter: I = MR²/4 Solid Sphere (radius R, mass M): • About diameter: I = 2MR²/5 Hollow Sphere (radius R, mass M): • About diameter: I = 2MR²/3 Solid Cylinder/Disc about symmetry axis: I = MR²/2 Hollow Cylinder about symmetry axis: I = MR² Solid Cone about its symmetry axis: I = 3MR²/10 Hollow Cone about its symmetry axis: I = MR²/2

Card 4

Angular Velocity (ω), Angular Acceleration (α), Linear (tangential) velocity और tangential acceleration के बीच संबंध लिखें।

mool sambandh: v = ω × r → |v| = ωr (when ω ⊥ r) a_tangential = α × r → |a_t| = αr Centripetal acceleration: a_c = ω²r = v²/r Vector form: v⃗ = ω⃗ × r⃗ a⃗ = α⃗ × r⃗ + ω⃗ × (ω⃗ × r⃗) ↑ tangential part ↑ centripetal part JEE tip: a_c हमेशा axis की ओर (inward) होती है; a_t tangent direction में। Units: ω → rad/s, α → rad/s², r → m

Card 5

Torque (τ) की परिभाषा, vector form और dimension लिखें।

Scalar form: τ = r F sinθ = F × perpendicular distance from axis Vector form: τ⃗ = r⃗ × F⃗ यहाँ r⃗ position vector of point of application of force w.r.t. axis/O, F⃗ applied force है। Dimensions: [M¹ L² T⁻²] SI unit: N·m (जूल नहीं, क्योंकि torque scalar energy नहीं है) ⚠️ ध्यान रखें: torque का magnitude Force × lever arm के बराबर है। Lever arm = r sinθ = perpendicular distance from axis to line of action of force।

Card 6

Newton's Second Law for Rotation लिखें और इसका relation torque, angular momentum और angular acceleration से बताएं।

mool samikaran: τ = Iα Angular momentum से संबंध: τ = dL/dt यदि I constant है तो τ = I(dω/dt) = Iα मिलता है। Translational equivalent: F = ma ↔ τ = Iα F = dp/dt ↔ τ = dL/dt ⚠️ सावधानी: τ और α हमेशा same axis पर होने चाहिए। Mixed_axis लेना गलती का कारण बनता है।

Card 7

Angular Momentum (L) की परिभाषा, single particle और rigid body दोनों के लिए लिखें।

Single particle: L⃗ = r⃗ × p⃗ = r⃗ × (mv⃗) Scalar: L = mvr sinθ = mv × (perpendicular distance from axis) Rigid body rotating about fixed axis: L = Iω Units: kg·m²/s या J·s Dimensions: [M¹ L² T⁻¹] ⚠️ JEE trap: यदि particle axis पर सीधी रेखा में चल रहा है तो भी उसके लिये L ≠ 0 हो सकता है — तब r⊥ (impact parameter) non-zero होता है।

Card 8

Conservation of Angular Momentum की शर्त लिखें और दो classic JEE उदाहरण दीजिए।

Shart: यदि external torque τ_ext = 0 हो, तो L स्थिर रहता है: L_initial = L_final → I₁ω₁ = I₂ω₂ उदाहरण 1: Ice skater pulling arms in I घटता है → ω बढ़ता है (energy नहीं, KE बढ़ जाता है — internal work)。 उदाहरण 2: Rotating platform पर person weight लेकर खड़ा हो I बदलता है, ω inverse proportionally बदलता है。 ⚠️ सावधानी: Angular momentum केवल तभी conserved होता है जब NET external torque शून्य हो। Gravity torque दे सकता है यदि pivot COM के ऊपर न हो — तब conserve नहीं होगा।

Card 9

Rotational Kinetic Energy लिखें। Translational + Rotational combined kinetic energy कब और कैसे use करनी है?

Rotational KE: KE_rot = ½ I ω² Total KE (rolling body): KE_total = ½ mv_cm² + ½ I_cm ω² ⚠️ Important: v_cm = Rω condition purely rolling ke liye. Example: Solid sphere rolling without slipping KE = ½ mv² + ½(2/5 mR²)(v/R)² = ½ mv²(1 + 2/5) = 7/10 mv² Note: Energy Conservation tabhi apply karein jab friction न भटकने वाला हो (pure rolling mein static friction no work करता है)।

Card 10

Pure Rolling ( बिना फिसले लुढ़कना) की शर्त लिखें, और pure rolling mein static friction की роль बताएं।

Pure rolling ki shart: v_cm = Rω ( ya a_cm = Rα ) Contact point per instantaneous velocity = 0: v_contact = v_cm − Rω = 0 Static friction का role: • Pure rolling maintain karne ke liye static friction ki zarurat pad sakti hai — par friction ka kaam zero hota hai kyunki contact point at rest hai (no displacement at point of contact)。 • Static friction ka magnitude ≤ μ_s N。 • Agar required static friction > μ_s N ho jaye, to slipping occur karegi。 ⚠️ JEE trap: Rolling condition v = Rω hamesha apply mat karo jab tak confirm na karo ki pure rolling hai। Slipping mein v ≠ Rω hota hai।

Card 11

Instantaneous Axis of Rotation (IAR) क्या है? Pure rolling mein IAR कहाँ होती है?

IAR ek aisi axis hai jo usi instant mein body ke har point ko उस instant के liye rotation का center maan sakti hai।Is par us instant mein saare points ki linear velocity purely tangential hoti hai, aur velocity profile ω×r ke anusar hoti hai। Pure rolling ke liye IAR ground ke contact point se hoti hai (kyunki contact point ke liye v = 0)। IAR use karke: KE = ½ I_contact ω² = ½ mv_cm² + ½ I_cm ω² (by parallel axis theorem) ⚠️ IAR har instant badal sakti hai — ye fixed axis nahi hoti。

Card 12

Angular impulse (कोणीय आवेग) क्या है? इसka dimension और numerical use कब करते हैं?

Angular Impulse = ∫τ dt = ΔL Scalar form: J_angular = τ × Δt (constant torque ke liye) Iska use tab karte hain jab impulsive force (बहुत कम samay ke liye) lagti ho — impulse-linear analogous angular mein। Units: N·m·s या kg·m²/s Dimensions: [M L² T⁻¹] — yahi dimensions angular momentum ki hain。 JEE application: Ek stick ke ek end par ball girti hai; impulse = change in momentum, then torque = impulse × perpendicular distance = ΔL।

Card 13

Work-Energy theorem for rotation लिखें।

W_net = ΔKE_rot W = ∫τ dθ Constant τ ke liye: W = τ θ = ½ I ω_f² − ½ I ω_i² Combined translation + rotation ke liye: W_net = Δ(½ mv_cm² + ½ I_cm ω²) ⚠️ Note: Pure rolling mein static friction zero work karti hai (contact point stationary hai) — isliye energy conservation use karna safe hai।

Card 14

A solid sphere (mass M, radius R) ko inclined plane (angle θ) par pure rolling down hone diya gaya hai. a_cm, friction ka direction aur magnitude, aur minimum μ क्या होंगे?

Forces along incline: mgsinθ − f = ma_cm … (1) Torque about COM: fR = Iα = (2/5 mR²)(a_cm/R) … (2) (2) se: f = (2/5)ma_cm (1) mein rakhein: mgsinθ − (2/5)ma_cm = m·a_cm → a_cm = (5/7)g sinθ f = (2/7)mg sinθ Friction ki disha: upthe incline (rolok ke niche slip ko rokne ke liye upward lagti hai) Min μ: f ≤ μ N = μ mg cosθ → μ_min = (2/7) tanθ ⚠️ Note: a_cm = (5/7)g sinθ — sliding ke liye (μ=0) g sinθ se chhota hota hai।

Card 15

Ek uniform solid disc (M, R) ko ek string ke saath horizontally pull kiya jaata hai jo disc ke uppermost point se tangentially khinch rahi hai (force F). Disc pura rolling hai. a_cm, friction ka direction aur magnitude nikalein।

Force diagram: Applied force F rightwards (upper tangential) Friction f leftwards (assume; sign check kareinge) Normal reaction N = Mg Translation: F − f = m·a_cm … (1) Torque about COM (clockwise positive): F·R + f·R = I·α = (½ mR²)(a_cm/R) … (2) → R(F + f) = ½ mR·a_cm → F + f = ½ m·a_cm … (2') (1)+(2'): 2F = (3/2)m·a_cm → a_cm = (4F)/(3m) f = F − m·a_cm = F − (4/3)F = −(1/3)F Negative sign means friction actually acts in direction of F (rightwards)। |f| = F/3, direction = rightwards (same as F) ⚠️ Note: Friction ka direction hamesha assumption se check karo; negative milne par opposite le lo।

Card 16

Ring (M, R) ko horizontal surface par initial speed v₀ ke saath pura rolling ke bina (only translational) start karti hai। Pure rolling kab tak achieve hogi? Find final v_cm and time t।

Shuruat: v = v₀, ω = 0 Friction (kinetic) leftwards (opposes slipping of bottom point rightwards relative to ground) Translation: −f_k = m·a_cm → a_cm = −μ_k g Rotation: f_k·R = I·α = MR²·α → α = μ_k g / R Condition for pure rolling: v_cm = Rω v₀ − μ_k g·t = R·(μ_k g·t / R) = μ_k g·t → v₀ = 2μ_k g t → t = v₀/(2μ_k g) Final v_cm = v₀ − μ_k g·t = v₀ − v₀/2 = v₀/2 ⚠️ Note: Work done by kinetic friction converts some translational KE into rotational KE; total energy decreases (heat produced)।

Card 17

Solid sphere aur hollow sphere dono ko same height se inclined plane par slide kiye bina (pure rolling) release kiye jaate hain। Kaun pehle ground tak pahunchega? Ratio kya hoga?

Acceleration for pure rolling down incline (θ): a = g sinθ / (1 + K²/R²) Jahan I = MK² (K = radius of gyration) Solid sphere: I = 2/5 MR² → K² = 2/5 R² a_solid = g sinθ / (1 + 2/5) = (5/7)g sinθ Hollow sphere: I = 2/3 MR² → K² = 2/3 R² a_hollow = g sinθ / (1 + 2/3) = (3/5)g sinθ Since 5/7 > 3/5, solid sphere reaches first। Ratio of accelerations: a_solid / a_hollow = (5/7) / (3/5) = 25/21

Card 18

Ek uniform rod (length L, mass M) horizontal par balanced hai, ek end ko support se cut kar diye jaate hain। Just after release, angular acceleration α aur reaction at hinge kya hoga? (JEE repeated concept)

Rod pivoted at one end, released from horizontal。 Torque about hinge: τ = Mg(L/2) MOI about hinge: I = ML²/3 α = τ/I = Mg(L/2) / (ML²/3) = (3g)/(2L) Linear acceleration of COM: a_cm = α·(L/2) = (3g)/4 (downwards) Vertical forces: Mg − R = M·a_cm R = Mg − M(3g/4) = Mg/4 (upwards) ⚠️ Common mistake: α = g/L ya R = Mg lena — DON'T। Correct answers upar hain।

Card 19

A uniform solid cylinder (M, R) rolling on horizontal surface with speed v collides elastically with a step of height h (h < R)। After impact, it pivots about the edge of step। Minimum v to surmount the step kya hoga? Energy method se nikalein।

Step ke corner par impact ke baad cylinder pivot ke around rotate karta hai।Energy conservation (take step edge as reference for PE): Initial energy just before impact: KE_trans + KE_rot + PE = ½Mv² + ½(½MR²)(v/R)² + 0 = (3/4)Mv² At top of step (just barely makes it): COM must reach height h above step top → COM vertical rise = R − (R−h) = h? Wait — COM initial height = R; final height = R+h − hmm careful. Better approach using energy about step edge: Initial about corner: ½Mv² + ¼Mv² + Mg(R−h) = ¾Mv² + Mg(R−h) At top of step (momentarily at rest rotationally about corner): ½ I_corner ω_f² + MgR I_corner = I_cm + MR² = (3/2)MR² Using pure rolling condition before and after isn't valid across impact — use conservation of energy: Minimum v: ω_f → 0 at top → ¾Mv² + Mg(R−h) = MgR → ¾Mv² = Mgh → v_min = 2√(gh/3)

Card 20

A rod AB (length L, mass M) lying on smooth horizontal table. A perpendicular impulse J is applied at end B। Find angular velocity about COM immediately after impulse।

Impulse J gives linear momentum: P = J = M·v_cm → v_cm = J/M Impulse also gives angular impulse about COM: τ_impulse = J·(L/2) ΔL = I_cm · ω → J(L/2) = (ML²/12)·ω → ω = (6J)/(ML) Also: v_end_A = v_cm − ω(L/2) = J/M − (6J/ML)(L/2) = J/M − 3J/M = −2J/M (moves backward!) v_end_B = v_cm + ω(L/2) = J/M + 3J/M = 4J/M ⚠️ Important: Center of percussion concept — point which doesn't move when impulse applied at other end।

Card 21

Two identical thin rods (each mass m, length l) are joined to form a cross at their midpoints. Find MOI about an axis passing through junction and perpendicular to plane।

Each rod about its own COM (perpendicular to plane): I_ROD = ml²/12 Since both rods' COM are at the junction point, we can directly add: I_total = I_rod1 + I_rod2 = ml²/12 + ml²/12 = ml²/6 ⚠️ Careful: If axis were along one rod's length, that rod contributes zero MOI (all mass on axis)。

Card 22

A ring of mass M and radius R rotates about its diameter with angular velocity ω। Its rotational KE is: ? And if it's rotating about tangent in its plane with same ω, find ratio of KEs।

About diameter: I = MR²/2 KE_diam = ½(MR²/2)ω² = MR²ω²/4 About tangent in plane: Using parallel axis theorem: I_tangent = I_diameter + MR² = MR²/2 + MR² = 3MR²/2 KE_tangent = ½(3MR²/2)ω² = 3MR²ω²/4 Ratio: KE_diam : KE_tangent = 1 : 3 ⚠️ Common error: Using I = MR² for tangent — wrong! Must use parallel axis theorem.

Card 23

Assertion: For pure rolling, the acceleration of the contact point is always directed towards the centre of the body. Reason: Contact point has zero velocity but non-zero centripetal acceleration ω²R.

Assertion: TRUE Reason: TRUE And Reason correctly explains Assertion। Explanation: For pure rolling, contact point instantaneous velocity = 0, but acceleration = ω²R towards center (centripetal) plus possibly tangential component if α ≠ 0। Actually total acceleration of contact point = a_cm + α×r + ω×(ω×r) = a_cm (horizontal) + αR (horizontal, opposite direction) + ω²R (vertical, upward toward center) Since a_cm = αR for pure rolling, horizontal components cancel, leaving only a = ω²R upward (towards center)। ⚠️ JEE gold fact: Contact point acceleration is NOT zero even though velocity is zero!

Card 24

Three particles of masses 1kg, 2kg, 3kg are placed at corners of equilateral triangle of side a = 1m. Find MOI about axis passing through 1kg particle and perpendicular to triangle plane।

Let masses be at vertices: m₁ = 1kg (at A), m₂ = 2kg (at B), m₃ = 3kg (at C). Axis through A perpendicular to plane。 Distance from A: r₁ = 0, r₂ = a, r₃ = a I = m₁(0)² + m₂(a)² + m₃(a)² = 2a² + 3a² = 5a² = 5 kg·m² General formula for system of particles: I = Σ mᵢ rᵢ²

Card 25

A solid sphere rolls up a rough inclined plane (θ = 30°) with initial velocity v₀ = 14 m/s। How far up the incline will it travel before coming to rest? (g = 10 m/s²)

Using energy conservation (pure rolling, static friction does no work): Initial KE = ½Mv₀² + ½Iω₀² = ½Mv₀² + ½(2/5 MR²)(v₀/R)² = ½Mv₀²(1 + 2/5) = (7/10)Mv₀² Final PE = Mgh = Mg·d·sinθ, where d is distance along incline。 (7/10)Mv₀² = Mgd sinθ (7/10)(196) = 10·d·(1/2) 137.2 = 5d d = 27.44 m ≈ 27.4 m ⚠️ If surface were frictionless, sphere would slide (not roll), and only translational KE converts to PE, giving different (larger) distance.

Card 26

Derive expression for angular momentum of Earth (treating as uniform sphere) about its axis. Take M = 6×10²⁴ kg, R = 6400 km, period T = 24 hours। Evaluate order of magnitude।

I = (2/5)MR² = (2/5)(6×10²⁴)(6.4×10⁶)² = (2/5)(6×10²⁴)(40.96×10¹²) = (2/5)(245.76×10³⁶) ≈ 9.83×10³⁷ kg·m² ω = 2π/T = 2π/(86400 s) ≈ 7.27×10⁻⁵ rad/s L = Iω ≈ (9.83×10³⁷)(7.27×10⁻⁵) ≈ 7.1×10³³ kg·m²/s Order of magnitude: ~10³⁴ kg·m²/s ⚠️ Note: Earth is not uniform sphere; actual value differs slightly due to density distribution.

Card 27

A uniform disc (M, R) rotating about its central axis with ω₀ is gently placed on a rough horizontal surface (initial ω₀, v_cm = 0)। Find time when pure rolling starts and velocity at that instant।

Kinetic friction f = μmg acts backwards on disc。 Translation: a = −μg (slows down COM) Rotation: τ = μmgR, I = ½mR² α = τ/I = (μmgR)/(½mR²) = 2μg/R (decelerates rotation) v_cm(t) = μgt ... wait, friction opposes slip. Bottom point moves backwards relative to ground, so friction acts forward。 Correct: Friction acts FORWARD on disc。 v_cm = μgt ω = ω₀ − αt = ω₀ − (2μg/R)t Pure rolling condition: v = Rω μgt = R(ω₀ − 2μgt/R) = Rω₀ − 2μgt 3μgt = Rω₀ t = Rω₀/(3μg) v_final = μg·Rω₀/(3μg) = Rω₀/3 ⚠️ Direction insight: Friction pushes disc forward (increases v_cm) while slowing ω until v = Rω achieved।

Card 28

A hollow sphere (M, R) rolls without slipping on horizontal surface with speed v। What fraction of total KE is rotational? What fraction is translational?

I = (2/3)MR² for hollow sphere ω = v/R KE_rot = ½Iω² = ½(2/3 MR²)(v²/R²) = (1/3)Mv² KE_trans = ½Mv² KE_total = ½Mv² + 1/3 Mv² = (5/6)Mv² Fraction rotational: KE_rot/KE_total = (1/3)/(5/6) = 2/5 = 40% Fraction translational: KE_trans/KE_total = (1/2)/(5/6) = 3/5 = 60% General rule: Fraction rotational = (I/R²)/(M + I/R²) = β/(1+β) where β = I/(MR²)。 For hollow sphere β = 2/3 → fraction rot = (2/3)/(5/3) = 2/5।

Card 29

Find the moment of inertia of a uniform square plate (mass M, side a) about an axis along one of its diagonals। Hint: use perpendicular axis theorem + symmetry।

For square plate about axis perpendicular to plane through center: I_z = I_x + I_y (perpendicular axis theorem) By symmetry I_x = I_y (any two perpendicular axes in plane through center) I_z = Ma²/6 (known result for square plate about perpendicular axis through center) So 2I_axis = Ma²/6 → I_axis = Ma²/12 Since diagonal is just another axis in plane through center by symmetry: I_diagonal = Ma²/12 ⚠️ Verify: For rectangular plate about axis along length: I = Mb²/12 (b = width)。For square b = a, so I = Ma²/12。Matches!

Card 30

A sphere of radius R is rotating with angular velocity ω about a diametric axis. A small object of mass m is gently placed on the sphere at the North Pole. Will it stay? What is minimum μ to prevent sliding off? Find critical angle।

Object at angle θ from top (measured from vertical through center)。 Forces: mg downward, Normal N radially outward, Friction f tangential。 Radial: mg cosθ − N = mω²R sinθ ... wait, centripetal requires inward net force。 Actually: N − mg cosθ = mω²R sin²θ ... Let me be careful。 Tangential direction (along surface): mg sinθ must be balanced by friction for object to not slide。 f = mg sinθ Normal: N = mg cosθ + mω²R sin²θ ... the normal increases due to rotation。 Condition: f ≤ μN mg sinθ ≤ μ(mg cosθ + mω²R sin²θ) At equator (θ = 90°): mg ≤ μ(mω²R) → μ_min = g/(ω²R) If ω²R < g, object can stay at any angle with sufficient μ。 ⚠️ Critical: At what angle does sliding first occur? Set equality and solve।

Card 31

Two discs (M₁, R₁) and (M₂, R₂) rotating with ω₁ and ω₂ respectively are brought into contact (coaxially)。 Find final common angular velocity and loss in KE। When is KE loss maximum?

Conservation of angular momentum (no external torque): I₁ω₁ + I₂ω₂ = (I₁ + I₂)ω_f I₁ = ½M₁R₁², I₂ = ½M₂R₂² ω_f = (I₁ω₁ + I₂ω₂)/(I₁ + I₂) Loss in KE: ΔKE = ½I₁ω₁² + ½I₂ω₂² − ½(I₁+I₂)ω_f² = ½·[I₁I₂/(I₁+I₂)]·(ω₁−ω₂)² ΔKE is maximum when (ω₁−ω₂) is maximum, i.e., discs rotating in opposite directions with equal speeds give max loss。 ⚠️ Note: KE is NEVER conserved in such inelastic rotational collisions — always some loss as heat!

Card 32

A particle moves in a circle of radius R with constant speed v। Its angular momentum about a point on the circumference (but not the center) — does it remain constant in magnitude and direction?

About center of circle: L = mvR (constant magnitude and direction perpendicular to plane)。 About a point P on circumference: r⃗ from P to particle changes direction as particle moves, but perpendicular distance from P to velocity vector also changes。 Actually, L_P = m(v⃗ × r⃗_PR) As particle moves, r⃗ changes, but v is always tangent to circle。 Consider geometry: For any point on circumference, the angular momentum magnitude is NOT constant — it varies as particle orbit rotates。 However, for a point on the circle: L = mvr sinφ where φ is angle between r and v。 At closest approach: φ = 90°, L = mvR At farthest point: φ = 90° again (diametrically opposite), L = mvR same! But at intermediate positions, φ ≠ 90°... Actually rechecking: L about point on circumference IS constant in magnitude (= 2mvR at diametrically opposite points, mvR at closest) — NO, it varies continuously。 Magnitude oscillates between mvR and 2mvR。 Direction remains perpendicular to plane (constant direction) but magnitude changes → L⃗ is NOT conserved (torque exists about P)।

Card 33

A ladder of length L and mass M leans against a smooth vertical wall and rests on rough horizontal ground (μ)。 Find minimum angle θ with ground for no slipping。Derive expression।

Forces: Wall is smooth: only normal N_w (horizontal, away from wall) Ground normal: N_g = Mg (vertical balance) Ground friction: f = μN_g = μMg (toward wall, prevents slipping) Torque about bottom contact point (A): N_w·Lsinθ = Mg·(L/2)cosθ → N_w = (Mg/2) cotθ No slip condition: f ≥ N_w μMg ≥ (Mg/2) cotθ μ ≥ (1/2) cotθ → tanθ ≥ 1/(2μ) θ_min = arctan[1/(2μ)] ⚠️ Key insight: As μ decreases, θ_min increases (ladder must be more upright)।

Card 34

Derive MOI of a thin spherical shell (mass M, radius R) about any diameter using integration or shell method। State result clearly।

Using thin ring method: Consider spherical shell as stack of rings at angle θ from pole。 Ring radius: r = R sinθ Ring width: dl = R dθ Ring mass: dm = (M/4πR²)·2πRsinθ·Rdθ = (M/2)sinθ dθ Ring's MOI about diameter: dI = ½(dm)·r² = ½·(M/2 sinθ dθ)·R²sin²θ = (MR²/4) sin³θ dθ I = (MR²/4)∫₀^π sin³θ dθ = (MR²/4)·(4/3) = MR²/3 Result: I = ²⁄₃ MR² for thin spherical shell about diameter。 Verification: Solid sphere I = ²⁄₅ MR². Shell > solid sphere (mass concentrated farther) ✓ ⚠️ Confusion alert: Hollow sphere (shell) = 2/5 vs solid sphere = 2/3? NO — solid sphere = 2/5 MR², hollow sphere = 2/3 MR²。Shell has larger MOI for same M and R。

Card 35

A solid cylinder (M, R) is rolling down an incline of height h without slipping। Find velocity at bottom। Compare with block sliding down frictionless incline of same height।

Energy conservation for rolling cylinder: mgh = ½mv² + ½Iω² = ½mv² + ½(½mR²)(v/R)² = ½mv² + ¼mv² = ¾mv² v_cylinder = √(4gh/3) = 2√(gh/3) For frictionless sliding block: mgh = ½mv² → v_block = √(2gh) Comparison: v_cylinder < v_block since some PE goes into rotational KE。 General formula: v = √[2gh/(1 + K²/R²)] where K²/R² = β (shape factor)。 Ranking for same mass and radius rolling down: hoop < shell < disc/cylinder < sphere (sphere fastest, hoop slowest)。 ⚠️ Key JEE concept: Final speed depends ONLY on shape (β), not on mass or radius!

Card 36

Find the angular momentum of a uniform rod (length L, mass M) rotating about one end with angular velocity ω about a horizontal axis perpendicular to rod। Then find KE of rotation।

I about end = ML²/3 Angular momentum: L = Iω = (ML²/3)ω Rotational KE: KE = ½Iω² = ½(ML²/3)ω² = ML²ω²/6 Alternative check: v_cm = ω(L/2) KE = ½Mv_cm² + ½I_cmω² = ½M(ωL/2)² + ½(ML²/12)ω² = Mω²L²/8 + Mω²L²/24 = Mω²L²(3+1)/24 = Mω²L²/6 ✓ ⚠️ Remember: I about end ≠ I about COM. Always use correct axis!

Card 37

A ball of mass m moving horizontally with speed v hits a stationary uniform rod (mass M, length L) pivoted at one end at its free end. Ball sticks to rod. Find angular velocity of system after collision। Is KE conserved?

Angular momentum about pivot is conserved (pivot exerts external force but zero torque about itself)。 Initial L = m·v·L (ball at distance L from pivot, perpendicular) Final I_total = I_rod + I_ball = ML²/3 + mL² = L²(M/3 + m) ω = m·v·L / [L²(M/3 + m)] = mv / [L(M/3 + m)] = 3mv / [L(M + 3m)] KE conserved? NO — perfectly inelastic collision (ball sticks)। Initial KE = ½mv² Final KE = ½I_totalω² = ½[M/3+m]L² · [9m²v²/(L²(M+3m)²)] = (9m²v²)/(2(M+3m)) Loss = ½mv² − (9m²v²)/(2(M+3m)) > 0 always (except m=0)。 ⚠️ Never assume KE conservation in collision problems unless explicitly stated 'elastic'!

Card 38

What is the physical significance of the radius of gyration K? Give expressions for K for: (a) Ring about central axis, (b) Disc about diameter, (c) Solid sphere about diameter, (d) Thin rod about end।

Radius of gyration K is defined by: I = MK² Physical meaning: K is the distance from axis at which if whole mass M were concentrated, MOI would remain same। It characterizes mass distribution about axis। Expressions: (a) Ring about central axis: I = MR² → K = R (b) Disc about diameter: I = MR²/4 → K = R/2 (c) Solid sphere about diameter: I = ²⁄₅MR² → K = R√(2/5) ≈ 0.632R (d) Thin rod about end: I = ML²/3 → K = L/√3 ≈ 0.577L ⚠️ K depends on both shape AND axis choice. Same body, different axis → different K!

Card 40

A yo-yo (inner radius r, outer radius R, mass M, MOI about center I = ½MR²) has string wrapped around inner radius and released. Find acceleration of COM and tension in string।

Forces: Mg downward, T upward (string tension) Equation of motion (down positive): Mg − T = Ma … (1) Torque about COM (string unwinds from inner radius r): Tr = Iα = (½MR²)α … (2) Constraint (string doesn't slip on axle): a = αr → α = a/r Substitute into (2): Tr = (½MR²)(a/r) T = (MRa)/(2r²)… wait, simplify: T = ½MR²·a/r² = MRa²/(2r²)? No. Tr = ½MR²(a/r) → T = ½MRa/r² × r... let me redo: T·r = ½MR²·(a/r) T = MR²a/(2r²) = MA × ... T = M Ra · a/(2r) Hmm. Card 41

A conical pendulum: mass m attached to string of length L, rotating in horizontal circle with angular speed ω। Find tension T, angle θ with vertical, and period T_period।

Vertical equilibrium: Tcosθ = mg … (1) Horizontal (centripetal): Tsinθ = mω²(Lsinθ) … (2) From (2): T = mω²L ...(provided sinθ ≠ 0) Substitute in (1): mω²L cosθ = mg → cosθ = g/(ω²L) This requires ω² > g/L (otherwise θ = 0, no conical motion)。 Period: T_period = 2π/ω Also: cosθ = g/(ω²L) → ω = √(g/(Lcosθ)) Height of cone: h = Lcosθ = g/ω² ⚠️ Note: Period independent of mass m; depends only on L and θ (or ω)。 Kepler's 3rd law analogy for circular motion around vertical axis!

Card 42

Two solid spheres (same material, radii R and 2R) are released from same height on identical inclined planes (pure rolling)। Which reaches bottom first? Compare their times and final velocities।

Both are solid spheres → same shape factor β = ²⁄₅。 a = gsinθ/(1+β) = gsinθ/(1+²⁄₅) = (5/7)gsinθ Acceleration is INDEPENDENT of mass and radius for same shape! Both have same acceleration → reach bottom simultaneously。 Final velocity: v = √(2ah) ... no, using energy: v = √[2gh/(1+β)] = √[2gh/(7/5)] = √(10gh/7) Same final speed for both! Time: t = √(2d/a) where d = h/sinθ. t = √[2h/(gsinθ·a)] = √[2h·7/(7gsinθ)] = √[2h/(gsinθ)] — independent of R, M。 ⚠️ General principle: For same SHAPE rolling down same incline, time and final speed are identical regardless of size/mass!

Card 43

A thin rod (M, L) is suspended vertically from one end and released to swing as a physical pendulum。 Find its time period for small oscillations। Compare with simple pendulum of equivalent length।

Physical pendulum formula: T = 2π√(I/mgh_cm) For rod about end: I = ML²/3 Center of mass distance from pivot: h = L/2 T = 2π√[(ML²/3)/(Mg·L/2)] = 2π√[(L/3)/(g/2)] = 2π√(2L/3g) Equivalent simple pendulum length: L_eq = I/(mh) = (ML²/3)/(M·L/2) = 2L/3 T_simple = 2π√(L_eq/g) = 2π√(2L/3g) ✓ ⚠️ Key result: Physical pendulum period depends on I about pivot AND distance to COM。Length equivalent = I/(mh_cm)。

Card 44

A circular disc of radius R is cut to form a circular hole of radius R/2 whose centre is at R/2 from disc centre。 Find MOI of remaining portion about axis perpendicular to disc through original centre।

Use superposition (subtract negative mass)。 Original disc: I₁ = ½MR² Hole: mass m = M·(π(R/2)²)/(πR²) = M/4 Hole's MOI about its own center: I_hole_cm = ½m(R/2)² = ½(M/4)(R²/4) = MR²/32 Hole's MOI about original centre (parallel axis, d = R/2): I_hole_about_O = MR²/32 + m(R/2)² = MR²/32 + (M/4)(R²/4) = MR²/32 + MR²/16 = 3MR²/32 Remaining I = I₁ − I_hole_about_O = ½MR² − 3MR²/32 = (16−3)MR²/32 = 13MR²/32 ⚠️ Superposition trick is essential: treat removed portion as negative mass。Always apply parallel axis theorem to the removed piece about the desired axis。

Card 45

Explain why a spinning bicycle wheel held by one end of its axle precesses。 Derive relationship between precession angular velocity Ω, spin ω, and parameters। When does precession fail?

Torque due to gravity about pivot: τ = Mg·l (where l = distance from pivot to CM) This torque changes angular momentum: dL/dt = τ → Ω × L = τ Magnitudes: Ω·Iω = Mgl Ω = Mgl/(Iω) Precession angular velocity Ω is inversely proportional to spin ω。 If ω is large, Ω is small (slow precession)。 If ω is small, Ω becomes large → wheel appears to fall. Precession fails when: • Spin is too low (Ω approaches or exceeds ω, nonlinear effects dominate) • Friction at pivot provides external torque • Applied torque has component along L (nutation occurs) ⚠️ Critical JEE fact: Precession direction follows right-hand rule with τ = dL/dt。Wheel's axle rotates toward direction of gravitational torque。
