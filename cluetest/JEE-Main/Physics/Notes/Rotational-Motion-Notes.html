1. रोटेशनल गति की मूल अवधारणाएँ और रैखिक गति से तुलना

['रोटेशनल गति वह है जब कोई कठोर वस्तु अपने किसी निश्चित अक्ष के परितः घूमती है। इस अध्याय का मुख्य उद्देश्य रैखिक (प्रेस्थानीय) गति के सभी सूत्रों को कोणीय (angular) रूप में व्यक्त करना और फिर जड़े हुए द्रव्यमान के कारण जो भौतिक राशियाँ जुड़ती हैं, उन्हें परिभाषित करना है।', 'मुख्य तुलनापट्टी:', 'रैखिक गति → r (स्थािान), v (वेग), a (तवर्ण), m (जड़त्वीय द्रव्यमान), F (बल), p (रैखिक संवेग), KE = ½mv², W = Fs, P = Fv', 'कोणीय गति → θ (कोणीय विस्थापन), ω (कोणीय वेग), α (कोणीय त्वरण), I (जड़त्व आघूर्ण), τ (बलआघूर्ण), L (कोणीय संवेग), KE = ½Iω², W = τθ, P = τω', 'दोनों समीकरण एक-दूसरे के analogous हैं। परीक्षा में सबसे ज्यादा पूछी जाने वाली बात: r की जगह I, m की जगह I, F की जगह τ रखकर लिखो; यह शॉर्टकट नोटिेशन JEE Main का हिस्सा बन चुका है।', 'अदिश vs सदिश: ω और α सदिश राशि हैं; दिशा दाएँ हथेली नियम (right-hand thumb rule) से मिलती है। I और KE अदिश हैं। ध्रुवीय निर्देशांक में r, v, a, ω, α सभी एक-दूसरे से v = rω, a_tangential = rα, a_centripetal = ω²r द्वारा जुड़े होते हैं — ये तीन संबंध रोलिंग और घूर्णन प्रश्नों की बुनियाद हैं।', 'कठोर शरीर (rigid body) की परिभाषा: घूर्णन के दौरान किसी भी दो बिंदुओं के बीच की दूरी अपरिवर्तित रहती है। यही शर्त है जिससे ω सारा शरीर के लिए समान रहता है।']

9. अभ्यास योग्य सूत्र सारांश और परीक्षा हेतु त्वरित पुनरावृत्ति

['इस खंड में सभी सूत्रों का सारांश दिया गया है जिन्हें JEE Main में रोटेशनल मोशन से संबंधित प्रश्नों के हल करने के लिए याद रखना चाहिए।', 'वेलोसिटी संबंध: v = rω, a_t = rα, a_c = ω²r = v²/r. Couple: τ = Fd.', ' Angular momentum: L = Iω, τ = dL/dt, conservation when τ_ext = 0.', ' Moment of inertia formulas must be memorized: rod center ML²/12, rod end ML²/3, disc/solid cylinder MR²/2, ring/hollow cylinder MR², solid sphere 2MR²/5, hollow sphere 2MR²/3.', ' Parallel axis: I = I_cm + Md². Perpendicular axis (lamina only): I_z = I_x + I_y.', ' Rolling: v = Rω for pure rolling. KE_total = ½mv² + ½Iω². Linear impulse: ∫Fdt = Δp = mΔv.', ' Common errors to avoid: confusing r in τ = rFsinθ with distance along the object rather than perpendicular distance; forgetting that I depends on axis choice; mixing up v = rω sign conventions in rolling; using KE = ½mv² alone for rolling objects.']

Important facts

  • Pure rolling condition: v_cm = Rω and velocity of contact point is zero. This is the most tested concept in JEE Main rotational motion.
  • Parallel axis theorem: I = I_cm + Md² applies to any rigid body for any parallel axis. This is essential for finding I about endpoints, edges, and corners.
  • For rolling down an incline without slipping, acceleration a = g sinθ/(1 + β) where β = I/(mR²). Solid sphere (β=2/5) is fastest, ring (β=1) is slowest.
  • Friction in pure rolling down an incline acts UP the incline (toward the top), providing the torque needed for angular acceleration. This direction often surprises students.
  • Conservation of angular momentum applies when net external torque is zero. Ice skater pulling arms in is the classic example: I decreases → ω increases, but KE increases because internal work is done.
  • Moment of inertia depends on mass distribution relative to the axis, not total mass alone. Two objects with same mass can have very different I values.
  • Perpendicular axis theorem (I_z = I_x + I_y) applies ONLY to planar laminae (2D objects). Do not apply it to 3D objects like spheres or cylinders.
  • In combined translation and rotation, KE = ½mv_cm² + ½I_cmω². You cannot use either term alone unless the motion is purely translational or purely rotational.
  • Angular impulse equals change in angular momentum: ∫τdt = ΔL. This is the rotational analogue of linear impulse-momentum theorem.
  • When a string unwinds from a cylinder/pulley, the linear acceleration of the string equals Rα (tangential acceleration at the rim). This kinematic constraint is frequently used.
  • Center of mass of a symmetric uniform object lies at its geometric center. However, COM can lie outside the material (e.g., ring, boomerang shape).
  • For a rod of length L pivoted at one end and released from horizontal: ω = √(3g/L) at the bottom position using energy conservation. This is a standard result worth memorizing.

Formulas and relationships

v = rω — tangential velocity of a point at distance r from axis of rotation
a_t = rα — tangential acceleration; a_c = ω²r = v²/r — centripetal acceleration
τ = r × F = rF sinθ — torque (moment of force); direction by right-hand rule
τ = Iα — rotational analog of Newton's second law (for fixed axis, constant I)
τ = dL/dt — general form; if τ_ext = 0, then L = constant (conservation of angular momentum)
L = Iω — angular momentum for rotation about a fixed axis
I = Σmᵢrᵢ² (discrete) or I = ∫r²dm (continuous) — definition of moment of inertia
I = I_cm + Md² — parallel axis theorem (d = perpendicular distance between axes)
I_z = I_x + I_y — perpendicular axis theorem (laminae only)
KE_rot = ½Iω² — rotational kinetic energy
KE_total = ½mv_cm² + ½I_cmω² — König's theorem for combined motion
W = τθ (constant τ) or W = ∫τdθ (variable τ) — work by torque
P = τω — instantaneous power delivered by torque
v_cm = Rω — pure rolling constraint (no slipping at contact point)
a_cm = Rα — pure rolling acceleration constraint
a = g sinθ / (1 + I/(mR²)) — acceleration of object rolling down incline without slipping
v = √(2gh / (1 + I/(mR²))) — speed at bottom of incline from height h (energy method)
∫τdt = ΔL — angular impulse-angular momentum theorem
∫Fdt = Δp — linear impulse-momentum theorem (used alongside angular impulse)

Common mistakes

  • Using KE = ½mv² for a rolling object without adding rotational KE. Always use KE = ½mv² + ½Iω² for rolling bodies unless specified 'sliding without rotation'.
  • Forgetting that the parallel axis theorem requires I_cm to be about an axis through the center of mass parallel to the new axis. You cannot apply it to just any two axes.
  • Applying the perpendicular axis theorem to 3D objects (spheres, cylinders). It is valid ONLY for planar laminar objects.
  • Confusing the direction of friction in pure rolling down an incline. Friction acts UP the incline (opposing the tendency to slip down), providing the torque for angular acceleration.
  • Using v = rω with r measured along the object instead of the perpendicular distance from the axis. For a rod rotating about one end, points along the rod have different r values — use integration or the standard formula.
  • Assuming angular momentum is always conserved. It is conserved ONLY when net external torque is zero. External forces at the pivot can exert torque.
  • Mixing up sign conventions for ω and α. Choose a consistent positive direction (e.g., counter-clockwise) and stick with it throughout the problem.
  • Forgetting that torque depends on the point about which it is calculated. Always specify the reference point when computing τ.
  • Using the wrong moment of inertia formula. For example, using I = MR² (ring) for a solid disc. Memorize the standard values and identify the geometry correctly.
  • In pure rolling problems, assuming friction always does negative work. Static friction in pure rolling does NO work (contact point has zero instantaneous velocity). Only kinetic friction (during slipping) does work.
  • Applying conservation of energy incorrectly in rolling with slipping. When slipping occurs, mechanical energy is NOT conserved (kinetic friction dissipates energy as heat). Use work-energy theorem instead.
  • Forgetting that the angular velocity ω is the same for ALL points of a rigid rotating body. Only v = rω varies with r. Confusing ω with v is a very common error.
