JEE MainHindiFormula Sheet
P = (1/3) · (N/V) · m · v²ᵣₘₛ

Symbols: P = Pressure (Pa), N = number of molecules, V = volume (m³), m = mass of one molecule (kg), vᵣₘₛ = root-mean-square speed (m/s)

Unit: Pascal (Pa); N/m²; v in m/s, m in kg, V in m³

When to use: Derives pressure of an ideal gas from microscopic molecular motion. Used when N, V, molecular mass, or rms speed are given.

Conditions: Ideal gas (no intermolecular forces, point molecules). Elastic collisions with walls assumed.

Common mistake: Confusing m (mass of one molecule) with M (molar mass). Also, forgetting that v² means square of speed, not speed squared separately.

PV = nRT or PV = NkT

Symbols: P = pressure (Pa), V = volume (m³), n = moles, R = 8.314 J/(mol·K), T = absolute temperature (K), N = number of molecules, k = Boltzmann constant = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J/K

Unit: SI units required: P in Pa, V in m³, T in K. R = 8.314; sometimes R = 0.0821 L·atm/(mol·K) for atm-L problems.

When to use: Fundamental equation of state for ideal gas. Use in any problem linking P, V, T, n.

Conditions: Ideal gas only. At high pressure/low temperature real gases deviate. Must use Kelvin for T.

Common mistake: Using Celsius instead of Kelvin. Forgetting to convert mL to m³ or cm³ to m³ when using SI R value.

vᵣₘₛ = √(3RT/M) = √(3kT/m) = √(3P/ρ)

Symbols: vᵣₘₛ = rms speed (m/s), R = gas constant, M = molar mass (kg/mol), k = Boltzmann constant, m = molecular mass (kg), P = pressure, ρ = density of gas (kg/m³)

Unit: m/s (ensure M in kg/mol, not g/mol)

When to use: Most important speed formula in JEE. Finds rms speed from T, M, or from P and ρ directly.

Conditions: Ideal gas. M must be in kg/mol for SI consistency (e.g., O₂ = 32 × 10⁻³ kg/mol).

Common mistake: Using M in g/mol without converting to kg/mol gives wrong answer by factor √1000. Confusing vᵣₘₛ with vₐᵥ or vₘₚ.

vₘₚ = √(2RT/M)

Symbols: vₘₚ = most probable speed (m/s), R = gas constant, T = temperature (K), M = molar mass (kg/mol)

Unit: m/s

When to use: Speed at which the maximum number of molecules move (peak of Maxwell distribution). Used in Maxwell distribution and fraction-of-molecules problems.

Conditions: Ideal gas in thermal equilibrium at temperature T.

Common mistake: Mixing up vₘₚ and vᵣₘₛ formulas. vₘₚ < vₐᵥ < vᵣₘₛ always.

vₐᵥ = √(8RT/πM)

Symbols: vₐᵥ = average (mean) speed (m/s), R = gas constant, T = temperature (K), M = molar mass (kg/mol), π ≈ 3.1416

Unit: m/s

When to use: Average speed of molecules in a gas sample. Often asked alongside vᵣₘₛ and vₘₚ for ratio questions.

Conditions: Ideal gas at thermal equilibrium.

Common mistake: Forgetting π in denominator. Confusing with vᵣₘₛ = √(3RT/M).

vₘₚ : vₐᵥ : vᵣₘₛ = √2 : √(8/π) : √3 ≈ 1 : 1.128 : 1.224

Symbols: Ratio of most probable speed : average speed : rms speed

Unit: Dimensionless ratio

When to use: Direct JEE fact-based question. Frequently asked as "ratio of speeds" or to identify curves in Maxwell distribution graphs.

Conditions: Holds for any ideal gas at any temperature.

Common mistake: Reversing the order. Remember: vₘₚ is smallest, vᵣₘₛ is largest.

KE_avg per molecule = (3/2)kT

Symbols: KE_avg = average translational kinetic energy per molecule, k = Boltzmann constant (1.38 × 10⁻²³ J/K), T = temperature (K)

Unit: Joules (J)

When to use: Energy of one molecule. Direct JEE question: "What is avg KE at room temp?"

Conditions: Translational KE only. Independent of nature/type of gas — depends ONLY on T.

Common mistake: Including rotational/vibrational KE here. (3/2)kT is purely translational. Total KE depends on degrees of freedom.

KE_avg (total, per molecule) = (f/2)kT

Symbols: f = total degrees of freedom (translational + rotational + vibrational contributions counted appropriately), k = Boltzmann constant, T = temperature (K)

Unit: Joules (J)

When to use: Total average KE per molecule including all active modes. f = 3 for monoatomic, 5 for diatomic (at moderate T), 7 for diatomic with vibration.

Conditions: Equipartition of energy applies. Each quadratic degree of freedom contributes (1/2)kT. Vibrational modes contribute kT each (½kT KE + ½kT PE).

Common mistake: For diatomic gas at moderate T, f=5 not 7. Vibration activates only at very high T (~several thousand K).

f values: Monoatomic = 3, Diatomic (rigid) = 5, Diatomic (with vibration) = 7, Linear polyatomic = 5 + 2×(number of vibrational modes activated), Non-linear polyatomic = 6 + 2×(vibrational modes)

Symbols: f = degrees of freedom. Translational f_t = 3 always. Rotational f_r = 2 for linear, 3 for non-linear. Vibrational adds 2 per active mode.

Unit: Dimensionless count

When to use: Essential for finding CP, CV, γ for any gas. Must memorize standard f values.

Conditions: At ordinary temperatures (≈300–700 K), diatomic has f = 5. Vibration frozen out.

Common mistake: Taking f = 5 for all diatomic gases regardless of T. At very high T, vibration activates and f → 7.

U = n · (f/2) · RT or U = (f/2) · nRT or U = (f/2) · PV

Symbols: U = total internal energy, n = moles, f = degrees of freedom, R = gas constant, T = temperature (K), P = pressure, V = volume

Unit: Joules (J)

When to use: Internal energy of ideal gas. Key for first-law problems, adiabatic processes, energy change calculations.

Conditions: Ideal gas (no PE between molecules). U depends only on T for ideal gas.

Common mistake: Using U = (3/2)nRT for diatomic gas — correct only if f = 3 (monoatomic). For diatomic, U = (5/2)nRT at moderate T.

C_V = (f/2)R ; C_P = C_V + R = ((f/2)+1)R = ((f+2)/2)R ; γ = C_P/C_V = (f+2)/f

Symbols: C_V = molar heat capacity at constant volume (J/(mol·K)), C_P = molar heat capacity at constant pressure, R = gas constant (8.314), γ = adiabatic index (ratio of specific heats)

Unit: J/(mol·K) for C_P and C_V; dimensionless for γ

When to use: Core set of related formulas. Used in adiabatic processes, sound speed, Carnot efficiency, etc.

Conditions: Ideal gas. Values: Monoatomic (f=3): C_V=3R/2, C_P=5R/2, γ=5/3≈1.67. Diatomic (f=5): C_V=5R/2, C_P=7R/2, γ=7/5=1.4.

Common mistake: For γ: monoatomic = 5/3, diatomic = 7/5, triatomic (non-linear) = 4/3. Don't swap these.

Mean Free Path: λ = 1 / [√2 · π · d² · (N/V)] = kT / (√2 · π · d² · P)

Symbols: λ = mean free path (m), d = molecular diameter (m), N/V = number density (m⁻³), k = Boltzmann constant, T = temperature (K), P = pressure (Pa)

Unit: meter (m)

When to use: Average distance traveled by a molecule between successive collisions. JEE frequently asks how λ changes with P, T, or d.

Conditions: Ideal gas approximation. Hard-sphere model of molecules.

Common mistake: Forgetting the √2 factor (comes from relative motion of molecules). Forgetting to convert d to meters. Confusing number density with molar density.

Collision Frequency: Z = vₐᵥ / λ = √2 · π · d² · (N/V) · vₐᵥ

Symbols: Z = collision frequency (collisions per second per molecule), vₐᵥ = average speed, λ = mean free path, d = molecular diameter, N/V = number density

Unit: s⁻¹

When to use: Number of collisions per second experienced by one molecule. Related to diffusion rates and viscosity of gases.

Conditions: Same ideal gas assumptions as mean free path.

Common mistake: Using vᵣₘₛ instead of vₐᵥ. Remember: Z = vₐᵥ/λ, not vᵣₘₛ/λ.

Van der Waals Equation: (P + a/Vₘ²)(Vₘ - b) = RT

Symbols: P = pressure, Vₘ = molar volume (V/n), T = temperature, R = gas constant, a = measure of intermolecular attraction (Pa·m⁶/mol²), b = excluded volume per mole (m³/mol)

Unit: Use SI: P in Pa, Vₘ in m³/mol, a in Pa·m⁶/mol², b in m³/mol

When to use: Real gas equation. Used to find a and b from critical constants, or to understand deviation from ideal behavior.

Conditions: Applicable to real gases, especially near liquefaction. Not needed for ideal gas problems.

Common mistake: Using V instead of Vₘ (molar volume). Vₘ = V/n. Also, critical constants relations: T_c = 8a/(27Rb), P_c = a/(27b²), V_c = 3b.

Critical Constants: T_c = 8a/(27Rb) ; P_c = a/(27b²) ; V_c = 3b ; Z_c = PV_c/(RT_c) = 3/8 = 0.375

Symbols: T_c = critical temperature (K), P_c = critical pressure (Pa), V_c = critical molar volume (m³/mol), Z_c = compressibility factor at critical point

Unit: SI units. Z_c is dimensionless.

When to use: JEE occasionally asks relations between a, b and critical constants. Useful for finding a/b ratio of a gas.

Conditions: Van der Waals gas model.

Common mistake: Memorizing wrong factors (e.g., T_c = a/(27Rb) misses the 8). For Z_c, some books use V (not Vₘ) — always confirm definition.

Maxwell Speed Distribution (fraction of molecules with speed v): dN/N = 4π · (M/2πRT)^(3/2) · v² · exp(-Mv²/2RT) · dv

Symbols: dN/N = fraction of molecules in speed range v to v+dv, M = molar mass (kg/mol), R = gas constant, T = temperature (K), v = speed (m/s)

Unit: Dimensionless fraction; v in m/s, M in kg/mol

When to use: Describes distribution of molecular speeds. JEE asks to identify features: peak position (vₘₚ), area under curve = 1, shift with temperature.

Conditions: Ideal gas in equilibrium at temperature T.

Common mistake: Not knowing that area under entire distribution curve = total number of molecules (= 1 for fraction form). Curve shifts right and flattens as T increases.

Pressure in terms of kinetic energy density: P = (2/3) · (KE_total / V) = (2/3) · u

Symbols: P = pressure, KE_total = total translational KE of all molecules, V = volume, u = translational KE per unit volume (energy density)

Unit: P in Pa, u in J/m³

When to use: Connects macroscopic pressure to microscopic energy density. Useful in derivations and conceptual questions.

Conditions: Ideal gas, isotropic motion.

Common mistake: Writing P = (2/3)KE without dividing by volume. Must be energy density, not total energy.

Graham's Law of Diffusion/Effusion: Rate₁/Rate₂ = √(M₂/M₁) = √(ρ₂/ρ₁)

Symbols: Rate = rate of diffusion or effusion, M = molar mass, ρ = density

Unit: Dimensionless ratio

When to use: Comparison of rates of two gases through a small hole or across a porous barrier. JEE numeric/conceptual type.

Conditions: Same T and P for both gases. Effusion through tiny orifice (molecular flow regime).

Common mistake: Inverting the ratio. Heavier gas (larger M) diffuses SLOWER, so Rate₁/Rate₂ = √(M₂/M₁), not √(M₁/M₂).

Speed of Sound in Gas: v_sound = √(γRT/M) = √(γP/ρ)

Symbols: v_sound = speed of sound (m/s), γ = adiabatic index (C_P/C_V), R = gas constant, T = temperature (K), M = molar mass (kg/mol), P = pressure, ρ = density

Unit: m/s (M in kg/mol for SI consistency)

When to use: JEE sometimes links kinetic theory to wave phenomena. Speed of sound depends on γ and T.

Conditions: Sound propagation is adiabatic (fast compression/rarefaction). Uses γ, not isothermal bulk modulus.

Common mistake: Using isothermal bulk modulus (γ = 1) instead of adiabatic. For air (diatomic), γ ≈ 1.4.

Law of Equipartition of Energy: Each quadratic degree of freedom contributes (1/2)kT per molecule or (1/2)RT per mole

Symbols: Energy contribution per DOF = (1/2)kT per molecule = (1/2)RT per mole, k = Boltzmann constant, R = gas constant, T = temperature

Unit: J per molecule or J per mole

When to use: Foundation for calculating C_V, C_P, γ, and internal energy. Justifies f-based energy assignments.

Conditions: Classical statistical mechanics; valid when kT >> energy level spacing (i.e., not at very low T where quantum effects freeze modes).

Common mistake: Counting vibrational degree as (1/2)kT only. It has BOTH KE and PE, so it contributes kT per mode (2 × ½kT).

Combined Gas Relation (for JEE quick use): vᵣₘₛ ∝ √T ; vᵣₘₛ ∝ 1/√M ; vᵣₘₛ ∝ 1/√d³ (via density)

Symbols: vᵣₘₛ proportional to square root of absolute temperature; inversely proportional to square root of molar mass

Unit: Proportionality only — no unit

When to use: Quick comparison questions: "If T doubles, vᵣₘₛ becomes..." or "Which gas has higher rms speed at same T?"

Conditions: Same gas or comparing different gases at same/different T.

Common mistake: Saying vᵣₘₛ ∝ T (linear) instead of √T. Doubling T increases vᵣₘₛ by √2, not 2.
