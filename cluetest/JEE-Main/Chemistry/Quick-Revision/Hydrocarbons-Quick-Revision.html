जलसंयोजक (Hydrocarbons) — जल्दी पुनरावलोकन | JEE Main रसायन विज्ञान

JEE MainHindiQuick Revision

Definitions

  • जलसंयोजक (Hydrocarbon): केवल कार्बन और हाइड्रोजन परमाणुओं से बने संयोजन। इन्हें संतृप्त (Alkanes), असंतृप्त (Alkenes, Alkynes) और एरोमैटिक (Aromatic) में वर्गीकृत किया जाता है।
  • आल्केन (Alkane/Paraffin): सिग्मा (σ) बंधों से बना संतृप्त जलसंयोजक; सामान्य सूत्र CnH2n+2। सबसे कम क्रियाशील; 'पैरिफिन' अर्थ — कम अभिक्रिया करने वाला।
  • आल्कीन (Alkene/Olefine): कम से कम एक C=C द्वि-बंध रखने वाला असंतृप्त जलसंयोजक; सामान्य सूत्र CnH2n। π-बंध के कारण addition प्रतिक्रियाएँ देते हैं।
  • आल्काइन (Alkyne): कम से कम एक C≡C त्रि-बंध रखने वाला असंतृप्त जलसंयोजक; सामान्य सूत्र CnH2n−2।terminal alkyne (RC≡CH) में H अम्लीय होता है।
  • बेंजीन (Benzene, C6H6): षट्कोणीय समतलीय संयोजन, प्रत्येक C sp², सभी C–C बंध समान लंबाई (~1.39 Å); कक्सल संरचना के बजाय अनुनाद संरचना सही है।
  • Hückel का नियम: (4n+2) π-इलेक्ट्रॉन, समतलीय, संवृत्त (cyclic) और पूर्ण संयुग्मित (fully conjugated) वलय एरोमैटिक होती है। n = 0,1,2,...
  • π-बंध: side-wise overlap द्वारा बनता है, σ-बंध की तुलना में较弱 (weaker) और अधिक अभिक्रियाशील। addition प्रतिक्रियाएँ मुख्यतः π-बंध टूटने से होती हैं।
  • इंडक्शन प्रभाव (Inductive Effect): σ-बंध के अनुदिश इलेक्ट्रॉन घनत्व का स्थानांतरण; −I (इलेक्ट्रॉन आकर्षक, जैसे −NO2, −CN) और +I (इलेक्ट्रॉन दाता, जैसे −CH3, −OH)। दूरी के साथ घटता है।
  • रेजोनेंस/अनुनाद प्रभाव (Resonance Effect): π-इलेक्ट्रॉनों या Lone pair का समानांतर p-ऑर्बिटल ओवरलैप से विस्थानीकरण; एरोमैटिक यौगिकों में स्थिरता प्रदान करता है। −R (जैसे −NO2, −CHO) और +R (जैसे −OH, −OCH3, −NH2)।

Key concepts

  • IUPAC नामकरण: longest carbon chain चुनें; numbering ऐसी हो कि substituents को न्यूनतम संख्या मिले; alkenes/alkynes के लिए principal chain में multiple bond होना चाहिए और उसे lowest number मिलना चाहिए। suffix: -ane (alkane), -ene (alkene), -yne (alkyne)। Cyclic alkane = cycloalkane।
  • समघटकता (Isomerism): structural isomerism (chain, position, functional) + stereoisomerism (geometric/CIP में cis-trans ή E/Z alkene में)। Alkyne में terminal vs internal position isomerism। Cycloalkanes में ring-chain isomerism भी।
  • Alkanes की तैयारी: (i) Hydrogenation of alkenes/alkynes (Pt/Pd/Ni), (ii) Wurtz reaction: 2R−X + 2Na + dry ether → R−R + 2NaX (सममित alkane; asymmetrical mixed products मिलते हैं), (iii) Kolbe electrolysis: 2RCOONa + 2H2O → R−R + 2CO2 + H2 + 2NaOH (anode पर), (iv) Corey-House: R−X + LiAlH4 or (R2CuLi) — mainly for higher yields.
  • Alkenes की तैयारी: (i) Dehydration of alcohol: R−CH2−CH2−OH →(conc.H2SO4/Al2O3/heat)→ alkene (Zaitsev product major), (ii) Dehydrohalogenation of alkyl halide: alc.KOH, β-elimination, (iii) Partial hydrogenation of alkyne: Lindlar catalyst (cis-alkene); Na/NH3(l) → trans-alkene, (iv) Hofmann elimination (amine oxide), (v) Saytzeff rule: more substituted alkene major.
  • Alkynes की तैयारी: (i) Dehalogenation of vicinal dihalide with Zn/dust or NaNH2, (ii) Carbide method: CaC2 + H2O → HC≡CH (acetylene), (iii) Double dehydrohalogenation: R−CHCl−CH2Cl + 3NaNH2 → RC≡CNa + ... then acidify, (iv) Kolbe from tetrahalide with alcoholic KOH.
  • Alkanes की रासायनिक प्रतिक्रियाएँ: (i) Combustion: CnH2n+2 + (3n+1)/2 O2 → nCO2 + (n+1)H2O + heat (exothermic). (ii) Halogenation (Free radical substitution): UV light/heat initiation; Cl2 > Br2 (Br2 selective: 3°>2°>1° H). Radical stability: 3° > 2° > 1° > CH3•. (iii) Cracking: high temp + catalyst → smaller alkanes + alkenes.
  • Alkenes की addition प्रतिक्रियाएँ: (i) HX addition: Markovnikov — H adds to C with more H; carbocation intermediate (3°>2°>1° stability decides rearrangement possible). (ii) Peroxide effect (Kharasch): HBr only shows anti-Markovnikov in presence of organic peroxide (radical mechanism); HCl और HI peroxide effect नहीं दिखाते (thermodynamics/kinetics mismatch). (iii) Hydration: alkene + H2O/H+ → alcohol (Markovnikov), या oxymercuration-demercuration (Markovnikov, no rearrangement), या hydroboration-oxidation (anti-Markovnikov, syn addition). (iv) Halogenation: X2/CCl4 → vicinal dihalide (anti addition via halonium ion). (v) Ozonolysis: O3 then Zn/H2O → aldehydes/ketones; reductive workup। (vi) Polymerisation: addition polymer (ethylene → polythene).
  • Alkynes की प्रतिक्रियाएँ: (i) Acidic H: terminal alkyne RC≡CH + NaNH2 → RC≡C⁻Na⁺ (acetylide) — useful for C−C bond formation (alkylation). (ii) Addition of H2: Lindlar → cis-alkene; Na/NH3 → trans-alkene; excess H2/Pt → alkane. (iii) Addition of X2: 1 eq → trans-dihalide alkene; 2 eq → tetrahalide. (iv) Addition of HX: Markovnikov per step; geminal dihalide final. (v) Hydration:dil.H2SO4 + HgSO4 → enol → tautomerises to ketone (ketone from internal alkyne; acetaldehyde from ethyne). (vi) Ozonolysis → carboxylic acids. (vii) Combustion gives sooty flame (high C:H ratio).
  • बेंजीन की संरचना: sp² hybridised, planar hexagon, 120° bond angles; delocalized π-system above and below plane; all C−C bonds equal (1.39 Å, between single 1.54 Å and double 1.34 Å). Resonance energy ~150 kJ/mol stabilizes it. Not additive but aromatic.
  • Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution (EAS): (i) Nitration: HNO3/H2SO4 → NO2⁺ (nitronium ion); (ii) Halogenation: X2/FeX3 or AlX3 → X⁺ equivalent; (iii) Sulphonation: fuming H2SO4 or SO3/H2SO4 → SO3H; (iv) Friedel-Crafts alkylation: RCl/AlCl3 → R⁺; (v) Friedel-Crafts acylation: RCOCl/AlCl3 → acylium ion (RCO⁺); (vi) Gattermann-Koch: CO/HCl/AlCl3 → −CHO. EAS always preserves aromaticity; not addition.
  • Directing effects: Activating groups (ortho/para director): −OH, −OCH3, −NH2, −CH3, −C6H5 (+R or +I). Deactivating groups (meta director): −NO2, −CN, −COOH, −CHO, −COR, −SO3H (−R/−I). Exception: −Halogen (−I dominates, but +R still o/p directing → overall deactivating but o/p director). Steric hindrance can make para major at elevated temperature.
  • Side-chain reactions of alkylbenzenes: (i) Side-chain halogenation: Cl2/hν or NBS → benzylic radical substitution (most stable radical at benzylic position). (ii) Oxidation: hot KMnO4 or K2Cr2O7/H2SO4 oxidizes any alkyl group to −COOH provided there is at least one benzylic H; tert-butylbenzene does not oxidize under these conditions. (iii) Electrophilic substitution on substituted rings depends on directing nature.

Important facts

  • संतृप्ति सूचकांक (Degree of Unsaturation / IHD): IHD = (2C + 2 − H − X + N)/2, where X = halogens. Each ring or π-bond contributes 1; benzene ring contributes 4 (3 π + 1 ring).
  • बेंजीन का C−C बंध लंबाई 1.39 Å; C−H 1.08 Å; bond angle 120°; molar mass 78 g/mol; bp 80.1°C; mp 5.5°C. It is a colorless liquid with characteristic odor.
  • Wurtz reaction limited to symmetrical alkanes only when two different alkyl halides are used, a mixture of three alkanes results. Best for C2–C8 range; higher members have low yield due to side reactions.
  • Kolbe electrolysis works best with sodium salts of carboxylic acids; CO2 evolves at anode, H2 at cathode. Dimerization gives symmetrical alkane.
  • Peroxide effect is ONLY observed with HBr among HX; HCl bond is too strong for radical abstraction; HI radical combination is endothermic. Thus anti-Markovnikov fails for HCl/HI.
  • Hydroboration-oxidation (BH3·THF then H2O2/OH−): anti-Markovnikov, syn addition of H and OH; gives primary alcohol from terminal alkene. No carbocation intermediate → no rearrangement.
  • Ozonolysis reductive workup (Zn/H2O): cleaves C=C to give aldehydes/ketones. Oxidative workup (H2O2): gives carboxylic acids from aldehydes. Used to determine position of double bond in unknown alkene.
  • Acidic character of alkynes: pKa ≈ 25 for terminal alkyne; stronger than ammonia (pKa 38 for NH3) but weaker than water (pKa 15.7). Acetylene can form explosive silver acetylide (Ag2C2) and copper acetylide (Cu2C2) — handle with care; never dry these precipitates.
  • Benzene does NOT decolorise bromine water (unlike alkenes); confirms aromaticity. Nitrobenzene is meta-directing; chlorobenzene is ortho/para directing but deactivating — key exception tested frequently.
  • Zaitsev's rule: in elimination, the more substituted alkene is the major product. Hofmann rule (bulky base like t-BuOK): less substituted alkene major. Applied both in alcohol dehydration and dehydrohalogenation.
  • Combustion flame: saturated hydrocarbons give clean blue flame; unsaturated/aromatic give sooty yellow flame due to higher carbon percentage incomplete combustion.
  • Boiling points: for same carbon count, alkane < alkene < alkyne (slightly); branching lowers bp (decreases surface area). Solubility: non-polar, insoluble in water; soluble in organic solvents (ether, benzene, CCl4).

Memory aids

  • Markovnikov mnemonic: "Rich get richer" — the carbon with more hydrogens gets the hydrogen; halide goes to more substituted carbon. For anti-Markovnikov remember "peroxide breaks the rule only for HBr".
  • Directing effect flow: "Happy groups hug o/p positions" (activators −OH, −NH2, −CH3 give o/p); "Grumpy groups force meta" (deactivators −NO2, −CN, −COOH give meta). Halogens are grumpy but still visit o/p — exception.
  • Hückel rule quick check: count π electrons in cycle. Benzene 6 → aromatic. Cyclobutadiene 4 → anti-aromatic (unstable). Cyclooctatetraene 8 → non-planar tub shape, non-aromatic.
  • EAS mechanism steps mantra: "Generate electrophile → Attack ring (slow, forms arenium ion/σ-complex) → Deprotonate (fast, restores aromaticity)". The rate-determining step is always the formation of arenium ion.
  • Free radical halogenation selectivity: Bromination highly selective (3°H : 2°H : 1°H ≈ 1600 : 82 : 1); Chlorination less selective (1000 : 82 : 1 roughly). Use bromine when you need position-specific substitution.
  • Lindlar vs Na/NH3: Lindlar (Pd poisoned by Pb/quinoline) gives CIS alkene from alkyne; sodium in liquid ammonia gives TRANS alkene. Remember: Lindlar = "stopped halfway gently" (cis); dissolving metal = "violent reduction" (trans).
  • Ozonolysis product identification: If ozonolysis of unknown gives acetone + formaldehyde, the original alkene was 2-methylpropene. Work backwards from carbonyl fragments to reconstruct C=C position.
  • Wurtz limitation rhyme: "Wurtz makes symmetrical chains, paired halides happily join; mix two different, chaos ensues in triple chain confusion."
  • Acidity order rhyme: "Terminal alkyne more acidic than amine, but water beats them all in acidity line." Order: H2O > terminal alkyne > NH3 > alkane.

Exam tips

  • JEE Main frequently asks: identify product of ozonolysis given carbonyl products (reverse engineering alkene structure). Practice reconstructing C=C from fragmentation patterns.
  • Directing effects comparison questions are high-yield: arrange compounds by reactivity towards EAS (Aniline > Toluene > Benzene > Nitrobenzene). Know activating/deactivating strength ranking.
  • Numerical problems may ask IHD from molecular formula, or calculate moles of H2 absorbed during catalytic hydrogenation. Remember: each π-bond absorbs 1 mol H2; ring does not.
  • PYQ pattern: peroxide effect conceptual question, mechanism-based MCQ (mark arrow pushing), comparative acidity (alkane vs alkyne vs alcohol), and benzene derivatization sequences.
  • When asked about stability: aromatic > conjugated alkene > isolated alkene > cycloalkane. Conformational stability of cyclohexane: chair > boat > twist-boat.
  • For numerical yield/calculation: use balanced equations carefully. Combustion of alkane: use CnH2n+2 + (3n+1)/2 O2; track moles of O2 consumed and CO2 produced.
  • Separation techniques: fractional distillation separates alkanes by bp difference; gas chromatography identifies isomers; extraction with conc.H2SO4 removes unsaturated hydrocarbons from saturated ones.
  • Safety note for exam context: acetylene stored in acetone-filled cylinders (not pure compressed gas) because pure acetylene explodes above 2 atm. This factual point appears occasionally.
  • Key distinction: Electrophilic addition (alkenes/alkynes) vs Electrophilic substitution (arenes). Test which type a reaction belongs to; wrong classification is a common trap option.
  • Revision strategy: draw complete reaction map on one sheet — alkanes (substitution, combustion), alkenes (all additions with mechanisms noted), alkynes (acidic H, additions, polymerization), benzene (all EAS reactions with electrophile structures). This visual network earns quick revision marks.

Common traps

  • Markovnikov applies to HX addition via carbocation; peroxide effect is ONLY for HBr, NOT HCl or HI. Many students incorrectly extend it to all HX.
  • Bromination of benzene requires Lewis acid catalyst (FeBr3/AlBr3); Br2 alone does not react with benzene. Without catalyst, no reaction occurs — confused often with alkene decolorisation test.
  • Degree of unsaturation formula mistake: forgetting to add nitrogen term or subtract halogens. Correct: IHD = (2C + 2 − H − X + N)/2. Halogens count as H; oxygen/sulfur ignored.
  • Friedel-Crafts fails on strongly deactivated rings (nitrobenzene, aniline protonated in acidic medium) and fails with vinyl/aryl halides. Also, polyalkylation occurs in FC alkylation but not in acylation.
  • Confusing Zaitsev vs Hofmann: Zaitsev = thermodynamic control (more substituted alkene major, small base); Hofmann = kinetic control (less substituted, bulky base like t-BuOK or LDA).
  • Benzene does not undergo addition under normal conditions (would break aromaticity). Only under extreme conditions (UV + excess Cl2 → BHC/gammexane; high T/P + H2/Ni → cyclohexane).
  • Alkyne hydration without Hg2+ catalyst gives poor yield; dilute H2SO4 alone is insufficient. HgSO4 acts as catalyst for keto-enol tautomerism equilibrium shift towards ketone.
  • Side-chain oxidation with KMnO4 requires at least one benzylic hydrogen. Tert-butylbenzene has no benzylic H → no oxidation to carboxylic acid. Students often assume all alkylbenzenes oxidize.
  • Free radical chlorination of methane gives mixture of mono-, di-, tri-, and tetra-chlorinated products unless CH4 is in large excess. Controlled monochlorination needs excess methane.
  • Anti-addition of Br2 to alkene proceeds through bromonium ion intermediate giving trans product; students sometimes predict syn addition. Ozonolysis workup type (reductive vs oxidative) changes product identity.
