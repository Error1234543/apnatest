JEE Main Chemistry — Organic Compounds with Nitrogen: Formula Sheet

JEE MainEnglishFormula Sheet
R-NH2 (primary amine), R2NH (secondary amine), R3N (tertiary amine)

Symbols: R = alkyl/aryl group; NH2 = amino group

Unit: N/A

When to use: Classification of amines based on degree of substitution

Conditions: General structural representation

Common mistake: Confusing secondary amine (R2NH) with diamines (two separate -NH2 groups)

R-CN (nitrile/cyanide) and R-NC (isocyanide)

Symbols: CN = cyano group (C bonded); NC = isocyano group (N bonded)

Unit: N/A

When to use: Distinguishing cyanides from isocyanides; structural isomerism

Conditions: Both are functional isomers of each other

Common mistake: Writing R-NC as R-CN; confusion between bonding atom (C vs N)

R-NO2 (nitro compound)

Symbols: NO2 = nitro group

Unit: N/A

When to use: Nitroalkanes and nitroarenes; precursor to amines via reduction

Conditions: Ar-NO2 more stable than R-NO2 toward base; nitromethane has acidic alpha-H

Common mistake: Considering NO2 as non-acidic for all nitro compounds — only primary nitroalkanes show aci-nitro tautomerism

Ar-NH2 + NaNO2 + HCl (0–5°C) → Ar-N2+Cl- (diazonium salt) + 2H2O

Symbols: Ar = aryl group; NaNO2 = sodium nitrite; HCl = hydrochloric acid; Ar-N2+Cl- = arenediazonium chloride

Unit: N/A

When to use: Diazotization of aromatic amines; preparation of diazonium salts

Conditions: Temperature must be maintained at 0–5°C; aliphatic amines give unstable diazonium salts releasing N2 immediately

Common mistake: Using temperature above 5°C causing decomposition of diazonium salt; applying this reaction to aliphatic amines expecting stable product

Ar-N2+X- + H2O (warm) → Ar-OH + N2 + HX

Symbols: Ar-N2+X- = diazonium salt; Ar-OH = phenol

Unit: N/A

When to use: Conversion of diazonium salt to phenol; one of the most important diazonium reactions

Conditions: Warm water (not ice cold); diazonium salt must be used freshly prepared

Common mistake: Forgetting that N2 gas evolution is a driving force; confusing with Cu-mediated replacements

Ar-N2+Cl- + Cu2Cl2/HCl → Ar-Cl + N2 + CuCl

Symbols: Cu2Cl2 = cuprous chloride (Sandmeyer reagent); Ar-Cl = chlorobenzene derivative

Unit: N/A

When to use: Sandmeyer reaction — replacing diazo group with Cl or Br

Conditions: Cu(I) salt catalyst required; Gattermann uses Cu powder with HCl/HBr

Common mistake: Confusing Sandmeyer (Cu salt) with Gattermann (Cu powder); writing wrong copper halide

Ar-N2+Cl- + KI → Ar-I + N2 + KCl

Symbols: KI = potassium iodide; Ar-I = aryl iodide

Unit: N/A

When to use: Conversion of diazonium salt to aryl iodide; no copper catalyst needed

Conditions: Simple nucleophilic displacement by I-

Common mistake: Thinking Cu catalyst is needed for iodide replacement — it is not

Ar-N2+X- + Ar'-NH2 (cold) → Ar-N=N-Ar' (azo dye, para to -NH2)

Symbols: Ar-N=N-Ar' = azo compound; Ar'-NH2 = coupling component (activated aromatic amine)

Unit: N/A

When to use: Azo coupling reaction; preparation of azo dyes

Conditions: Coupling occurs at para position if available; otherwise at ortho; pH matters — weakly acidic for amines, weakly basic for phenols

Common mistake: Forgetting orientation preference (para优先); confusing coupling conditions for amines vs phenols

RCONH2 + Br2 + 4NaOH → RNH2 (1° amine) + Na2CO3 + 2NaBr + 2H2O

Symbols: RCONH2 = primary amide; RNH2 = amine with one carbon less; Br2/NaOH = Hofmann reagent

Unit: N/A

When to use: Hofmann bromamide degradation reaction; preparation of lower homologue primary amine

Conditions: Only primary amides undergo this; migratory aptitude: aryl > alkyl; rearrangement involves isocyanate intermediate

Common mistake: Applying Hofmann degradation to secondary/tertiary amides; forgetting the loss of one carbon atom

Phthalimide + KOH → Potassium phthalimide; then + R-X → N-alkylphthalimide; hydrazinolysis (NH2NH2) → R-NH2 + phthalhydrazide

Symbols: R-X = alkyl halide; Phthalimide = cyclic imide of phthalic acid

Unit: N/A

When to use: Gabriel phthalimide synthesis; exclusive preparation of 1° alkyl amines

Conditions: Only primary alkyl halides work well; aryl halides do not undergo nucleophilic substitution easily

Common mistake: Trying to use aryl halides in Gabriel synthesis; forgetting that final step requires hydrazine (NH2NH2) not simple hydrolysis

R-NO2 + 6[H] (Sn/HCl or Fe/HCl) → R-NH2 + 2H2O

Symbols: [H] = reducing agent; Sn/HCl or Fe/HCl are common reducing systems

Unit: N/A

When to use: Reduction of nitro compounds to primary amines

Conditions: Catalytic hydrogenation (H2/Ni, Pt, Pd) also works; Sn/HCl reduces both aliphatic and aromatic nitro compounds

Common mistake: Forgetting that nitro group reduction gives 1° amine; confusing with partial reduction products

R-CN + 4[H] (LiAlH4 or Na/C2H5OH) → R-CH2-NH2

Symbols: R-CN = nitrile; R-CH2-NH2 = primary amine with one additional carbon

Unit: N/A

When to use: Reduction of nitriles to primary amines; introduces one extra carbon

Conditions: LiAlH4 in dry ether is common; Na + ethanol is alternative

Common mistake: Confusing nitrile reduction with amide reduction; forgetting the extra carbon introduced

R-X + KCN (alc.) → R-CN + KX; R-X + AgCN → R-NC + AgX

Symbols: KCN = potassium cyanide (ionic, C-attack); AgCN = silver cyanide (covalent character, N-attack)

Unit: N/A

When to use: Preparation of nitriles vs isocyanides from alkyl halides

Conditions: KCN gives predominantly nitrile (cyanide); AgCN gives predominantly isocyanide due to covalent Ag-C bond limiting C-attack

Common mistake: Assuming both give same product; forgetting the crucial role ofAgCN covalency directing N-attack

R-NH2 (1°) + HNO2 (NaNO2 + dil.HCl, 0–5°C) → R-N2+Cl- (unstable) → ROH + N2 + H2O

Symbols: HNO2 = nitrous acid (generated in situ); ROH = alcohol

Unit: N/A

When to use: Reaction of aliphatic primary amines with HNO2; evolution of N2 gas measured for amine characterization

Conditions: Only 1° aliphatic amines give N2 quantitatively; basis of von Braun and nitrous oxide test

Common mistake: Applying this to aromatic amines (they give stable diazonium salts instead of N2 + alcohol)

Ar-NH2 (1°) + HNO2 (NaNO2 + dil.HCl, 0–5°C) → Ar-N2+Cl- (stable diazonium salt)

Symbols: Ar-NH2 = aromatic primary amine; Ar-N2+Cl- = arenediazonium chloride

Unit: N/A

When to use: Diazotization; stable diazonium salt formation is unique to aromatic amines at low temp

Conditions: Temperature 0–5°C essential; used as intermediate for further substitutions/couplings

Common mistake: Not distinguishing aliphatic (unstable, N2 evolution) from aromatic (stable) diazonium formation

R-NH2 (2°) + HNO2 → N-nitrosoamine (yellow oily liquid)

Symbols: N-nitrosoamine = R2N-N=O; yellow oil is characteristic

Unit: N/A

When to use: Test to distinguish secondary amines; carciogenic yellow oil formed

Conditions: Both aliphatic and aromatic 2° amines give this test

Common mistake: Confusing with 1° amine test; forgetting the yellow oily nature

R-NH2 (3°) + HNO2 → salt (no visible reaction at low temp)

Symbols: 3° amine forms soluble nitrite salt; no N2 or yellow oil

Unit: N/A

When to use: Distinguishing 3° amines from 1° and 2° using HNO2 test

Conditions: No permanent reaction; salt may decompose on heating

Common mistake: Expecting N2 evolution or coloured product from 3° amines

R-NH2 (1°) + CHCl3 + 3KOH (alc.) → R-NC (carbylamine/isocyanide) + 3KCl + 3H2O

Symbols: CHCl3 = chloroform; R-NC = foul-smelling isocyanide

Unit: N/A

When to use: Carbylamine test; specific test for 1° amines (both aliphatic and aromatic)

Conditions: Only 1° amines give this test; 2° and 3° amines do not react

Common mistake: Applying carbylamine test to 2°/3° amines; confusing with other tests

R-NH2 (1°) + CS2 + HgCl2 → R-N=C=S (isothiocyanate) + 2HCl; Hoffmann mustards oil test

Symbols: CS2 = carbon disulphide; HgCl2 = mercuric chloride; R-N=C=S = isothiocyanate (pungent oil)

Unit: N/A

When to use: Hoffmann's mustard oil test for 1° amines

Conditions: 1° amines (aliphatic/aromatic) give pungent isothiocyanate; 2° and 3° do not

Common mistake: Confusing with carbylamine test; both detect 1° amines but use different reagents

Ar-NH2 + HNO3/H2SO4 (conc.) → mixture of o-Nitroaniline + p-Nitroaniline; p-isomer major

Symbols: HNO3/H2SO4 = nitrating mixture; ortho and para products formed

Unit: N/A

When to use: Electrophilic substitution on aniline; gives ortho/para nitration products

Conditions: Direct nitration causes oxidation; often aniline is protected by acetylation first (Ac2O/pyridine) then nitrated

Common mistake: Forgetting that direct nitration of aniline gives tarry oxidation products; acetylation protection is preferred

Ar-NH2 + CH3COCl (or (CH3CO)2O) → Ar-NHCOCH3 (acetanilide) + HCl

Symbols: Ac2O or AcCl = acetylating agent; Ar-NHCOCH3 = acetamide derivative

Unit: N/A

When to use: Protection of -NH2 group before electrophilic substitution; reduces activating power and directs ortho/para

Conditions: Pyridine often used as base; hydrolysis of acetanilide restores -NH2

Common mistake: Forgetting the protection-hydrolysis sequence for controlled mono-substitution on aniline

RNH2 + R'COCl → RNHCOR' (amide) + HCl; Acylation of amines

Symbols: R'COCl = acyl chloride; RNHCOR' = N-substituted amide

Unit: N/A

When to use: Acylation of 1° and 2° amines; 3° amines do not undergo acylation (no H to replace)

Conditions: Pyridine used to neutralize HCl; Schotten-Baumann reaction uses aqueous NaOH

Common mistake: Applying acylation to 3° amines; forgetting that acylation deactivates -NH2 toward further substitution

Ar-N2+Cl- + Ar'-OH (cold, weakly basic) → HO-Ar-N=N-Ar' (azo dye, para to OH)

Symbols: Ar'-OH = phenol (coupling component); azo compound formed

Unit: N/A

When to use: Coupling of diazonium salt with phenol; preparation of azo dyes like methyl orange

Conditions: Weakly basic medium (NaOH) preferred for phenol coupling; para position preferred

Common mistake: Using strongly basic medium (phenoxide couples at ortho too); confusing pH conditions for amine vs phenol coupling

Basicity order (gas phase): 3° > 2° > 1° > NH3 (due to +I effect of alkyl groups)

Symbols: +I = inductive electron-donating effect; increases electron density on N

Unit: N/A

When to use: Understanding basic strength trends in aliphatic amines (gas phase)

Conditions: Only applicable in gas phase; solvation effects reverse trend in aqueous phase

Common mistake: Applying gas-phase order to aqueous solution; confusing with aqueous phase order

Basicity order (aqueous phase, aliphatic): 2° > 1° > 3° > NH3 (for methyl); 2° > 3° > 1° > NH3 (for ethyl)

Symbols: Balance of +I effect, solvation stabilisation of conjugate acid, and steric hindrance

Unit: N/A

When to use: Determining relative basicity of amines in aqueous solution; most frequently asked comparison

Conditions: Competing factors: inductive effect (favors 3°), solvation (favors 1°), steric (favors 2°)

Common mistake: Memorizing one order for all cases; forgetting that methyl and ethyl series differ

Aromatic amine basicity: Ar-NH2 < NH3 (due to resonance delocalisation of lone pair into ring)

Symbols: Lone pair on N participates in conjugation with benzene π-system

Unit: N/A

When to use: Explaining why aniline is weaker base than ammonia

Conditions: Electron-withdrawing groups (-NO2, - halogens) decrease basicity further; electron-donating groups (+CH3, +OCH3) increase basicity

Common mistake: Forgetting that resonance decreases electron availability on N; confusing with aliphatic amine behaviour

pKb order for substituted anilines: p-NO2-C6H4-NH2 > p-CH3O-C6H4-NH2 > p-CH3-C6H4-NH2 > C6H5-NH2 (increasing basicity: NO2 most deactivating, OCH3 most activating)

Symbols: pKb inversely related to basic strength; lower pKb = stronger base

Unit: N/A

When to use: Comparing basicity of substituted anilines using electronic effects

Conditions: -I and -R groups decrease basicity; +I and +R groups increase basicity

Common mistake: Confusing pKb with acidity; mixing up the direction of electronic effects on basicity

R-NH2 + HONO (from NaNO2 + HCl) cold → diazonium salt (if aromatic) / alcohol + N2 (if aliphatic 1°)

Symbols: HONO = nitrous acid in situ; key distinguishing reaction

Unit: N/A

When to use: Differentiating 1°, 2°, 3° amines using nitrous acid test

Conditions: Temperature and nature of amine (aromatic vs aliphatic) determine product

Common mistake: Not distinguishing aromatic vs aliphatic 1° amines; both are 1° but give very different products with HNO2

Ar-NH2 + Cl2/H2O → 2,4,6-trichloroaniline (white precipitate)

Symbols: Cl2/H2O = chlorinating agent; trisubstitution occurs due to strong activation by -NH2

Unit: N/A

When to use: Bromination/chlorination of aniline gives polysubstitution without protection

Conditions: To get monosubstitution, protect -NH2 by acetylation first

Common mistake: Expecting mono-chlorination; forgetting the activating power of -NH2 causes over-substitution

Ar-NH2 + Br2/H2O → 2,4,6-tribromoaniline (white ppt)

Symbols: Br2/H2O = brominating agent

Unit: N/A

When to use: Characteristic test reaction; white precipitate of 2,4,6-tribromoaniline

Conditions: Same issue as chlorination — protection needed for mono-bromination

Common mistake: Forgetting white precipitate identification; confusing with nitration products

Ar-N2+Cl- + H3PO2 + H2O → Ar-H + N2 + H3PO3 + HCl

Symbols: H3PO2 = hypophosphorous acid; reduces diazonium to arene

Unit: N/A

When to use: Removal of -NH2 group (deamination) via diazonium intermediate

Conditions: Useful when -NH2 needs to be replaced by -H selectively

Common mistake: Confusing H3PO2 with other reducing agents; forgetting this is a method for denitrogenation

R-NC + 2H2O (H+/heat) → R-COOH + NH4+; R-CN + 2H2O (H+/heat) → R-COOH + NH4+

Symbols: Acidic hydrolysis converts both isocyanides and nitriles to carboxylic acids

Unit: N/A

When to use: Hydrolysis of nitriles and isocyanides; both yield same carboxylic acid (R-COOH)

Conditions: Complete hydrolysis requires excess water and heat; partial hydrolysis gives amide intermediate

Common mistake: Forgetting isocyanide hydrolysis also gives carboxylic acid + ammonium; confusing product of partial vs complete hydrolysis

Ar-NH2 + NaNO2 + HCl (0–5°C) → Ar-N2+Cl-; then + CuCN → Ar-CN (Gattermann reaction)

Symbols: CuCN = cuprous cyanide; Ar-CN = arylonitrile

Unit: N/A

When to use: Introduction of cyano group via diazonium salt (Gattermann cyana tion)

Conditions: Alternative to Sandmeyer; Cu powder with HCN also used

Common mistake: Confusing Gattermann (Cu powder/HCN) with Sandmeyer (Cu2Cl2/Cu2Br2); misidentifying the reagent

Reductive amination: RCHO/RCOR' + NH3/1° amine/2° amine + H2/Ni or NaBH3CN → corresponding amine

Symbols: RCHO = aldehyde; RCOR' = ketone; NaBH3CN = sodium cyanoborohydride (selective reducing agent)

Unit: N/A

When to use: Preparation of 1°, 2°, 3° amines from carbonyl compounds; avoids over-alkylation

Conditions: NaBH3CN works at mild pH; H2/Ni is broader reducing agent

Common mistake: Forgetting that reductive amination is superior to direct alkylation (avoids polyalkylation)

Amide + Br2 + 4KOH (alc.) → Amine (1°) + K2CO3 + 2KBr + 2H2O (Hofmann degradation)

Symbols: Same as bromamide degradation but with KOH instead of NaOH

Unit: N/A

When to use: Hofmann bromamide reaction using alcoholic KOH

Conditions: Migratory aptitude: Ar > tertiary alkyl > secondary alkyl > primary alkyl > methyl

Common mistake: Forgetting migratory aptitude order; assuming any group migrates equally

R-NH2 + CHCl3 + 3KOH → R-NC (foul smell) + 3KCl + 3H2O (Carbylamine reaction)

Symbols: R-NC = alkyl isocyanide; extremely unpleasant odour

Unit: N/A

When to use: Specific test for 1° amines (distinguishes from 2° and 3°)

Conditions: Heating required; both aliphatic and aromatic 1° amines respond

Common mistake: Applying to 2°/3° amines; confusing carbylamine test with other tests for amines

Ar-N2+Cl- + Ar'-NH2 (acetate medium) → Ar-N=N-Ar' (azo dye); used in methyl orange synthesis

Symbols: Ar'-NH2.C2H4O2 = aniline acetate; coupling component is amine

Unit: N/A

When to use: Industrial preparation of azo dyes; methyl orange is classic example

Conditions: Weakly acidic medium (acetate) preferred for amine coupling to avoid diazonium decomposition

Common mistake: Using wrong pH; strongly acidic media protonate coupling component deactivating it

Hofmann's rule in elimination: Less substituted alkene is major product when bulky base/group is involved

Symbols: Applies to amine oxidation products (Hofmann elimination of quaternary ammonium hydroxides)

Unit: N/A

When to use: Predicting product of Hofmann elimination of amine oxides/quaternary salts

Conditions: Anti-periplanar geometry required; least substituted alkene favoured due to steric/electronic factors

Common mistake: Confusing Hofmann elimination with Saytzeff elimination; using wrong rule for ammonium hydroxide pyrolysis

Ar-NH2 + (CH3CO)2O → Ar-NHCOCH3 (acetanilide); then NO2+ /H2SO4 → o/p-nitroacetanilide; then hydrolysis → o/p-nitroaniline

Symbols: (CH3CO)2O = acetic anhydride; protection-deprotection sequence

Unit: N/A

When to use: Controlled mono-nitration of aniline via protection strategy

Conditions: Acetylation reduces -NH2 activating power; para major due to steric; hydrolysis restores -NH2

Common mistake: Skipping protection step and getting poly/nitration + oxidation; forgetting hydrolysis is needed to recover free amine

R-NO2 + Zn/NH4Cl (neutral) → R-NHOH (hydroxylamine); R-NO2 + Zn/NaOH → R-N=O (nitroso); R-NO2 + Sn/HCl → R-NH2 (amine)

Symbols: Different reducing conditions give different products from nitro compound

Unit: N/A

When to use: Partial reduction of nitro compounds; classic frame test reactions

Conditions: Neutral Zn/NH4Cl gives hydroxylamine; alkaline Zn gives nitroso; acidic Sn/HCl gives amine

Common mistake: Mixing up the three reduction pathways; forgetting product depends on medium pH

Beilstein test: Copper wire + organic compound heated in flame → green/blue-green flame indicates halogen presence (not N-specific, but useful for mixed tests)

Symbols: Cu halide volatile species produce characteristic green flame

Unit: N/A

When to use: Lassaigne's test supplementary; detection of halogens in organic compounds

Conditions: Compound must contain halogen; copper forms volatile copper halide

Common mistake: Applying Beilstein test for nitrogen detection (it tests for halogens only)

Na + organic compound (N present) → NaCN (sodium cyanide in fusion extract); FeSO4 + NaCN + H2SO4 → Fe4[Fe(CN)6]3 (Prussian blue ppt)

Symbols: NaCN = sodium cyanide from fusion; Fe4[Fe(CN)6]3 = ferric ferrocyanide (Prussian blue)

Unit: N/A

When to use: Lassaigne's test for nitrogen detection in organic compounds

Conditions: Fusion must be complete; excess FeSO4 and acidification critical for colour development

Common mistake: Forgetting that sulphur also gives positive Lassaigne's (Na2S) — both N and S together give Fe4[Fe(CN)6]3 directly from fusion; confused with thiocyanate test

Ar-NH2 + NaNO2 + HCl + β-naphthol → Orange/red azo dye (coupling at para to OH of naphthol)

Symbols: β-naphthol = 2-naphthol; coupling occurs at position 1 (para to OH)

Unit: N/A

When to use: Identification test for aromatic primary amines via azo dye formation

Conditions: Diazotization first at 0–5°C, then coupling with β-naphthol in alkaline medium

Common mistake: Coupling in wrong pH; forgetting β-naphthol couples in alkaline while aniline couples in weakly acidic medium

RCONH2 + NaOH + NaOBr → RNH2 + Na2CO3 + NaBr + H2O (alternate equation for Hofmann bromamide)

Symbols: NaOBr = sodium hypobromite (generated in situ from Br2 + NaOH)

Unit: N/A

When to use: Hofmann degradation written with NaOBr instead of Br2/NaOH

Conditions: Same mechanism and outcome as Br2/4NaOH version

Common mistake: Writing unbalanced equation; forgetting carbonate is produced
