R-OH + HX → R-X + H_2O

Symbols: HX = HCl, HBr, HI; R = alkyl group

Unit: N/A (reaction equation)

When to use: Preparation of alkyl halides from alcohols using hydrogen halides

Conditions: Reactivity order of HX: HI > HBr > HCl; Reactivity order of alcohols: tertiary > secondary > primary; ZnCl_2 catalyst required for HCl (Lucas reagent)

Common mistake: Forgetting that primary alcohols do not react readily with HCl even with ZnCl_2 at room temperature

3R-OH + PBr_3 → 3R-Br + H_3PO_3

Symbols: PBr_3 = phosphorus tribromide; R = alkyl group

Unit: N/A (reaction equation)

When to use: Preparation of alkyl bromides from alcohols without rearrangement

Conditions: Works well for primary and secondary alcohols; gives inversion of configuration via SN2

Common mistake: Confusing PBr_3 with PCl_5 — PCl_5 can cause rearrangement in some cases

R-OH + SOCl_2 → R-Cl + SO_2↑ + HCl↑

Symbols: SOCl_2 = thionyl chloride; R = alkyl group

Unit: N/A (reaction equation)

When to use: Preferred method for preparing pure alkyl chlorides (gaseous by-products escape)

Conditions: Pyridine added to scavenge HCl and prevent side reactions; proceeds with inversion if pyridine is absent (SN2)

Common mistake: Forgetting that SOCl_2 with pyridine gives retention while without pyridine it gives inversion

R-Cl + Na (dry ether) → R-R + NaCl (Wurtz Reaction)

Symbols: Na = sodium metal; dry ether = solvent

Unit: N/A

When to use: Coupling of two identical alkyl halides to form symmetrical alkane with double the carbon atoms

Conditions: Must use dry ether; works best with primary alkyl halides; mixture formed if two different halides are used

Common mistake: Using two different alkyl halides gives a mixture of three products, not a single product

Ar-X + 2Na + X-Ar → Ar-Ar + 2NaX (Fittig Reaction)

Symbols: Ar = aryl group; X = halogen; Na in dry ether

Unit: N/A

When to use: Coupling of two aryl halides to form biaryl compound

Conditions: Dry ether solvent; sodium metal; elevated temperature

Common mistake: Confusing Fittig with Wurtz — Fittig is for aryl halides only

R-X + Ar-Na → Ar-R + NaX (Wurtz-Fittig Reaction)

Symbols: R = alkyl; Ar = aryl; Na = sodium; dry ether solvent

Unit: N/A

When to use: Cross-coupling between an alkyl halide and an aryl halide using sodium

Conditions: Dry ether; both alkyl and aryl halide present with Na

Common mistake: Can give multiple products when different halides are used together

CH_3COR + X_2 / NaOH → CHX_3 + RCOONa (Haloform Reaction)

Symbols: X = Cl, Br, or I; CH_3COR = methyl ketone or ethanol/secondary alcohol with CH_3CH(OH)-

Unit: N/A

When to use: Identification of methyl ketones and ethanol; preparation of haloform (CHI_3 is yellow precipitate)

Conditions: Iodine test: CH_3CO- group gives yellow precipitate of CHI_3; NaOH is the base

Common mistake: Forgetting that ethanol and secondary alcohols with CH_3CH(OH)- also give positive haloform test because they oxidize to methyl ketone/acetaldehyde first

k_{SN2} ∝ [R-X][Nu^-]; rate = k[R-X][Nu^-]

Symbols: k = rate constant; [R-X] = alkyl halide conc.; [Nu^-] = nucleophile conc.

Unit: mol L^{-1} s^{-1}

When to use: Bimolecular nucleophilic substitution — rate depends on both substrate and nucleophile

Conditions: Primary > secondary >> tertiary halides; strong nucleophiles; polar aprotic solvents favor SN2

Common mistake: Applying SN2 rate law to tertiary halides which undergo SN1 instead

k_{SN1} ∝ [R-X]; rate = k[R-X]

Symbols: k = rate constant; [R-X] = alkyl halide conc.

Unit: mol L^{-1} s^{-1}

When to use: Unimolecular nucleophilic substitution — rate depends only on substrate (carbocation intermediate)

Conditions: Tertiary > secondary >> primary halides; weak nucleophiles; polar protic solvents stabilize carbocation

Common mistake: Assuming SN1 gives optically pure product — it actually gives racemization (partial inversion + retention)

δ(E-C bond) ↓ ⇒ δ(reactivity toward Nu) ↓ for haloarenes

Symbols: δ = partial charge; C-X bond in aryl halide has partial double bond character due to resonance

Unit: Qualitative trend

When to use: Explaining why aryl halides are less reactive toward nucleophilic substitution than alkyl halides

Conditions: Resonance involving lone pair of halogen with benzene ring; sp^2 hybridized carbon is more electronegative

Common mistake: Thinking halogen is deactivating only — it also makes C-X bond stronger via resonance

Ar-Cl + 2NaOH (623K, 300 atm) → Ar-OH + NaCl + H_2O (Dow Process)

Symbols: Ar-Cl = chlorobenzene; NaOH = aqueous; high T and P

Unit: N/A

When to use: Industrial preparation of phenol from chlorobenzene via benzyne intermediate

Conditions: High temperature (623 K) and pressure (300 atm); proceeds through benzyne mechanism

Common mistake: Forgetting this is not a simple SN2 — it goes via benzyne (elimination-addition) mechanism

Ar-X + Mg (dry ether) → Ar-Mg-X (Grignard Reagent)

Symbols: Ar = aryl/alkyl; X = Cl, Br, I; dry ether = solvent

Unit: N/A

When to use: Preparation of Grignard reagent used as carbon nucleophile in C-C bond formation

Conditions: Must be absolutely dry ether; moisture destroys Grignard reagent immediately

R-X + AgNO_2 → R-NO_2 + AgX↓ (major: nitroalkane)

Symbols: AgNO_2 = silver nitrite (ambidentate nucleophile); R-X = alkyl halide

Unit: N/A

When to use: Nitroalkane preparation via SN2 displacement

Conditions: AgNO_2 is covalent, N-atom attacks first giving nitroalkane as major product

Common mistake: Confusing with NaNO_2 which gives alkyl nitrite (R-O-N=O) as major product because NaNO_2 is ionic and O-atom attacks

R-X + NaNO_2 → R-O-N=O + NaX (major: alkyl nitrite)

Symbols: NaNO_2 = sodium nitrite (ionic); R-X = alkyl halide

Unit: N/A

When to use: Alkyl nitrite preparation

Conditions: Ionic reagent — O-atom (harder site) is the nucleophilic center

Common mistake: Reversing products of AgNO_2 vs NaNO_2 — remember: AgNO_2→nitroalkane, NaNO_2→alkyl nitrite

R-X + KCN (alc.) → R-CN + KX (major: alkyl cyanide/nitrile)

Symbols: KCN = potassium cyanide (ionic); R-X = alkyl halide

Unit: N/A

When to use: Preparation of nitriles (C-C bond formation, one carbon more)

Conditions: Alcoholic KCN; CN^- is ambidentate but C-attack predominates with ionic cyanide

Common mistake: Expecting isocyanide with KCN — isocyanide is major product only with AgCN (covalent)

R-X + AgCN → R-N≡C (isocyanide / carbylamine) + AgX

Symbols: AgCN = silver cyanide (covalent); R-X = alkyl halide

Unit: N/A

When to use: Preparation of isocyanides ( foul-smelling compounds)

Conditions: Covalent AgCN — N-atom is the attacking site giving R-NC

Common mistake: Forgetting that carbylamine test uses CHCl_3 + KOH with primary amine, not this reaction

ΔT_f = i × K_f × m and π = iCRT

Symbols: i = van't Hoff factor (i=2 for R-X dissociating salts); K_f = cryoscopic constant; m = molality; π = osmotic pressure; R = gas constant; T = temperature

Unit: K_f: K kg mol^{-1}; π: atm or Pa

When to use: Determining molecular mass and nature (ionic/covalent) of organometallic/halide compounds from colligative properties

Conditions: i > 1 indicates dissociation; i < 1 indicates association; for non-electrolyte organic halides i ≈ 1

Common mistake: Assuming all organic halides give i=1 — some like CH_3HgX show association

Ar-N_2^+Cl^- + H_2O (warm) → Ar-OH + N_2↑ + HCl

Symbols: Ar-N_2^+Cl^- = diazonium salt; warm water

Unit: N/A

When to use: Conversion of aryl diazonium salt to phenol (important route to substituted phenols)

Conditions: Warm water (not boiling); N_2 gas evolution drives reaction

Common mistake: Confusing with Sandmeyer reaction which uses CuX for direct halogen substitution

CHCl_3 + O_2 (light) → COCl_2 (phosgene) + 2HCl

Symbols: COCl_2 = phosgene; highly toxic gas

Unit: N/A

When to use: Safety consideration — storage of chloroform in closed dark bottles to prevent phosgene formation

Conditions: Sunlight and oxygen promote oxidation; ethanol added as stabilizer (forms non-toxic diethyl carbonate)

Common mistake: Forgetting why ethanol is added to stored chloroform — it prevents phosgene formation

CCl_4 + 2HF (SbF_3 cat.) → CF_2Cl_2 (Freon-12) + 2HCl

Symbols: SbF_3 = antimony trifluoride (Swarts reaction reagent)

Unit: N/A

When to use: Preparation of halothane/Freon refrigerants and non-flammable solvents

Conditions: Swarts reaction: uses SbF_3 or CoF_3 to replace Cl with F

Common mistake: Confusing Swarts reaction with Friedel-Crafts — Swarts is for alkyl/aryl fluoride preparation

Reactivity toward SN2: RI > RBr > RCl > RF

Symbols: I > Br > Cl > F based on C-X bond strength and leaving group ability

Unit: Qualitative order

When to use: Predicting relative rates of nucleophilic substitution reactions

Conditions: Bond dissociation energy decreases down the group; I^- is best leaving group

Common mistake: Assuming RF is most reactive because F is most electronegative — actually C-F bond is strongest

Reactivity toward SN1: 3° > 2° > 1° (based on carbocation stability)

Symbols: Carbocation stability: 3° > 2° > 1° > CH_3^+ ; hyperconjugation and +I effect stabilize

Unit: Qualitative order

When to use: Predicting which alkyl halide undergoes SN1 faster

Conditions: Solvent polarity matters; polar protic solvents stabilize carbocation intermediate

Common mistake: Applying SN1 order to SN2 — SN2 order is opposite: 1° > 2° > 3°

Ar-X + e^- (Na/NH_3 liq.) → Ar-H + NaX (Birch-type reduction)

Symbols: Na/NH_3(liq.) = reducing agent; Ar-X = aryl halide

Unit: N/A

When to use: Reduction of aryl halides to arenes (dehalogenation)

Conditions: Liquid ammonia solvent; sodium metal; electron transfer mechanism

Common mistake: Confusing with Clemmensen/Wolff-Kishner which reduce C=O, not C-X

C_nH_{2n+1}X (general formula for mono-haloalkane)

Symbols: n = number of carbon atoms

Unit: Molecular formula

When to use: Determining molecular formula from homologous series; calculating degree of unsaturation

Conditions: Saturated mono-haloalkane; each halogen counts as replacing one H

Common mistake: Forgetting that halogen replaces H — degree of unsaturation = (2n+2 - (2n+1+1))/2 = 0 for saturated haloalkane
