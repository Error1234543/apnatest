Quick Revision: Fundamentals of Accounting and Auditing — CS Foundation

CSEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Accounting is the art of recording, classifying, and summarizing financial transactions in a systematic manner, and interpreting the results thereof.
  • Book-keeping is the process of recording business transactions in the books of account; it is the foundation upon which accounting is built.
  • Auditing is an independent examination of financial statements to express an opinion on whether they present a true and fair view in accordance with applicable financial reporting framework.
  • Double Entry System is the fundamental principle that every transaction has a dual effect — a debit and a corresponding credit of equal amount.
  • Journal is the book of original entry where transactions are first recorded chronologically with dates, particulars, debits and credits.
  • Ledger is a principal book containing all accounts, summarizing journal entries under respective heads to determine balances.
  • Trial Balance is a statement prepared to verify the arithmetic accuracy of ledger transactions by summing total debits and credits.
  • Final Accounts comprise the Trading Account, Profit and Loss Account, and Balance Sheet, showing profit/loss and financial position.
  • Accounting Standards are authoritative statements issued by professional bodies specifying how particular transactions should be accounted for.
  • Depreciation is the progressive reduction in the book value of a fixed asset due to use, passage of time, obsolescence, or depletion.

Key concepts

  • Accounting Concepts (Assumptions): Going Concern Concept — business will continue indefinitely; Money Measurement Concept — only monetary transactions are recorded; Accounting Period Concept — life divided into equal periods; Business Entity Concept — owner and business are separate; Dual Aspect Concept — every transaction affects two accounts (Assets = Liabilities + Capital).
  • Accounting Conventions: Consistency Convention — methods used should remain consistent across periods; Conservatism (Prudence) Convention — anticipate losses but not profits; Materiality Convention — disclose all material items regardless of size; Realisation Convention — revenue recognised when risk and reward transfer.
  • Accounting Equations: Assets = Owner's Equity + Liabilities; Capital = Assets − Liabilities; Purchase of asset on credit: Asset ↑, Liability ↑; Payment of liability: Asset ↓, Liability ↓; Introduction of capital: Asset ↑, Capital ↑.
  • Rules of Three Classes of Accounts: Personal Account — Debit the receiver, Credit the giver; Real Account — Debit what comes in, Credit what goes out; Nominal Account — Debit all expenses and losses, Credit all incomes and gains.
  • Golden Rules of Modern Approach: Asset/Expense accounts — Debit increase, Credit decrease; Liability/Income/Equity accounts — Credit increase, Debit decrease.
  • Important Journal Entries: Opening entries on commencement; Cash introduced by proprietor; Goods purchased on credit; Goods sold on credit; Sale of fixed asset; Payment of rent; Receipt of commission; Bad debts written off; Provision for discounts; Goods distributed as free samples — treated as donation; Purchases of assets on credit (debits asset account, credits supplier).
  • Types of Errors and Their Effects: Error of Omission — transaction not recorded at all, no effect on trial balance; Error of Commission — wrong account but correct side, no effect; Error of Principle — recording capital expenditure as revenue, no effect on TB; Error of Original Entry — wrong amount entered, may cause TB imbalance; Compensating Errors — two or more errors cancel each other, no effect on TB.
  • Rectification of Errors: If trial balance agrees but error found — pass reversal entry if both sides wrongly affected; one-sided error → suspense account; if both sides affected wrongly → reverse both entries and pass correct one; omissions → pass complete entry; commissions → debit wrong account, credit correct; principles → reverse wrong treatment, record correct classification.
  • Depreciation Methods: Straight Line Method — equal annual charge over useful life; Written Down Value Method — fixed percentage on diminishing book value; Sum of Years Digits Method — accelerated depreciation based on sum of digit years; Annuity Method — includes interest cost on capital employed.
  • Factors Affecting Depreciation: Cost of asset, Estimated useful life, Estimated residual/scrap value, Maintenance policy, Obsolescence, Usage intensity, Inflation and price changes.
  • Accounting Standards Coverage: AS-1 — Disclosure of Accounting Policies; AS-2 — Valuation of Inventories (FIFO/Weighted Avg); AS-3 — Cash Flow Statements; AS-4 — Contingencies and Events After Reporting Date; AS-6 — Depreciation and Amortisation; AS-7 — Construction Contracts; AS-10 — Accounting for Fixed Assets; AS-14 — Goodwill and Share Premium; AS-15 — Employee Benefits; AS-16 — Borrowing Costs; AS-17 — Segment Reporting; AS-18 — Related Party Disclosures; AS-20 — Consolidated Financial Statements; AS-21 — Separate Financial Statements; AS-29 — Provisions, Contingent Liabilities and Contingent Assets; AS-32 — Revenue Recognition. Note: Ind AS also applies for listed/large entities.
  • Inventory Valuation (AS-2): Methods — FIFO (First In First Out), Weighted Average Cost, Specific Identification. LIFO is prohibited under current AS/Ind AS. Inventory valued at lower of cost and net realisable value.
  • Cash Flow Statement (AS-3): Three activities — Operating Activities, Investing Activities, Financing Activities. Direct Method and Indirect Method both permitted. Net cash flow shows increase/decrease in cash and cash equivalents.
  • Audit Objectives: Verify existence and ownership of assets; Confirm liabilities are complete and accurate; Ensure compliance with relevant laws and standards; Detect and prevent errors and fraud; Assess internal control effectiveness; Express opinion on true and fair view.
  • Types of Audit: Statutory Audit — mandatory under Companies Act; Internal Audit — continuous examination by employees of entity; Stock Audit — verification of inventory by specialised auditor; VAT/GST Audit — tax compliance check; Tax Audit u/s 44AB of Income Tax Act; Forensic Audit — investigation of fraud/corruption; Cost Audit — verification of cost records; Concurrent Audit — ongoing audit during financial year.
  • Audit Procedures: Examination of vouchers and supporting documents; Verification of physical assets; Confirmation from third parties; Analytical procedures; Testing of internal controls; Review of board minutes and legal documents; Checking mathematical accuracy; Comparison with previous years' figures and budgets.
  • Vouching: Examination of original documents to verify authenticity and accuracy of transactions recorded. The voucher is the primary evidence. Items vouched: purchases, sales, wages, office expenses, rent, interest, dividend, etc. Vouching tests legitimacy, completeness, accuracy, and authorisation.
  • Internal Check vs Internal Control vs Internal Audit: Internal Check — automatic error detection via division of duties; Internal Control — system designed to safeguard assets and ensure reliability of records; Internal Audit — independent appraisal of control system performed regularly. All three complement each other.
  • Materiality in Auditing: Information is material if its omission or misstatement could influence economic decisions. Both quantitative and qualitative factors considered. Auditor sets materiality thresholds and evaluates uncorrected misstatements collectively.

Important facts

  • As per Companies Act 2013, statutory audit is mandatory for every company. The auditor must report on financial statements within prescribed time.
  • Income Tax Audit u/s 44AB is mandatory if turnover exceeds Rs. 1 crore (Rs. 10 crore for qualifying businesses), or profit is shown less than prescribed percentages.
  • Cost Audit is mandatory for specified industries as per Companies (Cost Records and Audit) Rules.
  • GST Audit is required under Section 35(5) read with Rule 80 of CGST Act for persons whose turnover exceeds Rs. 2 crore.
  • AS 2 prohibits LIFO method for inventory valuation. Only FIFO or Weighted Average is allowed.
  • Under AS 10, self-constructed fixed assets are measured at cost similar to purchased assets.
  • Contingent Assets are not recognised in financial statements but disclosed in notes unless realization is virtually certain (AS 29).
  • Borrowing Costs directly attributable to acquisition/construction of qualifying asset are capitalised (AS 16).
  • Related Party disclosures (AS 18) require disclosure of name, nature of relationship, and details of transactions with related parties.
  • The balance sheet always balances because of the dual aspect concept — Assets must equal Liabilities plus Owner's Equity.
  • Suspense Account is used temporarily when trial balance does not agree; all unrectified errors affecting single side are posted here initially.
  • Compen­sating errors do not affect the trial balance agreement because they offset each other mathematically.
  • Net Realisable Value is estimated selling price less estimated costs of completion and costs necessary to make the sale.
  • In Straight Line Method, depreciation remains constant every year; in Written Down Value Method, it decreases every year.
  • Auditor's Report under Companies Act contains qualifications, adverse remarks, or clean opinion based on findings.
  • Statutory Auditors are appointed at First AGM and hold office till conclusion of Sixth AGM, subject to reappointment.
  • Internal Audit reports are submitted to management, unlike statutory auditors who report to shareholders.
  • AS 1 requires disclosure of significant accounting policies adopted for preparation and presentation of financial statements.
  • Key auditor responsibilities include planning audit, assessing risk of material misstatement, obtaining sufficient appropriate audit evidence, and forming opinion.

Memory aids

  • Mnemonic for Accounting Concepts: GEMS CAR — Going Concern, Entity, Money Measurement, Accounting Period, Consistency, Accrual, Realisation.
  • Mnemonic for Rules of Accounts — GAR: Giver gets Credit (Personal), Receiver gets Debit; Ashram gives Credit (Real — Asset incoming), Ashram receives Debit; Expenses & Losses always Debit, Incomes & Gains always Credit.
  • Golden Rules shortcut: DEAR — Debit Expenses, Assets, Drawings; Credit Incomes, Liabilities, Revenue/Gains. Remember: 'Dearest Income, Liabilities'.
  • For error rectification: 'Wrong but same side → Suspense'; 'Wrong side both → Reverse and redo'; 'Capital written off as expense → Capital A/c Dr, P&L A/c Cr'.
  • For audit types: 'STICCF' — Statutory, Tax, Internal, Cost, Concurrent, Forensic. Remember: 'Scientists Test Inside Chemical Composites Carefully'.
  • For inventory methods: FIFO = First In First Out (oldest stock sold first); LIFO banned. Use mnemonic 'First Flour Out' — like bread, oldest goes first.
  • For cash flow categories: 'OIF' — Operating, Investing, Financing. Think: 'Our Investment Fund' drives cash flows.
  • To remember AS coverage: AS-1 Policies, AS-2 Inventory (FIFO/WA), AS-3 Cash Flow, AS-4 Contingencies, AS-6 Depreciation, AS-7 Construction, AS-10 FA, AS-14 Goodwill, AS-15 Employee Ben, AS-16 Borrowing, AS-17 Segment, AS-18 RP, AS-29 Provisions, AS-32 Revenue.

Exam tips

  • For journal entry questions: always write date format, ledger folio if asked, and ensure debit equals credit. One mark is often lost for missing narration if specifically asked.
  • In error rectification problems: first determine type of error, then decide whether suspense account is involved. Show journal entries clearly with proper debits and credits.
  • For depreciation: identify whether SLM or WDV method is asked. Write formula before substitution. Show workings stepwise to secure partial marks.
  • In audit-related MCQs: focus on distinguishing types of audit and their legal requirements. Read the question carefully to see if it asks about objectives, scope, or report contents.
  • When answering accounting standards questions: recall number-wise mapping. Use mnemonics developed. For numerical standards, remember key limits like materiality, recognition criteria, disclosure requirements.
  • For final accounts: remember adjustments must be applied to both Trading/P&L and Balance Sheet where relevant. Common adjustment traps: outstanding expenses, prepaid income, depreciation on new assets, closing stock valuation.
  • In internal control/internal audit questions: match the concept correctly. Keywords matter — 'preventive' = internal check, 'appraisal' = internal audit, 'statutory obligation' = external/statutory audit.
  • Always eliminate obviously wrong options first. For MCQ-based questions, look for absolute words like 'always', 'never', 'only' — these are often incorrect in accounting context.
  • Practice double-entry logic for all theory questions. Even MCQs can be solved by applying golden rules systematically.
  • Time management: Quick Revision means focusing on high-weightage areas — journal, ledger, TB, final accounts adjustments, depreciation, errors rectification, audit types, and important AS numbers. Don't ignore any sub-topic entirely.

Common traps

  • Confusing Accounting Principles with Accounting Standards — Principles are general assumptions/guidelines derived from practice; Standards are specific mandatory pronouncements issued by ICAI/ICSI.
  • Thinking LIFO is permissible under current Indian Accounting Standards — it is expressly prohibited. Only FIFO and Weighted Average are allowed.
  • Recording purchase of machinery on credit as a Purchase entry — it should be debited to Machinery A/c (Real Account), not Purchases A/c (Nominal). This is an Error of Principle.
  • Forgetting that contingent liabilities are disclosed but never recognised in the balance sheet, while contingent assets are only disclosed if probable, never recognised.
  • Mixing up Internal Check with Internal Audit — Internal Check is preventive via duty segregation; Internal Audit is detective/appraisive conducted periodically by internal staff.
  • Treating depreciation as a valuation account — it is an allocation of cost, not a valuation mechanism. Book value after depreciation does not necessarily reflect market value.
  • Confusing materiality threshold with a fixed numerical limit — materiality is qualitative AND quantitative; immaterial items may still need disclosure if nature warrants.
  • Assuming all errors affect Trial Balance — Error of Omission, Commission, Principle, and Compensating Errors do NOT affect TB agreement.
  • Recording bad debt recovery by crediting Bad Debts A/c instead of Profit and Loss A/c — correct treatment: Bank Dr, P&L Cr (income recovered).
  • Not capitalising borrowing costs for qualifying assets under AS-16 — borrowing costs on general working capital are expensed, but those on qualifying assets are capitalised.
  • Treating GST payable/receivable as income or expense — GST is a transit element and should be recorded separately, not booked to P&L.
  • Misunderstanding net realisable value — it is selling price LESS costs to sell, NOT cost less selling price. Confusion here leads to wrong inventory valuation.
