Business Management, Ethics and Entrepreneurship — Quick Revision

CSEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Management: The process of planning, organising, staffing, directing/leading, and controlling organisational resources to achieve defined objectives efficiently and effectively.
  • Efficiency: Achieving organisational goals with minimum use of resources (doing things right).
  • Effectiveness: Achieving the intended goals or outcomes regardless of resource use (doing the right things).
  • Entrepreneur: A person who identifies business opportunities, organises resources, bears risk, and creates value through innovative ventures.
  • Entrepreneurship: The process of designing, launching, and running a new business, characterised by innovation, risk-bearing, and opportunity recognition.
  • Business Ethics: The system of moral principles and values that guide behaviour in the world of business; distinguishing right from wrong in professional conduct.
  • Corporate Governance: The framework of rules, practices, and processes by which a company is directed and controlled, ensuring accountability, fairness, and transparency.
  • CSR (Corporate Social Responsibility): A business approach where companies integrate social and environmental concerns into operations and stakeholder interactions, beyond legal compliance.
  • Delegation: The assignment of authority and responsibility by a superior to a subordinate to carry out specific activities on their behalf.
  • Span of Control: The number of subordinates directly reporting to a single manager/supervisor.

Important facts

  • Henri Fayol is known as the Father of Modern Management; he proposed 14 principles and the five functions of management.
  • Frederick Taylor is known as the Father of Scientific Management; his key contribution is Time and Motion Study for efficiency.
  • Mary Parker Follett introduced the concept of 'power with' rather than 'power over'; she emphasised integration as a conflict resolution method.
  • Elton Mayo conducted the Hawthorne Studies (1924–1932) which established that social and psychological factors affect productivity.
  • The term 'entrepreneur' was originally used by Richard Cantillon (18th century); later developed by Jean-Baptiste Say.
  • Drucker popularised the concept of management by objectives (MBO).
  • Under Companies Act, 2013, Section 135 mandates CSR spending; Section 177 deals with Audit Committee; Section 178 deals with Nomination and Remuneration Committee.
  • SEBI (Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2015 govern corporate governance for listed entities.
  • The term 'Corporate Governance' was first used at the 1994 European Business Summit.
  • PESTLE Analysis examines Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal, and Environmental factors in the external environment.
  • Budgetary and non-budgetary controls: Budgetary include budgets, standard costing, break-even analysis; non-budgetary include direct observation, periodic reports, swot analysis.
  • Gandhi's 7 Social Sins: Wealth without work, Politics without principle, Commerce without morality, Science without humanity, Knowledge without character, Pleasure without conscience, Religion without sacrifice.

Memory aids

  • POSDCAC for functions: Plan, Organise, Staff, Direct, Control, Coordinate, Actuate — mnemonic 'Please Only Send Direct Copies Carefully'.
  • Fayol's 14 Principles — group them: Division of Work, Authority-Responsibility, Discipline, Unity of Command/Direction, Subordination, Remuneration, Centralisation, Scalar Chain, Order, Equity, Stability, Initiative, Esprit de Corps.
  • Maslow's hierarchy from bottom to top: Physiological, Safety, Social, Esteem, Self-actualisation — remember 'Please Stop Sending Expensive Shoes'.
  • SWOT — internal = Strengths, Weaknesses; external = Opportunities, Threats. 'SW is Inside, OT is Outside'.
  • Types of entrepreneurs: Innovative (I), Imitative (I), Fabian (F), Drone (D) — 'I Fed Ice Cream Donkeys'.
  • Line, Staff, Functional authority — Line (command), Staff (advise), Functional (limited specialist).
  • CSR 2% rule — link to 2% of average net profits under Section 135.

Exam tips

  • CS Foundation Business Management questions are predominantly conceptual and definition-based. Focus on distinguishing between similar terms (e.g., efficiency vs effectiveness, Theory X vs Theory Y, centralisation vs delegation).
  • Memorise authors and their contributions: Fayol (principles/functions), Taylor (scientific management), Mayo (Hawthorne studies), Maslow (hierarchy), Herzberg (two-factor), Drucker (MBO). These are frequently tested.
  • For CSR and Corporate Governance, remember specific section numbers of the Companies Act, 2013 — Section 135 (CSR), Section 177 (Audit Committee), Section 178 (Nomination & Remuneration Committee), Section 134(5)(e) (board report including sustainability).
  • Practice matching questions: match management thinker with their theory/contribution, match leadership style with其特征, match departmentation with examples.
  • When a question asks about 'first' or 'father/mother', these are common direct-recall formats — memorise the key names.
  • For Entrepreneurship questions, focus on characteristics and types — MCQs often ask to identify the type from a given scenario description.
  • Ethics questions may present a situation and ask for the most ethical course of action — choose the option that promotes stakeholder welfare, transparency, and legal compliance.
  • Pay attention to 'NOT' questions (e.g., which is NOT a principle of management) — these are designed to test precise knowledge rather than general familiarity.
  • Review PESTLE and SWOT applications: know which factors are internal vs external, and which are controllable vs uncontrollable.

Common traps

  • Confusing Efficiency with Effectiveness: Efficiency = doing things right (resource use); Effectiveness = doing the right things (goal achievement). Both are needed.
  • Confusing Delegation with Departure of authority: Authority CAN be delegated but ACCOUNTABILITY CANNOT. The delegator remains answerable.
  • Confusing Unity of Command with Unity of Direction: Unity of Command = one boss per employee; Unity of Direction = one plan per objective.
  • Thinking Theory X is always negative: Theory X describes a management assumption about workers being lazy — it is a descriptive theory, not an endorsement.
  • Hygiene factors vs Motivators: Hygiene factors (pay, conditions) prevent dissatisfaction but do NOT motivate; only motivators (achievement, recognition) create satisfaction.
  • Centralisation does not mean all decisions at top: It is a matter of degree; even in centralised organisations some decisions are decentralised.
  • Span of control and hierarchy relationship: Wide span = flat structure (fewer levels); Narrow span = tall structure (more levels). This is often reversed in MCQ options.
  • CSR threshold: Remember it is based on net worth, turnover, OR net profit — any ONE criterion triggers the obligation.
  • Stakeholder vs Shareholder: Stakeholders include ALL affected parties (employees, community, environment); shareholders are only owners/investors. Corporate governance serves both but balances interests.
  • Fabian entrepreneurs are not lazy — they are cautious and rational, adopting change only when compelled by circumstances.
