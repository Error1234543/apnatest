Business Economics — CS Foundation Quick Revision

CSEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Business Economics: Application of economic theory and methods to business decision-making and planning; bridges microeconomics (individual firm/consumer behaviour) and macroeconomics (economy-wide variables).
  • Demand: The quantity of a commodity that consumers are willing and able to purchase at various prices during a given period of time.
  • Supply: The quantity of a commodity that producers are willing and able to offer for sale at various prices during a given period of time.
  • Elasticity of Demand: Measure of responsiveness of quantity demanded to a change in any one of the determinants of demand (price, income, prices of related goods, etc.).
  • Perfect Competition: A market structure with a large number of buyers and sellers, homogeneous product, free entry and exit, perfect information, and price-taker behaviour.
  • Monopoly: A market structure with a single seller, no close substitutes, barriers to entry, and price-maker power.
  • Oligopoly: A market structure dominated by a small number of large firms, with interdependent decision-making and significant barriers to entry.
  • GDP (Gross Domestic Product): Total market value of all final goods and services produced within the domestic territory of a country during an accounting year.
  • GNP (Gross National Product): GDP plus net factor income from abroad (income earned by residents from overseas minus income earned by foreigners domestically).
  • NNP (Net National Product at Market Price): GNP minus depreciation (capital consumption allowance); NNP at Factor Cost = NNP at MP – Indirect Taxes + Subsidies.
  • Aggregate Demand (AD): Total expenditure in the economy, AD = C + I + G + (X – M), where C = consumption, I = investment, G = government spending, X = exports, M = imports.
  • Fiscal Deficit: Excess of total government expenditure over total government receipts (excluding borrowings).
  • Revenue Deficit: Excess of revenue expenditure over revenue receipts; indicates government is borrowing to fund day-to-day operations.
  • Money Supply (M1): Currency with public + Demand deposits with banks + Other deposits with RBI; narrowest commonly used measure.
  • Repo Rate: Rate at which the central bank lends short-term funds to commercial banks against securities; a key monetary policy tool.

Important facts

  • Law of Demand was formally stated by Alfred Marshall in 'Principles of Economics' (1890), though the concept existed earlier.
  • Demand curve is normally downward sloping due to diminishing marginal utility (Gossen's First Law).
  • The coefficient of price elasticity of demand is NEGATIVE in sign (because price and quantity move in opposite directions), but conventionally stated as absolute value.
  • A vertically parallel demand curve to the Y-axis means perfectly inelastic demand (Ed = 0) — quantity does not change regardless of price.
  • A horizontal demand curve parallel to X-axis means perfectly elastic demand (Ed = ∞) — any price increase causes demand to drop to zero.
  • Diminishing marginal returns set in after the point of maximum total product; marginal product becomes zero at maximum total product and negative thereafter.
  • In short run, at least one factor is fixed (typically capital/land); in long run, all factors are variable.
  • Shutdown point in short run: price = minimum AVC. Below this, firm shuts down; above but below ATC, firm continues operating at loss to cover some fixed costs.
  • Long-run equilibrium in perfect competition: P = MC = MR = min ATC (productive efficiency); in monopoly: P > MC (allocative inefficiency).
  • CRR in India was 4% (recently revised periodically — always check latest RBI notification for exact figure in current exam context).
  • RBI was established on 1 April 1935 and nationalised on 1 January 1949.
  • SEBI (Securities and Exchange Board of India) was established in 1988 (statutory power in 1992) — regulates securities market.
  • GST (Goods and Services Tax) was introduced in India w.e.f. 1 July 2017 — destination-based indirect tax replacing multiple cascading taxes.
  • Budget is presented annually on 1 February (from 2017 onwards; earlier it was last day of January) by the Finance Minister.
  • NITI Aayog (National Institution for Transforming India) replaced Planning Commission on 1 January 2015.
  • Simpson's Rule or trapezium rule is NOT part of this syllabus — keep focus on economic concepts only.

Memory aids

  • FOGTIP — Demand Shifters: Foreign prices, Other goods' prices, Government policy, Tastes/preferences, Income, Population.
  • TRICKSUP — Supply Shifters: Technology, Raw material/input costs, Indirect taxes/subsidies, Costs (wages/rent), Number of firms, Uncertainty/expectations, Prices of related goods, Government policy.
  • Cost Minimization Mantra: MC cuts AC at its minimum point. When MC < AC → AC falls; MC > AC → AC rises; MC = AC → AC is flat (minimum).
  • Market Structure Cheat Sheet: Perfect Comp (many, homogeneous, P=MR, zero LP profit) | Monopoly (one, unique, P>MR, LP super-normal) | Oligopoly (few, interdependent, kinked demand) | Monopolistic Comp (many, differentiated, zero LP profit but excess capacity).
  • National Income Chain: GDPmp → GDPfc (–IT+Sub) → GNPmp (+NFIA) → NNPmp (–Dep) → NNPfc (= National Income, –IT+Sub) → PI (–CorpTax–Retained+Transfer) → DI (–DirectTax).
  • Multiplier: Spending Multiplier = 1/MPS; Tax Multiplier = –MPC/MPS. Always remember: Spending Multiplier > Tax Multiplier in absolute value.
  • Money Aggregates: M1 = currency + demand deposits (narrow); M3 = M1 + time deposits (broad). Easy: M1 is 'in your pocket and account', M3 includes 'locked away' deposits.
  • RBI Monetary Tools rhyme: 'Repo goes up, money goes down' — raising repo rate contracts money supply (contractionary); lowering it expands money supply.

Exam tips

  • CS Foundation Business Economics questions are mostly direct conceptual MCQs. Focus on definitions, distinctions, and formula-based numericals (especially elasticity and multiplier calculations).
  • When asked about the shape of a demand/supply curve, always note the axes labels — vertical axis = Price, horizontal axis = Quantity. Reversed axes flip the visual interpretation.
  • For numerical problems on elasticity: carefully identify whether percentage change is calculated on original values or midpoint values. Use the formula exactly as given in your textbook.
  • In cost theory numericals, remember: MC = ΔTC/ΔQ = ΔTVC/ΔQ (since TFC doesn't change with Q). This shortcut saves time.
  • When comparing market structures, use a table format in your mind: number of firms, product nature, entry barriers, long-run profit, price-making power, efficiency.
  • National Income conversion questions are frequent — memorise the chain: GDPmp → GDPfc → GNPmp → NNPmp → NNPfc. Practice conversions in both directions.
  • For monetary policy questions, know whether a tool is quantitative (repo rate, CRR, SLR) or qualitative (margin requirement, moral suasion).
  • Eliminate options by checking for absolute words like 'always', 'never', 'only' — economic laws have exceptions, so such statements are usually incorrect.
  • In demand-schedule/table questions, verify that quantity demanded consistently decreases as price increases — any exception in the table signals a trick question.
  • Time management: Business Economics is scoring if you know concepts cleanly. Spend max 2-3 minutes per question. Flag numericals you're unsure of and return later.

Common traps

  • Confusing movement along the demand curve (caused by price change of the same good) with a SHIFT of the demand curve (caused by change in any non-price determinant like income or tastes). Exam questions frequently test this distinction.
  • Mixing up Income Elasticity sign: Positive Ey = normal good; Negative Ey = inferior good. Students often reverse this.
  • Assuming every firm in imperfect competition has a horizontal demand curve. Only perfect competition has a perfectly elastic (horizontal) firm demand curve. All other market structures have downward-sloping demand curves.
  • Confusing Accounting Profit (TR – TC including explicit costs) with Economic Profit (TR – TC including explicit AND implicit/opportunity costs). Economic profit ≤ Accounting profit.
  • Thinking that in long run, all firms in all market structures earn ZERO profit. In monopoly, super-normal profits persist in long run due to barriers to entry.
  • Wrongly assuming GNP is always greater than GDP. GNP > GDP only when NFIA is positive (India earns more from abroad than foreigners earn in India); otherwise GDP > GNP.
  • Treating Fiscal Deficit and Revenue Deficit as the same. Revenue deficit = Revenue Exp – Revenue Receipts. Fiscal deficit = Total Exp – (Total Receipts excl. borrowings).
  • Forgetting that in the Value Added Method, the value of INTERMEDIATE goods is excluded to avoid DOUBLE COUNTING. Only value added at each stage is counted.
  • Mixing up CRR and SLR: CRR is cash reserve kept with RBI (earning no interest); SLR is liquid assets kept by banks themselves (like government securities). Both constrain lending capacity but differently.
  • Confusing MPC (Marginal Propensity to Consume) and MPS (Marginal Propensity to Save): MPC + MPS = 1 always. Confusing these makes multiplier calculations wrong.
  • Assuming the supply curve is always upward sloping. Labor supply curve can be backward-bending at high wage levels; short-run supply of perishable goods can be perfectly inelastic.
