Definitions

  • Business Environment: The aggregate of all individuals, institutions, factors, and forces outside an enterprise that affect its performance, decisions, and strategic choices.
  • Social Responsibility of Business: The obligation of business enterprises to pursue policies and actions that benefit society beyond statutory compliance, balancing economic goals with ethical and social considerations.
  • Consumer Protection: The legal and institutional framework designed to safeguard consumers against unfair trade practices, defective goods, and deficient services, ensuring redressal of grievances.
  • Competition Law: Legal provisions aimed at preventing anti-competitive agreements, abuse of dominant position, and regulating combinations to maintain fair market competition.
  • Negotiable Instrument: A document enabling transfer of a certain sum of money from one person to another, where the transferee becomes entitled to receive the amount; it must be freely transferable by delivery or endorsement.
  • Society: A group of individuals united by permanent relations sharing common interests, traditions, and customs, with an objective of achieving common purposes.
  • Business Environment Factors: Internal (controllable) and external (uncontrollable) factors including political, economic, social, technological, legal, and environmental dimensions.
  • Limited Liability Partnership (LLP): A hybrid business structure combining features of partnership and company — partners enjoy limited liability while retaining managerial flexibility.

Key concepts

  • Components of Business Environment: ['Political Environment: Government stability, policies, and legislative framework affecting business operations and strategy.', 'Economic Environment: Fiscal and monetary policies, inflation rates, GDP growth, taxation regime, and market conditions.', 'Social Environment: Demographic patterns, cultural values, education levels, consumer attitudes, and lifestyle trends.', 'Technological Environment: Innovation pace, R&D investment, digital transformation, automation, and intellectual property landscape.', 'Legal Environment: Statutory compliance requirements, regulatory frameworks, labor laws, environmental regulations, and consumer protection norms.', 'Environmental/Green Environment: Sustainability obligations, carbon footprint management, climate change policies, and green business practices.']
  • Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR): ['CSR represents the voluntary commitment of companies to contribute to socio-economic development beyond profit maximization.', 'Under the Companies Act 2013 (Section 135), certain companies must spend at least 2% of average net profits on CSR activities.', 'Key CSR areas include eradicating hunger, promoting education, gender equality, environmental sustainability, rural development, and healthcare.', 'Unspent CSR amounts must be transferred to a specified fund within 30 days if unutilized during the financial year.', 'CSR governance requires a three-member CSR Committee on the Board for eligible companies.']
  • Consumer Protection Framework (CPA 2019): ['The Consumer Protection Act 2019 replaces the 1986 Act and strengthens consumer rights enforcement in India.', 'Six fundamental consumer rights: Right to Safety, Right to Be Informed, Right to Choose, Right to Be Heard, Right to Seek Redressal, Right to Consumer Education.', 'E-filing of complaints is now permitted through the online complaint registration system.', 'Mediation is introduced as an alternative dispute resolution mechanism before conducting proceedings.', 'Product liability action allows consumers to claim compensation for harm caused by defective products.', 'Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA) has been established with powers to investigate violations and issue cease-and-desist orders.', 'Penalties for misleading advertisements and product defects have been significantly enhanced.']
  • Competition Law (Competition Act 2002): ['The Competition Commission of India (CCI) is the adjudicatory body that enforces the Act.', 'Anti-competitive agreements (Section 3): Horizontal agreements (cartels, price-fixing, market allocation) are presumed to cause appreciable adverse effect on competition (AAEC). Vertical agreements are evaluated under rule of reason.', 'Abuse of dominant position (Section 4): Includes imposing discriminatory prices, limiting production, denying market access, and tying arrangements by dominant enterprises.', 'Combination regulation (Sections 5 & 6): Mergers, amalgamations, and acquisitions above threshold limits require prior approval from CCI.', 'Penalties for contravention can extend up to 10% of average turnover for three preceding years.']
  • Nature and Classification of Contracts: ['A contract is an agreement enforceable by law — every agreement becomes a contract when made lawful by consideration and free consent.', 'Classification: Valid, Void, Voidable, Illegal, and Quasi-contracts (Sections 68–75).', 'Essential elements: Offer and acceptance, lawful consideration, competent parties, free consent, lawful object, and certainty of terms.', 'Free consent requires absence of coercion, undue influence, fraud, misrepresentation, and mistake.', 'Consideration must be real, lawful, and move at the desire of the promisor — past consideration is generally invalid except in specific circumstances.']

  • Goods include all moveable property other than money and actionable claims; stock, shares, growing crops, and things attached to land are included.
  • Conditions vs Warranties: Conditions are essential terms whose breach permits repudiation; warranties are collateral terms whose breach gives rise only to damages.
  • Sale vs Agreement to Sell: Sale transfers ownership immediately; agreement to sell transfers ownership at a future date or upon fulfillment of conditions.
  • Nemo dat quod non habet: A seller cannot transfer better title than they possess — exceptions include sale by mercantile agent, sale under voidable title, and sale by purchaser in possession.
  • Unpaid seller remedies: Right of lien, right of stoppage in transit, and right of resale.
