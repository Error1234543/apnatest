Tax Laws and Practice — Quick Revision | CS Executive Group 2

CSEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Income: Every receipt of money or money's worth that falls within the natural meaning of 'income' as interpreted by the Supreme Court in Carson Burns Ltd v CIT — broad, untechnical meaning; includes both capital and revenue receipts unless specifically excluded.
  • Residential Status (Sec 6): An individual is Resident if (a) present in India ≥ 182 days in preceding FY, OR (b) present ≥ 60 days in preceding FY AND ≥ 365 days in 4 preceding FYs. For departure from India during a year, the 60-day period is extended to 182 days. NRIs are Non-Residents.
  • Assessable Income / Total Income (Sec 80): Total income means all income received or deemed received under the Act, computed according to provisions thereof.
  • Assessment Year (Sec 2(9)): The period commencing on 1st April of every year and ending on 31st March of the next year — during which income of the Previous Year is assessed and tax is charged.
  • Previous Year (Sec 3): The financial year immediately preceding the Assessment Year in which tax is payable. For normal cases, it is the FY prior to AY.
  • Goods and Services Tax (GST): A comprehensive, multi-stage, destination-based indirect tax levied on supply of goods and services, subsuming most indirect taxes. Governed by Constitution (101st Amendment) Act, 2016; enacted via CGST Act, SGST/UTGST Act, and IGST Act.
  • Customs Duty: A tax levied on import and export of goods under the Customs Act, 1962. Includes Basic Customs Duty (BCD), Integrated GST (IGST) on imports, and social welfare surcharge.
  • Tax Deducted at Source (TDS): Mechanism under Chapter XVII-B (Secs 192-206B) where the payer deducts tax at prescribed rates before making specified payments, remitting to government, and issuing TDS certificates.
  • Advance Tax (Sec 207): Tax payable in instalments during the FY itself when estimated tax liability exceeds Rs. 10,000 in that FY.
  • Input Tax Credit (Sec 16, CGST Act): Right of a registered person to deduct the GST already paid on inputs/input services from the output GST liability, preventing cascading.
  • Place of Supply: Determines whether an transaction is intra-state or inter-state, thereby deciding applicability of CGST+SGST vs IGST. Varies by nature of supply (goods, services, immovable property).
  • Registered Person (GST): A person registered under Section 22 (compulsory registration) or voluntarily under Section 25 of the CGST Act, and who holds a valid GSTIN.

Key concepts

  • HEADS OF INCOME (Sec 14): All incomes are classified under five heads: (i) Salary — wages, pension, gratuity, perquisites, profits in lieu of salary (Sec 17); (ii) House Property — annual value of house property (let or not let) with deductions for municipal taxes and 30% standard deduction; (iii) Profits and Gains of Business or Profession — gross profits after allowable/deductible expenses (Secs 30-43A); (iv) Capital Gains — short-term (Sec 45, held < 36 months generally; 12 months for listed securities) and long-term, with special provisions for indexation; (v) Income from Other Sources — residuary head (interest, dividends, winnings, rent from machinery, etc.) after deduction u/s 57.
  • COMPUTATION OF TOTAL INCOME — KEY DEDUCTIONS (Chapter VI-A, Secs 80C-80U): Sec 80C (₹1.5 lakh limit) — life insurance premium, EPF, PPF, NSC, tuition fees, 5-year锁 EDLL, home loan principal repayment, unit-linked insurance plans. Sec 80D — health insurance premiums (self, family, parents) with enhanced limits for senior citizens. Sec 80E — interest on education loan (no ceiling, only interest component). Sec 80G — donations to approved funds, subject to percentage-based qualifying limits. Sec 80TTA/80TTB — savings account interest (up to Rs. 10,000; for senior citizens u/s 80TTB up to Rs. 50,000). Sec 80JJAA — employment of new employees (30% of salaries paid). Sec 80EEA — additional deduction for first-time home-buyer interest up to Rs. 1.5 lakh.
  • TDS UNDER INCOME TAX (SELECT RATES): Sec 192 — salary at applicable slab rates. Sec 194A — interest (other than on securities) 10% (15% if PAN not furnished); deposit with bank/Post Office exempt. Sec 194C — contractor/payment to sub-contractor 1% (individual/HUF) or 2% (others); 10% if PAN not furnished. Sec 194H — commission/brokerage 5%. Sec 194J — professional/technical fees 10%; director's fee 10%. Sec 194I — royalty 10%, rent on plant/machinery/furniture/equipment 2% (individual/HUF) or 5% (others); 10% for rent on land/building/floor. Sec 194Q — purchase of goods by buyer above threshold 0.1% (seller's PAN available; otherwise 5%). Sec 194T — dividend to members/FVCI 10% (post Budget 2020).
  • ADVANCE TAX (Sec 207-211): Payable when estimated tax liability ≥ Rs. 10,000 in a FY. Slab dates: (a) on or before 15th June — 15% of estimated tax; (b) on or before 15th Sept — 45%; (c) on or before 15th Dec — 75%; (d) on or before 15th March — 100%. Under-survey interest u/s 234B (1% p.m. on shortfall) and interest u/s 234C for deferment of instalment tax.
  • PROVISIONS RELATED TO FILING OF RETURN: Sec 139 — regular return, belated return (by 31st Dec of AY or before completion of assessment, whichever earlier), revised return u/s 139(5). Sec 141-142 — scrutiny, production of evidence. Sec 143(1) — intimation after computation. Notice u/s 148 for reassessment if income escaped assessment. Default penalties u/s 270A for under-reporting/misreporting.
  • GST STRUCTURE AND HIERARCHY: Dual GST — Centre levies CGST + IGST; States levy SGST/UTGST. Intra-state supply → CGST + SGST. Inter-state supply → IGST. Union territories (without legislature) → UTGST. GST Council under Art 279A — chaired by Union FM, decides rates, thresholds, exemptions, and procedural matters. Composition scheme (Sec 10) — eligible taxpayers pay @ 1% (intra-state), 2% (inter-state) with no input credit; turnover ≤ Rs. 1.5 crore (₹75,000 for special category states; increased thresholds may apply as per notifications). E-way bill requirement u/s 31A for movement of goods above Rs. 50,000 value.
  • INPUT TAX CREDIT RULES (Sec 16-18, CGST Act): ITC can be claimed by (i) possession of tax invoice/credit note; (ii) supply received; (iii) GST paid is deposited with government u/s 39; (iv) return filed u/s 39. Blocked credits u/s 17(5) include motor vehicles (unless specified business), food/beverages, construction of immovable property (except when used for outward taxable supply), personal use goods/services, goods lost/stolen, and construction of premises for own use. Reverse charge mechanism (Sec 9(4)) applies where notified suppliers supply to GST-registered persons.
  • GST INVOLVING SUPPLY WITHOUT VALUE AND WITH VALUE: Schedule II para 5 defines supply of goods and services composite/mixed supplies. Composite supply — two or more supplies naturally bundled, supplied together in ordinary course of business; the principal supply determines taxability. Mixed supply — two or more supplies offered together in course of business with no natural bundling; highest rate applies.
  • PLACE OF SUPPLY RULES (SELECT): For immovable property — location of property. For service provided to real estate — location of service relates to immovable property. For restaurant services — place where food is actually consumed. For ticketed events — location of event. For transport of goods — destination. For goods not involving transport — location where goods are supplied. For accommodation services — location of facility.
  • CUSTOMS DUTY BASICS: Basic Customs Duty (BCD) — duty charged on imported/exported goods under Customs Tariff Act, 1975. Additional duty of customs equal to aggregate of Excise Duty (Section 3(1)) plus SAD (Special Additional Duty, Section 3(2)) — this equals countervailing duty (CVD/SAD). Social Welfare Surcharge 10% on total duty. Valuation u/s 14 read with Customs Valuation (Determination of Value of Imported Goods) Rules, 2007 — transaction value is primary basis. Anti-dumping duty (ADD), Countervailing duty (CVD), and Safeguard duty are compensatory/excise duties.
  • APPELLATE STRUCTURE IN TAX LAW (INDICATIVE): Income Tax — ITAT (first appellate), High Court (on question of law), Supreme Court. GST — Appellate Tribunal (GSTAP), High Court, Supreme Court. Customs — GCIS (First Appellate Authority), Customs, Excise and Service Tax Appellate Tribunal (CESTAT), High Court.
  • KEY CASE LAWS FOR REFERENCE: Carson Burns Ltd v CIT — broad interpretation of income. Godfrey Phillips India Ltd v CIT — perquisites valuation. CIT v Tata Engineering & Locomotive Co — perquisites for non-giving employers. S. Visvesvaraya v CIT — development bonus as revenue receipt. CIT v Calcutta Discount Co — test of recurring vs non-recurring. For GST: State of Andhra Pradesh v Coromandel Fertilizers — constitutional validity of GST; FedEx India v State of Karnataka — GST vs Service Tax confusion pre-GST; Union of India v Haldiram — GST composition scheme issue.

Important facts

  • Section 4 of Income Tax Act: Charge of income-tax is made annually by the Income-tax Act for each Assessment Year, in respect of total income of the previous year of every person.
  • Section 5: Scope of total income depends on residential status — resident is taxed on global income; non-resident on income received/deemed received/accrued/arisen in India or from any source outside India.
  • Section 17 definition of 'Salary' includes: wages, pension, annuity, profit in lieu of salary, fees/perks, contributions to recognised provident fund, gratuity, notice pay, termination compensation.
  • Section 16: Deductions from Salary — statutory contribution to employee's RPF (deductible in full), entertainment allowance (only for govt employees), professional tax (paid to State).
  • Section 24: House Property deductions — standard deduction 30% of annual value; interest on borrowed capital — self-occupied up to Rs. 2 lakh (Rs. 30,000 pre-Budget 1989-90); pre-construction period interest is allowed in 5 equal annual instalments starting from PY of completion.
  • Section 80C to 80U: Overall ceiling under Chapter VI-A for Secs 80C-80T is ₹2 lakh for normal taxpayers (increased to ₹2.5 lakh only if 80CCD(1B) and 80D are included separately — but total of 80C-80U including 80CCD(1B) can exceed ₹2 lakh; the ₹2 lakh limit applies to sum of 80C, 80CCC, 80D, 80DD, 80DDB, 80E, 80G, 80GG, 80GGA, 80GGB, 80H to 80HH, 80RR, 80RRB, 80VV, 80WWD, 80WZE, 80YYC, 80YYYB as applicable)
  • Budget 2023 increased turnover threshold for GST composition scheme: ₹1.5 crore normal (₹75,000 for special category states); for GTSC, ₹2 crore normal (₹1.5 crore for special states). Threshold limits are subject to Government notifications from time to time.
  • GST Nil-rated supplies (Schedule III): sale of land, sale of building (differentiated from construction services), services by RBI, services by general insurance in co-operative form, funeral services, burial/cremation services, services towards passage of railway passengers.
  • GST Exempt via notification: agricultural services, healthcare services, educational services (up to pre-university level or certain higher ed), housing rental services, transport by metro/rail/bus/taxi, interest on government securities — these are core essentials that remain GST-exempt.
  • GST Rates: Most essential items like food grains, fresh vegetables/fruits, milk, eggs, flour, salt, unbranded edible oil at 0%. Common items (tea, coffee, sugar) @ 5%. Food, confectionery, Pan masala @ 18%. Gold @ 3%. Luxury/automobiles, sin goods @ 28% + cess. Special rate items like gems/jewellery, precious stones @ 0.25-3%.
  • GST Compensation Cess exists on demerit goods (cigarettes, aerated water, etc.) to compensate States for loss of revenue during the transition period. The Compensation Cess Act, 2017 governs this. Thecess rate varies — e.g., cigarettes @ 75% ad valorem based on average retail price.
  • TDS is deductible only by a person who has deducted the same. If a non-deductor (person not liable to deduct TDS) makes payment without deducting, the income is still chargeable in hands of payee, but there is no penal consequence on deductor side.
  • Section 194-IA: TDS @ 1% on transfer of immovable property exceeding Rs. 50 lakh — payable by buyer.
  • Section 194N: TDS @ 1% on withdrawal from co-op banks/payment to recipient if cumulative withdrawals exceed Rs. 20 lakh in a FY; however this was withdrawn w.e.f. 1-10-2020 with a threshold revert to earlier position.
  • Form No. 26AS is consolidated tax statement showing all TDS/TCS deducted against PAN; Form 16/A is TDS certificate for salary; Form 16B/16C for property sale and other transactions.
  • Revised return u/s 139(5) can be filed before end of relevant AY or completion of assessment, whichever is earlier. Belated return u/s 139(4) before end of AY or completion of assessment. Interest u/s 234A for delay in filing return (₹200/day w.e.f. FY 2022-23 — subject to change).
  • Section 245 — Power of Board to grant relief in cases of double taxation (subject to double taxation avoidance agreements).
  • GST Registration compulsory if aggregate turnover exceeds ₹40 lakh (₹20 lakh for normal, ₹10 lakh/₹5 lakh for special category states); inter-state supply regardless of turnover; casual taxable person; e-commerce operator requiring deduction TCS.
  • E-way bill u/s 31A: Required for movement of goods > ₹50,000; must be generated before movement; Form GST TRAN-1; valid period based on distance — 1 day per 200 km for road transport, 1 day per 200 km for rail/air/water.
  • Composition dealers cannot claim ITC, cannot make inter-state supply, cannot supply through e-commerce operator requiring TCS, and cannot file GSTR-1 (instead file GSTR-4 quarterly).

Memory aids

  • HEADS OF INCOME mnemonic — SHOCK: Salary, House Property, OCI (Other/Capital Gains) — K (Capital Gains) + P&G = 5 heads. Or: SH-CAP (Salary, House, Capital gains, Profits, Other sources).
  • TDS rates quick recall: Interest 194A = 10%, Contractor 194C = 1%/2%, Commission 194H = 5%, Professional fees 194J = 10%, Royalty/Rent 194I = 2%/5% (rent on plant/machinery 2%) / 10% (royalty). Goods purchase 194Q = 0.1%. Property 194-IA = 1%.
  • Advance Tax installments (15/45/75/100): Think 'June 15, Sep 45, Dec 75, Mar 100' — cumulative percentages rising. Remember by counting 15, 45 (+30), 75 (+30), 100 (+25).
  • GST dual structure: Centre = CGST + IGST; State = SGST. 'CGST-IGST for Centre, SGST for State' — Centre gets its share in every supply; State gets its share either as SGST (intra) or shares IGST (inter).
  • Blocked Credits under Sec 17(5): MOVIE — Motor vehicles, Outdoor food/dining, Immovable property (construction), Vacation homes (personal use), Entertainment services. Also remember: lost/stolen goods and services used for non-business purposes.
  • Place of supply quick guide: Land/building → where located. Restaurant → where food is consumed. Events → venue. Transport goods → destination. Accommodation → location of property. These three Rs rule: Real estate where situated, Restaurant where consumed, Events where held.
  • Chapter VI-A mnemonic — 80C to 80U: Remember the alphabet sequence. Key ones: C = investments (₹1.5L), D = health (varies), E = education loan (interest only), G = donations (50%/100% depending on category), TT = savings interest (₹10K/₹50K for senior), JJ = new employees (30% of salary).
  • GST Council constitutional backing: Article 279A inserted by 101st Amendment Act 2016. Council chaired by Union Finance Minister; members are State Finance Ministers. Decisions require 3/4 majority weighted — Centre has 1/3 vote, States have 2/3 combined.
  • Pre-GST vs Post-GST mapping: VAT replaced by GST on goods; Service Tax replaced by GST on services; Central Excise on manufactured goods largely subsumed; Entertainment tax (by State/Local bodies except cinema theatres) still outside GST; Taxes on lottery/gambling/stamping now under GST; Alcohol for human consumption still outside GST (GST to be extended later).
  • Tax audit u/s 44AB: Mandatory for business turnover exceeding ₹1 crore (₹10 crore if cash transactions are ≤ 5% of total turnover/ receipts). For profession, gross receipts exceeding ₹50 lakh (₹100 lakh if cash ≤ 5%). Deposit in PRADAN/PM CARES fund not counted as donation for u/s 80G.

Exam tips

  • For CS Executive, expect descriptive questions on head-wise income computation, TDS scenarios, GST composite/mixed supply distinctions, and place of supply application. Practice at least 5-7 numerical/computational problems per chapter covering salary computation with perquisites, house property with multiple houses, business profit with adjustments, and capital gains with indexation.
  • In GST, focus on: (a) identification of intra-state vs inter-state supply, (b) composite vs mixed supply problems, (c) ITC blocking u/s 17(5), (d) place of supply in service transactions, (e) GST on import/export. Use diagrammatic flowcharts for GST chain and credit mechanism.
  • Tax laws questions are often fact-based with scenario — practice applying provisions rather than rote memorising sections. ICSI values ability to apply law to practical situations; hence answer structure should be: (i) state relevant provision, (ii) analyse facts, (iii) conclude with reasoning.
  • Remember current limit changes from recent Budgets (as of July 2024). Budget 2023-24 did significant changes in GST thresholds and Section 80C deductions. Keep updated on amendments notified by CBIC/ICSI.
  • For TDS, prepare a consolidated table of sections, designated payments, deductor categories, deductee categories, rates, and thresholds. This helps in quick revision and answering scenario-based questions.
  • GST cases often involve overlap between CGST/SGST/IGST — in answers, explicitly mention the applicable Act(s) and draw a small table if needed showing CGST, SGST/IGST split along with amounts. Clarity in final computation earns full marks.
  • Important previous trends: frequently asked topics include (i) residential status determination (very common), (ii) computation of total income from salary or house property, (iii) TDS on various specified payments, (iv) GST place of supply and composition scheme issues, (v) difference between GST exempt and nil-rated supplies.
  • Always cite section numbers and case law (where relevant) in answers. ICSI values precision in citation. Where uncertain about a section number, write the legal principle accurately — partial credit is better than no attempt.
  • Revision technique: create one-page formula/limit sheets per topic — e.g., one page for TDS rates, one for deduction u/s 80C to 80U, one for GST rates/exceptions, one for place of supply rules. These serve as last-minute quick reference.
  • Do not ignore the 'practice' portion — solve past papers and ICSI specimen questions. The examination tests application speed; hence time yourself while solving computational problems (budget 15-18 minutes per 10-mark question).

Common traps

  • Perquisites in kind for non-labour employees: Perquisite value in hands of employer not deductible u/s 37(1). Carson Burns principle applied — perquisites given by employer (not labour) is deductible only if wholly and exclusively for business. Case law: Godfrey Phillips India Ltd v CIT (perquisite valuation for non-labour employer is not disallowed u/s 37(1)).
  • Capital vs Revenue distinction in losses: Loss on sale of fixed asset (capital) is not deductible under P&G head; only trading losses/bad debts or depreciation are allowed. Exception: if it is a stock-in-trade sold, the loss is revenue.
  • Interest on overdue tax vs TDS: Interest u/s 234B is payable on unpaid advance tax; interest u/s 234C on shortfall in instalment. Do not confuse with penalty u/s 271AA/BB/CC which may also apply on non-compliance of TDS provisions.
  • TDS rates often tested with exceptions: For Sec 194C, 1% for individual/HUF contractors; 2% for others — NOT the same as usual 10% section. Similarly, Sec 194J professional fees is 10%, but technical fees also 10%. Sec 194I — rent on plant/machinery is 2% (not 10%), but royalty is 10%. These differential rates are heavily examined.
  • GST Composite vs Mixed supply: Mistake is confusing mixed supply with composite supply. In composite supply, one is principal and other is ancillary; in mixed supply, each component could be independent — highest rate applies. For example, a hotel package (room + breakfast) is composite; while a gift hamper with unrelated items is mixed.
  • Nil-rated vs Exempt under GST: Nil-rated means 0% GST but ITC is blocked/reversal required (Schedule III items); exempt means 0% GST but ITC reversal required proportionately. Both lead to zero output tax but treatment differs — nil-rated is defined in Act, exempt via notification. Exempt supplies attract ITC blockage u/s 17(2), while nil-rated is excluded from calculation of ITC.
  • TDS on Salary: Employer is agent of employee for TDS purposes. Deduction is at slab rates. However, TDS is computed on total income estimate including all heads, not just salary; hence, TDS on salary is an estimate-based deduction, not exact.
  • Interest on delayed TDS deposit: Sec 201(1A) — interest @ 1.5% per month (18% p.a.) from date of deduction till actual deposit. Penalty u/s 271BA for failure to obtain TAN/issue certificate. Both can be imposed simultaneously.
  • Revising Return: A revised return can only be filed where return has been initially filed under Sec 139(1) or 139(4) — cannot revise a return filed u/s 142(1) (scrutiny) or 148 (reassessment). Also can revise u/s 139(8) if correction is needed, but can only reduce tax liability (cannot increase).
  • GST reverse charge: Only specific notified categories attract RCM — e.g., legal services, SAI (Security Agency), PGD (Person providing Goods Delivery), courier services, etc. GST registration is not a condition for reverse charge applicability. Do not assume all services from unregistered suppliers attract RCM.
  • Valuation of perquisites: Free travelling pass/value of free boarding/lodging — valued at fair market value as per rules. Medical benefit provided by employer to employee — valued at actual cost incurred by employer. Car provided by employer — perquisite value calculated per rules (based on engine capacity and cost).
  • Short-term vs Long-term capital gains holding period: Listed securities — 12 months; Any other capital asset — 36 months. For immovable property — 24 months (changed from 36 in Budget 2017). For equity shares/USS — 12 months. Holding period for jewellery etc remains 36 months.
  • GST registration threshold exceptions: Even if turnover is below threshold, registration is mandatory for (i) inter-state suppliers, (ii) e-commerce operators (TCS u/s 52), (iii) persons supplying on behalf of others (agent), (iv) casual taxable person, (v) composition scheme switch-over from regular, (vi) persons required to deduct TCS/TDS u/s 51/55.
