Quick Revision: Corporate Accounting and Financial Management — CS Executive Group 2

CSEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Amalgamation is the fusion of two or more companies into a single entity, governed by AS-14 (or Ind AS-103 where applicable). It is classified as merger (satisfying all five conditions of AS-14) or amalgamation in the nature of purchase otherwise.
  • Purchase Consideration is the amount payable by the purchasing company to the shareholders of the transferor company, determinable by either the Net Assets Method or the Net Payment Method.
  • Holding Company is a company that controls the composition of the Board of Directors or holds more than half of the equity share capital of another company (the subsidiary) per Section 2(46) of the Companies Act, 2013.
  • Consolidated Financial Statements are financial statements presenting the financial position and performance of a group (holding + subsidiaries) as a single economic entity, where intra-group balances and transactions are eliminated.
  • Minority Interest (Non-Controlling Interest) represents the portion of equity in a subsidiary not attributable, directly or indirectly, to the parent company.
  • Goodwill of a company is the excess of purchase consideration over the fair value of identifiable net assets acquired; it reflects reputation, customer relationships, location advantage, and other unidentifiable intangible benefits.
  • Time Value of Money is the principle that a rupee today is worth more than a rupee tomorrow because of its potential earning capacity at a given rate of return.
  • Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC) is the blended required rate of return on a firm's financing sources (equity, preference, debentures, debt), weighted by their proportional share in the capital structure.
  • Operating Leverage measures the sensitivity of EBIT to changes in sales; calculated as Contribution / EBIT. Higher fixed costs mean higher operating leverage.
  • Financial Leverage measures the sensitivity of EPS to changes in EBIT; calculated as EBIT / EBT. Higher fixed financial charges mean higher financial leverage.
  • Buy-back of Securities is the repurchase by a company of its own shares or other specified securities from its security holders, regulated under Section 68 of the Companies Act, 2013 and SEBI (Buy-back of Securities) Regulations, 2018.
  • Dividend refers to the distribution of a company's profits to its shareholders, declared out of current or accumulated profits after complying with Section 123 of the Companies Act, 2013 and relevant accounting standards.

Important facts

  • AS-14 governs accounting for amalgamations; Ind AS 103 applies where adopted. Merger conditions under AS-14 must ALL be met for merger treatment, otherwise purchase method applies.
  • Section 68, Companies Act 2013: Buy-back limits, time limit (1 year), exclusion of shares bought from insiders.
  • Section 123, Companies Act 2013: Dividend declaration rules — out of current/past profits, depreciation first, 10% to reserves, unclaimed dividend to Unpaid Dividend Account within 30 days, transferred to IEPF after 7 years.
  • AS-17 Segment Reporting requires disclosure of segment revenue, results, assets, liabilities for each reportable segment based on primary (operating segments) and secondary (geographic, industry) formats.
  • AS-11 (revised) deals with the effects of changes in foreign exchange rates — monetary and non-monetary items treatment, translation differences.
  • AS-15 (revised) prescribes employee benefit accounting — short-term benefits, post-employment benefits (provision for gratuity, pension), other long-term benefits. Actuarial assumptions must be stated.
  • AS-19 Leases distinguishes between finance leases (substantially all risks/rewards transferred) and operating leases (lessor retains risks/rewards). Finance lease asset and liability are recognised at the lower of fair value and present value of minimum lease payments.
  • AS-26 Intangible Assets: Capitalise development costs when technical feasibility, intention to complete, ability to use/sell, and reliable measurement criteria are met. Research expenditure is always expensed.
  • Ind AS 115 Revenue from Contracts with Customers: Five-step model — identify contract, identify performance obligations, determine transaction price, allocate price to obligations, recognise revenue when/as obligation is satisfied.
  • Section 2(45) and 2(46) of the Companies Act define group and holding/subsidiary relationships — control may exist through board composition, dominant voting power, or board appointment rights.
  • NCLT approval is required for issue of shares at discount (Sec 54). No such approval is needed for issue at premium beyond the usual conditions.
  • Debenture Premium Account is classified under 'Other Equity' in Schedule III of the Companies Act, 2013.

Memory aids

  • AMALGAMATION Mnemonic — 'MASTER': M = Merger/Purchase classification; A = Asset/Liability transfer; S = Shareholders' ratio (90% rule); T = Transferor's business continued; E = Equity consideration rule (non-cash adjustment banned in merger). All must be met for a 'Merger'.
  • LEASE Mnemonic — Finance Lease means 'FL' = Fully Liable (lessee records asset and liability); Operating Lease means 'OL' = Only Lease rent expense (no asset recorded).
  • CAPITAL STRUCTURE mnemonics — NI = New Income rises with Debt (optimism); NOI = No Opinion on structure (irrelevant); Traditional = Trade-off (balanced middle path); MM with tax = More debt = More shield.
  • DIVIDEND MODELS — 'Walter wants Reward, Gordon seeks Growth': Walter's Model focuses on the r vs k comparison; Gordon's Model uses the perpetual growth formula P₀ = D/(k-g). Remember: Gordon allows g to approach k (but not exceed it).
  • WORKING CAPITAL — 'CASH': Current assets – Current liabilities = Net WC; Accounts Receivable management; Short-term funding sources; Holdings of inventory. The four pillars.
  • BUY-BACK CHECKLIST: B = Below 25% cap; U = No default in 3 years; Y = Completed within 1 year; B = Balance Sheet adjustment; A = Approved by Special Resolution; C = Comply with SEBI/NCLI rules; K = Knowledge of restrictions (no insider buy-back).

Exam tips

  • For CS Executive Corporate Accounting problems, always start by drawing a clear schedule showing the computation of Purchase Consideration, Goodwill/Capital Reserve, Minority Interest, and Reserves before preparing any balance sheet. Examiners award marks for methodical schedules even if the final answer has a small error.
  • In Consolidated Balance Sheet questions, create separate working notes for each subsidiary (NCW at acquisition, Post-acquisition reserves, Goodwill computation) and cross-check that Investment in Sub + Minority Interest = NCW + Goodwill.
  • When solving capital budgeting problems with unequal lives of mutually exclusive projects, use the Equivalent Annual Annuity (EAA) method or the Replacement Chain method rather than comparing raw NPVs directly.
  • Always state the assumption regarding whether the question requires AS or Ind AS treatment. Where the syllabus has shifted to Ind AS, present answers under the relevant Ind AS framework explicitly.
  • For financial management numericals involving cost of capital, compute the component costs individually first, then apply the weights. Show every formula substitution step — partial credit is awarded for correct methodology.
  • In dividend policy questions using Walter's/Gordon's model, identify clearly whether the firm is a growth firm (r > k) or a mature firm (r < k), and then recommend the corresponding optimal dividend policy with reasoning.
  • For buy-back and dividend questions, cite the relevant section of the Companies Act, 2013 (e.g., Sec 68 for buy-back, Sec 123 for dividends) along with the numerical answer — this demonstrates statutory awareness valued in CS exams.
  • Practice interpreting Schedule III of the Companies Act for company balance sheet format and note how intra-group items, minority interest, and segment disclosures are presented.
  • Be thorough with Amalgamation questions: differentiate treatment of pool method vs purchase method regarding reserves, goodwill, and share capital adjustment entries. In purchase method, the transferee company records assets and liabilities at fair value and recognises goodwill.

Common traps

  • Computing Goodwill using book values instead of fair values in the Net Assets Method of purchase consideration — always revalue assets and liabilities to fair value before computing NCW.
  • Forgetting to adjust pre-acquisition reserves of the subsidiary in the consolidated balance sheet — these are absorbed in goodwill calculation, not shown as minority interest.
  • Applying amortisation of discount on issue of shares to the Forfeited Shares Account on reissue — the 'loss on reissue' is first adjusted from Forfeited Shares Account, but Securities Premium is also available under Sec 53 for write-offs, not always intuitively used here.
  • Treating debenture discount as a current asset — it is a deferred expense shown as a separate line under non-current assets (or deducted from the debenture liability depending on presentation), not a current asset.
  • Using gross working capital interchangeably with net working capital — GMWC = total current assets; NW = CA – CL. Different decisions use different measures.
  • Not deducting depreciation before computing profit available for dividend — Sec 123 mandates depreciation provision before any dividend is declared.
  • Confusing the Internal Rate of Return (IRR) with the Cost of Capital — IRR is project-specific; WACC is the hurdle rate. IRR is compared against WACC for acceptance/rejection.
  • In consolidation, failing to eliminate unrealised profit in stock-in-trade — intra-group sales with unrealised profit must be eliminated from both inventory and consolidated retained earnings.
  • Using the average investment base versus initial investment base for ARR — the two methods yield different ARR figures and exam questions must clarify which to use. Default under many Indian curricula: Average Investment = (Initial + Scrap)/2.
  • Overlooking that the Securities Premium Account can be used ONLY for specific purposes under Sec 53 — using it to write off losses is illegal and attracts liability.
