Quick Revision — Company Law and Practice (CS Executive, Group 1)

CSEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Company: A voluntary association of persons, incorporated under the Companies Act, with perpetual succession, a common seal, separate legal personality, limited liability, and transferable shares.
  • Section 2(1)(c) defines a 'company' as a company incorporated under this Act or under any previous company law.
  • One Person Company (OPC): A company having only one person as a member (Section 2(62)).
  • Private Company: A company that restricts the right to transfer its shares, limits the number of its members to 200, and restricts any invitation to the public to subscribe for its securities (Section 2(68)).
  • Public Company: A company other than a private company (Section 2(71)).
  • Section 2(75): 'Debentable' includes debentures, bonds and any other securities of a company.
  • Section 2(78): 'Listing' means bringing of any securities on a recognized stock exchange.
  • Section 2(104): 'Record' means any diary, notebook, index, instrument, book or paper.
  • Section 2(135): 'CSR' means Corporate Social Responsibility activities undertaken by a company as specified under sub-section (5) of that section.
  • Key Managerial Personnel (KMP): The Chief Executive Officer, Managing Director, or Manager; whole-time director; Chief Financial Officer; Company Secretary; Lead Officer (for listed companies); and any other officer as may be prescribed (Section 2(51)).
  • Charge: An interest or lien created on the assets or property of a company or any of its undertakings or assets as security (Section 2(15)).

Memory aids

  • CIN = Corporate Identity Number — like an Aadhaar for companies (unique identifier).
  • MoA = Charter/Mother document; AoA = Bylaws/Internal rules.
  • CLMV = Company Limited by Shares, Company Limited by Guarantee, Unlimited Company — three types under Section 3.
  • 3 Directors, 1 Year gap max between Board meetings, 15 months between AGMs — key numericals.
  • For independent directors: 1/3rd for listed, 1/2nd for certain public companies.
  • CSR: 500/1000/5 (net worth/turnover/profit thresholds), 2% spend, 3-member committee.
  • Buy-back limit: 25% of paid-up capital in a financial year.
  • Redeemable preference shares: max 5 years, max 25% of paid-up capital.
  • Oppression & Mismanagement: Sections 241-242, NCLT, 10% members can petition.
  • Agile-Mind = AGILE-PRO-S + SPICe+ — fast-track incorporation.
  • MCA21 portal is the single window for all company filings.

Exam tips

  • Focus on numbered sections — ICSI frequently asks direct section-based questions.
  • Practice numerical problems on Managerial Remuneration (Schedule V calculations).
  • Memorize key numerical thresholds: independent directors (1/3rd, 33%), CSR (500/1000/5), buy-back (25%), redeemable preference (5 years, 25%).
  • Draw flowcharts for schemes of arrangement, winding up, and buy-back procedures.
  • For descriptive answers, always cite relevant section numbers and Schedule references.
  • Oppression & Mismanagement answers should cover grounds, relief, and NCLT powers.
  • CSR answers must mention Schedule VII categories and unspent amount treatment.
  • Amalgamation questions require distinguishing between merger, demerger, and reconstruction.
  • Practice drafting resolutions (special/ordinary) and board meeting notices.
  • Recent amendments (2019, 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023) are important — focus on new provisions added.
  • For case studies, apply law to facts step-by-step: identify issue → cite section → state rule → apply → conclude.

Common traps

  • Confusing OPC (One Person Company) with sole proprietorship — OPC has separate legal personality; proprietorship does not.
  • Premature commencement of business without Certificate of Commencement was a requirement earlier; now not needed post-COVID amendment.
  • Every company needs an auditor — even private companies need appointment within 30 days of incorporation.
  • Section 177 applies to ALL companies (not just listed) for audit committee constitution — but composition differs.
  • Independent director tenure is 2 terms of 5 years EACH — total maximum 10 years.
  • Related party transaction approval: Board + shareholders both needed for material RPTs under Section 188.
  • Share transfer vs transmission: transfer is voluntary act of owner; transmission is by operation of law.
  • Dividend can be declared only out of 'profits' — not from reserves directly, though reserves can be utilized under certain conditions.
  • Buy-back cannot exceed 25% in a financial year — confusion with share capital reduction.
  • Winding up vs dissolution: winding up leads to dissolution but they are distinct stages.
  • NCLT vs NCLAT: NCLT is adjudicating authority; NCLAT is appellate authority.
  • Charge registration: 30 days default period with additional fees — not automatic extension.
