CS Professional – Professional Group 2: Multidisciplinary Case Studies

CSEnglishNotes

1. Nature and Objective of the Paper

The Multidisciplinary Case Studies paper in the CS Professional programme is designed to test a practicing Company Secretary's ability to identify, analyse, and resolve complex real-world corporate problems that span multiple legal and regulatory domains. Unlike conventional theory papers, this paper does not reward rote memorisation; instead, it evaluates analytical reasoning, application of law to facts, and the capacity to offer practical, commercially viable recommendations.

A typical case study presents a factual matrix involving one or more entities — companies, shareholders, creditors, regulators, employees, or the public — and raises issues under two or more of the following laws: Companies Act 2013, SEBI Regulations, FEMA 1999, RBI Guidelines, Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code 2016, Competition Act 2002, GST/Income-tax laws, or environmental and ESG norms. The candidate must first distil the legal issues from the facts, then apply the appropriate provisions, and finally suggest a course of action that a prudent Company Secretary would take.

Key competencies tested include: (a) issue-spotting from an unstructured fact pattern; (b) identification of the correct statute, section, or regulation; (c) appreciation of conflict or overlap between multiple laws; (d) understanding of procedural compliance; and (e) ability to balance legal correctness with commercial pragmatism.

2. Company Law Case Study Dimensions

Company law forms the single largest component of multidisciplinary case studies. The following sub-topics are examined with particular frequency:

(a) Corporate Governance and Board Matters:
- Composition and independence of directors under Section 149 of the Companies Act 2013
- Related party transactions under Sections 188 and 186 — approval thresholds, disclosure requirements, and exceptions
- Conflict of interest, disclosure obligations, and voting restrictions at board/shareholder meetings
- Role of the Company Secretary in ensuring proper conduct of meetings and maintenance of statutory registers
- Insider trading and circulation of unpublished price-sensitive information (UPSI) — coordination with SEBI LODR

(b) Meetings and Resolutions:
- Validity of board and general meetings — notice periods, quorum, chairperson eligibility
- Ordinary vs special resolution requirements and their practical implications
- Postal ballot procedures and e-voting compliance
- Condonation of delay in holding annual general meetings

(c) Corporate Dilution and Capital Structure:
- Issues and buy-back of shares — procedural compliance, sources of funds, disclosure norms
- Debenture issuance — terms, conditions, registration with ROC
- Rights issue and preferential allotment — pricing guidelines, valuation reports, shareholder approval
- Inter-se transfer of shares and ESOPs

(d) Winding Up and Insolvency Interface:
- Voluntary winding up — solvency declaration, liquidator appointment
- NCLT jurisdictions under Section 408 — conversion from voluntary to compulsory winding up
- Intersection with IBC when insolvency proceedings are initiated simultaneously

(e) Fraud Reporting:
- Section 143(12) — reporting fraud to the Central Government by auditors
- Section 131 and Section 197 — penalties and punishment for frauds
- Duty of the CS to facilitate compliance with fraud investigation procedures

3. SEBI Regulations and Capital Market Case Studies

SEBI-related case studies test the candidate's ability to apply listing obligations and securities regulations to live corporate scenarios. The major areas include:

(a) Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements (LODR) Regulations 2015:
- Substantive disclosure obligations — quarterly results, material events, shareholder meetings
- Schedule III disclosures — related party transactions, risk management, internal financial controls
- Continuous disclosure requirements during events such as mergers, takeovers, rights issues, and delisting
- Role of the Compliance Officer and the Company Secretary in ensuring timely and accurate disclosures

(b) Substantial Acquisition of Shares and Takeovers (SAST) Regulations 2011:
- Trigger points for open offer — acquisition of 25% or more, incremental acquisition above 5%
- Exemptions — acquisitions through subscription of rights shares, inter-se transfers, pledge encumbrances
- Open offer price calculation — floor price, highest price paid, valuation norms
- Procedural steps — filing of Letter of Offer, escrow arrangements, share transfer mechanics

(c) Insider Trading (PPT) Regulations 2015:
- Definition of UPSI and its transmission path
- Code of Fair Conduct for designated persons and insiders
- Restricted periods and trading plan compliance
- Liability of the company and officers who default

(d) Issue of Capital and Disclosure Requirements (ICDR) Regulations:
- Public issues — book building vs fixed price, anchor investor norms, grievance redressal
- Rights issue — proportionate allotment, waiver rules, pricing
- Preferential allotment — lock-in, pricing floor, Eligible Eligible Issuers framework

(e) Delisting of Equity Shares:
- Voluntary delisting — exit offer to minority shareholders, price determination, minimum public shareholding exemption
- Compulsory delisting — persistent non-compliance with minimum public shareholding norms

(f) Mutual Fund and AIF Regulations:
- Applicable where case studies involve investment by listed entities in alternative investment funds or mutual fund schemes

4. FEMA and RBI Regulatory Case Studies

Foreign exchange management issues frequently appear in multidisciplinary case studies, often in combination with company law or SEBI questions. The principal areas are:

(a) External Commercial Borrowings (ECB):
- Eligible entities and all-allowed sectors/projects under the ECB policy
- Minimum average maturity, maximum maturity, and prudential norms
- Route of borrowings — automatic vs government route
- Reporting requirements — ECB acknowledgment number, quarterly return to RBI
- Common violations — utilisation of funds for impermissible purposes, breach of maturity norms

(b) Overseas Direct Investment (ODI) and Forward Commitments:
- Scheme-I and Scheme-II of FEMA 2004 — equity investment limits, capital base calculation
- Valuation guidelines — independent valuer certification
- Reporting to AD Category-I bank and RBI
- Reinvestment of dividends and capital gains

(c) Foreign Portfolio Investment (FPI):
- Registration categories — Category-I FPIs, Category-II FPIs, and exempt categories
- Investment ceiling in Indian securities
- Know-your-customer (KYC) requirements and beneficial ownership reporting
- Exit obligations and repatriation of proceeds

(d) Reverse Mortgage and External Trade Credit:
- Eligibility criteria and RBI guidelines
- Limits, purpose restrictions, and reporting norms

(e) RBI Master Directions on Composite Finance and NBFCs:
- Applicable where a case study involves a company's financing through an NBFC or composite loan facility
- Prudential norms on classification of assets, provisioning, and exposure limits

(f) Penalty and Adjudication under FEMA:
- Compoundable and non-compoundable offences
- Procedure before the Adjudicating Officer and Appellate Tribunal
- Direction for realization of foreign exchange and imposition of penalty up to three times the sum involved

5. Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) Interface

Case studies increasingly test the intersection of company law and insolvency law. The key dimensions are:

(a) Initiators and Triggers:
- Financial creditor, operational creditor, or corporate debtor may initiate corporate insolvency resolution process (CIRP) under Section 7, 9, or 10 respectively
- Threshold of default — no minimum threshold prescribed in the Code but must be genuine debt
- Admission test by NCLT — prima facie case, existence of default, no pending resolution plan

(b) Role of Company Secretary during CIRP:
- Convening meeting of Creditors' Committee (CoC) — notice, agenda, voting patterns
- Appointment and functioning of the interim resolution professional (IRP) and later the resolution professional (RP)
- Preservation of corporate records, statutory compliances during moratorium period under Section 14
- Prevention of alienation of assets during the moratorium

(c) Resolution Plan:
- Submission process, approval by CoC (minimum 66% voting share), NCLT sanction
- Priority of payment — resolution professional's remuneration, workmen dues (last 24 months), financial debts, operational creditors, secured creditors not having charged collateral
- Binding nature of approved resolution plan on all stakeholders including guarantors

(d) Liquidation:
- Triggers — rejection of resolution plan, failure to implement approved plan, expiry of CIRP period
- Order of priority under Section 53 — liquidation costs, workmen dues, secured creditors (if they surrender security), employee dues, unsecured creditors, government dues, preference creditors, remaining debts
- Role of CS in handover to liquidator and closure formalities

(e) Voluntary Liquidation under Chapter IV:
- Declaration of solvency by directors with verbatim statement of having verified the affairs of the company
- Liquidation timeline — not less than 12 months, extension up to 12 months by NCLT
- Distribution of proceeds under voluntary liquidation mirrors Section 53 priority

6. Competition Law and Anti-Competitive Agreements

Competition law aspects in case studies typically arise in situations involving cartelisation, abuse of dominant position, or anti-competitive acquisitions:

(a) Relevant Market and Dominant Position:
- Definition under Section 2(t) — market defined by product, geographic, and interbrand dimensions
- Presumption of dominant position under Section 4(2) — market share threshold of 50% (rebuttable)
- Factors considered — entry barriers, countervailing buyer power, dependence of consumers, vertical integration

(b) Abuse of Dominant Position:
- Predatory pricing — below cost pricing to eliminate competition
- Discriminatory pricing and delivery conditions
- Limitation or restriction of production or technical development
- Imposition of unfair conditions — tying arrangements, exclusive supply agreements

(c) Anti-Competitive Agreements (Section 3):
- Cartels — price fixing, bid-rigging, market sharing, output restriction (presumed to have appreciable adverse effect on competition — AAEC)
- Horizontal agreements — competition among enterprises at same level of production/distribution
- Vertical agreements — resale price maintenance, exclusive distribution, tie-in arrangements
- Exception — efficiency defence and pro-competitive benefits under Section 3(5)

(d) Combination Control (Section 5 and 6):
- Notification threshold — asset/value crossing specified limits
- Procedure — notice to CCI, opposition by Commission, approval or conditional approval
- Implementation without approval — penalty of up to 1% of combined assets/income

(e) Penalties:
- Cease and desist orders, modification of agreements
- Penalty up to 10% of average turnover for three preceding financial years for abuse of dominance
- Penalty on cartels — up to 3× or 10% of turnover, whichever is higher
- Leniency mechanism for cartel disclosures

7. Tax Laws Integration in Case Studies

Taxation is integrated into case studies in several thematic combinations. The candidate should be comfortable with the following intersections:

(a) Dividend and Share Buy-back Taxation:
- Dividend distribution tax abolished; dividends taxable in hands of shareholders under Section 115-O/112A of IT Act
- Buy-back of securities — deduction at source under Section 115QA, excise duty implications
- Disallowance of expenses incurred in relation to tax-free income under Section 14A

(b) Transfer Pricing:
- Applicable to international transactions and specified domestic transactions
- Arm's length price (ALP) determination methods — comparable uncontrolled price, resale price, cost plus, profit split, transactional net margin
- Penalty for failure to maintain contemporaneous documentation under Section 271BA
- Interface with FEMA — pricing of cross-border transactions must also comply with RBI guidelines

(c) Permanent Establishment (PE):
- Service PE under double taxation avoidance agreements (DTAA) — 183-day rule
- Dependency agency PE — habitual exercise of authority to conclude contracts
- Impact on taxability of foreign enterprise's income in India

(d) Goods and Services Tax (GST) in Corporate Transactions:
- Supply definition — Section 7 — includes composite and mixed supplies
- Place of supply — goods and services distinctions
- GST implications on amalgamation, demerger, scheme of arrangement — whether it constitutes supply
- Input tax credit restrictions — Section 17(5) blocked credits

(e) Indirect Tax and FEMA Overlap:
- Payment of royalties and technical service fees — withholding tax, GST, and FEMA compliance
- Brand usage charges and management fees — transfer pricing and FEMA considerations

8. ESG, Environment, Labour and Social Compliance Case Studies

Environmental, Social and Governance (ESG) case studies test integrated knowledge across sustainability regulations, labour law, and governance norms:

(a) Environmental Compliance:
- Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) Notification 2006 — projects requiring environmental clearance
- Consent to establish and operate under Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act and Air Act
- Green bonds and ESG-linked borrowing — SEBI/BSE/NSE guidelines
- Business Responsibility and Sustainability Report (BRSR) — top 100 listed entities mandatory filing

(b) Labour and Social Law Interface:
- Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code (OSH Code) compliance
- Code on Wages — minimum wages, gender discrimination, equal remuneration
- Industrial Disputes Act — retrenchment compensation, closure permissions, lay-off provisions
- POSH Act — prevention of sexual harassment at workplace, internal committee constitution, inquiry procedure
- Contract Labour (Regulation and Abolition) Act — licensing, primary employer liability

(c) Governance Enhancements:
- Board evaluation under Section 166(3) — process, criteria, reporting
- Audit committee composition and effectiveness
- Whistle blower mechanism under Section 177(9) — procedure, protection, maintenance of register
- CSR compliance under Section 135 — spending requirement (2% of average net profits), unspent amount utilisation, reporting format

(d) Data Protection and Digital Governance:
- Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 — data fiduciary obligations, consent architecture, data principal rights, breach notification
- Corporate cybersecurity governance and reporting requirements under IT Act Section 43A and Spice Rules
- Interface with SEBI LODR — disclosure of cybersecurity incidents

9. Case Study Solving Methodology

A structured approach to solving multidisciplinary case studies is essential for scoring well. The following methodology should be adopted systematically:

Step 1 — Read and Underline Facts:
Highlight names, dates, monetary figures, entity relationships, and any actions taken or proposed. Distinguish between primary facts (given directly) and inferred facts.

Step 2 — Identify Legal Issues:
Frame each issue as a question. For example, instead of writing 'company law issue,' write 'Whether the proposed right issue is validly priced below the floor price prescribed under ICDR regulations?' Issue-spotting is the most critical skill being tested.

Step 3 — Locate Applicable Legal Provisions:
Cite the specific Act, section, regulation, or rule. If the exact section number is uncertain, state the legal principle accurately and reference the provision generally. Prioritise the most recent amendment or circular.

Step 4 — Apply Law to Facts:
Draw a logical link between the legal provision and the factual matrix. Use IRAC format (Issue, Rule, Application, Conclusion) where appropriate, especially in longer answers.

Step 5 — Give Practical Recommendations:
Conclude with specific, actionable steps. Avoid vague suggestions. Recommend who should do what, within what timeframe, and under which procedural requirement. Where multiple courses of action exist, discuss merits and demerits briefly.

Step 6 — Cross-Check for Overlaps:
Ensure that recommendations under one area (e.g., company law) do not conflict with another (e.g., FEMA or competition law). Multidisciplinary case studies are specifically designed to test this integrative thinking.

10. Frequently Tested Case Study Scenarios and High-Yield Points

Based on the pattern of previous assessments, the following scenario types and high-yield points recur with notable frequency:

Scenario Type 1 — Shareholder Activism and Takeover Attempt:
Key points: SAST trigger calculations, open offer price computation, exemption claims, SEBI LODR disclosure obligations, lock-in on acquired shares, role of independent directors in recommending the offer to shareholders.

Scenario Type 2 — Fundraising Through ECB or Foreign Investment:
Key points: FEMA route and limits, SEBI ICDR overlap for listed entities, RBI reporting, taxation of interest payments including TDS and transfer pricing, company law compliance for issue of debentures or instruments convertible into equity.

Scenario Type 3 — Corporate Restructuring (Amalgamation/Merger): 0
Key points: NCLT approval process under Section 230-232, creditor and shareholder approval thresholds, stamp duty implications, GST treatment, competition commission notification if combination threshold is crossed, SEBI disclosure during scheme pendency.

Scenario Type 4 — Board Misconduct and Insider Trading:
Key points: UPSI determination, circularisation of UPSI, punitive action under PPT Regulations, Section 188(RPT) violation, Section 166(director duties) breach, penalty under Section 184-185, criminal liability considerations.

Scenario Type 5 — Insolvency Initiation by Operational Creditor:
Key points: Demand notice under Section 8, dispute verification, admission, IRP appointment, CoC formation, resolution plan consideration, liquidation risk if plan fails.

Scenario Type 6 — ESG/BRSR and Sustainability Disclosure Failure:
Key points: Mandatory reporting entities, BRSR Core vs BRSR structure, gap analysis, consequences of non-compliance, interaction with SEBI LODR Regulation 34(2)(f).

Scenario Type 7 — Labour Law Violation in the Context of Restructuring:
Key points: Retrenchment compensation under Section 25F, retrenchment prohibition under Section 25M (for large establishments), notice period, consultation with works committee, wage payment, notice to appropriate government.

Important facts

  • Section 14 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code imposes a moratorium on all legal proceedings against the corporate debtor upon NCLT admission of a CIRP application.
  • Under SAST Regulations, an acquisition triggering a 25% equity holding requires making an open offer to the remaining shareholders at the floor price.
  • The Company Secretary is statutorily obligated to report frauds to the Central Government under Section 143(12) of the Companies Act 2013 if detected during audit.
  • Under FEMA, External Commercial Borrowings must be utilised only for permissible purposes as notified by the RBI and reported via the ECB Acknowledgment Number.
  • Section 4 of the Competition Act, 2002 prohibits abuse of dominant position, including predatory pricing and discriminatory treatment.
  • The floor price for preferential allotment under SEBI ICDR is the higher of the average market price over the preceding two weeks or the face value.
  • Section 188 of the Companies Act 2013 requires board and shareholder approval for related party transactions, with related parties abstaining from voting.
  • BRSR (Business Responsibility and Sustainability Report) is mandatory for the top 100 listed entities by SEBI under LODR Regulations.
  • In a resolution plan under IBC, the waterfall mechanism under Section 53 determines the priority of payment distribution.
  • Under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023, data fiduciaries must appoint a Data Protection Officer if processing involves significant volume or sensitive data.
