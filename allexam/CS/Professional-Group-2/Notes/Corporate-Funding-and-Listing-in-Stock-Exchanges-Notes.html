Corporate Funding and Listing in Stock Exchanges – Complete Notes

CSEnglishNotes

Overview of Securities Markets and Regulatory Framework

['The Indian securities market is bifurcated into the primary market (where fresh capital is raised through issuance of securities) and the secondary market (where existing securities are traded among investors). Corporate funding operations fall squarely within the primary market domain, while listing governs the eligibility and subsequent conduct of issuers in the secondary market.', 'The regulatory architecture is anchored by the Securities and Exchange Board of India Act, 1992 (SEBI Act). SEBI derives its powers from Sections 11, 11B, and 11A of the SEBI Act to protect investors, promote development, and regulate securities markets. The principal regulations governing corporate funding and listing are: (i) SEBI (Issue of Capital and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2018 (ICDR Regulations), which consolidate earlier ICDR, SAST, and certain offer-related provisions; (ii) SEBI (Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2015 (LODR Regulations), which prescribe continuous disclosure and governance standards for listed entities; and (iii) SEBI (Share-Based Employee Benefits) Regulations, 2014 / SEBI (Buyback of Securities) Regulations, 2018, as applicable. The Companies Act, 2013 provides the underlying corporate law backdrop for share capital, prospectuses, and compliance.', 'Key market participants include issuers (companies / NBFCs), underwriters, book runners, lead managers, merchant bankers (Category I), registrars, depositaries, stock exchanges, rating agencies, and credit information companies. A company secretary in practice or in enterprise must interface with these intermediaries to ensure that issue sizing, pricing, allotment, listing, and post-listing disclosures conform to both SEBI and Company Law mandates.', 'Distinction between regulated and unregulated instruments is vital. Regulated instruments (equity shares, convertible securities, warrants, debt securities with maturity > 1 year) typically attract SEBI ICDR/LODR requirements. Unregulated instruments (short-term money market instruments, non-convertible debentures/maturity ≤ 1 year issued to sophisticated investors without public invitation) may be exempt from certain issue/disclosure norms but remain subject to general anti-fraudulent and insider trading regulations if traded on recognized stock exchanges.']

Primary Market: Modes of Corporate Funding and Eligibility

['Public Issue: A company may raise capital through a prospectus inviting subscriptions from the public. Public issues are classified as: (a) Rights Issue — pro-rata offer to existing shareholders; (b) Follow-on Public Offer (FPO) — further issue by an already listed/unlisted company; (c) Initial Public Offer (IPO) — by an unlisted company seeking first-time listing. Each category carries distinct eligibility criteria under ICDR Regulation 27.', 'Rights Issue: Eligible entities include listed companies and unlisted companies with at least three years of audited financials. The issue must be pro-rata to shareholders on record, discounted shares require board/shareholder approval, and the open market component (if any) is subject to lock-in and pricing norms. A rights issue is exempt from the requirement of a prospectus if it satisfies the conditions of Section 42 of the Companies Act, 2013 (private placement/compliance with offer guidelines).', 'FPO/IPO: For listed entities, an FPO must comply with post-listing compliance history and disclosure record. For unlisted IPOs, the issuer must satisfy criteria including net worth, deployable surplus, and appropriate corporate governance. ICDR stipulates minimum promotor contribution and lock-in for promoter participation in public offers.', 'Preferential Allotment and Qualified Institutions Buy (QIB)/Private Placement: Preferential allotment is governed by SEBI (Issue of Capital and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, Part B — Chapter VI. It permits issuance of equity or fully/partially convertible securities to specified persons (QIBs, promoters, strategic investors) at a price determined via market-based pricing mechanisms or a negotiated price with floor-price constraints. Key restrictions include: prohibition on preferential allotment to persons connected with promoters beyond specified thresholds, compliance with related-party transaction provisions under Companies Act, and mandatory disclosure in the annual report. QIP (Qualified Institutions Placement) is an expedient route for listed companies to raise equity from QIBs without a full public issue, subject to pricing floor based on average market price and disclosure in the annual general meeting (AGM) notice.', 'Debt Funding Instruments: Companies/NBFCs may raise long-term funds through secured/unsecured debentures, bonds, commercial paper (CP), certificates of deposit (CD), and NCDs. SEBI regulates public debt offerings under ICDR Chapter VII and specific notifications for CP/CD. Credit ratings are mandatory for most public debt issues, and continuous disclosure of rated debt securities is required under LODR.', 'Alternative Routes: Employee Stock Option Plans (ESOPs) and employee stock purchase schemes are regulated under SEBI (Share-Based Employee Benefits) Regulations, 2014 and require adherence to valuation, pricing, and disclosure norms. Foreign Portfolio Investor (FPI) subscriptions are subject to FEMA and SEBI (FPI) Regulations, 2017. Masala bonds and Rupee-denominated external commercial borrowings have separate FEMA/SEBI interfaces.']

SEBI ICDR Regulations – Pricing, Allotment, and Issue Mechanics

['Pricing Framework: Public issues must specify either a fixed price or a price band. For price-band offers, the book-running lead manager (BRLM) fixes the final issue price within the band after book-building. The floor price cannot be lower than the face value. For rights issues, the discount (if any) requires board resolution and shareholder approval where the discount exceeds prescribed limits. Preferential allotment pricing is generally linked to market price (volume-weighted average market price over preceding trading days) with a minimum threshold; deviations require specific justifications and investor protections.', 'Book-Building Process: Mandatory for most public equity issues above a threshold size. The process involves preliminary prospectus filing, marketing, receipt of bids from institutional/retail applicants, price discovery, and final allotment. Retail Individual Investors (RIIs) and High Net-Worth Individuals (HNIIs) receive reserved quotas. Over-subscription ratios are monitored, and anchor investors may participate subject to lock-in.', 'Allotment and Refund Timeline: Allotment must be completed within 10 working days from closure of the issue. Refunds for unsuccessful applications must be processed within 2 working days of allotment order. Listing application must be filed within 3 working days of allotment. The BRLM/merchant banker issues a certification that all statutory and regulatory requirements have been complied with before allotment.', 'Underwriting and Stability: Issue underwriting is voluntary but common for large issues. Price stabilization in primary market is permitted under SEBI (Issue of Capital and Disclosure Requirements) and related circulars, allowing bidders to support the market price post-issue within prescribed limits. The company secretary must coordinate with the BRLM and exchange for compliance logs.', 'Use of Proceeds and Investor Protection: Prospectus/offer document must clearly state objectives and allocation of proceeds. Misuse of proceeds attracts penal consequences. Investor grievance redressal mechanisms and escrow arrangements (for certain offer types) are mandated. Lock-in obligations for promoters and pre-issue shareholders safeguard against sudden dilution.']

Listing Requirements and Obligations under LODR Regulations

['Eligibility for Listing: A company seeking listing must fulfill the exchange-specific listing requirements (ELRs) as notified by the Recognized Stock Exchange (RSE) and approved by SEBI. ELRs typically encompass minimum public shareholding (usually 25% for mainboard, with phased reduction provisions for larger market caps), capital structure, corporate governance, disclosure record, and compliance with ICDR norms. Debt securities require separate eligibility assessment, including credit rating and issuer eligibility.', 'Application and Approval: Upon allotment, the issuer files a listing application with the stock exchange along with requisite documents (allotment proof, merchant banker certificate, compliance report, draft listing agreement). The exchange grants listing subject to execution of the listing agreement. Until listing is granted, trading cannot commence.', 'Minimum Public Shareholding (MPS): Prescribed by SEBI; currently 25% for most listed entities with phased compliance for larger companies. Promoter and promoter group holdings, excluding certain eligible foreign investors, count toward MPS calculation. Non-compliance triggers corrective action plans and potential trading restrictions.', 'Listing Agreement and Ongoing Obligations: The issuer must execute the listing agreement with the stock exchange. Ongoing obligations are categorized under LODR into: (a) Continuous Disclosure — financial results, material events, shareholding patterns, corporate actions; (b) Corporate Governance — Board composition, committee structure, related-party transactions, code of conduct; (c) Investor Services — grievance redressal, shareholder meetings, dividend payments; (d) Information Standards — accuracy, timeliness, equal access to information.', 'Disclosure Thresholds: Material event disclosure (LODR Reg 30) requires intimation to the exchange within 24 hours of occurrence. Examples include changes in control, substantial acquisitions/disposals, defaults, litigation, change in auditor, issuance/buyback of securities, declaration of dividends, and changes in business. Shareholding pattern must be updated monthly and published by the 5th of the following month.', 'Corporate Actions: Rights issues, bonus issues, splits, mergers, amalgamations, buybacks, and dividend declarations by listed entities require prior exchange communication, SEBI compliance certification, and timely disclosure. Each action has specific procedural requirements, investor notification duties, and record date logistics managed by the issuer secretary/RTA.']

Continuous Disclosure, Corporate Governance, and Risk Management

['Financial Disclosure: Listed entities must publish quarterly financial results (within 40 days for Q1/Q3, 60 days for Q2, and 60 days for annual results) along with a limited review report by the statuary auditor. Annual financial statements require an audit report and must comply with Indian Accounting Standards (Ind AS) or Companies (Accounting Standards) as applicable.', "Event-Based Disclosure: Under LODR Regulation 30, listed entities must immediately disclose any event or information that may materially affect the company's performance, operations, or share price. This includes board recommendations for dividend, change in directorship, resignation of key managerial personnel (KMP), default under debt instruments, alteration in memorandum/articles, approval of schemes of arrangement, and external audits qualifications. The company secretary is ordinarily the designated compliance officer responsible for collating and transmitting such disclosures.", 'Governance Disclosures: Annual reports must contain a management discussion and analysis (MD&A), corporate governance report (including board committees, remuneration, related-party transactions, risk management framework), and a statement on internal financial controls. The CEO and CFO must certify financial statements and internal controls.', 'Risk Management: Listed entities are required to establish a risk management framework and disclose principal risks and how they are mitigated. The board must approve risk management policies, and the company secretary ensures that risk registers, internal audit findings, and compliance reports are integrated into board agendas.', 'Related Party Transactions (RPTs): Compliance with Section 188 of the Companies Act, 2013 and LODR disclosures is essential. Material RPTs require board and shareholder approval, contemporaneous disclosure, and inclusion in the annual report. The company secretary maintains the RPT register and ensures proper recording.']

Role of Intermediaries and Post-Issue Compliance

['Merchant Bankers (Category I): Serve as BRLMs for public issues, QIPs, and preferential allotments. Their responsibilities include due diligence, drafting offer documents, coordinating with exchanges and depositories, ensuring price discovery compliance, issuing certificates of compliance, and monitoring investor subscription. They are the primary point of contact with SEBI and the exchange during the issue lifecycle.', "Registrars and Transfer Agents (RTAs): Manage investor applications, allotment processing, refund distribution, dematerialization coordination, and share certificate issuance. The RTA's integrity directly affects investor satisfaction and regulatory compliance; delays in allotment/refund attract penalties.", "Depositories (NSDL/CDSL): Facilitate dematerialized holding and settlement. Post-allotment, securities must be credited to investor demat accounts. The company secretary ensures that the issuer's demat account is active and that corporate action instructions (bonus, rights, splits) are accurately transmitted to the depository.", 'Stock Exchanges (BSE/NSE/ICEX/MCX etc.): Grant listing, monitor trading, enforce listing agreements, accept disclosures, and take corrective action for non-compliance. The issuer must respond promptly to exchange queries and comply with trading circuit norms and suspension procedures.', 'Post-Issue Reporting: After completion of the issue, the issuer must file a post-issue report with SEBI and the exchange within 15 days, detailing utilization of proceeds, deviation from objectives (if any), and compliance certifications. Quarterly progress on use of proceeds is disclosed in financial results. If proceeds are diversified or deferred beyond one financial year, shareholder approval and enhanced disclosure are required.']

Extinguishment, Delisting, and Trading Mechanisms

["Suspendation and Delisting: A listed entity's securities may be suspended for trading upon failure to comply with listing requirements, pending disclosure, or regulatory investigation. Delisting may occur voluntarily (by the issuer) or mandatorily (by the exchange/SEBI for persistent non-compliance or insolvency). Voluntary delisting requires a take-over offer to minority shareholders at a fair valuation determined by a registered merchant banker, and compliance with SEBI (SAST) Regulations to the extent applicable. Mandatory delisting follows due process and appeal mechanisms.", 'Trading Mechanisms: Equity trading occurs via multiple segments (EQ, BE, SME, etc.) with different eligibility criteria. SME exchanges have relaxed listing requirements but stricter investor protections. Debt trading occurs on dedicated segments with separate liquidity and disclosure norms. The company secretary must ensure that the correct segment designation is maintained and that segment-specific obligations are met.', 'Buyback and Redemptions: Buyback of securities is regulated under SEBI (Buyback of Securities) Regulations, 2018 and Section 68 of the Companies Act, 2013. Procedures include board/shareholder approval, offer document filing, escrow maintenance, and completion timeline. redemption of convertible securities follows the terms of issue and LODR event disclosures.', 'Corporate Restructuring Impact on Listing: Mergers, demergers, scheme approvals, and capital restructuring involving listed entities require simultaneous compliance with SEBI LODR, SAST takeover norms, and Companies Act provisions. The listing status may be affected by change in controlling interest, requiring adjustments to promoter holding disclosures and potentially triggering open offer obligations.']

Exam-Weighted Themes and Practical Focus Areas

['High-yield examination themes for CS Professional focus on: (1) distinguishing between rights issues, FPOs, QIPs, and preferential allotments — including eligibility, pricing, and disclosure implications; (2) identifying material events under LODR Regulation 30 and the 24-hour intimation window; (3) calculating minimum public shareholding compliance and understanding phased reductions; (4) recognizing the role and certification duties of merchant bankers and BRLMs; (5) tracing the sequence from allotment to listing application to exchange approval; (6) applying SEBI ICDR pricing rules for book-built issues and preferential allotments; (7) articulating the continuous disclosure calendar (quarterly results, shareholding pattern updates, annual governance report); and (8) explaining procedures for delisting, voluntary delisting fairness opinion, and minority shareholder protection.', 'Practical case-study elements often tested: scenario-based questions on whether an issuance constitutes a preferential allotment or a rights issue; determining compliance breaches when disclosure timelines are missed; evaluating whether a corporate action (bonus/rights/buyback) requires prior exchange intimation; and assessing the impact of a change in promoter holding on listing status and SAST obligations.', 'Common pitfalls include confusing the thresholds for private placement vs preferential allotment, omitting the 24-hour event disclosure rule, misapplying pricing norms for QIP versus rights issue, and neglecting the post-issue utilization report requirement. Additionally, candidates must not conflate LODR disclosure timelines with Companies Act filing deadlines, though both may apply concurrently.']

Important facts

  • SEBI ICDR Regulations, 2018 consolidate the primary issuance framework; LODR Regulations, 2015 govern ongoing listing obligations.
  • Material event disclosure under LODR Regulation 30 must be made to the stock exchange within 24 hours of occurrence.
  • Minimum public shareholding (MPS) for listed entities is generally 25%, with phased reduction provisions for larger market-cap companies.
  • Book-building is mandatory for most public equity issues above prescribed size; retail and HNI quotas are reserved.
  • Allotment must be completed within 10 working days from issue closure; refunds processed within 2 working days thereafter.
  • Listing application must be filed within 3 working days of allotment; trading commences only after exchange approval.
  • Preferential allotment pricing is typically linked to volume-weighted average market price with floor-price constraints.
  • QIP allows listed companies to raise equity from QIBs without a full public issue, subject to pricing floor and AGM disclosure.
  • Post-issue utilization report must be filed with SEBI and the exchange within 15 days of issue closure.
  • Voluntary delisting requires a take-over offer to minority shareholders at fair valuation determined by a registered merchant banker.

Formulas and relationships

Floor Price / Issue Price determination in book-built issues: Final price is fixed within the declared price band; for rights issues with discount, the discount percentage is subject to board/shareholder approval thresholds prescribed in ICDR.
Minimum Public Shareholding calculation: MPS = (Public持有的 Shares / Total Paid-up Equity Share Capital) × 100%; compliance requires ≥ 25% for most listed entities.
Volume-Weighted Average Market Price (VWAP) for preferential allotment/QIP pricing: VWAP = (Sum of Volume-Weighted Prices over specified preceding trading days) / (Number of trading days considered); used as the floor price basis with prescribed discounts/premiums allowed by SEBI notifications.
Lock-in period for promoter/pre-promoter contributions in public issues: Typically 3 years from date of allotment, reducible to 1 year on fulfillment of conditions (disclosure, non-violation) as per ICDR.
Open Offer consideration under SEBI (SAST) triggered by change in control: Consideration per equity share is the higher of the average price paid by the acquirer in the preceding 26 weeks or the prevailing market price at the time of public announcement.

Common mistakes

  • Confusing preferential allotment with rights issue: preferential allotment targets specified persons (QIBs, promoters, strategic investors) with market-linked pricing; rights issue is pro-rata to existing shareholders and may include discounts.
  • Missing the 24-hour disclosure deadline for material events under LODR Regulation 30, leading to compliance violations and exchange penalties.
  • Treating QIP and FPO interchangeably: QIP is a private placement route to QIBs with simplified documentation; FPO is a public issue open to all investor categories with stricter prospectus requirements.
  • Overlooking the post-issue utilization report filing within 15 days; failure to file or report diversification of proceeds attracts SEBI action.
  • Incorrectly computing minimum public shareholding by excluding eligible foreign investors or miscounting partially paid-up capital.
  • Assuming listing commences immediately after allotment without exchange approval; trading is prohibited until the listing grant is received.
  • Mixing up disclosure timelines: quarterly results (40/60 days), shareholding pattern (by 5th of following month), and annual governance report (with annual results) are distinct deadlines.
  • Neglecting the requirement of a merchant banker's certificate of compliance before making allotment in public issues and preferential allotments.
  • Misapplying price stabilization rules: price stabilization is permitted only within SEBI-prescribed limits and must be disclosed; unauthorized stabilization constitutes market manipulation.
