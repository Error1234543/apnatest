Quick Revision: Corporate Funding and Listing in Stock Exchanges

CSEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Corporate Funding refers to the process by which a company raises capital from various sources including equity shares, debt instruments, convertible securities, external commercial borrowings, and other financial instruments.
  • Initial Public Offer (IPO) means the first issue of securities or capital by an unlisted company to the public, whether through book building or offer for sale.
  • Rights Issue is an offer of securities to existing shareholders proportionate to their holdings, made at a price and terms determined under the SEBI ICDR Regulations.
  • Qualified Institutions Placement (QIP) is a scheme under which a listed entity issues fully/convertibly redeemable preference shares or special warrants to Qualified Institutional Buyers (QIBs) on a preferential basis.
  • Preferential Allotment is an issue of equity/debt securities by a listed entity to selected persons on a private placement basis, requiring shareholder approval under Section 81(1A) of the Companies Act.
  • Book Building Process is a mechanism where a prospectus invites bids from investors at a price band, with the final price determined based on demand at or above the floor price.
  • Placement Agent is a person who assists the issuer in placing securities with investors but does not take on the liability of underwriting.
  • Merchant Banker is a person engaged in the business of issue management by managing the issues, including corporate counselling, promotion, structuring, financial turn-around, management of production and marketing facilities, leasing, financing, and portfolio management.

Key concepts

  • SEBI ICDR Regulations: Issuance of Capital and Disclosure Requirements (2018, amended) govern public issues, rights issues, preferential allotments, QIPs, and placement of listed convertible securities. Key categories of issues include Mainstreet Public Issues (MPI), SME IPOs, Right Issues, Preferential Allotments, and QIPs.
  • SME IPO Framework: Introduced under SEBI ICDR (Fourth Amendment) Regulations, 2020. Securities are traded only on the SME platform of a stock exchange (BSE SME or NSE EBS). Pricing is determined by book building. Issue size must not exceed Rs. 250 crores. Minimum public shareholding of 10% is required.
  • Book Building Details: Price band shall not exceed 20% of the floor price. Anchor investors may be invited prior to the book building period for anchoring purposes. QIB portion is allocated 50% basis bidding (at least 75% to domestic QIBs, with at least 5% reserved for domestic banks/NBFCs as applicable). Retail individual investor (RIA) portion gets proportional bidding. Non-institutional investors get 15% reservation (minimum 5% reserved to retail shareholders).
  • Lock-in and Cooling-off Period: Promoter/Founders subject to cooling-off period — 6 months for book-built issues and 1 year for offer-for-sale. Lock-in for promoters/promoter group — 3 years from date of commencement of commercial production for IPO/SME IPO, reducible to 1 year for companies meeting specific conditions. For preferential allotment, lock-in is 3 years from date of allotment to promoter group.
  • Disclosure Requirements: Draft Red Herring Prospectus (DRHP) filed with SEBI. Final Red Herring Prospectus (FRHP) after SEBI observations. Prospectus to be filed with Registrar of Companies. Listing application with stock exchange. Key disclosures include object of the issue, management discussion and analysis, risk factors, corporate governance details, and use of proceeds.
  • SEBI LODR Regulations: Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements regulate ongoing compliance of listed entities. Includes disclosures for public announcements, related party transactions, price-sensitive information, quarterly results, and annual reporting.
  • Related Party Transactions (RPTs): Material related party transactions require approval of the Board and shareholders. Non-material RPTs require Board approval only. Independent directors' opinion required for material RPTs.
  • Price Sensitive Information: Any information that can materially affect the price of securities. Listed entities must immediately disclose such information to the stock exchange. Regulatory disclosures under Regulation 30 of LODR include changes in control, board decisions on dividends/acquisitions, insider trading, corporate announcements.
  • Quarterly Results Disclosure: Listed entities must declare quarterly financial results within 40 days from the end of each quarter. Half-yearly results within 60 days. Annual results within 60 days from closure of the financial year.
  • Foreign Portfolio Investment (FPI): Registered under SEBI (FPI) Regulations, 2019. FPIs invest in Indian securities including listed equities, bonds, and derivatives. Category I, II, and III classification applies based on the investor profile.
  • External Commercial Borrowings (ECBs): Governed by RBI's FEMA regulations. ECBs are all debts in foreign currency including banker's credits, trade credits, supplier's credit, etc. The automatic route ceiling, maturity structure, and end-use restrictions apply. Current threshold: USD 750 million per annum under the automatic route with a minimum average maturity of 5 years.
  • ESOPs: Employee Stock Option Schemes governed by SEBI (Share Based Employee Benefits) Regulations, 2014. Provides for grant of options or securities to employees at a discount/specific terms. Vesting period, exercise period, and lock-in provisions specified.
  • Conversion of Securities: All listed convertible securities shall be compulsorily converted into equity shares within the time specified. Unlisted public companies issuing convertible securities face restrictions under Section 62 of the Companies Act.
  • Public Issue Thresholds and Conditions: Minimum public shareholding requirement is 25% for listed entities (subject to relaxations for SMEs). Gross issue size of more than Rs. 10,000 crores requires SEBI registration. Minimum application size prescribed under the regulations.
  • Preferential Allotment under Section 81(1A) of Companies Act: Requires prior approval of the Board followed by shareholder approval through special resolution. Price shall not be less than the average of weekly high and low prices of the closing price of the shares during the two weeks preceding the date of allotment.

Important facts

  • Maximum issue size under SME IPO framework: Rs. 250 crores. Minimum issue size: Rs. 10 crores.
  • Cooling-off period for founders/promoters after IPO: 6 months for book-built issues and 1 year in case of offer for sale.
  • Lock-in for promoters post-IPO: 3 years from commencement of commercial production (reducible to 1 year for specific conditions).
  • Lock-in period for preferential allotment to promoter/promoter group: 3 years from the date of allotment.
  • Minimum public shareholding requirement: 25% for listed companies; 10% for SME-listed entities.
  • SEBI ICDR: Issuance of Capital and Disclosure Requirements Regulations, 2018.
  • SEBI LODR: Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements Regulations, 2015.
  • Floor price band maximum permitted: 20% of the floor price.
  • Anchor investors: Maximum investment allowed is 20% of the net issue on a pro-rata basis.
  • QIB portion allocation: 50% of the net issue — at least 75% to domestic QIBs, with 5% reserved to domestic scheduled banks/NBFCs (as applicable).
  • Retail Individual Investor quota: At least 35% of the net issue (in public issues).
  • Non-institutional Investor quota: 15% of the net issue (at least 5% reserved to retail shareholders).
  • DRHP filing: Required for all public issues above the SME category.
  • Quarterly results submission deadline: 40 days from quarter end; half-yearly: 60 days; annual: 60 days from financial year end.
  • Materiality threshold for RPTs: As notified from time to time under LODR; significant related party transactions require shareholder approval.
  • ECB automatic route: Up to USD 750 million per annum per company with a minimum average maturity of 5 years and maximum maturity of 15 years.
  • ESOPs vesting period: Not less than 1 year from the date of grant; exercise period not exceeding 3 years from vesting.
  • Section 81(1A) Companies Act: Preferential allotment price floor is based on average of weekly high and low prices of the preceding two weeks.
  • Green Shoe Option: Maximum 15% of the base issue size. Allows over-allotment in IPOs.
  • Cut-off date for book building: Bid closing date determined by issuer within the specified period.
  • SEBI's grievance redressal mechanism: Investors may file complaints through SCORES (SEBI Complaints Redress System).

Memory aids

  • ICDR = Issue Capital Disclosure Regulations — remember 'I see D-R-H' (see disclosure rules here). Lock-in periods: 3 years for IPO allotments, 3 years for preferential allotments — both 'three-year locks'.
  • LODR = Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements — 'List Only Detailed Reports' for ongoing compliance.
  • Anchor Investors: 'Anchoring the ship before the storm' — they commit early before the general book builds. Max 20% of issue size.
  • Price Band Rule: 'Twenty percent band' — like a belt that doesn't stretch beyond 20% of the floor price.
  • ECB Limits: '750 million at five — 750 crore limit with 5-year maturity' mnemonics.
  • Cooling-off Periods: 'Six months for bids, one year for sale' — if it's a book-building IPO, 6 months; if offer-for-sale, 1 year.
  • RPT Approval Ladder: Board → Independent Directors → Shareholders (for material transactions). Think: 'Board first, then eyes (ID), then the whole house (shareholders).'
  • SEBI Categories for FPI: 'First, Second, Third' — Category I (government/govt entities), II (investment vehicles), III (portfolio investors).

Exam tips

  • In CS Professional, Corporate Funding questions are often linked with Company Law (Sections 39-45, 49, 62, 81 of Companies Act, 2013). Integrate legal provisions with regulatory knowledge from SEBI.
  • When answering descriptive questions on ICDR, always cite the specific regulation number and distinguish between different types of issues (public, rights, preferential, QIP, SME).
  • Practice numerical problems on pricing of preferential allotments, QIP pricing, and issue size calculations. These frequently appear in objective and case-study questions.
  • Remember that CS Professional exams increasingly test applied/analytical questions. Practice drafting DRHP summaries, identifying disclosure gaps, and advising on compliance with both SEBI and Companies Act provisions.
  • For listing compliance questions, structure answers by categorising into: pre-listing, post-listing, and ongoing disclosure requirements.
  • Cross-reference SEBI LODR disclosure timelines with Companies Act annual filing deadlines — mismatched timelines are a common trap in multi-concept questions.
  • Use flowcharts or tables in your answers to compare: public issue vs preferential allotment vs QIP vs rights issue on parameters such as pricing, eligibility, approval process, lock-in, and cooling-off.

Common traps

  • Confusing ICDR with LODR: ICDR deals with issuance/capital raising; LODR deals with listing/ongoing disclosure. They are two separate regulatory frameworks.
  • Forgetting that offer-for-sale has a 1-year cooling-off period while book-building has 6 months. This is frequently tested.
  • Mixing up the lock-in period: It is 3 years from commencing commercial production, NOT 3 years from the date of issue. The distinction matters.
  • Treating all QIP issues the same as IPOs: QIPs have distinct pricing norms (based on volume-weighted average market price) and do not require SEBI scrutiny in the same way.
  • Incorrectly assuming that SME IPOs have the same public shareholding requirement as regular mainboard listings — SME requires only 10%.
  • Ignoring that anchor investors' lock-in is 30 days; this is shorter than the promoter cooling-off period.
  • Confusing ECB maximum maturity (15 years) with minimum average maturity (5 years). Both must be satisfied simultaneously.
  • Assuming that all preferential allotments require SEBI approval — in fact, preferential allotment needs SEBI registration only if the total consideration exceeds a threshold.
  • Not recognizing that ESOPs have a mandatory 1-year vesting period — any scheme promising immediate exercise is non-compliant.
  • Overlooking that RIA allocation in book building can be proportionally bid, meaning small investors can submit more than the application size without disqualification.
