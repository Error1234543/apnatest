Section 1: Conceptual Framework and Evolution of Indian Tax System

Indian taxation operates under a dual architecture established by the Constitution — the Union levies central taxes (Income Tax under the Income-tax Act, 1961; IGST and Customs under the Constitution's Entry 82 and Entry 92A) while States levy state taxes (SGST/UTGST under Entries 54 and 62 of State List, and GST under the 101st Amendment Act, 2016). The GST regime subsumed multiple erstwhile indirect taxes including Central Excise duty, Service Tax, VAT/Sales Tax, Octroi, Entry Tax, Luxury Tax, and Cess on various goods and services.

The Constitution (101st Amendment) Act, 1961 introduced Article 246A, granting both Parliament and State Legislatures concurrent power to legislate on GST. This enabled the dual GST model — CGST+SGST for intra-state supplies and IGST for inter-state supplies, each governed by separate statutes but coordinated through the GST Council (Article 279A). The Goods and Services Tax Council, chaired by the Union Finance Minister, decides rates, exemptions, threshold limits, and model GST laws.

Key milestones: GST came into effect on 1 July 2017; e-way bill and e-invoicing mechanisms were introduced progressively; the rate rationalisation has moved from four brackets (5%, 12%, 18%, 28%) plus exempt/special categories toward a more uniform structure. The CBIC and state GST departments share enforcement jurisdiction, with the GST Network (GSTN) serving as the technology backbone.

Section 2: Residential Status and Its Tax Implications

Residential status under the Income-tax Act determines the scope of total income chargeable to tax in India. It is distinct from citizenship and is determined separately for each previous year under Sections 6 and 6B.

(a) Individual: Status is classified as Resident or Non-Resident based on stay in India during the relevant previous year and the preceding 10 years. Under Section 6(1), an individual is 'resident' if he satisfies at least one of two conditions: (i) present in India for 182 days or more during the previous year; or (ii) present in India for 60 days or more during the previous year AND 365 days or more in the 4 preceding years. The 60-day limit is extended to 182 days for a person who leaves India during the previous year for employment outside India or as a member of a crew of an Indian ship. For individuals leaving India during the previous year who earn more than ₹15 lakh from Indian sources, the 60-day condition applies unless they qualify for the extended 182-day relaxation.

Under Section 6(6A), an individual who is not a resident under Section 6(1) may still be deemed resident if his total income (other than foreign income) exceeds ₹12 lakh and he is in India for 120 days or more during the previous year AND 401 days or more during the 7 preceding years. An NRI becomes an 'Ordinary Resident' if he qualifies as resident under Section 6(1) with 182 days but not under the 'deemed resident' provisions — the second condition for Ordinary Residence requires that the individual has been a resident in at least 2 out of the 10 preceding previous years AND has stayed in India for 730 days or more during the 7 preceding previous years. Failure to meet either condition makes one a 'Non-Ordinary Resident'.

(b) Company (Section 6(3)): A company is resident in India in any previous year if its place of effective management (POEM) in that year is in India. POEM means the place where key management and commercial decisions necessary for the conduct of business are made on a day-to-day basis — ordinarily the place where the Board of Directors meets. If POEM cannot be ascertained, a company incorporated in India is always resident; a foreign company is resident only if its POEM is in India.

(c) Firm and other persons (Section 6(4)): A firm or LPF is resident if the control and management of its affairs is wholly/partially situated in India during the relevant previous year.

(d) Person other than company/firm (Section 6(5)): A HUF, AOP, BOI, or artificial judicial person is resident if control and management of its affairs is wholly or partly in India.

(e) Section 6B — Non-resident specified persons: A person (other than a company or HUF) who is in India during the previous year and carries on a business or profession whose profits are assessable to tax in India is a non-resident if: (i) total income does not exceed ₹50 lakh; and (ii) no tax on such income has been paid or deductible in India.

Tax implications: A Resident Ordinarily Resident (ROR) is taxable on global income; a Resident Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR) is taxable only on Indian-sourced income and foreign income received in India; a Non-Resident is taxable solely on income received or accruing in India.

Section 3: Heads of Income — Advanced Provisions and Special Cases

Income is classified under five heads per Section 14 of the Act: Salaries, House Property, Profits and Gains of Business or Profession (PGBP), Capital Gains, and Other Sources. Advanced areas examined include:

(a) Salaries: Section 17 defines salary to include wages, annuity, pension, gratuity, fees, commissions, perquisites, and profits in lieu of salary. Perquisites under Section 17(2) include any benefit privilege not included in 'specified perquisites' (Sec 17(2)(iii)) and 'specified perquisites' (Sec 17(2)(iv)), plus any contribution to recognised/unrecognised provident fund. Rule 3 prescribes valuation of perquisites. Employer's contribution to NPS (Sec 16(2)(ix)) is exempt up to 10% of salary. Value of rent-free accommodation is prescribed under Rule 3 read with Sec 17(2); for government employees with high-scale pay, specific rules apply. Medical perquisite valuation depends on type of facility provided. Clubbing of salary income applies when a relative receives salary benefits from the assessee's employer.

(b) House Property: Sections 22–27 govern computation. Annual value is determined under Sections 23–25. Standard deduction of 30% of annual value is available u/s 24(b), along with interest on borrowed capital (up to ₹2,00,000 for let-out/pre-constructed property; up to ₹1,50,000 subject to pre-construction period restrictions for self-occupied). In case of let-out property where interest exceeds annual value, negative annual value cannot be set off against other heads except PGBP — it can be carried forward. Under the revised Section 24(b) w.e.f. FY 2019-20, interest on pre-construction period is deductible in 5 equal instalments commencing from the year of acquisition/completion. Interest on housing loan for self-occupied property is deductible up to ₹2,00,000 per year under Section 24(b).

(c) Capital Gains: Sections 45–54D deal with capital gains. Capital asset definition (Section 2(14)) excludes personal effects, jewellery held for personal use (but NOT if used for business), residential house, and bonds/debentures held by a Individual/HUF. Capital gain arises on transfer (Section 2(47)) including sale, exchange, relinquishment, extinguishment of rights, compulsory acquisition, and conversion from stock-in-trade to capital asset. Long-term capital gain (LTCG) applies to assets held >24 months (12 months for listed equity shares/units — changed by Finance Act 2018). Short-term capital gain (STCG) applies to all other transfers. Indexation benefit is available for LTCG on real estate and debt-oriented instruments but NOT for LTCG on listed equity shares/units (u/s 112A, flat 12.5% tax above ₹1.25 lakh). Cost of acquisition indexed under Section 48 with index numbers from PIB (Base Year 2001 = 100). Exemptions u/s 54 (residential house), 54F (any long-term capital asset), 54EC (specified bonds within 6 months), 54GB (shares in residential house company), 54D (scheduled tribes), 54F/54F(1A) for specific cases. Securities Transaction Tax (STT)-paid equity transactions have special provisions. Roll-over relief is available on compulsory acquisition u/s 54B/54F/54DA.

(d) PGBP: Sections 28–44D govern business income. Deductions u/s 30–37: depreciation u/s 32, welfare of employees u/s 36(1)(vii) (subject to limits), bad debts u/s 36(1)(vi), advertisement u/s 36(1)(adv) (with limits), repayment of overseas borrowings u/s 36(1)(v), donation u/s 80G. Disallowances u/s 40A: cash payments exceeding ₹10,000 to a person (u/s 40A(3)); expenses where TDS not deducted u/s 40(a)(ia)/(a)(i); excessive remuneration to partners u/s 40(b). Business loss set-off rules u/s 72A; carry forward restricted to certain losses without audit report. Presumptive taxation u/s 44AD (6% of turnover for digital, 8% otherwise for eligible assessees) and 44ADA (60% of gross receipts for specified professions).

(e) Other Sources: Sections 56–59 — income from winnings, dividends, interest, rent, etc. Exemption u/s 10 for dividends (taxed in hands of company under section 115-O prior to abolition; now taxed in hands of recipient u/s 56(2)(x)). Interest on securities (Sec 10(2)), family pension (Sec 57), gift income (Sec 56(2)(x) — gifts above ₹50,000 in aggregate taxable; exceptions for relative gifts and marriage gifts). Clubbing provisions u/s 60–65.

Section 4: Set-Off, Carry Forward and Set-Off of Losses

Sections 70–75 prescribe the mechanics of inter-head and intra-head adjustment of losses.

Inter-head set-off (Section 70): Loss under one head can be set off against income under another head, subject to specific restrictions. Loss under 'Capital Gains' cannot be set off against 'Salaries'; loss under 'Other Sources' cannot be set off against 'Salaries' or 'House Property' in certain cases; loss from speculative business u/s 73 cannot be set off against non-speculative income.

Intra-head set-off: Within the same head, losses and gains are aggregated. For PGBP, all business incomes/gains are computed across the year, and loss from one business can be set off against profit from another.

Carry forward of losses (Sections 72–75):
- Loss from business/profession (other than speculative) u/s 72: Can be carried forward for 8 assessment years. Must file return on time u/s 139(1). Cannot be set off against capital gains or salary income.
- Speculative business loss u/s 73: Carried forward for 4 years; can be set off only against speculative business income.
- Capital loss u/s 74: LTCG can be set off only against LTCG; STCG only against STCG. Carried forward for 8 years.
- Loss under 'Other Sources' u/s 74A: Carried forward for 8 years; can be set off only against income from the same source category.
- Loss from agricultural activity u/s 74B: Carried forward for 8 years where business is related to development of tea business.
- Loss in case of change of business constitution u/s 72A: Carbuck forward where eligible presumptive scheme conditions are met.
- House property loss u/s 71: Negative annual value — only set off against income under other heads; balance carried forward u/s 71(2) for 8 years; set off only against income under house property head.
- Unabsorbed depreciation u/s 72(2): Treated as business loss — can be carried forward indefinitely, set off against any head of income.
- Loss from job change in case of specified companies u/s 72AA: Carbuck forward by successor entity subject to conditions.
- Loss from operation of ship u/s 73A: Carried forward for 8 years.
- Loss from horse races u/s 58(3)/74: Not carryable forward.

Conditions for carry forward: Return of loss must be filed within the due date u/s 139(1). Default in filing returns defeats carry-forward in most cases, except where return can be filed as belated return u/s 139(4) before the expiry of one year from end of relevant AY.

Section 5: Tax Deduction at Source (TDS) and Tax Collection at Source (TCS)

TDS under Chapter XVII-B (Sections 192–206) is a mechanism where the payer deducts tax at prescribed rates from specified payments and deposits it with the Government. The assessee claims credit u/s 203A/TAN.

Key TDS sections:
- Section 192: Salary — employer deducts tax on estimated total income at applicable slab rates.
- Section 192A: PPF account interest exceeding ₹50,000 — 10% TDS.
- Section 193: Interest on securities — 10% (higher if PAN not furnished, currently 20%).
- Section 194: Dividends — abolished u/s 194 for dividend distribution; now u/s 194K for mutual fund/dividend from company (10%).
- Section 194BB: Winnings from horse races — 30%.
- Section 194B: Lottery/game winnings — 30%.
- Section 194C: Payments to contractors/sub-contractors — 1% (individual/HUF) / 2% (others); 10% if PAN not furnished.
- Section 194Q: Purchase of goods — 0.1% on purchase exceeding ₹50 lakh (buyer's turnover > ₹10 crore).
- Section 194H: Commission/brokerage — 5%.
- Section 194J: Professional/technical fees, director's fee, contract fees — 2% (professional/technical), 2% (contract), 10% (broadcasting/telecasting), 1% (director's fee).
- Section 194JA:Fee for professional/technical services — 2%.
- Section 194M: Payments by individuals/HUF to professionals — 5% on payments/receipts exceeding ₹25,000 (effective till March 2025).
- Section 194O: E-commerce operator TDS on sales — 1% on net goods/services sold through e-commerce platform.
- Section 194P: Tax deduction at source on payment to resident assessee engaged in business/profession — employer deducts tax after considering deductions/exemptions; assessee provides statement of income.
- Section 194N: Withdrawal from FDR/OD — 2% (individual/HUF) / 1% (others) if withdrawal exceeds ₹10 lakh (with certain exemptions).

Threshold limits and rates: Most TDS sections have minimum threshold limits (e.g., Section 194C — ₹30,000 per payment/₹1,00,000 aggregate in a financial year; Section 194J — ₹30,000; Section 194H — ₹15,000). Rates default to 20% without applicable lower rate certificate (u/s 197).

TCS under Section 206C: Collector collects tax at source from buyer. Section 206C(1F): 0.1% on sale consideration exceeding ₹5 lakh where seller's turnover exceeds ₹10 crore in preceding FY (effective from 1 October 2020). Section 206C(1G): 0.1% on receipt exceeding ₹50 lakh in financial year for goods/services traded through e-commerce operator. TCS on LRS (Section 206C(1G)), on foreign travel (Section 206C(1H)), and on withdrawal from accounts (Section 206C(1D)) also exist.

Defaults: Failure to deduct/collect/tender results in interest u/s 201A (1% per month from date of deduction to date of deduction and 1% from date of deduction to actual payment). Penalty u/s 271C (amount equal to tax sought to be deducted) and u/s 271HB (₹200/day minimum ₹20,000 maximum ₹2,00,000).

Section 6: Transfer Pricing and Arm's Length Pricing

Transfer Pricing (TP) under Chapters X (Sections 92–92F) applies to 'international transactions' between 'associated enterprises'. An international transaction includes any transaction involving buying/selling of goods, provision of services, lending of money, management agreements, marketing agreements, auxiliary services, and use/transfers of intangible property.

Associated enterprise (Section 92B): Two enterprises are associated if one participates materially in the management, control, or capital of the other, OR the same person(s) participate materially in both. 'Material participation' generally means ownership of ≥26% of voting power/capital. The AO, Assessee, or International Taxation Dispute Resolution Panel may determine AE status.

Arm's Length Price (ALP) (Section 92C): TP adjustment is made if the ALP differs from the transaction price. ALP is determined using prescribed methods (Section 92C(2)): (a) Comparable Uncontrolled Price (CUP) method; (b) Resale Price Method; (c) Cost Plus Method; (d) Profit Split Method; (e) Discounted Cash Flow Method; (f) Transaction Net Margin Method (TNMM); (g) Any other method prescribed. Primary method selection depends on nature of transaction and comparability. TNMM is most commonly used for Indian assessee.

Provisions related to TP:
- Section 92D: Duties of Transfer Pricing Officer (TPO). TPO is appointed by AO for international transactions. TPO must refer methodology book, prepare working papers, and provide findings to AO.
- Section 92E: Meaning of 'international transaction'.
- Section 92F: Definitions — 'arm's length price', 'associated enterprise', 'business combination', 'capital', 'comparables', 'cost of production', 'duration of agreement', 'financial services', 'goods', 'intangible property', 'international transaction', 'ordinary course of business', 'related party', 'services', 'specie'.
- Section 92CA: Reference to TPO; powers of AO after TPO report.
- Section 92CC: Penalties — failure to maintain documents (1% of TP adjustment); failure to furnish report u/s 92E (₹1,00,000); concealment/false statement u/s 271(1)(c) applicable for failing to report international transaction.
- Section 92CB: Penalties u/s 270A for under-reporting due to non-TP compliance.
- Section 92CD: Dispute Resolution Panels.
- Section 92CE: Penalties for false statements or furnishing of incorrect particulars.
- Section 92H: Reporting of international transactions and domestic transactions with AE (Form 3CEB).
- Section 288(2)(ib): Penalty for failure to comply with TP documentation requirements.

Specified Domestic Transactions (SDT) — Section 92BA: Transactions with non-AEs entered into with primary purpose of obtaining tax benefit are treated as international transactions for TP purposes.

Safe Harbour (Section 92CM): Assessess may declare transactions falling within safe harbour thresholds; TP authority shall accept declared ALP unless special circumstances warrant departure.

Advance Pricing Agreement (APA) — Section 92CC: Application for APA can be made to the Principal Commissioner. APA ensures certainty in pricing for future years. Types: unilateral (India only), bilateral/multilateral (under Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement). Application u/s 245GA.

Section 7: General Anti-Avoidance Rules (GAAR) and Angel Tax

GAAR (Sections 154A–154D, Chapter VIII): Introduced to counter aggressive tax avoidance schemes lacking commercial substance. GAAR is invoked when the main purpose of an arrangement is to obtain a tax benefit, and the arrangement involves any of the following impermissible avoidance actions: (a) misuse or abuse of provisions of the Act; (b) absence of commercial substance; (c) manner of creation/destruction of rights and obligations; (d) manner in which the concerned person is constituted; (e) use of loss, unutilised credits, or other advantages in a manner inconsistent with the object of the provision.

Section 154A: 'Tax benefit' means reduction, avoidance, deferral, or delay of tax liability. If the chief purpose is to obtain a tax benefit, the arrangement is a 'shadow arrangement' amenable to GAAR.

Immigration Committee / Appellate Authority: Section 154C provides for Immigration Committee to decide whether GAAR applies; appeals go to Income Tax Appellate Tribunal.

Guard Rails: Even if GAAR is applicable, it will not be invoked if the total tax benefit exceeds ₹3 crore (threshold), or if the arrangement is covered by a safe harbour, or if the taxpayer's income exceeds ₹5 crore and the tax benefit is not 'impermissible'. Additionally, GAAR will not apply if the arrangement is covered by an Advance Ruling or APA.

Angel Tax (Section 56(2)(viB)): Income from issue of shares by a 'company' (not listed on recognised stock exchange) where consideration exceeds fair market value is chargeable to tax under 'Other Sources'. Fair Market Value (FMV) of unlisted shares is determined per Rule 11UA: (a) if shares are quoted on recognised stock exchange — closing price on the date of issue; (b) if not quoted — discounting method (DCF) with prescribed parameters; for unlisted shares of private companies, the ASBA (Account Securities Based Approach) method may be used. The income is taxable at the slab rate applicable to the shareholder. Exceptions: (i) shares issued by venture capital company/fund registered with SEBI u/s 115TA — exempt; (ii) shares issued by start-up u/s 80-IAC — exempt; (iii) angel investment exceeding ₹25 lakh made through specified angel funds — exempt. The burden of proof lies on the company to demonstrate that the premium was justified by valuation reports from SEBI-registered merchant bankers or chartered accountants.

Section 8: GST — Supply, Value of Supply, Input Tax Credit, and Composition Scheme

Supply (Section 7): Every supply of goods or services (or both) made for a consideration in the course or furtherance of business, except items listed in Schedule II (activities to be treated as supply of goods or supply of services) and excluded under Schedule III (goods/services that neither constitute supply of goods nor supply of services). 'Supply' includes import of services u/s 5. Key supplies: permanent transfer/disposal of goods (goods supply); transfer of business assets (goods supply); lease/rental of immovable property (services supply).

Negative list u/s 9(1): Supplies mentioned in Schedule III are not supply — examples include sale of land, sale of building, service by employee to employer, funeral services, burial/cremation services, gambling/betting, lottery.

Composite/Mixed Supply (Sections 8–9): Composite supply = naturally bundled supply in ordinary course (e.g., hotel stay with breakfast) — taxed at rate of principal supply. Mixed supply = two or more supplies in combination but not naturally bundled — taxed at highest rate among the supplies.

Value of Supply (Section 15): Transaction value is the price actually paid/payable where supplier and recipient are unrelated and price is sole consideration. Inclusions: packing, freight, insurance, commissions, subsidies directly linked to supply, interest/penalty for late payment, agent charges, depreciation. Exclusions: GST itself, Cess, discounts per common account, secondary audience participation awards, and value of goods supplied free/at concessional rate by supplier.

Time of Supply (Sections 12–13): For goods — earlier of invoice date or receipt of payment, whichever is later (service — invoice date + 30 days). Extended time for services — invoice within 30 days from completion.

Input Tax Credit (ITC) — Section 16: Every registered person is entitled to ITC of input tax on goods/services used in course/business if conditions satisfied: (a) possession of tax invoice/debit note; (b) receipt of goods/services; (c) tax charged in invoice has been actually deposited to Government u/s 39; (d) return furnished u/s 39.

Blocked credits u/s 17(5): Motor vehicles (seats ≤ 13), food/beverages, outdoor catering, beauty treatment, health services, cosmetic surgery, life insurance, health insurance, works contract services for construction of immovable property (except plant/machinery), goods/services received for construction of immovable property (other than plant/machinery), gymkhana club membership, advertising, branding, national emblem/trademark, employee benefits, works contract services if used for construction of immovable property. ITC reversal u/s 16(4) for blocked credits — full reversal required.

Composition Scheme (Section 10): Small taxpayers with aggregate turnover ≤ ₹1.5 crore (₹75 lakh for NE states) can opt for composition levy. Rate: 1% (CGST+SGST) for traders/manufacturers (other than restaurants), 5% for restaurants (not serving liquor), 6% for manufacturers (CGST+SGST). Cannot collect tax from customers. No ITC available. Filing quarterly (GSTR-4). No inter-state supply allowed. Limited to specified categories of goods/services.

Section 9: GST — E-Invoicing, E-Way Bill, Returns, and Refunds

E-Invoicing (Section 16A and Invoice Rules, 2016 as amended): An 'electronic invoice' is a supply invoice uploaded on the Invoice Registration Portal (IRP) generating an Invoice Reference Number (IRN) and QR code. Mandatory for B2B invoices of taxpayers with aggregate turnover exceeding ₹500 crore in any previous year (threshold reduced progressively — currently applicable to persons with turnover above specified limit notified from time to time). E-invoice must be generated prior to dispatch of goods/services. E-way bill integration required. IRP authenticates invoice and generates IRN. QR code contains PAN, GSTIN, invoice number, date, value, and HSN of goods/services.

E-Way Bill (Rule 138): Required for movement of goods where value exceeds ₹50,000. Part A contains details of consignor/consignee, transport document, HSN, value. Part B contains transport vehicle details. EWB generated on portal and valid as per distance covered (1 day per 200 km for normal transport, additional for hazardous cargo). Validity extensions possible.

Return filing (Section 39 and Rules):
- GSTR-1: Outward supplies summary (monthly/quarterly).
- GSTR-3B: Summary return with self-assessment tax payment (monthly).
- GSTR-4: Composition dealer return (quarterly).
- GSTR-5: Non-resident taxable person (monthly).
- GSTR-5A: OIDAR (Online Information Technology Facilitator) registered person (monthly).
- GSTR-6: Input Service Distributor (monthly).
- GSTR-7: Tax deducted at source by deductor (monthly).
- GSTR-8: E-commerce operator TCS return (monthly).
- GSTR-9: Annual return (for regular taxpayers).
- GSTR-9A: Annual return for composition dealers.
- GSTR-10: Final return upon cancellation/term of registration.
- GSTR-11: UIN holders.

Refunds (Chapter V): Various refund scenarios — export of goods/services u/s 16 (refund of unutilised ITC); inverted duty structure u/s 16(1); advance ruling grants; excess payment u/s 16(3). Refund claim must be made within 2 years from relevant date. Provisional refund u/s 16(4) up to 90% of claimed amount permitted pending final disposal.

Cancellation (Section 29): Registration may be cancelled by officer suo motu, on application of registered person, or due to non-compliance (non-filing of returns for consecutive 2 years — automatic cancellation). Revocation u/s 30.

Section 10: Assessment Procedures, Appeals, and Compliance Mechanisms

Assessment Types (Sections 143–147):
(a) Summary assessment u/s 143(1): Machine-driven preliminary verification; no inquiry. Defective return notices u/s 143(1)(a) are mandatory.
(b) Scrutiny assessment u/s 143(2): Where AO considers it necessary to examine return for correctness. Notice u/s 143(2) within 3 years from end of AY for regular scrutiny; within 4 years for cases where income has escaped assessment.
(c) Revised return u/s 139(5): Filed before expiry of assessment year or completion of assessment, whichever is earlier.
(d) Completion/making up assessment u/s 144: Best judgment assessment where return not filed or audit report not furnished.
(e) Fresh assessment u/s 147: Income escaping assessment discovered after assessment is completed. Can be reopened if AO has reason to believe income chargeable to tax has escaped assessment. Time limit: u/s 148A — notice must be issued within 4 years (for cases without property outside India) or 6 years (for cases involving assets outside India).
(f) Reassessment u/s 148: Revised assessment after notice u/s 148A.
(g) rectification u/s 254: Rectification of mistake apparent from record by AO/Appellate Tribunal within 4 years from end of AY in which order passed.
(h) Revision u/s 263: Revisional powers with Principal Commissioner/Commissioner — only when order is erroneous and prejudicial to revenue. Cannot revise own orders.
(i) Review u/s 256A: By AO/Appellate Tribunal under prescribed conditions.

Appeals (Sections 246–250): First appeal to Commissioner (Appeals) u/s 246 — against orders u/s 143(3), 144, 147, 148, 245D(2), 245DA(2), etc. Second appeal to ITAT u/s 253 — within 60 days from receipt of order. Further appeal to High Court u/s 254 on substantial question of law. Extension of limitation u/s 255 for sufficient cause.

Compliance mechanisms: GST compliance audit u/s 35(5) — registered person with turnover > ₹2 crore must get accounts audited by CA/CMA and file GSTR-9A. Self-assessment return u/s 39. Audit u/s 44AB: Chartered accountant audit where turnover exceeds ₹1 crore (₹10 crore if 95% payments received via digital mode and 95% receipts via digital mode). Surveys u/s 133A; search and seizure u/s 132; book seizure u/s 132A; recovery u/s 222–238 (distraint, arrest, attachment).

Prosecution u/s 276A–277: Punishment for wilful attempt to evade tax, wilful failure to deduct/collect TDS/TCS, wilful withholding of material facts.

Section 11: Penalties, Prosecutions, and Dispute Resolution

Penalties (Chapter XXIII, Sections 270A–275):

Section 270A — Penalty for under-reporting/mis-reporting income: 50% of tax payable on under-reported income if misreporting is due to failure to maintain documents; 100% of tax on under-reported income for concealment/providing false information. Under-reported income = assessed income minus assessed income as per return + undisclosed income. Additional penalty u/s 271AAC for inaccurate assessment; u/s 271AAB for concealment in case of international/SDT; u/s 271AABD for non-disclosure in case of foreign income/assets.

Section 271 — Specific penalties: For concealment of income (u/s 271(1)(c)) — 100% to 300% of tax sought to be evaded. For failure to maintain books u/s 271B — 0.5% of total turnover/gross receipts or ₹1,50,000, whichever is less. Failure to get accounts audited u/s 271B — lower of 0.5% of turnover or ₹1,50,000.

Section 272A — Penalties for default in furnishing statements, incorrect PAN, failure to intimate changes.

Section 274 — Penalty u/s 270A applicable to penalty proceedings before income tax authorities.

Section 276A — Wilful attempt to evade tax: imprisonment up to 3 years + fine.

Section 276B — Wilful attempt to evade TDS/TCS: imprisonment up to 7 years + fine.

Section 276C — Wilful attempt to evade duty: imprisonment up to 7 years + fine.

Dispute Resolution Mechanism:
- Advance Ruling u/s 245Q: Rulings by AAR/State AAR on questions regarding liability to pay tax, eligibility for exemption, classification, applicability of notification.
- Settlement Commission u/s 245H: Person can apply for settlement before full assessment/ascertainment of true income. Full disclosure required. Order binding on department and applicant.
- Compromise u/s 245C: Power of Principal Commissioner/Commissioner to compromise cases where penalty proceedings are pending and amount involved > ₹50 lakh. Maximum compromise — 25% of tax outstanding + interest + penalty.
- Dispute Resolution Panels u/s 245RR: For cases involving transfer pricing, dispute resolution panels may be constituted by Principal Chief Commissioner. Binding on Commissioner unless tribunal sets aside.
- Mediation u/s 245RE: For pending appeals before CIT(A)/ITAT, mediation may be attempted through authorised mediators.

Important facts

  • India follows dual GST — CGST (Centre) + SGST/UTGST (State) for intra-state; IGST (Centre) for inter-state.
  • Residential status differs for individual, company, firm, and other persons — each governed by distinct provisions under Section 6.
  • Place of Effective Management (POEM) test determines residential status of foreign companies.
  • Long-term capital gains on listed equity shares/units are taxed at 12.5% u/s 112A without indexation benefit (for FY 2020-21 onwards).
  • Standard deduction of 30% u/s 24(b) is available on house property income.
  • Interest on housing loan for self-occupied property is deductible up to ₹2,00,000 u/s 24(b).
  • Unabsorbed depreciation can be carried forward indefinitely and set off against any head of income.
  • TDS on salary u/s 192 requires estimation of total income and deduction at applicable slab rates.
  • Threshold for TDS u/s 194C: ₹30,000 per payment or ₹1,00,000 aggregate in a financial year.
  • Transfer pricing applies to international transactions between associated enterprises (≥26% voting power).
  • Primary TP method is CUP; TNMM is most commonly used in practice for Indian assessee.
  • Angel tax u/s 56(2)(viB) exempts shares issued by SEBI-registered venture capital entities and start-ups u/s 80-IAC.
  • E-invoicing mandate applies to taxpayers with aggregate turnover exceeding notified threshold (currently ₹500 crore, phased reduction ongoing).
  • E-way bill threshold is ₹50,000 per consignment.
  • Composition scheme: 1% (traders/manufacturers other than restaurant), 5% (restaurant), 6% (manufacturers). No ITC availablerg.
  • Blocked credits u/s 17(5) include food, health insurance, cosmetic surgery, motor vehicles (≤13 seats), works contract for construction, employee benefits.
  • Reassessment u/s 147 can be initiated within 4 years generally, or 6 years where income escapes assessment involving assets outside India.
  • Revisional power u/s 263 is exercisable only when order is erroneous and prejudicial to revenue — not both conditions are mandatory.
  • Penalty u/s 270A ranges from 50% to 100% of tax on under-reported income depending on nature of default.
  • GST annual return u/s 39 is GSTR-3B (monthly) and GSTR-9 (annual); composition dealer files GSTR-4 (quarterly).

Formulas and relationships

Indexation factor = (CII of year of transfer / CII of year of acquisition). CII values notified by CBDT each year (FY 2001-02 = 100, FY 2023-24 = 331, FY 2024-25 = 348 approximately).
Indexed cost of acquisition = Original cost × (CII of year of transfer / CII of base year i.e., FY 2001-02)
Indexed cost of improvement = Improvement cost × (CII of year of transfer / CII of year of improvement)
Capital gain = Full value of consideration less indexed cost of acquisition less cost of improvement less expenditure incurred wholly and exclusively in connection with transfer.
TDS amount = Applicable rate × Base amount (after deducting threshold where applicable). Higher rate applies if PAN not furnished.
TP adjustment = Transaction price minus Arm's Length Price (ALP). Positive adjustment increases income; negative adjustment reduces.
E-way bill validity = 1 day for every 200 km of distance (or part thereof). Additional 1 day for hazardous goods.
GST e-invoice QR code contains: PAN, GSTIN, INVOICE NUMBER, DATE, VALUE, and HSN_CODE.
TCS under Section 206C(1F): 0.1% on sale consideration exceeding ₹5 lakh where buyer's turnover > ₹10 crore in preceding FY.
Compound Interest u/s 220(2): 1% per month or part thereof on overdue tax from due date to actual payment date.
Presumptive tax u/s 44AD: 6% of turnover (digital receipts) or 8% (non-digital) for eligible assessees with turnover ≤ ₹3 crore.
Presumptive income u/s 44ADA: 60% of gross professional receipts for specified professions with total income ≤ ₹75 lakh.

Common mistakes

  • Confusing 'resident' with 'ordinarily resident' — RNOR's foreign income is taxable only if received in India; ROR's global income is taxable.
  • For companies, applying the individual-based residential test instead of the POEM test under Section 6(3).
  • Missing that unabsorbed depreciation is carried forward indefinitely unlike other business losses (limited to 8 years).
  • Incorrectly applying indexation benefit to LTCG on listed equity shares — indexation is NOT available u/s 112A.
  • Applying Section 24(b) interest limit of ₹2,00,000 to pre-construction period interest of let-out property where the limit is ₹1,50,000 in total spread over 5 years.
  • Mixing up TDS sections — e.g., applying 194J rate to 194C payments or vice versa.
  • Selecting wrong TP method arbitrarily without considering comparability analysis and nature of transaction.
  • Forgetting that e-invoicing mandate applies only to B2B invoices and not to B2C invoices (where e-way bill suffices).
  • Not reversing blocked credits u/s 17(5) — this is a frequent audit observation and leads to demand notices.
  • Assuming revision u/s 263 can be exercised merely because the AO's order is 'wrong' — it requires error AND prejudice to revenue simultaneously.
  • Treating GST registration cancellation as automatic — actually, cancellation requires formal order unless non-filing for 2 consecutive years triggers automatic cancellation.
  • Confusing Time of Supply under GST with Transfer of Property under Income-tax — they serve different purposes and follow different rules.
  • Forgetting that e-way bill validity extends only for the distance actually travelled and recalculating if goods are transported via multiple vehicles.
  • Applying TDS rate of 20% flat on all payments without checking whether lower certificate u/s 197 is available.
  • Missing that TP provisions apply to specified domestic transactions u/s 92BA even with non-associated enterprises.
