Quick Revision: Advanced Tax Laws and Practice (CS Professional, Professional Group 3)

CSEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Advanced Tax Laws and Practice is the module covering post-enactment tax administration — GST procedural intricacies, indirect-tax dispute mechanisms, direct-tax compliance architecture, transfer pricing, advance rulings, MAT/AMT, and the practical tools a practicing Company Secretary uses in tax advisory and representation.
  • GST (Goods and Services Tax) is a comprehensive, multi-stage, destination-based indirect tax levied on supply of goods and services, replacing multiple central and state levies; it operates through CGST, SGST/UTGST, and IGST with integrated input tax credit across the value chain.
  • Input Tax Credit (ITC) is the mechanism under Section 16 of the CGST Act allowing a registered person to credit the GST paid on inputs and input services against output liability, subject to conditions in Sections 16–18 and Rule 36–37 of the CGST Rules.
  • Transfer Pricing (TP) under Chapter X of the Income Tax Act governs cross-border and specified domestic transactions between associated enterprises, requiring arm's length pricing and documentation to prevent profit shifting.
  • Advance Ruling (AR) under Section 245HR of the Income Tax Act and Chapter XVII-B of the CGST Act provides a binding pre-decision on specified questions relating to eligibility, taxability, valuation, or classification before the transaction is undertaken.
  • MAT (Minimum Alternate Tax) under Section 115JB and AMT (Alternate Minimum Tax) under Section 115JC are anti-avoidance provisions ensuring that taxpayers claiming deductions/exemptions pay a minimum tax at a prescribed rate on book profits.
  • Dispute Resolution Mechanism in taxation comprises internal remedies (appeals to APT/CTA), appellate authorities (GCAD, ITAT, GST Appellate Tribunal), judicial remedies (High Courts, Supreme Court), and alternative mechanisms (compromise, settlement, VOCAL scheme).
  • VOCAL (Voluntary Compliance Across the Globe) Scheme is a Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs (CBIC) initiative offering time-bound relaxation for rectifying certain indirect-tax defaults, typically waiving interest/penalty in exchange for voluntary disclosure and payment.

Key concepts

  • GST Procedural Architecture: registration thresholds and composition scheme, invoicing and e-invoicing mandates under Rule 48, e-way bill under Rule 138, GSTR-1/GSTR-3B/GSTR-9 reconciliation, and the concept of 'supply' under Section 7 read with Schedule I–IV covering continuous supply, composite/mixed supply, and works contract.
  • ITC Mechanics: blocked credits under Section 17(5) (cafes, cosmetics, motor vehicles unless in supply chain, construction of immovable property except plant/plant machinery), reversals on free samples/gifts/samples, apportionment of credit for exempt supplies, and the proportionality rule under Rule 36(4) capping ineligible ITC at 5% of eligible ITC.
  • Valuation in GST: Section 15 includes price actually paid, linked transactions, abatement for wholesaler discount, and inclusion of interest/freight where borne by supplier; Rule 27 provides transaction value as default, then sequential fallback methods; RCM valuation under Rule 28–30 for goods/services supplied between related persons.
  • Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM): Section 9(3) covers notified categories (PGD, ARC, recovery agent, supplier to unregistered person above threshold, etc.); Section 9(4) was struck down as unconstitutional; RCM applies even to registered persons with specified exceptions; time of supply under RCM is earlier of payment or 60 days from invoice.
  • Place of Supply and Integrated GST: Section 10 distinguishes goods (destination) vs services (recipient location); rules for immovable property, transport, hotel accommodation, restaurant, events; IGST applicability on export/import and inter-state supplies; Section 13 contains POOS for imported services.
  • GST Refunds: Chapter XVIII provides refunds for exports/zero-rated supplies under LUT or payment of IGST with refund, ineligibility of ITC and rebate under Section 16, refund of unutilized ITC under Section 16(3), and provisional refund mechanism; Section 38 handles provisional and final adjustment.
  • Transfer Pricing Methodologies: Arm's Length Price (ALP) determination under Section 92C using CUP, Resale Price, Cost Plus, Profit Split, and TNMM as the preferred method; Threshold limit of INR 10 crore for international/specified domestic transactions; Failure to maintain documentation attracts penalties under Section 271AA.
  • TP Documentation and PAP: Form 3CEB must be filed by tax audit u/s 44AB; international transactions include import/export of goods/services, capital transfers, management expenses; Specified Domestic Transactions (SDTs) cover service fees, royalties, interest, transfer of intangible assets u/s 92B(2); Advance Pricing Agreement under Section 92CC offers certainty for future years.
  • MAT/AMT Framework: MAT u/s 115JB levied at 18.5% u/s 115JB(1)(a) plus surcharge and cess on book profit after add-backs under Section 115JB(2); MAT credit allowed under Section 115JAA can be carried forward for 15 years; AMT u/s 115JC applies to professionals/firm partners at 18.5% u/s 115JC(1)(a) with credit under Section 115JDB.
  • Advance Rulings — Income Tax: Authority u/s 245HR passes rulings on taxability of transactions, operation of Section 90/90A/92/92B/92C/92D/92E, eligibility for deductions u/s 80H to 80HHE, and status u/s 65; ruling is binding on applicant and lower authority but not on Commissioner (Appeals) unless same facts; appeal to High Court u/s 245ZH within 90 days.
  • Advance Rulings — GST: Appointed by Central or State Govt.; Jurisdiction for GST matters includes determination of classification, valuation, admissibility of ITC, time/value of supply, liability to pay tax on goods/services; Ruling is binding on applicant, officer of same jurisdiction, and subordinate officers; appeal to GSTAA within 90 days.
  • GSTN and Technology Infrastructure: GST Network (GSTN) operates return filing, IRP for e-invoicing, EPC for e-way bills, and e-Way Bill Portals; e-Invoice via IRP generates IRN and QR code; cancellation of e-invoice within 24 hours permitted; e-Way Bill validity based on distance per Rule 138(4).
  • Audit and Investigation Powers: Commissioner has power u/s 67 to conduct raid/investigation; audit u/s 65 can be ordered for suspected suppression/false claims; self-assessment return verification u/s 61; scrutiny u/s 64 for GST registration and returns; audit under Rule 80–85 of CGST Rules.
  • Penalties and Prosecution: General penalty u/s 122 covers false statements, suppression, misuse of ITC, fake invoices; specific penalties range from 10% to 100% of tax evaded; prosecution u/s 132 for offenses with fine/ imprisonment; compounding u/s 128 by Commissioner.
  • Direct Tax Administration: Assessment types u/s 143–147 — scrutiny u/s 143(2)/(3), best judgment u/s 144, re-assessment u/s 147 (changed opinion/post-April 2021 restricted to 4-year block)；assessment u/s 153A for search cases；assessment u/s 158BC for requisition cases；penalties u/s 270A for under-reporting/misreporting with 50%–200% rates.
  • VOCAL Scheme and Amnesty Schemes: CBIC periodically issues amnesty/voluntary scheme notifications offering waivers on interest/penalty for deposit of outstanding tax demand; conditions typically require filing of statement and full payment; schemes are time-bound and non-extendable without fresh notification.
  • Goods and Services Tax Appellate Tribunal (GSTAA): Established under Section 107 of CGST Act; appeals from orders of Commissioner (Appeals) can be preferred within 90 days (60 days for GST Appellate Tribunal post-GST amendments); tribunal can remit factual issues and pass fresh orders; precedent-setting decisions published on GSTAA website.
  • Interface with Other Laws: Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA) interface with tax evasion via reporting of suspicious transactions; section 194Q TDS on sale of goods; section 194Q threshold changed to INR 50 lakhs; section 194O TDS by e-commerce operators; section 206AB higher TDS for specified defaulters.
  • Key Judicial Trends and Landmark Principles: Constitutional validity of Section 16(2)(a) ITC condition upheld (Google India case); composition scheme exit conditions; e-way bill as procedural vs substantive requirement (Supreme Court held non-compliance not vitiating proceedings if tax proved); nexus between supplier and recipient for ITC.
  • Company Secretary's Role in Tax Practice: Acting as representative assessee u/s 288 of IT Act before Assessing Officer/CIT(A)/ITAT/High Court/Supreme Court; advising on TP documentation and transfer pricing studies; drafting LUT for GST exports; handling RCM compliance; appearing before GSTAA; managing amnesty/scheme filings; coordinating with tax auditors u/s 44AB.

Important facts

  • CGST Act 2017, SGST Act 2017, IGST Act 2017, and CGST Rules 2017 came into effect from 1 July 2017; GST Council constituted u/s 246 with Chairperson (Union Finance Minister), State Finance Ministers, and Union Minister of State for Finance as members.
  • GST Council recommendations require 3/4 majority; its decisions are binding on Central and State Governments; Article 279A provides constitutional backing for GST Council.
  • E-invoice threshold for FY 2023–24 onwards: businesses with aggregate turnover exceeding INR 5 crore in previous FY are mandated to issue e-invoices; the threshold may change via GSTN notifications.
  • E-way bill required for movement of goods valued over INR 50,000 per consignment within a state (Rule 138); validity: 1 day for up to 200 km, +1 day per additional 200 km (or part thereof) for inland, extended for overland conveyance.
  • Time of supply for goods u/s 12: earlier of date of invoice, date of receipt of payment, or 30 days from dispatch (31 days for registered recipient); for services u/s 13: earlier of invoice date or 60 days from service provision.
  • Composition Scheme: applicable to taxpayers with turnover up to INR 1.5 crore (INR 75 lakh for NE states); rate 1% (CGST+SGST) for manufacturers/traders, 5% for restaurants not providing liquor, 2% for restaurant service providers without liquor; composition dealer cannot collect tax, cannot make inter-state supply, and must file annual return Form GSTR-4.
  • Zero-rated supply u/s 16: export of goods/services or supply to SEZ with payment of IGST and claim refund, or export with LUT and zero-rate benefit; unutilized ITC on zero-rated supply refundable u/s 16(3).
  • National Interest Ruling concept was scrapped; Advance Rulings replaced by Autonomous Rulings in some contexts and by specialized benches.
  • Section 92C(1) prescribes ALP determination using any of five methods; Transfer Pricing Officer (TPO) determines ALP and passes order u/s 92CA(3); Additional Commissioner/Dy Commissioner decides u/s 92CA(1) where TPO refers or directly if no reference made.
  • Penalty u/s 271AAC for failure to disclose income in respect of which TDS/TCS has been deducted — 10% to 5% of tax sought to be evaded; penalty u/s 271AAB for TP documentation failure — 0.5% of international transaction value.
  • Revised Limit for Re-assessment u/s 147: for assessment years beginning from FY 2020–21 onwards, reassessment possible only if income escaping assessment is INR 50 lakh or more for 4 years from end of relevant AY; for older AYs existing provisions apply.
  • Section 270A penalty rates: 50% of tax sought to be evaded for under-reporting, 200% for misreporting; under-reporting defined u/s 201(1) includes concealment, inaccurate return, disallowance of deduction, incorrect computation.
  • Section 115JB(2)(b) add-backs include depreciation claimed above actual, any amount withdrawn from reserve, and any dividend declared/distributed out of past profits; minimum alternate tax credit under Section 115JAA is adjustable against regular tax liability for up to 15 assessment years.
  • Section 194Q TDS on purchase of goods: buyer deducting at 0.1% on purchase exceeding INR 50 lakhs from seller whose turnover exceeds INR 10 crores; threshold and rates subject to budget amendments annually.
  • VOCAL scheme timeline and eligibility vary per notification; typical conditions include: (i) filing of statement in prescribed form, (ii) payment of outstanding tax along with interest, (iii) waiver of interest up to notified date, (iv) no prosecution pending.
  • Form GST DRC-03 is used for voluntary payment of tax/interest/penalty; Form GST DRC-01 for show cause notice response; Form GST DRC-02 for payment of demand via adjudication order.
  • GSTN portal provides Auto Draft of Demand (ADD) system generating draft demand u/s 73/74; Section 73 for non-fraud cases with 3-year limitation (5 years for fraud), Section 74 for fraud with longer periods and heavier penalties.
  • Service Tax residual provisions remain operative only for services not specified u/s 7 of IGST Act or excluded u/s 9 of CGST Act; historically levied at 15% combined rate before GST implementation.
  • Customs duties include Basic Customs Duty (BCD), Social Welfare Surcharge (SWS) at 10% of BCD, Integrated GST on imports u/s 3 of IGST Act, and Anti-Dumping/Countervailing duties as applicable.
  • Presumptive Taxation u/s 44ADA (professionals with gross receipts up to INR 75 lakh, 60% of receipts deemed profit) and u/s 44ADE (eligible business with turnover up to INR 300 crore, 6% for digital transactions) replaced by new presumptive schemes post-FY 2023–24 amendments.
  • Limitation for refund claims u/s 117 of CGST Act: 1 year from relevant date; for export refund, relevant date is date of shipping bill or payment of IGST and filing of claim.
  • Section 245HC of IT Act governs advance ruling procedure — application u/s 245HA, presumption of material facts disclosed being true, TPO can also refer TP matters u/s 245HB(1A).
  • GST composition dealer opting to register after availing composition scheme must file Form GST REG-11 within 30 days; cannot claim ITC during composition period.
  • Section 168 provides for emergency notifications where President/Governor is satisfied that circumstances exist requiring immediate action; notified provisions deemed enacted under Constitution; rarely invoked.
  • Special Economic Zone (SEZ) unit enjoys duty-free import u/s 5 of Customs Act and SEZ Act benefits; SEZ developer/unit must comply with Export Obligation and maintain books of account separately.

Memory aids

  • GST ITC blocks (Section 17(5)): Remember CAMEL — Cafes, Automobiles (motor vehicles), General Goods (cosmetics above INR 1000/person), Events/Membership, Ladies' paraphernalia (makeup/perfumes/cosmetics); exclude plant/plant machinery and construction services for factory/building.
  • Five TP Methods mnemonic: CRAFT — Comparable Uncontrolled Price, Resale Price, Cost Plus, TNMM (Transactional Net Margin Method), Profit Split; remember in order of preference: CUP first, then Resale/Cost Plus, then TNMM (most commonly applied), finally Profit Split.
  • MAT/AMT credit storage: Think of a 'Tax Locker' — MAT credit stored in Section 115JAA locker for 15 years, AMT credit stored in Section 115JDB locker for same duration; both can be 'withdrawn' when regular liability exceeds alternate minimum.
  • E-way bill rules: Use '50/200' — 50,000 rupees threshold, 200 km per day validity unit; extend by one day for every additional 200 km (or part thereof); NE/remote areas may have different norms per CBIC notifications.
  • Sections for GST supply: Memorize 7(Supply definition), 9(Liable to pay tax), 12(Time of supply for goods), 13(Time of supply for services), 15(Valuation), 16(ITC eligibility), 17(ITC restrictions/blocked credits), 24(Compulsory registration), 31(Invoice), 38(Provisional demand), 73/74(Determination of tax not billed).
  • Penalty matrix: 10% (minor delay/procedural), 25% (delay in filing return), 50% (under-reporting u/s 270A), 100% (fraud/suppression), 200% (misreporting u/s 270A); remember escalating severity from procedural lapse → concealment → fraud → misrepresentation.
  • VOCAL acronym reminder: V = Voluntary, O = Overdue, C = Compliance, A = Across, L = Globe; recall as 'voluntarily clearing old tax dues to come back to legal compliance'.
  • ITAT jurisdiction triggers: assessment order, penalty order u/s 270A/271, TP adjustment u/s 92CA, enhancement of loss/u/s 154 rectification — all first appeal destinations to the appropriate bench of ITAT.
  • GST portal forms mnemonic: REG for Registration, GSTR-1 for outward supply, GSTR-3B for summary return, GSTR-4 for composition, GSTR-9 for annual return, DRC-01/02/03 for disputes/payments, ITC-04/05/06 for special reversals/refunds, ARN for acknowledgment of every filing.
  • Limitation periods: 3 years for Section 73 (non-fraud), 5 years for Section 74 (fraud), 6 months for appeal to Commissioner (Appeals) u/s 107 (extended to 90 days per amendment), 90 days for GSTAA appeal u/s 108, 30 days for revision u/s 105 before Commissioner.
  • Section 194Q vs Section 206C(1H): 194Q deducts on purchase > INR 50L from seller with turnover > INR 10Cr; 206C(1H) was the earlier collection-at-source clause repealed; ensure distinction for exam purposes.
  • Ruling hierarchy: Advance Ruling Authority → GSTAA → High Court u/s 245ZH(1)/Article 226; for GST, ruling binding on applicant + same jurisdiction officer + subordinates; not binding on other jurisdictions unless identical facts.

Exam tips

  • In CS Professional descriptive papers, always cite the specific section of the relevant Act when answering — 'Section 17(5)' carries more weight than merely saying 'blocked credits'; for GST write CGST Act/SGST Act section, for direct tax write Income Tax Act section.
  • When asked about procedures, follow chronological sequence: Notification → Application → Verification → Adjudication → Appeal → Execution. Examiners reward structured step-wise answers over narrative descriptions.
  • For GST valuation questions, identify the default method first (transaction value u/s 15) then fall back sequentially if value is not ascertainable; state the fallback hierarchy explicitly to secure marks even if the final figure differs.
  • In TP problems, calculate the ALP using the most appropriate method with working notes; if the question allows choice, justify why TNMM or CUP is preferred; always compute the arm's length price difference and state the resultant adjustment amount clearly.
  • For MAT/AMT numericals, start with net profit per P&L as per Companies Act, then systematically add back items u/s 115JB(2) and 115JC(2) before applying the MAT rate — never jump to final tax liability without showing the book profit reconciliation.
  • Remember that ICSI evaluates application ability: frame answers with 'Given the facts, under Section X, the position is...' rather than reciting provisions verbatim; integration of fact + law earns higher marks.
  • For assertion-reason and case-study questions on tax practice, identify the trigger event first (date of transaction, date of default, date of remedy available) — time factor is critical in GST, TP, and direct-tax assessment windows.
  • In GST procedural questions, differentiate between show-cause notice under Section 73 (no fraud) and Section 74 (fraud) — the distinction affects limitation period, penalty range, and bond/compound feasibility; explicitly mention this differentiation in answers.
  • For RCM questions, verify whether the category is notified u/s 9(3), 9(4), or 9(5); currently only 9(3) and 9(5) are operative; answer accordingly even if the question appears outdated, with a brief note on the constitutional position.
  • Use a comparison table when discussing ITC under GST vs input tax under pre-GST regime — this demonstrates depth and avoids lengthy paragraphs; headings like 'Eligibility', 'Blocked Credits', 'Reversal', 'Documentation' keep the answer structured.
  • In dispute-resolution answers, always list the hierarchy of remedies: Departmental appeal → Tribunal → High Court → Supreme Court; mark the limitation and grounds for each stage concisely — this shows systematic understanding expected at professional level.
  • When handling a VOCAL/amnesty scheme scenario, state the eligibility criteria, conditions for waiver, filing deadline, and consequences of non-compliance; use a decision-tree format in your answer to present clarity.
  • For Company Secretary role questions, explicitly mention dual capacity — CS acts as representative assessee AND as compliance advisor; highlight documents the CS must ensure are maintained (TP study, GST registers, e-invoice logs, e-way bills, annual return).

Common traps

  • Assuming Section 9(4) RCM still applies: the Supreme Court in State of Karnataka v. Amazon Seller Services (2022) struck down Section 9(4) as unconstitutional; RCM now applies only to specifically notified categories under Section 9(3) and Section 9(5) for notified goods.
  • Confusing Time of Supply for Goods vs Services: For goods, it is earliest of invoice date or payment date or 30 days from dispatch; for services, it is earliest of invoice date or 60 days from service date or payment date — do not conflate the two.
  • Applying ITC blocklist generically: Section 17(5) does not uniformly block all motor vehicles — vehicles used in supply of transport services and construction of immovable property (other than plant/plant machinery) get differential treatment; exam questions often test these exceptions.
  • Treating e-way bill non-compliance as substantive offence: Supreme Court in Tanu Goyal (2022) held that detention of goods and confiscation cannot be solely on technical e-way bill irregularity if tax is paid and documents are verified; penalty is limited to late fee under Section 129.
  • Mixing up Section 73 and Section 74 limitation periods: Section 73 has 3-year general limitation, 5-year for fraud; Section 74 has 5-year general, 7-year for fraud — penalty u/s 74 is 100% whereas u/s 73 is maximum 100% of tax demanded; confusion here leads to wrong answer in MCQs.
  • Assuming all errors attract Section 270A penalty equally: there is a distinction between under-reporting (50%) and misreporting (200%); providing inaccurate particulars constitutes misreporting while failing to report income entirely is under-reporting; boundary depends on whether facts were concealed vs incorrectly stated.
  • Confusing TP with Transfer Pricing Adjustment: TPO's addition is not an outright penalty but attracts separate penalty u/s 271AAB only on documentation failure; the adjusted amount itself becomes part of total income without additional penalty, unlike ordinary under-reporting.
  • Thinking MAT credit expires after 15 years unconditionally: while Section 115JAA(1)(a) permits carry forward for 15 years, the credit lapses only if not set off within that period — it cannot be surrendered voluntarily and must be claimed automatically in subsequent years.
  • Believing that e-invoice cancellation is possible anytime: correction/cancellation of e-invoice can be done within 24 hours of generation only; after 24 hours, credit note/debit note mechanism must be followed — exams frequently present scenarios crossing this 24-hour window.
  • Assuming composition dealer can claim ITC on capital goods: composition dealer is explicitly barred from claiming ITC u/s 10; however, once opted out and registered under normal scheme, accumulated capital goods ITC can be claimed subject to Rule 38–40 conditions.
  • Confusing Place of Supply rules for goods: POoS for goods is generally the destination of goods (Section 10(1)); for immovable property it is where the property is situated; for transport services it is the place where the journey commences; misapplication leads to wrong IGST/CGST-SGST split.
  • Overlooking the distinction between International Transaction and Specified Domestic Transaction for TP: SDTs under Section 92B(2) were added by Finance Act 2023; before this, TP provisions applied only to international transactions; examination questions now frequently test this updated scope.
  • Assuming VAT/customs disputes are subsumed into GST: many central/state taxes were subsumed into GST from 1 July 2017, but some levies like Customs Duty, Stamp Duty, Electricity Duty, and GST compensation cess remain outside GST; students must distinguish subsumed vs non-subsumed taxes.
  • Mistaking the forum for challenging GST Order: first appeal lies to Commissioner (Appeals) u/s 107; second appeal to GSTAA u/s 108; from GSTAA to High Court u/s 117 only on substantial question of law; do not skip levels or confuse with ITAT jurisdiction.
  • Not distinguishing between 'advance ruling' and 'judicial precedents': advance ruling is binding only on the applicant and officers of the same jurisdiction on identical facts; it does not constitute binding precedent under Section 245HR(9) or GST equivalent provision; later facts/material differences release the binding character.
