Quick Revision: Governance, Risk Management, Compliances and Ethics — CS Professional

CSEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Corporate Governance: A system of rules, practices, and processes by which a company is directed and controlled. It involves balancing the interests of stakeholders such as shareholders, management, customers, suppliers, financiers, government, and the community.
  • Good Corporate Governance: Transparency, accountability, fairness, and responsibility in corporate decision-making, ensuring that the board acts in the best interest of the company and its shareholders while protecting stakeholder rights.
  • Risk: The possibility of an event occurring that will have an impact on the achievement of objectives. Measured in terms of risk incidence and risk impact.
  • Enterprise Risk Management (ERM): A comprehensive, integrated approach to managing all forms of risk across an organization, aligned with strategy and performed within the risk appetite of the entity.
  • Compliance: The state of adhering to applicable laws, regulations, standards, and internal policies. In a corporate context, it means conforming to legal requirements, regulatory guidelines, and ethical norms.
  • Compliance Function: The organizational mechanism through which a company ensures adherence to laws and regulations, typically led by a Chief Compliance Officer or compliance department.
  • Ethics: Moral principles that govern a person's or organization's behavior. Corporate ethics refers to the accepted standards of right and wrong guiding conduct in business.
  • Code of Conduct: A formal document setting out the values, ethical standards, and behavioral expectations for directors, employees, and officers of a company.
  • Whistleblower: An employee or stakeholder who reports unethical, illegal, or improper activities within an organization, typically protected under the Vigil Mechanism framework.
  • Risk Appetite: The amount and type of risk an organization is willing to seek or retain in pursuit of its objectives, as approved by the Board.
  • Risk Tolerance: The acceptable level of variation around the achievement of objectives, reflecting how much deviation from targets the organization can endure.
  • Internal Control: The process designed and implemented by the board, management, and other personnel to provide reasonable assurance regarding the achievement of objectives in operational efficiency, reliable reporting, and compliance with laws.
  • Nomination and Remuneration Committee: A committee of the Board responsible for formulating criteria for appointment of directors and key managerial personnel, and determining their remuneration.
  • Stakeholder: Any individual, group, or institution that has interests in, or is affected by, the activities and performance of the company.

Important facts

  • SEBI LODR 2015, Schedule IV contains the Legal and General Management Board Responsibilities that form the governance compliance checklist for listed entities.
  • Under Section 149(4), a company must have at least one-third of total directors as independent directors, and for top 1,000 listed entities, at least one-half must be independent.
  • Independent Director criteria: Section 149(6) — majority of time and attention, no material or pecuniary relationship with the company, not a relative of promoters or KMP, professional competence.
  • Section 166(2) lists six duties of directors: act in good faith, exercise proper care and skill, avoid conflict of interest, not achieve undue gain, not assign office, and act in accordance with the Act and articles.
  • Secretarial Audit under Section 204 requires a practicing Company Secretary to certify compliance with applicable laws; report in Form MR-3.
  • CSR mandate (Section 135): Companies with ₹1,000 crore+ turnover OR ₹500 crore+ net worth OR ₹5 crore+ profit must constitute a CSR Committee and spend at least 2% of average net profits.
  • SEBI has mandated that the MD/CEO and Whole-Time Directors cannot also serve as Independent Directors in the same company (amendment w.e.f. 2017).
  • Risk Management Committee is mandatory for top 1,000 listed entities by market capitalization under SEBI LODR.
  • Related Party Transactions under Section 188 require prior approval of the Audit Committee and, if material, shareholder approval via special resolution.
  • The Cadbury Committee Report (1992, UK) is the foundational document for modern corporate governance — it defined governance as the system by which companies are directed and controlled.
  • The Narayana Murthy Committee (2005) recommended reforms for Indian corporate governance, leading to SEBI's original COPS (Corporate Governance Standards).
  • ICSI's Code of Ethics for Company Secretaries includes five fundamental principles: Integrity, Objectivity, Professional Competence and Due Care, Confidentiality, Professional Behavior.
  • BRSR Core was introduced by SEBI for enhanced sustainability reporting, requiring assured disclosure for top 150 listed entities from FY 2024-25.
  • Clause 49 of the old Listing Agreement was replaced by SEBI LODR Regulations, 2015, making governance obligations regulatory rather than exchange-specific.
  • Under FEMA, the Board must ensure compliance with foreign exchange regulations; the Authorized Dealer Category-I bank serves as the primary reporting channel.
  • Insider Trading prohibition under SEBI PIT Regulations 2015 applies to 'trading in securities' while in possession of unpublished price sensitive information (UPSI).
  • The concept of 'comply or explain' under corporate governance allows entities to deviate from governance norms if they provide a reasoned explanation in the annual report.
  • Board Evaluation: Under SEBI LODR, the board must undertake an annual evaluation of its own performance, board committees, and individual directors.

Memory aids

  • DIRRAC to remember the 6 duties of Directors u/s 166: D — Don't assign office; I — Indulge in conflicts avoid; R — Respect Act & AoA; R — Reasonable care & skill; A — Act in good faith; C — No undue gain.
  • Remember top-1000 governance thresholds as '1-1': One-half independent, One committee each for Risk, Stakeholder (top-500), and Stakeholder grievance. The number 1,000 recurs often in SEBI LODR for governance mandates.
  • COSO ERM 2017 components — ABCDE mnemonic: A — Architecture & Culture; B — Business strategy & objectives; C — Performance (identify-assess-prioritize-respond); D — Review & revise; E — Information & communication & reporting.
  • Types of Risk — SCRFORT mnemonic: S — Strategic, C — Compliance, R — Reputational, F — Financial, O — Operational, R — Risk (cyber/technology), T — Territorial/Geopolitical.
  • Board Committees — CARD mnemonic: A — Audit, R — Remuneration & Nomination, D — Disclosure & related party oversight, plus Risk and CSR as specialized committees.
  • ESG pillars: Environment = Earth (planetary care), Social = Society (people first), Governance = Guardrails (rules and structures).
  • Whistleblower/Vigil Mechanism core protection: V-C-D-R — Victims protected, Confidentiality maintained, Direct access to Audit Committee Chair, Retaliation prohibited.

Exam tips

  • In CS Professional exams, questions on this paper frequently test the interplay between the Companies Act 2013 and SEBI LODR — always cite both sources when answering a governance compliance question.
  • For case-study based questions, read the facts carefully to identify which governance failure is being tested: board composition, RPT approval gap, risk management absence, whistleblowing breakdown, or ESG non-disclosure.
  • When asked about SEBI LODR governance provisions, mention Schedule IV and Regulation numbers where possible — it strengthens credibility of answers.
  • In describing ERM, always reference COSO framework as the benchmark unless the question specifies ISO 31000; COSO is more frequently tested in ICSI examinations.
  • Remember the threshold numbers: top 1,000 (market cap) triggers most additional governance obligations under SEBI LODR — this is the critical cutoff.
  • For ESG questions, link principles to specific reporting frameworks: BRSR for India, TCFD for climate, SASB for industry-specific metrics, GRI for global standards.
  • When writing about ethical decision-making, use the structured model (identify → evaluate → decide → act → review) rather than generic moral statements.
  • Distinguish between 'mandatory' and 'recommended' requirements under the Companies Act — this distinction is frequently tested in MCQ and descriptive both.
  • For the vigil mechanism, always highlight that the Audit Committee must review complaints every six months as per SEBI LODR Regulation 22(10).
  • In exam answers relating to corporate governance failures, suggest remedial measures alongside your analysis — marking schemes reward practical recommendations.

Common traps

  • Confusing the independent director ratio: For top 1,000 listed entities it is one-half (1/2); for other listed entities it is one-third (1/3). Do not mix these up.
  • Assuming all companies need a Risk Management Committee — it is only mandatory for the top 1,000 listed entities by market capitalization, not all listed companies.
  • Mixing up Section 188 (Related Party Transactions) with Section 177 (Audit Committee functions). RPT approval by the Audit Committee is a subset of Sec 177 but the substantive requirement is under Sec 188.
  • Thinking whistleblower protection is limited to employees — the vigil mechanism can extend to directors, vendors, and other stakeholders as the company deems appropriate.
  • Confusing BRSR with BRSR Core: BRSR is the full report; BRSR Core applies to the top 150 listed entities and requires restricted assurance of disclosures.
  • Assuming the CSR spending requirement is 2% of gross profits — it is 2% of average net profits during the immediately preceding three years.
  • Believing SEBI LODR and Companies Act requirements are identical — SEBI LODR imposes additional/stricter requirements on listed entities that do not apply to private companies.
  • Thinking that an Independent Director can also be a Key Managerial Personnel — this is explicitly prohibited.
  • Assuming vigil mechanism reporting goes to the Managing Director — SEBI LODR specifically requires direct access to the Chairperson of the Audit Committee.
  • Confusing the duties under Section 166 with the powers under Section 179 — duties are fiduciary obligations (what directors must do); powers are the authorities delegated to the board (what the board can do).
