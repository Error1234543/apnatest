NEET Physics — Laws of Motion: Complete Formula Sheet

NEETEnglishFormula Sheet
F = m a

Symbols: F = net external force (N), m = mass of body (kg), a = acceleration (m/s²)

Unit: Force in Newton (N) = kg·m/s²; mass in kg; acceleration in m/s²

When to use: Primary equation of Newton's second law. Used whenever net force, mass, or acceleration is known/required. Vector form: F_net = m·a.

Conditions: Inertial frame of reference. Valid when mass is constant. For variable-mass systems use F_ext = d(mv)/dt instead.

Common mistake: Using F = m a with only one force instead of NET force. Always sum all forces vectorially first.

F_net = d p / d t where p = m v

Symbols: p = linear momentum (kg·m/s); F_net = net external force

Unit: Momentum in kg·m/s; force in N

When to use: General form of Newton's second law, applicable to variable-mass systems (rockets, etc.).

Conditions: Must be used when mass changes with time. In inertial frames only.

Common mistake: Writing F = m(dv/dt) + v(dm/dt) but forgetting that dm/dt is negative for rockets losing mass, leading to wrong sign in thrust calculation.

J = F_avg · Δt = Δp = m(v − u)

Symbols: J = impulse (N·s or kg·m/s); F_avg = average force during impact; Δt = contact time; u = initial velocity; v = final velocity

Unit: Impulse in N·s (= kg·m/s)

When to use: Collision problems, impact forces, finding average force when contact time is given. Also used with F-t graphs: area under curve = impulse.

Conditions: Valid for any force (constant or variable). For variable force, J = ∫F dt.

Common mistake: Confusing impulse with energy. Impulse is a vector; also many students forget the sign — direction of impulse equals direction of change in momentum.

m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂

Symbols: m₁, m₂ = masses; u₁, u₂ = initial velocities before collision; v₁, v₂ = final velocities after collision

Unit: Velocities in m/s; masses in kg

When to use: Conservation of linear momentum for isolated two-body collisions (elastic, inelastic, or perfectly inelastic).

Conditions: No external net force acts on the system during collision. Applied along any chosen axis independently.

Common mistake: Forgetting that momentum is a vector — signs of velocities along the chosen positive direction must be strictly maintained, especially for rebound/reverse-direction cases.

e = (v₂ − v₁) / (u₁ − u₂)

Symbols: e = coefficient of restitution (0 ≤ e ≤ 1); u₁, u₂ = initial velocities; v₁, v₂ = final velocities along line of impact

Unit: Dimensionless

When to use: Classifying collision type and finding final velocities. e = 1 → elastic; e = 0 → perfectly inelastic; 0 < e < 1 → inelastic.

Conditions: Applied along the line of impact only. Both numerator and denominator are relative speeds of separation and approach respectively.

Common mistake: Swapping numerator and denominator, which gives 1/e instead of e. The denominator must be speed of APPROACH, numerator speed of SEPARATION.

f_s ≤ μ_s N (limiting: f_s(max) = μ_s N)

Symbols: f_s = static friction force; μ_s = coefficient of static friction; N = normal reaction

Unit: Both f_s and μ_s·N have units of force (N); μ_s is dimensionless

When to use: Determining whether an object is at rest or just about to slip on a surface. Static friction adjusts itself up to its maximum limit.

Conditions: f_s = μ_s N holds ONLY at the threshold of impending motion. For bodies truly at rest with no tendency to slide, f_s may be less than μ_s N and equals the applied force parallel to surface.

Common mistake: Always writing f = μN even when the body is not on the verge of sliding. For equilibrium on an incline, f_s = mg sinθ (which may be < μ_s mg cosθ).

f_k = μ_k N

Symbols: f_k = kinetic friction force; μ_k = coefficient of kinetic friction; N = normal reaction

Unit: Force in Newton; μ_k dimensionless

When to use: Calculating retarding force when a body is already sliding on a surface.

Conditions: Valid only when there is relative motion between surfaces. Usually μ_k < μ_s.

Common mistake: Using f_k = μ_k N on a body that is actually at rest. Check for motion first using static friction.

a = g (sinθ − μ_k cosθ) (down the plane) or a = g (sinθ + μ_k cosθ) (up the plane with retardation)

Symbols: θ = angle of incline; μ_k = coefficient of kinetic friction; g = acceleration due to gravity

Unit: Acceleration in m/s²

When to use: Block sliding on an inclined plane with friction — finding acceleration down or deceleration up the plane.

Conditions: Normal force on incline: N = mg cosθ. Friction opposes motion. Derivation assumes no other applied forces.

Common mistake: Sign error: friction always opposes the direction of motion, so if block moves up, friction acts down the plane (adds to gravitational component).

T = m(g − a) (descending with acceleration a) T = m(g + a) (ascending with acceleration a)

Symbols: T = tension in string; m = mass of body; g = gravitational acceleration; a = acceleration of lift

Unit: Tension in Newton

When to use: Finding apparent weight (tension) of a person/object in an accelerating elevator. Also general vertical-string problems.

Conditions: a is positive when directed upward. If a > g (downward), tension can become zero (free fall).

Common mistake: Forgetting the direction sign convention: if elevator accelerates downward, apparent weight DECREASES; if upward, it INCREASES.

a = (m₁ − m₂)g / (m₁ + m₂) T = 2 m₁ m₂ g / (m₁ + m₂) (Atwood machine, m₁ > m₂)

Symbols: m₁, m₂ = two masses; a = common acceleration magnitude; T = tension in light inextensible string over smooth pulley

Unit: a in m/s²; T in N

When to use: Standard Atwood machine — two masses hanging vertically connected by string over a frictionless pulley.

Conditions: String is massless and inextensible; pulley is smooth (frictionless) and massless. Tension is same on both sides.

Common mistake: Assuming T = mg for each mass individually. In an accelerating system T ≠ mg; always write separate F=ma equations for each mass.

a = (m₁ sinα − m₂ sinβ) g / (m₁ + m₂) T = m₁ m₂ g (sinα + sinβ) / (m₁ + m₂) (Double inclined plane, strings parallel to planes)

Symbols: m₁, m₂ = masses on two inclines; α, β = angles of the two inclines with horizontal

Unit: a in m/s²; T in N

When to use: Two blocks on two different inclined planes connected by a string over a pulley at the top.

Conditions: Smooth (frictionless) inclined planes unless μ is specified. String parallel to each respective incline.

Common mistake: Using mg directly instead of resolving components: the driving force along each plane is mg sinθ, not mg.

μ = tanθ (limiting equilibrium on rough incline)

Symbols: μ = coefficient of static friction; θ = angle of repose (angle of incline at which block just begins to slide)

Unit: Dimensionless

When to use: Finding coefficient of friction from the critical angle where sliding is impending, or finding the minimum angle to initiate sliding.

Conditions: Block just on the verge of sliding; N = mg cosθ and f_s(max) = μN = mg sinθ.

Common mistake: Applying μ = tanθ when the block is still at rest but NOT on the verge of sliding. The equality holds only at limiting friction.

v_max = √(μ g R) (no-banking road) v_max = √[R g (tanθ + μ) / (1 − μ tanθ)] (banked road with friction)

Symbols: v_max = maximum safe speed; μ = coefficient of friction; g = gravitational acceleration; R = radius of curved path; θ = banking angle

Unit: Speed in m/s; R in m; g in m/s²

When to use: Vehicle turning on a horizontal curve (first formula) or on a banked road (second formula). Determines speed beyond which skidding occurs.

Conditions: First formula: flat horizontal road. Second formula: road banked at angle θ with friction present. No vertical acceleration assumed.

Common mistake: Using v = √(μgR) for a banked road — that formula is only for a flat road. On a banked road, friction contributes additional centripetal support.

v₀ = √(R g tanθ) (ideal banking speed, no friction needed)

Symbols: v₀ = design speed for which no friction is required; R = radius; g = gravitational acceleration; θ = banking angle

Unit: Speed in m/s

When to use: Finding the speed at which a vehicle can safely navigate a banked curve without relying on friction at all.

Conditions: Smooth banked road (μ = 0) or vehicle traveling exactly at design speed. Derivation uses N sinθ providing centripetal force and N cosθ balancing weight.

Common mistake: Assuming this is the maximum speed. This is the SPEED WHERE friction is NOT needed; actual max speed (with friction) is higher.

F_pseudo = −m a_frame |F_pseudo| = m a_frame

Symbols: F_pseudo = pseudo (fictitious) force; m = mass of body; a_frame = acceleration of the non-inertial frame (relative to an inertial frame)

Unit: Force in Newton

When to use: Analyzing motion from inside an accelerating reference frame (e.g., block on accelerating wedge, pendulum in accelerating car).

Conditions: Only applicable in non-inertial (accelerating) frames. Always acts opposite to the frame's acceleration direction.

Common mistake: Treating pseudo force as a real interaction force. It arises purely from the acceleration of the frame of reference and has no reaction pair (violates Newton's third law).

F₁/m₁ = F₂/m₂ = ... = F_n/m_n (forced motion of connected bodies on smooth surface)

Symbols: F_i = individual external force on mass m_i; for common acceleration a: a = F_total / M_total where M_total = Σm_i

Unit: Acceleration in m/s²

When to use: Multiple bodies in contact pushed/pulled by external forces on a smooth surface — finding common acceleration and contact forces between bodies.

Conditions: All bodies move together (no slipping between them). Surface is smooth unless friction is specified.

Common mistake: Using the applied force directly on one body without accounting for contact forces. Always find common acceleration first, then isolate each body for internal forces.

T₁ = M a + f (tension in string pulling a body with friction)

Symbols: T = tension; M = mass being pulled; a = acceleration; f = frictional force opposing motion

Unit: Tension in Newton

When to use: Single body pulled by a string on a rough horizontal surface, or any similar single-body dynamics problem.

Conditions: String is light and inextensible. Friction f = μN if sliding; f adjusts if at rest.

Common mistake: Writing T = Ma without subtracting friction when the surface is rough. Net force = T − f = Ma, so T = Ma + f.

ΣF_x = 0, ΣF_y = 0 (equilibrium of a particle)

Symbols: ΣF_x = algebraic sum of all forces in x-direction; ΣF_y = algebraic sum of all forces in y-direction

Unit: Each component sum equals zero (Newtons)

When to use: Static equilibrium problems: finding unknown forces, tensions, or reactions when a particle/body is at rest.

Conditions: Applicable to a particle (all forces concurrent). For extended rigid bodies, also require Στ = 0 (rotational equilibrium).

Common mistake: Forgetting to resolve ALL forces into components before setting sums to zero. Common error: including a force component in the wrong direction.

W = m g / sinθ − μ m g cosθ / sinθ (minimum force to pull a body UP a rough incline, applied parallel to plane)

Symbols: W = applied force parallel to incline; m = mass; g = gravity; θ = incline angle; μ = coefficient of friction

Unit: Force in Newton

When to use: Finding minimum force required to just move a body up a rough inclined plane (applied parallel to the plane).

Conditions: Force applied parallel to the incline. Body is on the verge of moving up (limiting friction acts down the plane).

Common mistake: Adding friction in the wrong direction. When pulling UP, friction acts DOWN the plane, so it ADDS to the component of weight down the plane.

a_common = F / (M_total) T_contact (between bodies) = m_far · a_common

Symbols: F = total external force; M_total = sum of all masses in contact; m_far = mass farther from the applied force; T_contact = contact force between adjacent bodies

Unit: a in m/s²; T in N

When to use: Contact force problems: several bodies in contact on a smooth surface, one end pushed — find acceleration and inter-body contact forces.

Conditions: Bodies remain in contact (no separation). Surface smooth unless friction specified.

Common mistake: Assigning the full applied force to every body individually. Each body feels only the contact force from its neighbor, not the full applied force.

T = μ m g / (cosθ + μ sinθ) (minimum horizontal force to move a body on rough surface, pulled at angle θ above horizontal)

Symbols: T = applied force magnitude; μ = coefficient of friction; m = mass; θ = angle of applied force above horizontal

Unit: Force in Newton

When to use: Finding minimum pulling force at an angle to overcome friction on a horizontal rough surface.

Conditions: Body on horizontal rough surface. Force applied at angle θ to horizontal. Minimum force corresponds to impending motion.

Common mistake: Forgetting that pulling at an angle reduces the normal reaction (N = mg − T sinθ), which reduces friction — this is why angled pull can require less force than horizontal pull.
