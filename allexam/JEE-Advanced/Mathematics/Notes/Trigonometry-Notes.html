1. Basic Trigonometric Functions and Their Definitions

Trigonometry is built upon six fundamental functions defined using a right triangle or the unit circle.

Right Triangle Definition:
For an acute angle θ in a right triangle with perpendicular (P), base (B), and hypotenuse (H):
- sin θ = P/H
- cos θ = B/H
- tan θ = P/B
- cosec θ = H/P (reciprocal of sin)
- sec θ = H/B (reciprocal of cos)
- cot θ = B/P (reciprocal of tan)

Unit Circle Definition:
For any angle θ, if point P(x, y) on the unit circle corresponds to θ measured from the positive x-axis:
- sin θ = y
- cos θ = x
- tan θ = y/x (x ≠ 0)
- cosec θ = 1/y (y ≠ 0)
- sec θ = 1/x (x ≠ 0)
- cot θ = x/y (y ≠ 0)

Quadrant Signs (CAST Rule):
- Quadrant I (0 to π/2): All positive
- Quadrant II (π/2 to π): sin positive only
- Quadrant III (π to 3π/2): tan positive only
- Quadrant IV (3π/2 to 2π): cos positive only

Domain and Range:
- sin θ, cos θ: Domain = ℝ, Range = [-1, 1]
- tan θ, cot θ: Domain = ℝ except where cos θ = 0 (for tan) or sin θ = 0 (for cot); Range = ℝ
- sec θ, cosec θ: Domain = ℝ except where cos θ = 0 (for sec) or sin θ = 0 (for cosec); Range = (-∞, -1] ∪ [1, ∞)

JEE Advanced frequently tests the range of composite trigonometric expressions and equations involving domain restrictions. Always verify that solutions satisfy the original equation's domain constraints.

2. Fundamental Identities and Sign Conventions

The core identities form the foundation for all trigonometric manipulations.

Pythagorean Identities:
1. sin²θ + cos²θ = 1
2. 1 + tan²θ = sec²θ
3. 1 + cot²θ = cosec²θ

These follow directly from the Pythagorean theorem applied to the unit circle. For JEE Advanced, you must be able to derive one from another quickly.

Reciprocal Identities:
- sin θ · cosec θ = 1
- cos θ · sec θ = 1
- tan θ · cot θ = 1

Quotient Identities:
- tan θ = sin θ/cos θ
- cot θ = cos θ/sin θ

Sign Conventions for General Angles:
When solving sin θ = a (|a| ≤ 1), there are infinitely many solutions. The general solution structure depends on the quadrant in which the reference angle lies. For example:
- If sin θ = ½, then θ = nπ + (-1)ⁿ(π/6), n ∈ ℤ
- If cos θ = ½, then θ = 2nπ ± π/3, n ∈ ℤ

Important Derived Results:
- sin²θ = (1 - cos 2θ)/2
- cos²θ = (1 + cos 2θ)/2
- sin⁴θ + cos⁴θ = 1 - ½sin²2θ
- sin⁶θ + cos⁶θ = 1 - ¾sin²2θ
- tan²θ + cot²θ = sec²θ · cosec²θ - 2

JEE Advanced questions often require recognizing these higher-power reductions under time pressure.

3. Multiple Angle and Compound Angle Formulas

Compound angle formulas are essential for deriving all other results.

Compound Angle Formulas:
- sin(A ± B) = sin A cos B ± cos A sin B
- cos(A ± B) = cos A cos B ∓ sin A sin B
- tan(A ± B) = (tan A ± tan B)/(1 ∓ tan A tan B)
- sin(A + B)sin(A - B) = sin²A - sin²B = cos²B - cos²A
- cos(A + B)cos(A - B) = cos²A - sin²B = ½(cos 2A + cos 2B)

Multiple Angle Formulas:
- sin 2θ = 2 sin θ cos θ = 2tan θ/(1 + tan²θ)
- cos 2θ = cos²θ - sin²θ = 2cos²θ - 1 = 1 - 2sin²θ = (1 - tan²θ)/(1 + tan²θ)
- tan 2θ = 2tan θ/(1 - tan²θ)
- sin 3θ = 3sin θ - 4sin³θ
- cos 3θ = 4cos³θ - 3cos θ
- tan 3θ = (3tan θ - tan³θ)/(1 - 3tan²θ)

Double and Triple Angle Forms in Product-to-Sum:
- 2 sin A cos B = sin(A+B) + sin(A-B)
- 2 cos A cos B = cos(A+B) + cos(A-B)
- 2 sin A sin B = cos(A-B) - cos(A+B)
- sin A + sin B = 2 sin((A+B)/2)cos((A-B)/2)
- cos A + cos B = 2 cos((A+B)/2)cos((A-B)/2)
- sin A - sin B = 2 cos((A+B)/2)sin((A-B)/2)
- cos A - cos B = -2 sin((A+B)/2)sin((A-B)/2)

Sub-multiple Angle (Half-Angle) Formulas:
- sin θ/2 = ±√[(1 - cos θ)/2]
- cos θ/2 = ±√[(1 + cos θ)/2]
- tan θ/2 = sin θ/(1 + cos θ) = (1 - cos θ)/sin θ = √[(1-cos θ)/(1+cos θ)]

JEE Advanced commonly combines compound angle identities with equations — expect questions requiring simultaneous application of sin(A±B) and cos(A±B) expansions.

4. General Solutions of Trigonometric Equations

A general solution captures all possible values of the unknown angle, expressed using an integer parameter n.

Standard General Solutions:
- sin θ = sin α ⟹ θ = nπ + (-1)ⁿα, where α = arcsin(sin α) and α ∈ [-π/2, π/2]
- cos θ = cos α ⟹ θ = 2nπ ± α, where α = arccos(cos α) and α ∈ [0, π]
- tan θ = tan α ⟹ θ = nπ + α, where α = arctan(tan α) and α ∈ (-π/2, π/2)

Key Equations Frequently Appearing:
- sin θ = 0 ⟹ θ = nπ
- cos θ = 0 ⟹ θ = (2n+1)π/2
- tan θ = 0 ⟹ θ = nπ
- sin²θ = sin²α ⟹ θ = nπ ± α
- cos²θ = cos²α ⟹ θ = nπ ± α
- tan²θ = tan²α ⟹ θ = nπ ± α

Equation Types and Techniques:
1. Linear combinations: a sin θ + b cos θ = c
- Rewrite as R sin(θ + α) = c where R = √(a² + b²), tan α = b/a
- Condition for solvability: |c| ≤ R
2. Homogeneous equations of degree 2: a sin²θ + b sin θ cos θ + c cos²θ = d
- Divide by cos²θ (or sin²θ) to convert to a quadratic in tan θ
3. Equations reducible to quadratic in one function: e.g., 2sin²θ - 3sin θ + 1 = 0
- Solve the quadratic, then find θ for each valid root
4. Product form: sin θ · cos θ · tan θ = k
- Convert to a single function and solve
5. Equations with different angles: sin pθ = cos qθ
- Convert both sides to same function: sin pθ = sin(π/2 - qθ)
- Apply general solution formula

Important: Always check for extraneous solutions introduced by squaring or dividing by expressions that could be zero. JEE Advanced explicitly tests this attention to domain restrictions.

5. Inverse Trigonometric Functions

Inverse trigonometric functions are defined by restricting the domains of the original functions to make them bijective.

Principal Value Branches:
- sin⁻¹x: Domain = [-1, 1], Range = [-π/2, π/2]
- cos⁻¹x: Domain = [-1, 1], Range = [0, π]
- tan⁻¹x: Domain = ℝ, Range = (-π/2, π/2)
- cot⁻¹x: Domain = ℝ, Range = (0, π)
- sec⁻¹x: Domain = (-∞, -1] ∪ [1, ∞), Range = [0, π] · {π/2}
- cosec⁻¹x: Domain = (-∞, -1] ∪ [1, ∞), Range = [-π/2, π/2] · {0}

Fundamental Identities (valid within principal branches):
- sin⁻¹(-x) = -sin⁻¹x
- cos⁻¹(-x) = π - cos⁻¹x
- tan⁻¹(-x) = -tan⁻¹x
- sin⁻¹x + cos⁻¹x = π/2 for all x ∈ [-1, 1]
- tan⁻¹x + cot⁻¹x = π/2 for all x ∈ ℝ
- sec⁻¹x + cosec⁻¹x = π/2 for |x| ≥ 1

Addition Formulas:
- tan⁻¹x + tan⁻¹y = tan⁻¹[(x+y)/(1-xy)], if xy < 1
- tan⁻¹x + tan⁻¹y = π + tan⁻¹[(x+y)/(1-xy)], if xy > 1 and x, y > 0
- tan⁻¹x + tan⁻¹y = -π + tan⁻¹[(x+y)/(1-xy)], if xy > 1 and x, y < 0
- tan⁻¹x - tan⁻¹y = tan⁻¹[(x-y)/(1+xy)], if xy > -1
- 2tan⁻¹x = sin⁻¹[2x/(1+x²)] = cos⁻¹[(1-x²)/(1+x²)] = tan⁻¹[2x/(1-x²)], within appropriate domains

Graphical Properties:
- sin⁻¹x is odd and increasing on [-1, 1]
- cos⁻¹x is decreasing on [-1, 1]
- tan⁻¹x is odd, increasing on ℝ, with horizontal asymptotes y = ±π/2

JEE Advanced problems combine inverse trig functions with inequalities, limits, and continuity conditions. Pay special attention to the principal value restriction when applying identities — many陷阱 questions arise from ignoring the range.

6. Heights and Distances (Application of Trigonometry)

Problems involving heights and distances use trigonometric ratios in right triangles formed by lines of sight.

Key Terms:
- Angle of elevation: Angle between the horizontal line and the line of sight when looking upward
- Angle of depression: Angle between the horizontal line and the line of sight when looking downward
- Both angles are measured from the horizontal and are equal to each other when viewing between two points at different elevations (alternate interior angles)

Standard Configurations:
1. Single pole/station: Given height h and angle of elevation θ, distance d = h/tan θ = h cot θ
2. Two poles of different heights: If distances from observation points are known, set up simultaneous equations using tan θ for each
3. Moving object problems: A person/object moves horizontally; angles of elevation change. Use relative motion: d = h(cot θ₁ - cot θ₂) for time-based problems
4. Pair of angles problem: Two angles of elevation/depression from points on the same side or opposite sides of an object
5. Reflection problems: Angle of elevation from a point equals angle of depression to its image in water — use similar triangles

Important Formulas for Specific Cases:
- If angles of elevation from two points at distances a and b from the foot of a tower are complementary: h = √(ab)
- If a tower subtends equal angles at two points distant a and b from its foot: h = √(ab)
- From top of tower of height h, angles of depression of two objects on same side are α and β, separated by distance d: d = h(cot β - cot α)

Common Pitfalls:
- Confusing angle of elevation with the angle at the top of the triangle
- Forgetting that angle of depression equals angle of elevation (alternate angles)
- Not converting units consistently (meters vs kilometers, degrees vs radians)
- Misidentifying which side is opposite/adjacent in non-right-triangle configurations

7. Properties of Triangles and Trigonometric Applications

Trigonometry applied to general triangles uses the sine rule, cosine rule, and area formulas.

Sine Rule:
a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C = 2R
where R is the circumradius of the triangle.
This relates each side to the sine of its opposite angle.

Cosine Rule:
a² = b² + c² - 2bc cos A
b² = a² + c² - 2ac cos B
c² = a² + b² - 2ab cos C
Used when three sides are known (to find an angle) or two sides and included angle are known (to find the third side).

Area Formulas:
- Area = ½ bc sin A = ½ ca sin B = ½ ab sin C
- Area = √[s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)] (Heron's formula), where s = (a+b+c)/2
- Area = abc/(4R)
- Area = r·s, where r is the inradius and s is the semi-perimeter
- Area = 2R² sin A sin B sin C

Projection Formula:
a = b cos C + c cos B
b = c cos A + a cos C
c = a cos B + b cos A

Napier's Analogy (Tangent Rule):
- (a-b)/(a+b) = tan[(A-B)/2]/tan[(A+B)/2]
- (b-c)/(b+c) = tan[(B-C)/2]/tan[(B+C)/2]
- (c-a)/(c+a) = tan[(C-A)/2]/tan[(C+A)/2]

Key Triangle Identities:
- A + B + C = π (for any triangle)
- sin 2A + sin 2B + sin 2C = 4 sin A sin B sin C
- cos 2A + cos 2B + cos 2C = -1 - 4 cos A cos B cos C
- tan A + tan B + tan C = tan A tan B tan C
- sin A + sin B + sin C ≤ (3√3)/2 (maximum for equilateral triangle)

Special Triangles:
- Right triangle: one angle = 90°, Pythagorean theorem applies
- Equilateral triangle: all angles = 60°, a = b = c, R = a/√3, r = a/(2√3)
- Isosceles triangle: two sides equal, base angles equal

JEE Advanced frequently combines triangle properties with trigonometric equations — for instance, finding angles when given a relation between sides and trig functions.

8. Advanced Topics and JEE Advanced Specific Techniques

These topics represent the most challenging aspects of trigonometry for JEE Advanced.

Maxima and Minima of Trigonometric Expressions:
- a cos θ + b sin θ: Maximum = √(a²+b²), Minimum = -√(a²+b²)
- a cos θ + b sin θ + c: Maximum = c + √(a²+b²), Minimum = c - √(a²+b²)
- a sin θ + b cos θ where a, b > 0 and θ ∈ [0, π/2]: maximum value is √(a²+b²)
- For sinⁿθ + cosⁿθ: If n is even, minimum occurs at θ = π/4; maximum at θ = 0 or π/2
- For |a cos θ + b sin θ + c|, find critical points by differentiation or geometric interpretation

Trigonometric Series and Summation:
- Sum of sines with angles in AP: sin θ + sin(θ+α) + sin(θ+2α) + ... + sin(θ+(n-1)α) = sin(nα/2)/sin(α/2) · sin(θ + (n-1)α/2)
- Sum of cosines with angles in AP: cos θ + cos(θ+α) + ... + cos(θ+(n-1)α) = sin(nα/2)/sin(α/2) · cos(θ + (n-1)α/2)
- Σ tan⁻¹[x/(1+x+x²)] = tan⁻¹(n+1) - tan⁻¹(x), telescoping series

Complex Numbers and Trigonometry (De Moivre's Theorem):
- (cos θ + i sin θ)ⁿ = cos nθ + i sin nθ
- This allows derivation of cos nθ and sin nθ as polynomials in cos θ and sin θ
- Useful for proving identities and solving equations like cos nθ = cos nα

Transformation Formulas (Prosthaphaeresis):
- sin P + sin Q = 2 sin((P+Q)/2) cos((P-Q)/2)
- sin P - sin Q = 2 cos((P+Q)/2) sin((P-Q)/2)
- cos P + cos Q = 2 cos((P+Q)/2) cos((P-Q)/2)
- cos P - cos Q = -2 sin((P+Q)/2) sin((P-Q)/2)
- These convert sums/differences into products, simplifying equations.

Common JEE Advanced Trap Patterns:
1. Forgetting extraneous solutions when squaring trigonometric equations
2. Missing solutions when dividing by a trigonometric expression that could be zero
3. Applying inverse trig identities outside their valid domain
4. Ignoring quadrant restrictions when finding general solutions
5. Confusing degrees and radians in calculations

Important facts

  • sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 is the fundamental Pythagorean identity; derive sec²θ = 1+tan²θ and cosec²θ = 1+cot²θ from it by division.
  • General solution: sin θ = sin α ⟹ θ = nπ + (-1)ⁿα — the (-1)ⁿ factor accounts for the alternating sign pattern of sine across quadrants.
  • sin⁻¹x + cos⁻¹x = π/2 holds for all x ∈ [-1, 1]; this is a direct consequence of complementary angle relationships.
  • a cos θ + b sin θ ranges between -√(a²+b²) and +√(a²+b²); the maximum occurs when tan θ = b/a.
  • In any triangle ABC: tan A + tan B + tan C = tan A tan B tan C, since A+B+C = π.
  • De Moivre's theorem: (cos θ + i sin θ)ⁿ = cos nθ + i sin nθ — this is the bridge between trigonometry and complex numbers.
  • The sum of cosines in AP with first term α and common difference d is: [sin(nd/2)/sin(d/2)]·cos(α + (n-1)d/2).
  • For a right triangle with complementary acute angles: if tan θ = a/b, then tan(90°-θ) = b/a.
  • Half-angle formula: tan(θ/2) = (1-cos θ)/sin θ = sin θ/(1+cos θ) — memorize both forms to avoid sign ambiguity.
  • In triangle geometry, the sine rule a/sin A = 2R connects side lengths to the circumradius, a key tool for JEE Advanced.
