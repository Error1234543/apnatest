Vectors — Complete Notes for JEE Advanced

JEE AdvancedEnglishNotes

1. Fundamentals: Scalars, Vectors, and Classification

A scalar is a physical or mathematical quantity completely specified by its magnitude alone — examples include mass, temperature, time, energy, and electric charge. A vector is a quantity that requires both magnitude and direction for complete specification — examples include displacement, velocity, acceleration, force, and momentum.

In JEE Advanced, distinction between scalar and vector quantities is frequently tested conceptually. Key distinctions:
- Temperature is a scalar; it has magnitude but no direction.
- Current is a scalar despite having a direction of flow — it obeys algebraic, not vector, addition.
- Pressure is a scalar — it acts equally in all directions at a point.
- Speed is scalar (magnitude of velocity).

Classification of vectors essential for JEE Advanced:
• Zero vector (null vector): Denoted by 0→ or 0. Magnitude is zero; direction is undefined. For any vector a⃗, a⃗ + 0→ = a⃗.
• Unit vector: A vector of magnitude 1. The unit vector in the direction of a⃗ is â = a⃗ / |a⃗|.
• Like and unlike vectors: Two non-zero vectors are like if they have the same direction; unlike if they have opposite directions.
• Co-initial vectors: Vectors having the same initial point.
• Collinear (parallel) vectors: Vectors parallel to the same line, regardless of magnitude or direction. a⃗ and b⃗ are collinear iff a⃗ = λb⃗ for some scalar λ.
• Equal vectors: Vectors having the same magnitude and same direction (independent of initial point).
• Negative of a vector: −a⃗ has same magnitude as a⃗ but opposite direction.
• Reciprocal vector: (a⃗)⁻¹ = â / |a⃗|².
• Coterminal vectors: Vectors with the same direction but different magnitudes.
• Free vs bound vectors: A free vector is defined purely by magnitude and direction and can be translated anywhere; a bound vector has a fixed initial point. JEE treats most vectors as free vectors.

2. Representation, Components, and Direction Cosines

A vector in 3D space is represented as a⃗ = xî + yĵ + zk̂, where x, y, z are the scalar components (projections on the x, y, z axes respectively) and î, ĵ, k̂ are the standard unit vectors along the positive x, y, and z axes.

Magnitude: |a⃗| = √(x² + y² + z²)

Position vector of point P(x, y, z) with respect to origin O: OP⃗ = xî + yĵ + zk̂.

Vector joining two points: If A has position vector a⃗ and B has position vector b⃗, then AB⃗ = b⃗ − a⃗.

Direction cosines (DCs): If a⃗ makes angles α, β, γ with the positive x, y, z axes respectively, then the direction cosines are l = cos α, m = cos β, n = cos γ. They satisfy l² + m² + n² = 1. The unit vector in the direction of a⃗ = xî + yĵ + zk̂ is (x/|a⃗|)î + (y/|a⃗|)ĵ + (z/|a⃗|)k̂.

Direction ratios (DRs): Any three numbers proportional to the direction cosines are called direction ratios. If l:m:n = a:b:c, then a, b, c are DRs. To convert DRs to DCs: l = a/√(a²+b²+c²), m = b/√(a²+b²+c²), n = c/√(a²+b²+c²).

Angle between two vectors a⃗ and b⃗: cos θ = (a⃗·b⃗)/(|a⃗||b⃗|). The angle is acute if dot product > 0, obtuse if < 0, and right angle if = 0.

JEE-relevant derived results:
• If a⃗·b⃗ = |a⃗||b⃗|, then θ = 0° (vectors are parallel and like).
• If a⃗·b⃗ = −|a⃗||b⃗|, then θ = 180° (vectors are parallel and opposite).
• If a⃗×b⃗ = 0→, then a⃗ and b⃗ are parallel.
• Projection of a⃗ on b⃗ = (a⃗·b̂) = (a⃗·b⃗)/|b⃗|. This is a scalar.
• Vector projection of a⃗ on b⃗ = [(a⃗·b⃗)/|b⃗|²] b⃗.

3. Vector Addition, Subtraction, and Scalar Multiplication

Triangle Law of Vector Addition: If two vectors a⃗ and b⃗ are represented in magnitude and direction by two sides of a triangle taken in order, then their resultant is given by the third side taken in opposite order: a⃗ + b⃗ = c⃗. In terms of components: (a₁î + a₂ĵ + a₃k̂) + (b₁î + b₂ĵ + b₃k̂) = (a₁+b₁)î + (a₂+b₂)ĵ + (a₃+b₃)k̂.

Parallelogram Law: If two vectors a⃗ and b⃗ are represented by adjacent sides of a parallelogram, then the diagonal from the common initial point gives a⃗ + b⃗, while the other diagonal gives a⃗ − b⃗.

Magnitude of resultant: |a⃗ + b⃗|² = |a⃗|² + |b⃗|² + 2|a⃗||b⃗|cos θ. Special cases:
- If θ = 90°: |a⃗ + b⃗| = √(|a⃗|² + |b⃗|²)
- If θ = 0°: |a⃗ + b⃗| = |a⃗| + |b⃗|
- If θ = 180°: |a⃗ + b⃗| = ||a⃗| − |b⃗||

Subtraction: a⃗ − b⃗ = a⃗ + (−b⃗). Geometrically, it connects the tip of b⃗ to the tip of a⃗ when both are drawn from the same origin.

Scalar multiplication: For scalar λ, λa⃗ has magnitude |λ||a⃗|. Direction is same as a⃗ if λ > 0, opposite if λ < 0.

Key algebraic properties (all hold for vectors):
• Commutative: a⃗ + b⃗ = b⃗ + a⃗
• Associative: (a⃗ + b⃗) + c⃗ = a⃗ + (b⃗ + c⃗)
• Scalar distributive: λ(a⃗ + b⃗) = λa⃗ + λb⃗; (λ + μ)a⃗ = λa⃗ + μa⃗
• Identity: a⃗ + 0→ = a⃗; 1·a⃗ = a⃗
• Negation: a⃗ + (−a⃗) = 0→

Important JEE result: For any polygon ABCDE... with consecutive vertices, AB⃗ + BC⃗ + CD⃗ + DE⃗ + EA⃗ = 0→ (closed polygon). Also, for a triangle ABC: AB⃗ + BC⃗ + CA⃗ = 0→.

Median property: If D is midpoint of BC in ΔABC, then AD⃗ = ½(AB⃗ + AC⃗). In terms of position vectors with origin O: d⃗ = (b⃗ + c⃗)/2.

4. Section Formula and Midpoint

Section Formula (Internal Division): Point P dividing the line segment joining A(a⃗) and B(b⃗) internally in the ratio m:n has position vector p⃗ = (nb⃗ + ma⃗)/(m + n). Note carefully: the ratio m:n means AP:PB = m:n, so the vector b⃗ gets multiplied by m and a⃗ by n.

Section Formula (External Division): Point P dividing AB externally in ratio m:n has position vector p⃗ = (mb⃗ − na⃗)/(m − n).

Midpoint Formula: Midpoint of AB is p⃗ = (a⃗ + b⃗)/2 (a special case of internal division with m = n).

Centroid of triangle: For triangle with vertices having position vectors a⃗, b⃗, c⃗, the centroid G has position vector g⃗ = (a⃗ + b⃗ + c⃗)/3.

Incenter of triangle: i⃗ = (a·a⃗ + b·b⃗ + c·c⃗)/(a + b + c), where a, b, c are the lengths of sides opposite to vertices A, B, C respectively.

Orthocenter and circumcenter also have vector expressions but are less commonly asked directly.

Collinearity test using section formula: Points A(a⃗), B(b⃗), C(c⃗) are collinear if c⃗ = (1−t)a⃗ + tb⃗ for some scalar t. Equivalently, there exist scalars l, m, n not all zero such that la⃗ + mb⃗ + nc⃗ = 0→ with l + m + n = 0.

Division of a side in ratio: If P divides AB in ratio m:n and Q divides AB in ratio p:q, then PQ⃗ = [(m/(m+n)) − (p/(p+q))](b⃗ − a⃗).

JEE Advanced trap: Be very careful with the order. If P divides AB in ratio m:n (AP:PB = m:n), then p⃗ = (nb⃗ + ma⃗)/(m+n). Students frequently reverse the coefficients m and n.

5. Dot Product (Scalar Product)

Definition: a⃗ · b⃗ = |a⃗||b⃗|cos θ, where θ is the angle between a⃗ and b⃗ (0 ≤ θ ≤ π). In component form: if a⃗ = a₁î + a₂ĵ + a₃k̂ and b⃗ = b₁î + b₂ĵ + b₃k̂, then a⃗ · b⃗ = a₁b₁ + a₂b₂ + a₃b₃.

Key properties:
• Commutative: a⃗ · b⃗ = b⃗ · a⃗
• Distributive: a⃗ · (b⃗ + c⃗) = a⃗ · b⃗ + a⃗ · c⃗
• a⃗ · a⃗ = |a⃗|²
• λ(a⃗ · b⃗) = (λa⃗) · b⃗ = a⃗ · (λb⃗)
• If a⃗ · b⃗ = 0 and neither is zero, then a⃗ ⊥ b⃗ (perpendicular)
• Standard unit vectors: î·î = ĵ·ĵ = k̂·k̂ = 1; î·ĵ = ĵ·k̂ = k̂·î = 0

Applications of dot product:
• Work done: W = F⃗ · d⃗
• Power: P = F⃗ · v⃗
• Torque is NOT a dot product — it uses cross product.
• Finding angle: cos θ = (a⃗·b⃗)/(|a⃗||b⃗|)
• Work-energy theorem applications in mechanics.

Law of Cosines via dot product: |a⃗ − b⃗|² = |a⃗|² + |b⃗|² − 2a⃗·b⃗. This leads to c² = a² + b² − 2ab cos C in triangle geometry.

Cauchy-Schwarz inequality: |a⃗·b⃗| ≤ |a⃗||b⃗|, with equality iff a⃗ and b⃗ are parallel.

Triangle inequality for dot product context: a⃗·b⃗ ≥ −|a⃗||b⃗| and a⃗·b⃗ ≤ |a⃗||b⃗|.

JEE Advanced key identity: For any three vectors, |a⃗ + b⃗ + c⃗|² = |a⃗|² + |b⃗|² + |c⃗|² + 2(a⃗·b⃗ + b⃗·c⃗ + c⃗·a⃗).

If a⃗, b⃗, c⃗ are mutually perpendicular unit vectors, then a⃗·b⃗ = b⃗·c⃗ = c⃗·a⃗ = 0.

6. Cross Product (Vector Product)

Definition: a⃗ × b⃗ = |a⃗||b⃗|sin θ n̂, where θ is the angle from a⃗ to b⃗ (0 ≤ θ ≤ π) and n̂ is a unit vector perpendicular to both a⃗ and b⃗ following the right-hand rule. The magnitude |a⃗ × b⃗| = |a⃗||b⃗|sin θ equals the area of the parallelogram formed by a⃗ and b⃗.

Component form: If a⃗ = a₁î + a₂ĵ + a₃k̂ and b⃗ = b₁î + b₂ĵ + b₃k̂, then:
a⃗ × b⃗ = (a₂b₃ − a₃b₂)î − (a₁b₃ − a₃b₁)ĵ + (a₁b₂ − a₂b₁)k̂
This can be written as the determinant:
a⃗ × b⃗ = |î ĵ k̂|
|a₁ a₂ a₃|
|b₁ b₂ b₃|

Key properties:
• Anti-commutative: a⃗ × b⃗ = −(b⃗ × a⃗). This is crucial — order matters!
• Distributive: a⃗ × (b⃗ + c⃗) = a⃗ × b⃗ + a⃗ × c⃗
• Note: Cross product is NOT associative. (a⃗ × b⃗) × c⃗ ≠ a⃗ × (b⃗ × c⃗) generally.
• a⃗ × a⃗ = 0→; a⃗ × 0→ = 0→
• If a⃗ × b⃗ = 0→ and neither is zero, then a⃗ || b⃗ (parallel)
• Standard: î×ĵ = k̂, ĵ×k̂ = î, k̂×î = ĵ; and the reverse give negative values.

Area applications:
• Area of parallelogram with adjacent sides a⃗ and b⃗: |a⃗ × b⃗|
• Area of triangle with adjacent sides a⃗ and b⃗: ½|a⃗ × b⃗|
• Area of triangle with vertices A, B, C: ½|AB⃗ × AC⃗|
• Area of triangle given two sides and included angle: ½ab sin C

Vector area: The vector area of a triangle is ½(a⃗ × b⃗), a vector whose magnitude is the area and direction is perpendicular to the plane of the triangle.

JEE Advanced application — angular momentum: L⃗ = r⃗ × p⃗. Torque: τ⃗ = r⃗ × F⃗. Both use cross product inherently.

7. Scalar Triple Product and Vector Triple Product

Scalar Triple Product (STP): [a⃗ b⃗ c⃗] = a⃗ · (b⃗ × c⃗) = b⃗ · (c⃗ × a⃗) = c⃗ · (a⃗ × b⃗) = determinant |a₁ a₂ a₃; b₁ b₂ b₃; c₁ c₂ c₃|

Geometric meaning: |[a⃗ b⃗ c⃗]| = volume of the parallelepiped whose coterminous edges are a⃗, b⃗, c⃗. Volume of tetrahedron with these edges = (1/6)|[a⃗ b⃗ c⃗]|.

Cyclic property: [a⃗ b⃗ c⃗] = [b⃗ c⃗ a⃗] = [c⃗ a⃗ b⃗]. Swapping any two vectors changes the sign: [a⃗ c⃗ b⃗] = −[a⃗ b⃗ c⃗].

Key results from STP:
• [a⃗ b⃗ c⃗] = 0 iff a⃗, b⃗, c⃗ are coplanar (linearly dependent).
• If a⃗, b⃗, c⃗ are mutually perpendicular, [a⃗ b⃗ c⃗] = ±|a⃗||b⃗||c⃗|.
• [λa⃗ μb⃗ νc⃗] = λμν[a⃗ b⃗ c⃗]
• If a⃗ = l₁î + m₁ĵ + n₁k̂ etc., the STP equals the determinant of the coefficient matrix.
• Coplanarity of four points A(a⃗), B(b⃗), C(c⃗), D(d⃗): (b⃗−a⃗), (c⃗−a⃗), (d⃗−a⃗) are coplanar, i.e., [(b⃗−a⃗)(c⃗−a⃗)(d⃗−a⃗)] = 0.

Vector Triple Product (VTP): a⃗ × (b⃗ × c⃗) = (a⃗·c⃗)b⃗ − (a⃗·b⃗)c⃗. Mnemonic: 'bac minus cab' — the middle vector b⃗ gets the positive sign and is dotted with the outer vector of the first product.

Similarly: (a⃗ × b⃗) × c⃗ = (a⃗·c⃗)b⃗ − (b⃗·c⃗)a⃗.

Note the difference from scalar triple product — VTP yields a VECTOR.

JEE Advanced identities involving VTP:
• a⃗ × (b⃗ × c⃗) + b⃗ × (c⃗ × a⃗) + c⃗ × (a⃗ × b⃗) = 0→
• (a⃗ × b⃗) × (c⃗ × d⃗) = [a⃗ b⃗ d⃗]c⃗ − [a⃗ b⃗ c⃗]d⃗ = [b⃗ c⃗ d⃗]a⃗ − [a⃗ c⃗ d⃗]b⃗
• If a⃗, b⃗, c⃗ are non-coplanar, then (a⃗ × b⃗) × (b⃗ × c⃗) × (c⃗ × a⃗) = [a⃗ b⃗ c⃗]²(a⃗ × b⃗ × c⃗ is meaningless — use component evaluation)
• Four vectors a⃗, b⃗, c⃗, d⃗ are always linearly dependent in 3D, so any four vectors satisfy a non-trivial linear relation.

8. Lines and Planes in Vector Form

Equation of a Line:
• Vector form (point-direction): r⃗ = a⃗ + λb⃗, where a⃗ is the position vector of a known point on the line, b⃗ is a vector parallel to the line, and λ is a scalar parameter.
• Cartesian form: (x − x₁)/a = (y − y₁)/b = (z − z₁)/c, where (x₁, y₁, z₁) is a point on the line and (a, b, c) are direction ratios.
• Two lines r⃗ = a⃗₁ + λb⃗₁ and r⃗ = a⃗₂ + μb⃗₂ intersect if and only if there exist λ, μ such that a⃗₁ + λb⃗₁ = a⃗₂ + μb⃗₂. They are coplanar if [a⃗₂−a⃗₁, b⃗₁, b⃗₂] = 0.
• Angle between two lines: cos θ = |b⃗₁·b⃗₂|/(|b⃗₁||b⃗₂|). Note the absolute value — we take the acute angle.
• Perpendicular lines: b⃗₁·b⃗₂ = 0.
• Parallel lines: b⃗₁ × b⃗₂ = 0→.
• Shortest distance between two skew lines: d = |[a⃗₂−a⃗₁, b⃗₁, b⃗₂]|/|b⃗₁ × b⃗₂|. The line of shortest distance is parallel to b⃗₁ × b⃗₂.

Equation of a Plane:
• Vector form (normal form): (r⃗ − a⃗)·n̂ = 0, where a⃗ is position vector of a point on the plane and n̂ is the unit normal vector. Or: r⃗·n̂ = d, where d is the perpendicular distance from origin.
• Cartesian form: ax + by + cz = d, where (a, b, c) are direction ratios of the normal.
• General form: r⃗·(l₁î + m₁ĵ + n₁k̂) = d₁ and r⃗·(l₂î + m₂ĵ + n₂k̂) = d₂.
• Plane through three points: r⃗ · [a⃗ × b⃗] = [a⃗ b⃗ c⃗] is wrong notation — correct: (r⃗ − a⃗)·[(b⃗−a⃗)×(c⃗−a⃗)] = 0.
• Plane containing a line r⃗ = a⃗ + λb⃗ and passing through point with position c⃗: [r⃗ − a⃗, b⃗, c⃗ − a⃗] = 0.
• Distance of point P(p⃗) from plane r⃗·n̂ = d: |p⃗·n̂ − d|.
• Distance of point from plane ax + by + cz + d = 0: |ax₁ + by₁ + cz₁ + d|/√(a² + b² + c²).
• Angle between two planes: Same as angle between their normals. cos θ = |n̂₁·n̂₂|.
• Plane parallel to plane ax+by+cz=d has equation ax+by+cz=k.
• Foot of perpendicular from point to plane is found by writing the line through the point perpendicular to the plane and finding its intersection.

9. Applications: Collinearity, Coplanarity, and Area/Volume

Collinearity of points: Three points A(a⃗), B(b⃗), C(c⃗) are collinear iff AB⃗ × AC⃗ = 0→, or equivalently, (b⃗−a⃗) and (c⃗−a⃗) are parallel, i.e., (b⃗−a⃗) = λ(c⃗−a⃗).

Coplanarity of points: Four points A(a⃗), B(b⃗), C(c⃗), D(d⃗) are coplanar iff [(b⃗−a⃗)(c⃗−a⃗)(d⃗−a⃗)] = 0.

Coplanarity of three vectors: Vectors a⃗, b⃗, c⃗ are coplanar iff [a⃗ b⃗ c⃗] = 0. Equivalently, one can be written as a linear combination of the other two: c⃗ = la⃗ + mb⃗.

Area calculations:
• Triangle with sides a⃗ and b⃗: Area = ½|a⃗ × b⃗|
• Triangle with vertices at position vectors a⃗, b⃗, c⃗: Area = ½|(b⃗−a⃗) × (c⃗−a⃗)|
• In 2D (xy-plane): Area = ½|x₁(y₂−y₃) + x₂(y₃−y₁) + x₃(y₁−y₂)|

Volume calculations:
• Parallelepiped with edges a⃗, b⃗, c⃗: Volume = |[a⃗ b⃗ c⃗]|
• Tetrahedron with vertices a⃗, b⃗, c⃗, d⃗: Volume = (1/6)|(b⃗−a⃗)·[(c⃗−a⃗)×(d⃗−a⃗)]|
• Parallelepiped with diagonals: More complex — use edge vectors.

Angle problems:
• Angle between diagonals of a parallelogram with sides a⃗ and b⃗: The diagonals are (a⃗+b⃗) and (a⃗−b⃗). cos θ = |a⃗·a⃗ − b⃗·b⃗|/(|a⃗+b⃗||a⃗−b⃗|) = ||a⃗|²−|b⃗|²|/(|a⃗+b⃗||a⃗−b⃗|).
• Acute angle between diagonals of a rectangle: 90° only if square.
• Angle between a line and a plane: sin φ = |b⃗·n̂|/(|b⃗||n̂|), where b⃗ is the direction of the line and n̂ is the normal to the plane.

JEE Advanced classic result: If |a⃗|=3, |b⃗|=5, |c⃗|=7 and a⃗+b⃗+c⃗=0→, find angle between a⃗ and b⃗. From a⃗+b⃗=−c⃗, squaring: |a⃗|²+|b⃗|²+2a⃗·b⃗=|c⃗|² → 9+25+2(15)cos θ = 49 → cos θ = −1/2 → θ = 120°.

Another classic: If a⃗, b⃗, c⃗ are unit vectors with a⃗+b⃗+c⃗=0→, then angle between any pair is 120°. Similarly, |a⃗×b⃗| = |b⃗×c⃗| = |c⃗×a⃗| = √3/2.

10. Unit Vectors, Resolution, and Selected JEE Results

Resolution of a vector: Any vector a⃗ can be resolved into components along two non-parallel directions. In 3D, a⃗ = a₁î + a₂ĵ + a₃k̂ resolves it along the coordinate axes. More generally, if ñ₁ and ñ₂ are two non-parallel unit vectors, then a⃗ = (a⃗·ñ₁)ñ₁ + (a⃗·ñ₂)ñ₂ only if ñ₁⊥ñ₂; otherwise use the general resolution formula.

Unit vector perpendicular to both a⃗ and b⃗: ±(a⃗×b⃗)/|a⃗×b⃗|.

Unit vector in the direction of the bisector of angle between a⃗ and b⃗: (â + b̂)/|â + b̂| = (b⃗|a⃗| + a⃗|b⃗|)/|b⃗|a⃗| + a⃗|b⃗||.

If a⃗ and b⃗ are equal in magnitude, the bisector unit vector simplifies to (a⃗+b⃗)/|a⃗+b⃗|.

Important JEE Advanced results to memorize:
• (a⃗+b⃗+c⃗)×(a⃗×b⃗ + b⃗×c⃗ + c⃗×a⃗) = 0→ for any three vectors (vector identity).
• For any four coplanar vectors a⃗, b⃗, c⃗, d⃗ taken in order around a quadrilateral: area relations apply.
• If |a⃗|=1, |b⃗|=2, |c⃗|=3 and a⃗+b⃗+c⃗=0→, then a⃗·b⃗ = −5/2, b⃗·c⃗ = 3/2, c⃗·a⃗ = −2.
• |a⃗+b⃗|² + |a⃗−b⃗|² = 2(|a⃗|² + |b⃗|²) — Parallelogram law (always true).
• |a⃗×b⃗|² + (a⃗·b⃗)² = |a⃗|²|b⃗|² — Lagrange's identity.
• If a⃗·b⃗ = a⃗·c⃗ and a⃗×b⃗ = a⃗×c⃗, then b⃗ = c⃗ (necessary and sufficient).
• (a⃗×b⃗)·(c⃗×d⃗) = (a⃗·c⃗)(b⃗·d⃗) − (a⃗·d⃗)(b⃗·c⃗) — Lagrange's identity for dot of crosses.
• Four points are coplanar if the determinant of their position vectors (with a row of 1s) vanishes — actually use the STP of differences.
• If a⃗, b⃗, c⃗ are non-coplanar, then λ₁a⃗ + λ₂b⃗ + λ₃c⃗ = 0→ implies λ₁ = λ₂ = λ₃ = 0.

Important facts

  • a⃗ · b⃗ = |a⃗||b⃗|cos θ (dot product definition); a⃗ × b⃗ = |a⃗||b⃗|sin θ n̂ (cross product definition). Both are fundamental and used in nearly every JEE Advanced vector problem.
  • [a⃗ b⃗ c⃗] = a⃗ · (b⃗ × c⃗) is the scalar triple product. It equals zero iff the three vectors are coplanar. Its absolute value gives the volume of the parallelepiped formed by the three vectors.
  • a⃗ × (b⃗ × c⃗) = (a⃗·c⃗)b⃗ − (a⃗·b⃗)c⃗ (vector triple product, mnemonic: bac minus cab). Order matters critically — this is not associative.
  • Area of triangle with adjacent sides a⃗ and b⃗ = ½|a⃗ × b⃗|. Volume of tetrahedron with coterminous edges a⃗, b⃗, c⃗ = (1/6)|[a⃗ b⃗ c⃗]|.
  • Shortest distance between skew lines r⃗ = a⃗₁ + λb⃗₁ and r⃗ = a⃗₂ + μb⃗₂ is |[a⃗₂−a⃗₁, b⃗₁, b⃗₂]|/|b⃗₁ × b⃗₂|. The lines intersect iff [a⃗₂−a⃗₁, b⃗₁, b⃗₂] = 0.
  • Parallelogram law: |a⃗+b⃗|² + |a⃗−b⃗|² = 2(|a⃗|² + |b⃗|²). Lagrange's identity: |a⃗×b⃗|² + (a⃗·b⃗)² = |a⃗|²|b⃗|². Both are universally true for any two vectors.
  • Section formula (internal): p⃗ = (nb⃗ + ma⃗)/(m+n) where P divides AB in ratio m:n. Common JEE trap: students reverse the coefficient assignment.
  • Four points A(a⃗), B(b⃗), C(c⃗), D(d⃗) are coplanar iff [(b⃗−a⃗)(c⃗−a⃗)(d⃗−a⃗)] = 0. Three points are collinear iff (b⃗−a⃗) × (c⃗−a⃗) = 0→.
  • If three unit vectors sum to zero, the angle between any pair is 120°. This is a frequently tested configuration.
  • Dot product of perpendicular vectors is zero; cross product of parallel vectors is zero. These are the two most commonly used orthogonality and parallelism tests.
  • The vector projection of a⃗ on b⃗ is [(a⃗·b⃗)/|b⃗|²]b⃗. The scalar projection is (a⃗·b⃗)/|b⃗|. Both appear in physics and geometry applications.
  • Line-plane angle: sin φ = |b⃗·n̂|/(|b⃗||n̂|), where b⃗ is line direction and n̂ is plane normal. Note sine, not cosine — because φ is the angle between line and plane, complementary to the angle between line and normal.

Common mistakes

  • Reversing the section formula coefficients: writing p⃗ = (ma⃗ + nb⃗)/(m+n) instead of (nb⃗ + ma⃗)/(m+n). Remember: the vector of the far endpoint gets the near ratio. If AP:PB = m:n, the coefficient of b⃗ is m and of a⃗ is n.
  • Confusing dot product with cross product conditions: a⃗·b⃗ = 0 means perpendicular; a⃗×b⃗ = 0→ means parallel. Students often mix these up in MCQs testing vector relationships.
  • Applying the vector triple product formula incorrectly: writing a⃗ × (b⃗ × c⃗) = (a⃗·b⃗)c⃗ − (a⃗·c⃗)b⃗ (wrong order). The correct form is (a⃗·c⃗)b⃗ − (a⃗·b⃗)c⃗ — mnemonic: 'bac minus cab'.
  • Using cosine instead of sine for the angle between a line and a plane. The formula is sin φ = |b⃗·n̂|/(|b⃗||n̂|), not cosine, because φ is the complement of the angle between the line and the normal.
  • Forgetting the factor of ½ when computing triangle area from cross product. Area of triangle = ½|a⃗ × b⃗|, not |a⃗ × b⃗|. The latter is the area of the parallelogram.
  • Forgetting the factor of 1/6 when computing tetrahedron volume. Volume = (1/6)|[a⃗ b⃗ c⃗]|, not (1/3). The 1/3 formula applies to pyramids with known base area and height, but the vector formula uses 1/6 directly.
  • Assuming cross product is associative: (a⃗ × b⃗) × c⃗ = a⃗ × (b⃗ × c⃗). This is false in general. Always evaluate left-to-right or use the VTP formula explicitly.
  • Confusing coplanarity of four POINTS with coplanarity of three VECTORS. Four points A,B,C,D are coplanar iff [(b⃗−a⃗)(c⃗−a⃗)(d⃗−a⃗)] = 0. Three vectors are coplanar iff [a⃗ b⃗ c⃗] = 0. Do not mix these tests.
  • Sign errors in the cross product determinant expansion. The j-component has a negative sign: −(a₁b₃−a₃b₁)ĵ. Many students write +(a₁b₃−a₃b₁)ĵ.
  • In problems where a⃗+b⃗+c⃗=0→, forgetting to square the equation properly: |a⃗+b⃗+c⃗|² = 0 expands to |a⃗|²+|b⃗|²+|c⃗|²+2(a⃗·b⃗+b⃗·c⃗+c⃗·a⃗) = 0. Missing the factor of 2 on the dot products is a frequent error.
  • When finding the angle between diagonals of a parallelogram, taking the angle between a⃗+b⃗ and a⃗−b⃗ without considering that the diagonals may intersect at supplementary angles. Always take the absolute value in the cosine formula to get the acute angle.
