JEE Advanced — Mathematics: Analytical Geometry — Quick Revision

JEE AdvancedEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Analytical Geometry (Coordinate Geometry) is the study of geometric figures using a coordinate system. Points are represented as ordered pairs/triples, and curves are described by algebraic equations in those coordinates.
  • Distance between two points P(x₁,y₁) and Q(x₂,y₂): PQ = √[(x₂−x₁)² + (y₂−y₁)²]. In 3D with R(x₃,y₃,z₃), PR = √[(x₃−x₁)² + (y₃−y₁)² + (z₃−y₁)²].
  • Section formula: A point dividing the line joining (x₁,y₁) and (x₂,y₂) internally in ratio m:n has coordinates ((mx₂+nx₁)/(m+n), (my₂+ny₁)/(m+n)). Externally, replace n by −n.
  • Centroid of triangle with vertices (x₁,y₁),(x₂,y₂),(x₃,y₃) is G = ((x₁+x₂+x₃)/3, (y₁+y₂+y₃)/3). Orthocentre H satisfies OH = OA+OB+OC where O is origin. Circumcentre is equidistant from all three vertices. Incentre I = (aA+bB+cC)/(a+b+c) where a,b,c are side lengths opposite to A,B,C respectively.
  • Area of triangle ABC = ½|x₁(y₂−y₃)+x₂(y₃−y₁)+x₃(y₁−y₂)|. Three points are collinear if and only if this area equals zero.
  • Equation of a line through (x₁,y₁) with slope m: y−y₁ = m(x−x₁). Slope-intercept form: y = mx+c. Point-slope, two-point, intercept (x/a+y/b=1), normal (x cos α+y sin α=p), and general form ax+by+c=0 are all equivalent representations.
  • Angle between two lines with slopes m₁ and m₂: tan θ = |(m₁−m₂)/(1+m₁m₂)|. Two lines are parallel iff m₁=m₂; perpendicular iff m₁m₂=−1.
  • Distance from point (x₀,y₀) to line ax+by+c=0: d = |ax₀+by₀+c|/√(a²+b²). This formula is repeatedly used in JEE Advanced geometry problems.
  • A conic section is the locus of a point whose distance from a fixed point (focus) bears a constant ratio e (eccentricity) to its perpendicular distance from a fixed line (directrix). e<1 gives ellipse, e=1 parabola, e>1 hyperbola.
  • Degenerate conic: pair of straight lines. The general second-degree equation ax²+2hxy+by²+2gx+2fy+c=0 represents a pair of lines iff abc+2fgh−af²−bg²−ch²=0.

Important facts

  • For parabola y²=4ax, length of focal chord making angle θ with axis = 4a csc²θ. Minimum length = 4a (when θ=90°, the latus rectum).
  • Tangents at endpoints of a focal chord of parabola intersect at right angle on the directrix. This is a classic JEE Advanced fact.
  • Product of perpendicular distances from any point on ellipse/hyperbola to the pair of asymptotes (for hyperbola) or to conjugate diameters is constant.
  • For ellipse, sum of squares of lengths of conjugate diameters = a²+b² (constant). Product of their lengths = ab (constant, equal to semi-axes product).
  • Eccentric angles θ and θ+π/2 correspond to endpoints of conjugate diameters on ellipse.
  • Orthoptic locus of ellipse x²/a²+y²/b²=1 is the director circle x²+y²=a²+b² — locus of points from which perpendicular tangents can be drawn.
  • For rectangular hyperbola xy=c², the asymptotes are the coordinate axes. Eccentricity is always √2 regardless of c.
  • If a normal to parabola at P meets it again at Q, and PM is perpendicular from P to axis, then SQ=2SM where S is focus. This is a well-known property tested in JEE.
  • The equation of the pair of tangents from an external point (x₁,y₁) to conic S=0 is SS₁=T², where S is the conic, S₁ is value at the point, and T is the polar form.
  • Joint equation of bisectors of angles between lines represented by ax²+2hxy+by²=0 is (x²−y²)/(a−b)=xy/h. This is exact and frequently used.
  • Number of common tangents to two circles: 4 (separated externally), 3 (touching externally), 2 (intersecting), 1 (touching internally), 0 (one inside other).
  • For parabola y²=4ax, the portion of tangent between point of contact and directrix subtends a right angle at the focus.
  • In ellipse, the triangle formed by any point on ellipse and the two foci has maximum area when the point is at co-vertex: max area = b·ae.
  • Perimeter of triangle formed by three tangents to parabola y²=4ax and their chord of contact relates to focal distances — specific results appear in advanced problems.
  • For hyperbola x²/a²−y²/b²=1, the difference of focal distances equals 2a. For any chord through focus, the segment between curve and directrix subtends right angle at corresponding focus.

Memory aids

  • Parabola tangency memory: y=mx+c touches y²=4ax ↔ c=a/m. Easy to derive and remember — just substitute and set discriminant = 0.
  • Ellipse tangency: y=mx±√(a²m²+b²). Same structure as parabola but with ± and square root — because ellipse is bounded, both positive and negative tangents exist for each slope.
  • Hyperbola tangency: y=mx±√(a²m²−b²). Note the minus sign under the root — this is why hyperbola has regions where no tangent exists (when |m|<b/a, the line cuts both branches without being tangent).
  • All three conics share the pattern: line y=mx+c is tangent iff c² equals a quantity involving m². Parabola: a²m²; Ellipse: a²m²+b²; Hyperbola: a²m²−b². Remember: +b² for ellipse, −b² for hyperbola, 0 for parabola.
  • Conic eccentricities quick recall: Parabola e=1 (boundary), Ellipse 0<e<1 (closed), Hyperbola e>1 (open with two branches). Rectangular hyperbola e=√2 — the only hyperbola with perpendicular asymptotes.
  • Focal distance formulas: Parabola: r=a(1+t²); Ellipse: r=a(1−e cos θ) or a±ex₀; Hyperbola: r=a(e sec θ−1) or |a±ex₀|. All involve 'a' times something with eccentricity.
  • Latus rectum comparison: Parabola = 4a; Ellipse = 2b²/a; Hyperbola = 2b²/a. The ellipse and hyperbola share the same formula — just remember it's 2b²/a always.
  • Director circle radius memory: Ellipse: √(a²+b²); Hyperbola: √(a²−b²) (only real if a>b); Parabola: does not exist (no real point has two perpendicular tangents to a parabola).
  • Asymptotes of hyperbola: set RHS of hyperbola equation to zero. x²/a²−y²/b²=0 → y=±(b/a)x. Quick trick.
  • Tangent at parametric point: replace x²→xx₁, y²→yy₁, etc. This 'T' notation works uniformly for all conics — ellipse, hyperbola, parabola. T=0 is tangent (if point on curve), T=S₁ is chord with given midpoint.

Exam tips

  • In JEE Advanced, analytical geometry problems often combine two conics. Practice common tangent problems (e.g., common tangents to parabola and ellipse) and problems involving shared focal properties.
  • Use parametric forms extensively. For parabola, every point as (at²,2at) reduces complex locus problems to algebra in one variable t. This is almost always faster than Cartesian approach.
  • Memorize the T=S₁ formula pattern: for any conic, chord with midpoint (x₁,y₁) replaces x²→xx₁, y²→yy₁ etc. This single trick solves many 'chord with given midpoint' problems instantly.
  • For locus problems: eliminate parameters systematically. If t appears in coordinates (x(t),y(t)), find relation by eliminating t. Often the answer is another conic.
  • In multi-correct questions about conics, check edge cases: What if e=1 (parabola)? What if a=b (circle/square)? What if point is on the curve vs outside vs inside?
  • When a question involves 'number of tangents/normals from a point', count carefully: from external point to parabola, 2 tangents always; to ellipse, 2 tangents if outside, 0 if inside. Normals: up to 4 from any point to ellipse/hyperbola, 3 to parabola.
  • For assertion-reason type questions on conics: verify the reason independently before checking if it correctly explains the assertion. Many JEE Advanced AR questions have true assertion and true reason but wrong explanation link.
  • In 3D geometry, always draw a rough sketch first. Identify which lines are skew, parallel, or intersecting. The visual helps choose the right formula.
  • Time management: analytical geometry is calculation-heavy. Practice setting up equations quickly — the bottleneck is usually algebra, not concept. Learn to spot when to use a formula vs deriving from scratch.
  • For coordinate geometry in JEE Advanced, vector methods sometimes give shorter solutions than pure coordinate geometry. Know both approaches and choose based on the problem setup.
  • Quick verification trick: after solving a conic problem, substitute the answer back into original conditions. For locus problems, check if special points (vertices, foci) satisfy your final equation.

Common traps

  • Confusing the parameter t of parabola (at²,2at) with the actual geometric angle. The angle the radius vector makes with axis is NOT arctan(2/t). Only the eccentric angle of ellipse/hyperbola has special naming conventions.
  • Using the wrong sign for hyperbola tangency condition. y=mx+c touches x²/a²−y²/b²=1 iff c²=a²m²−b². If c²<a²m²−b², the line does not meet the hyperbola at all (in real points). Many students forget the −b² term.
  • Assuming the director circle always exists. It exists for ellipse always, for hyperbola only when a>b, and for parabola never (in real Euclidean plane).
  • For focal chord of parabola: forgetting that t₁t₂=−1 is the defining property. Students often try to use distance formula directly instead of this elegant parameter relation.
  • Mixing up ellipse and hyperbola polar/tangent formulas. The only difference in tangent equation form is the sign: x cos θ/a ± y sin θ/b = 1 (+ for ellipse, − for hyperbola). But parametric forms differ too: (a cos θ,b sin θ) vs (a sec θ,b tan θ).
  • In 3D shortest distance between skew lines: confusing the scalar triple product numerator with vector triple product. The formula is absolute value of scalar triple product [b−a, d₁, d₂] divided by magnitude of cross product |d₁×d₂|.
  • For pair of straight lines ax²+2hxy+by²=0: assuming they are always real. They are real and distinct iff h²>ab, coincident iff h²=ab, imaginary (only meeting at origin) iff h²<ab. JEE Advanced loves testing this distinction.
  • Using area formula for triangle formed by pair of lines and a third line without verifying the lines are real and distinct first.
  • When finding angle between line and plane in 3D: using cos instead of sin. Angle between line (direction d) and plane (normal n): sin θ = |d·n|/(|d||n|). The complement angle uses cos.
  • For chord of contact from external point: the chord of contact exists even if the point is inside the circle/ellipse, but it doesn't represent actual tangent contact points — it's still a valid line (the polar). Students often reject it incorrectly.
  • In ellipse, confusing minor axis with conjugate axis terminology. Minor axis is the specific conjugate diameter perpendicular to major axis. Not all conjugate diameters are equal to minor axis.
  • For parabola y²=4ax, forgetting that the vertex is at origin and focus is at (a,0), not (0,a). Confusing horizontal and vertical parabolas is a frequent error.
