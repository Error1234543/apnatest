JEE Advanced Mathematics — Vectors: Quick Revision

JEE AdvancedEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Scalar: A quantity having only magnitude (e.g., mass, temperature).
  • Vector: A quantity having both magnitude and direction (e.g., displacement, velocity).
  • Position vector: The vector from origin O to point P, denoted r⃗ = OP⃗.
  • Unit vector: A vector of magnitude 1 in the direction of v⃗; denoted v̂ = v⃗ / |v⃗|.
  • Zero vector: Vector with zero magnitude and arbitrary direction, denoted 0⃗.
  • Collinear vectors: Two or more vectors parallel to the same line; a⃗ and b⃗ are collinear iff a⃗ = λb⃗ for some scalar λ.
  • Equal vectors: Vectors with same magnitude and same direction, regardless of position.
  • Co-initial vectors: Vectors having the same initial point.
  • Like vectors: Collinear vectors with same direction; unlike vectors have opposite direction.
  • Displacement vector: Vector joining initial and final positions of a particle.
  • Free vector: A vector whose point of application is not fixed — it can be translated parallel to itself.
  • Bound vector: A vector with a specified initial point (applied at a specific location).
  • Negative of a vector: Vector with same magnitude but opposite direction; if a⃗ = AB⃗, then −a⃗ = BA⃗.
  • Dot product (scalar product): a⃗ · b⃗ = |a⃗||b⃗|cos θ, where θ is the angle between them.
  • Cross product (vector product): a⃗ × b⃗ = |a⃗||b⃗|sin θ n̂, where n̂ is the unit vector perpendicular to the plane of a⃗ and b⃗ (right-hand rule).
  • Scalar triple product: [a⃗ b⃗ c⃗] = a⃗ · (b⃗ × c⃗), giving a scalar value.
  • Vector triple product: a⃗ × (b⃗ × c⃗) = (a⃗ · c⃗)b⃗ − (a⃗ · b⃗)c⃗ (BAC–CAB rule).
  • Direction cosines: Cosines of angles made by a vector with positive x, y, z-axes; l = cos α, m = cos β, n = cos γ, satisfying l² + m² + n² = 1.
  • Direction ratios: Numbers proportional to direction cosines; if l : m : n = a : b : c, then direction ratios are a, b, c.
  • Coplanar vectors: Three or more vectors lying in the same plane; three non-coplanar vectors serve as a basis in 3D space.

Key concepts

  • Triangle Law: If a⃗ and b⃗ are two sides of a triangle taken in order, then a⃗ + b⃗ is the third side taken in the opposite order. Parallelogram Law: If two vectors are represented by adjacent sides of a parallelogram, their sum is along the diagonal through the common initial point. Polygon Law: For a closed polygon with successive sides a⃗₁, a⃗₂, ..., a⃗ₙ, we have a⃗₁ + a⃗₂ + ... + a⃗ₙ = 0⃗. Subtraction: a⃗ − b⃗ = a⃗ + (−b⃗), i.e., add the negative of b⃗.
  • If point P divides the segment joining A(a⃗) and B(b⃗) internally in the ratio m:n, then P = (nb⃗ + ma⃗)/(m+n). For external division in ratio m:n, P = (mb⃗ − na⃗)/(m−n). Midpoint: P = (a⃗ + b⃗)/2. Centroid of triangle with vertices a⃗, b⃗, c⃗: G = (a⃗ + b⃗ + c⃗)/3.
  • Commutative: a⃗ · b⃗ = b⃗ · a⃗. Distributive: a⃗ · (b⃗ + c⃗) = a⃗ · b⃗ + a⃗ · c⃗. If a⃗ = a₁î + a₂ĵ + a₃k̂ and b⃗ = b₁î + b₂ĵ + b₃k̂, then a⃗ · b⃗ = a₁b₁ + a₂b₂ + a₃b₃. a⃗ · a⃗ = |a⃗|². Perpendicularity condition: a⃗ ⊥ b⃗ ⇔ a⃗ · b⃗ = 0. Parallelism condition: a⃗ ∥ b⃗ ⇔ a⃗ × b⃗ = 0⃗. Projection of a⃗ on b⃗ = |a⃗| cos θ = (a⃗ · b⃗)/|b⃗|; vector projection = [(a⃗ · b⃗)/|b⃗|²] b⃗.
  • Anti-commutative: a⃗ × b⃗ = −(b⃗ × a⃗). Distributive: a⃗ × (b⃗ + c⃗) = a⃗ × b⃗ + a⃗ × c⃗. |a⃗ × b⃗| = |a⃗||b⃗|sin θ. a⃗ × a⃗ = 0⃗. If a⃗ = a₁î + a₂ĵ + a₃k̂ and b⃗ = b₁î + b₂ĵ + b₃k̂, then a⃗ × b⃗ = determinant |î ĵ k̂; a₁ a₂ a₃; b₁ b₂ b₃|. Magnitude equals area of parallelogram formed by a⃗ and b⃗.
  • [a⃗ b⃗ c⃗] = a⃗ · (b⃗ × c⃗) = b⃗ · (c⃗ × a⃗) = c⃗ · (a⃗ × b⃗) — cyclic permutation leaves value unchanged. Interchanging any two vectors changes sign: [a⃗ b⃗ c⃗] = −[b⃗ a⃗ c⃗]. Geometric meaning: absolute value equals volume of parallelepiped with edges a⃗, b⃗, c⃗. Volume of tetrahedron with OABC as vertices = |[a⃗ b⃗ c⃗]|/6. Coplanarity condition: [a⃗ b⃗ c⃗] = 0 ⇔ a⃗, b⃗, c⃗ are coplanar.
  • a⃗ × (b⃗ × c⃗) = (a⃗ · c⃗)b⃗ − (a⃗ · b⃗)c⃗ (BAC–CAB rule). Resultant vector lies in the plane of b⃗ and c⃗ and is perpendicular to a⃗. Similarly, (a⃗ × b⃗) × c⃗ = (a⃗ · c⃗)b⃗ − (b⃗ · c⃗)a⃗. Note: a⃗ × (b⃗ × c⃗) ≠ (a⃗ × b⃗) × c⃗ in general — cross product is not associative.
  • Area of triangle with adjacent sides a⃗ and b⃗ = ½|a⃗ × b⃗|. Area of parallelogram with adjacent sides a⃗ and b⃗ = |a⃗ × b⃗|. Area of triangle with vertices at position vectors a⃗, b⃗, c⃗ = ½|(b⃗−a⃗)×(c⃗−a⃗)| = ½|a⃗×b⃗ + b⃗×c⃗ + c⃗×a⃗|. If a⃗, b⃗, c⃗ are position vectors of A, B, C: area = ½|b⃗×c⃗ + c⃗×a⃗ + a⃗×b⃗|.
  • To find a unit vector perpendicular to both a⃗ and b⃗: first compute n⃗ = a⃗ × b⃗; then n̂ = n⃗/|n⃗|. There are two such unit vectors: ±n̂. This is frequently tested when finding normals to planes containing two given vectors.
  • Scalar projection of a⃗ along b⃗ = (a⃗ · b⃗)/|b⃗|. Vector projection of a⃗ along b⃗ = [(a⃗ · b⃗)/|b⃗|²] b⃗. Component of a⃗ along î, ĵ, k̂ are a₁, a₂, a₃ respectively. Any vector v⃗ can be resolved as v⃗ = (v⃗·n̂)n̂ + [v⃗ − (v⃗·n̂)n̂], the sum of its component parallel to and perpendicular to n̂.
  • From dot product: cos θ = (a⃗ · b⃗)/(|a⃗||b⃗|). From cross product: sin θ = |a⃗ × b⃗|/(|a⃗||b⃗|). tan θ = |a⃗ × b⃗|/(a⃗ · b⃗). Special cases: θ = 0° (same direction), θ = 90° (perpendicular), θ = 180° (opposite direction). For angle between lines (not directed vectors), take absolute value of cosine.
  • Three vectors a⃗, b⃗, c⃗ are coplanar iff one can be expressed as a linear combination of the other two: c⃗ = λa⃗ + μb⃗. Equivalently, [a⃗ b⃗ c⃗] = 0. Four points with position vectors a⃗, b⃗, c⃗, d⃗ are coplanar iff (b⃗−a⃗), (c⃗−a⃗), (d⃗−a⃗) are coplanar, i.e., [(b⃗−a⃗) (c⃗−a⃗) (d⃗−a⃗)] = 0.
  • If three forces F₁, F₂, F₃ acting at a point keep it in equilibrium, then F₁/sin α = F₂/sin β = F₃/sin γ, where α is the angle opposite to F₁ (between F₂ and F₃), β between F₁ and F₃, γ between F₁ and F₂. Directly derived from vector addition F₁ + F₂ + F₃ = 0 and sine rule applied to the force triangle.

Important facts

  • i⃗ × ĵ = k̂, ĵ × k̂ = i⃗, k̂ × i⃗ = ĵ; reverse cyclic gives negative.
  • i⃗ · i⃗ = ĵ · ĵ = k̂ · k̂ = 1; i⃗ · ĵ = ĵ · k̂ = k̂ · i⃗ = 0.
  • |a⃗ + b⃗|² + |a⃗ − b⃗|² = 2(|a⃗|² + |b⃗|²) — Parallelogram law of magnitudes.
  • (a⃗ · b⃗)² + |a⃗ × b⃗|² = |a⃗|²|b⃗|² — fundamental identity.
  • [a⃗ b⃗ c⃗] = 0 is the necessary and sufficient condition for three vectors to be coplanar.
  • For four points A, B, C, D to be coplanar, the volume of tetrahedron ABCD must be zero.
  • If a⃗, b⃗, c⃗ are mutually perpendicular unit vectors forming a right-handed system, they satisfy i⃗×ĵ=k̂ type relations.
  • Area of quadrilateral with diagonals d⃗₁, d⃗₂ is ½|d⃗₁ × d⃗₂|.
  • Work done by force F⃗ moving object through displacement s⃗ is W = F⃗ · s⃗.
  • Torque τ⃗ = r⃗ × F⃗, moment of force about a point.
  • If a⃗ + b⃗ + c⃗ = 0⃗, then a⃗ × b⃗ = b⃗ × c⃗ = c⃗ × a⃗.

Memory aids

  • Right-hand rule for cross product: curl fingers from first vector to second vector, thumb gives direction of result.
  • BAC-CAB rule for vector triple product: remember 'big-A little-C minus big-C little-A' → (a·c)b − (a·b)c.
  • Scalar triple product is zero when vectors are coplanar: think 'same plane → no volume → zero'.
  • Cyclic property: [a⃗ b⃗ c⃗] = [b⃗ c⃗ a⃗] = [c⃗ a⃗ b⃗] — rotate letters cyclically without changing sign.
  • Interchanging ANY two vectors flips sign: odd permutations change sign, even permutations don't.
  • Parallelogram law mnemonic: sum of squares of sides equals sum of squares of diagonals (twice each side).

Exam tips

  • JEE Advanced frequently tests the scalar triple product in 2-3 mark questions; always check cyclic properties before computing determinants.
  • Memorize the BAC-CAB identity by heart — it appears directly or via elimination in multiple ways.
  • For area questions, first identify which two vectors represent adjacent sides of the triangle or parallelogram.
  • When a problem asks for a unit vector perpendicular to a plane, use cross product of two non-parallel vectors in the plane.
  • For multi-correct questions on vector identities, verify each option independently using component forms when unsure.
  • Numerical answer type questions on vectors often involve integer answers derived from dot/cross products — compute carefully and check units.
  • In matrix-match questions, match vector properties (perpendicularity, coplanarity, collinearity) with their algebraic conditions systematically.
  • Always verify that a⃗ ≠ 0⃗ before dividing by a⃗ or taking a⃗/|a⃗| as a unit vector.
  • For questions involving angles between lines (not vectors), the answer should be in [0, π/2]; use absolute value of dot product divided by product of magnitudes.
  • Practice recognizing when three vectors form a basis for 3D space: check [a⃗ b⃗ c⃗] ≠ 0 for non-coplanarity.

Common traps

  • Confusing a⃗ × (b⃗ × c⃗) with (a⃗ × b⃗) × c⃗ — these are NOT equal in general. Always use the correct expansion form.
  • Writing a⃗ · (b⃗ × c⃗) as (a⃗ · b⃗) × c⃗ — dot product is not associative with cross product.
  • Assuming a⃗ · b⃗ = a⃗ · c⃗ implies b⃗ = c⃗ — false; b⃗ and c⃗ may differ by a vector perpendicular to a⃗.
  • Assuming a⃗ × b⃗ = a⃗ × c⃗ implies b⃗ = c⃗ — false; difference could be parallel to a⃗.
  • Using θ from dot product formula but picking wrong quadrant — dot product gives cosine, so for 0 ≤ θ ≤ π, the value is unique. However, for lines (undirected), angle range is 0 ≤ θ ≤ π/2.
  • Forgetting the ½ factor in area of triangle = ½|a⃗ × b⃗|. Triangle area is half the parallelogram area.
  • In section formula, swapping internal/external: internal uses +, external uses −.
  • Confusing scalar projection (a scalar) with vector projection (a vector). Scalar = (a·b)/|b|; vector = [(a·b)/|b|²]b.
  • When checking coplanarity of 4 points, using position vectors directly instead of using relative vectors like (b−a), (c−a), (d−a).
  • Forgetting that i⃗×j⃗ = k⃗ but j⃗×i⃗ = −k⃗. Order matters in cross product.
