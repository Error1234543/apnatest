\(\vec{a} = a_1 \hat{i} + a_2 \hat{j} + a_3 \hat{k}\)

Symbols: \(a_1, a_2, a_3\) = components of \(\vec{a}\) along x, y, z axes respectively; \(\hat{i}, \hat{j}, \hat{k}\) = unit vectors along coordinate axes

Unit: Same as the quantity represented (displacement → m, force → N, etc.)

When to use: Component form of a vector in 3D Cartesian coordinates

Conditions: For 2D problems, set \(a_3 = 0\)

Common mistake: Confusing direction cosines \((l,m,n)\) with components; remember \(a_1 = |\vec{a}| l\), not \(a_1 = l\)

|\vec{a}| = \sqrt{a_1^2 + a_2^2 + a_3^2}

Symbols: \(|\vec{a}| ight) = magnitude (scalar) of vector \(\vec{a}\)

Unit: Same SI unit as the vector quantity

When to use: Finding the length/modulus of a vector from its components

Conditions: Always non-negative; \(|\vec{a}| = 0\) iff \(\vec{a}\) is the zero vector

Common mistake: Forgetting the square root or squaring only one component

\(\hat{a} = \dfrac{\vec{a}}{|\vec{a}|} = \dfrac{a_1 \hat{i} + a_2 \hat{j} + a_3 \hat{k}}{\sqrt{a_1^2 + a_2^2 + a_3^2}}\)

Symbols: \(\hat{a}\) = unit vector in the direction of \(\vec{a}\)

Unit: Dimensionless

When to use: Resolving any vector into magnitude × direction; finding projection direction

Conditions: Only defined when \(\vec{a} \neq \vec{0}\)

Common mistake: Using \(\hat{a}\) when \(\vec{a}\) could be the zero vector without checking first

\(\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = a_1 b_1 + a_2 b_2 + a_3 b_3 = |\vec{a}||\vec{b}|\cos\theta\)

Symbols: \(\theta\) = angle between \(\vec{a}\) and \(\vec{b}\) when placed tail-to-tail; result is a scalar

Unit: Product of units of \(\vec{a}\) and \(\vec{b}\) (e.g., N·m for work)

When to use: Finding angle between two vectors; computing projections; dot product in geometry

Conditions: \(0 \leq \theta \leq \pi\); \(\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = 0\) iff \(\vec{a}\) or \(\vec{b}\) is zero OR they are perpendicular

Common mistake: Writing \(\cos\theta = \dfrac{\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}}{|\vec{a}||\vec{b}|}\) without ensuring neither vector is zero

\(\vec{a} \times \vec{b} = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ a_1 & a_2 & a_3 \\ b_1 & b_2 & b_3 \end{vmatrix} = (a_2 b_3 - a_3 b_2)\hat{i} - (a_1 b_3 - a_3 b_1)\hat{j} + (a_1 b_2 - a_2 b_1)\hat{k}\)

Symbols: Result is a vector perpendicular to both \(\vec{a}\) and \(\vec{b}\); direction given by right-hand rule

Unit: Product of units of \(\vec{a}\) and \(\vec{b}\)

When to use: Finding a vector perpendicular to two given vectors; area of parallelogram/triangle; torque; angular velocity

Conditions: \(\vec{a} \times \vec{b} = \vec{0}\) iff \(\vec{a}\) and \(\vec{b}\) are parallel or one is zero

Common mistake: Sign error in the \(\hat{j}\) component (the middle term carries a minus sign in the determinant expansion)

|\vec{a} \times \vec{b}| = |\vec{a}||\vec{b}|\sin\theta

Symbols: \(\theta\) = angle between \(\vec{a}\) and \(\vec{b}\) (\(0 \leq \theta \leq \pi\))

Unit: Product of the units of the two vectors

When to use: Magnitude of cross product without computing the vector; used in area calculations

Conditions: Max value \(|\vec{a}||\vec{b}|\) when \(\theta = \pi/2\); zero when parallel

Common mistake: Using \(\cos\theta\) instead of \(\sin\theta\) for cross product magnitude

\(\text{Area of }\triangle ABC = \dfrac{1}{2}|\vec{AB} \times \vec{AC}| = \dfrac{1}{2}|\vec{b} \times \vec{c}|\) (when position vectors of B,C are \(\vec{b}, \vec{c}\))

Symbols: \(\vec{AB} = \vec{b} - \vec{a}\), \(\vec{AC} = \vec{c} - \vec{a}\)

Unit: Square of the unit of length

When to use: Finding area of triangle given vertices or position vectors

Conditions: Also: \(\text{Area} = \dfrac{1}{2}|\vec{a}||\vec{b}|\sin\theta\) where \(\theta\) is angle at origin

Common mistake: Forgetting the factor \(\dfrac{1}{2}\); using parallelogram formula by mistake

\(\text{Area of parallelogram with adjacent sides }\vec{a}, \vec{b} = |\vec{a} \times \vec{b}|\)

Symbols: \(\vec{a}, \vec{b}\) = adjacent side vectors

Unit: Square of the unit of length

When to use: Area from vector representation of sides

Conditions: If \(\vec{a}, \vec{b}\) are position vectors of two vertices from origin, this gives area of parallelogram formed by them and the origin

Common mistake: Applying this when vectors are not adjacent sides but diagonals

\(\text{Projection of }\vec{a}\text{ on }\vec{b} = \dfrac{\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b}}{|\vec{b}|}\) (scalar projection) \\ \(\text{Vector projection of }\vec{a}\text{ on }\vec{b} = \dfrac{\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b}}{|\vec{b}|^2}\,\vec{b}\)

Symbols: Scalar projection = signed length of shadow of \(\vec{a}\) on line of \(\vec{b}\); Vector projection = actual vector along \(\vec{b}\)

Unit: Same as \(\vec{a}\)'s unit for scalar; same as \(\vec{b}\)'s unit for vector projection

When to use: Component of one vector along another; resolving forces/displacements

Conditions: Scalar projection can be negative (when angle > \(\pi/2\)); vector projection is always along \(\vec{b}\)

Common mistake: Dividing by \(|\vec{b}|\) instead of \(|\vec{b}|^2\) in the vector projection formula

\((\vec{a} \times \vec{b}) \cdot \vec{c} = \vec{a} \cdot (\vec{b} \times \vec{c}) = [\vec{a}\,\vec{b}\,\vec{c}]\) (scalar triple product) \\ = \(\begin{vmatrix} a_1 & a_2 & a_3 \\ b_1 & b_2 & b_3 \\ c_1 & c_2 & c_3 \end{vmatrix}\)

Symbols: Result is a scalar; represents signed volume of parallelepiped

Unit: Cube of the length unit

When to use: Testing coplanarity of three vectors; computing volumes; finding perpendicular to a plane

Conditions: \([\vec{a}\,\vec{b}\,\vec{c}] = 0\) iff \(\vec{a}, \vec{b}, \vec{c}\) are coplanar

Common mistake: Confusing scalar triple product with vector triple product; also sign changes under odd permutation of vectors

\(\vec{a} \times (\vec{b} \times \vec{c}) = (\vec{a} \cdot \vec{c})\,\vec{b} - (\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b})\,\vec{c}\) (vector triple product / \(\texttt{bac-cab}\) rule)

Symbols: Result is a vector lying in the plane of \(\vec{b}\) and \(\vec{c}\)

Unit: Product of units of all three vectors

When to use: Simplifying nested cross products; physics problems involving torque and angular momentum

Conditions: NOT associative: \(\vec{a} \times (\vec{b} \times \vec{c}) \neq (\vec{a} \times \vec{b}) \times \vec{c}\)

Common mistake: Writing the wrong order: it is \(BAC - CAB\), not \(ABC - ACB\) or any other arrangement

(\vec{a} \times \vec{b}) \times \vec{c} = (\vec{a} \cdot \vec{c})\,\vec{b} - (\vec{b} \cdot \vec{c})\,\vec{a}\)

Symbols: Result lies in the plane of \(\vec{a}\) and \(\vec{b}\)

Unit: Product of units of all three vectors

When to use: Nested cross product where the cross is on the left

Conditions: Different from \(\vec{a} \times (\vec{b} \times \vec{c})\); verify which grouping you have

Common mistake: Using the bac-cab rule blindly without identifying the correct grouping

\(\vec{a} \cdot (\vec{b} \times \vec{c}) = \vec{b} \cdot (\vec{c} \times \vec{a}) = \vec{c} \cdot (\vec{a} \times \vec{b})\) (cyclic property) \\ \(\vec{a} \cdot (\vec{b} \times \vec{c}) = -\vec{a} \cdot (\vec{c} \times \vec{b})\) (anti-commutative swap)

Symbols: Cyclic permutations preserve sign; swapping any two vectors reverses sign

Unit: Cube of length unit

When to use: Evaluating scalar triple products efficiently; proving coplanarity

Conditions: Valid for any three vectors in \(\mathbb{R}^3\)

Common mistake: Forgetting that an odd number of swaps changes the sign

\(\vec{a} \times (\vec{b} + \vec{c}) = \vec{a} \times \vec{b} + \vec{a} \times \vec{c}\) (distributive law) \\ (\vec{a} + \vec{b}) \cdot \vec{c} = \vec{a} \cdot \vec{c} + \vec{b} \cdot \vec{c}\) (dot product distributivity)

Symbols: Both operations are distributive over vector addition

Unit: N/A (algebraic identity)

When to use: Expanding and simplifying vector expressions

Conditions: Cross product is NOT commutative: \(\vec{a} \times \vec{b} = -\vec{b} \times \vec{a}\)

Common mistake: Assuming \(\vec{a} \times \vec{b} = \vec{b} \times \vec{a}\); this reverses the direction

\(\lambda(\mu\vec{a}) = (\lambda\mu)\vec{a}\); \((\lambda + \mu)\vec{a} = \lambda\vec{a} + \mu\vec{a}\); \(\lambda(\vec{a} + \vec{b}) = \lambda\vec{a} + \lambda\vec{b}\)

Symbols: \(\lambda, \mu\) = scalars (real numbers)

Unit: Same as \(\vec{a}\)'s unit

When to use: Scaling and combining vectors algebraically

Conditions: These are the vector space axioms for \(\mathbb{R}^3\)

Common mistake: Forgetting that scalar multiplication distributes over both scalar addition and vector addition

\(|\vec{a} + \vec{b}|^2 = |\vec{a}|^2 + |\vec{b}|^2 + 2\,\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}\) \\ \(|\vec{a} - \vec{b}|^2 = |\vec{a}|^2 + |\vec{b}|^2 - 2\,\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}\)

Symbols: Generalizes the law of cosines to vector form

Unit: Square of the length unit

When to use: Finding resultant magnitude; solving triangle problems with vectors

Conditions: When \(\vec{a} \perp \vec{b}\), reduces to Pythagoras: \(|\vec{a}+\vec{b}|^2 = |\vec{a}|^2 + |\vec{b}|^2\)

Common mistake: Writing \(+2\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}\) for the difference case

\(|\vec{a} + \vec{b}| \leq |\vec{a}| + |\vec{b}|\) (Triangle inequality) \\ ||\vec{a}| - |\vec{b}|| \leq |\vec{a} \pm \vec{b}| \leq |\vec{a}| + |\vec{b}| ight)

Symbols: Standard metric properties of vector magnitudes

Unit: Length unit

When to use: Establishing bounds; proving inequalities in vector problems

Conditions: Equality in \(|\vec{a}+\vec{b}| = |\vec{a}|+|\vec{b}|\) holds iff \(\vec{a}\) and \(\vec{b}\) are in the same direction

Common mistake: Applying triangle inequality in reverse without checking collinearity condition

\(\vec{a} \times \vec{b} = \vec{b} \times \vec{a}\) is FALSE; actually \(\vec{a} \times \vec{b} = -\,\vec{b} \times \vec{a}\) (Anti-commutativity)

Symbols: Swapping order reverses direction of resultant vector

Unit: N/A

When to use: Any computation involving cross products; must keep order in mind

Conditions: Only true when \(\vec{a} \times \vec{b} = \vec{0}\) (parallel or zero vectors)

Common mistake: Canceling \(\vec{a} \times \vec{b} = \vec{b} \times \vec{c}\) by 'canceling' \(\vec{b}\)

\(\vec{a} \times \vec{a} = \vec{0}\); \(\vec{a} \times \vec{0} = \vec{0}\); \(\vec{a} \times \vec{b} = \vec{0} \iff \vec{a} \parallel \vec{b}\) or one is zero

Symbols: Parallel means \(\vec{a} = k\vec{b}\) for some scalar \(k\)

Unit: N/A

When to use: Quick tests for parallelism; simplifying cross product expressions

Conditions: Direction of parallel: same if \(k > 0\), opposite if \(k < 0\)

Common mistake: Concluding \(\vec{a} = \vec{b}\) from \(\vec{a} \times \vec{b} = \vec{0}\) — they may only be parallel

\(\hat{i} \times \hat{j} = \hat{k},\; \hat{j} \times \hat{k} = \hat{i},\; \hat{k} \times \hat{i} = \hat{j}\) (Cyclic) \\ \(\hat{j} \times \hat{i} = -\hat{k},\; \hat{k} \times \hat{j} = -\hat{i},\; \hat{i} \times \hat{k} = -\hat{j}\) (Reverse) \\ \(\hat{i} \cdot \hat{i} = \hat{j} \cdot \hat{j} = \hat{k} \cdot \hat{k} = 1\); \(\hat{i} \cdot \hat{j} = \hat{j} \cdot \hat{k} = \hat{k} \cdot \hat{i} = 0\)

Symbols: Standard orthonormal basis in \(\mathbb{R}^3\)

Unit: N/A

When to use: Fundamental building blocks for all 3D vector computations; quick evaluation of cross/dot products

Conditions: Right-handed coordinate system assumed

Common mistake: Memorizing only clockwise cyclic order and missing the counter-clockwise signs

\(\text{Unit vector perpendicular to both }\vec{a}\text{ and }\vec{b} = \dfrac{\vec{a} \times \vec{b}}{|\vec{a} \times \vec{b}|}\) (and its negative)

Symbols: Gives two possible directions (opposite to each other)

Unit: Dimensionless

When to use: Finding normal to a plane containing \(\vec{a}\) and \(\vec{b}\); required for certain JEE geometry problems

Conditions: Requires \(\vec{a}\) and \(\vec{b}\) to be non-parallel (otherwise cross product is zero)

Common mistake: Forgetting the \(\pm\) — there are two opposite perpendicular directions

\(\dfrac{\vec{a} \times \vec{b}}{|\vec{a} \times \vec{b}|} = \pm \hat{n}\) where \(\hat{n}\) is the unit normal to the plane of \(\vec{a}, \vec{b}\)

Symbols: \(\hat{n}\) = unit vector perpendicular to the plane containing \(\vec{a}\) and \(\vec{b}\)

Unit: Dimensionless

When to use: Defining orientation of a plane; useful in physics applications (magnetic force, etc.)

Conditions: The sign depends on the order of \(\vec{a}\) and \(\vec{b}\) (right-hand rule)

Common mistake: Not accounting for the right-hand rule when the problem specifies a particular orientation

\(\vec{r} = x\hat{i} + y\hat{j} + z\hat{k}\) (position vector of point \(P(x,y,z)\)) \\ \(\vec{AB} = \vec{b} - \vec{a}\) where \(\vec{a}, \vec{b}\) are position vectors of A and B

Symbols: \(\vec{r}\) = position vector from origin O to P; \(\vec{AB}\) = displacement from A to B

Unit: Length unit

When to use: Converting between point coordinates and vector notation; finding displacement between two points

Conditions: Origin is \(O(0,0,0)\) in standard Cartesian system

Common mistake: Writing \(\vec{BA} = \vec{b} - \vec{a}\) instead of \(\vec{a} - \vec{b}\)

\(\text{Angle between }\vec{a}\text{ and }\vec{b}:\; \cos\theta = \dfrac{\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}}{|\vec{a}||\vec{b}|}\)

Symbols: \(\theta\) = acute or obtuse angle between the two vectors (\(0 \leq \theta \leq \pi\))

Unit: Radians or degrees

When to use: Finding angles in triangles, tetrahedra, between lines; most frequently tested concept

Conditions: Both vectors must be non-zero

Common mistake: Getting \(\theta > \pi/2\) when the question asks for the acute angle between two lines (take \(|\cos\theta|\))

\(\vec{a} \cdot (\vec{b} \times \vec{c}) = \vec{b} \cdot (\vec{c} \times \vec{a}) = \vec{c} \cdot (\vec{a} \times \vec{b})\) (Scalar triple product cyclic invariance)

Symbols: All equal to the determinant of the component matrix

Unit: Volume unit (length³)

When to use: Proving coplanarity; evaluating volumes; symmetric properties in proofs

Conditions: Zero iff the three vectors are coplanar

Common mistake: Replacing a dot with a cross or vice versa while using the cyclic property

\(\text{Volume of parallelepiped with edges }\vec{a}, \vec{b}, \vec{c} = |[\vec{a}\,\vec{b}\,\vec{c}]| = |[\vec{a}\,\vec{b}\,\vec{c}]|\)

Symbols: Absolute value of the scalar triple product

Unit: Cubic length unit

When to use: Volume problems in 3D geometry; JEE frequently combines with plane equations

Conditions: Edges must emanate from the same vertex

Common mistake: Forgetting the absolute value (triple product can be negative depending on orientation)

\(\text{Volume of tetrahedron with vertices }O,A,B,C = \dfrac{1}{6}|[\vec{a}\,\vec{b}\,\vec{c}]|\)

Symbols: \(\vec{a}, \vec{b}, \vec{c}\) = position vectors of A, B, C from origin O

Unit: Cubic length unit

When to use: Tetrahedron volume from position vectors; related to scalar triple product

Conditions: One vertex must be at origin, or use edge vectors from one vertex

Common mistake: Using \(\dfrac{1}{3}\) instead of \(\dfrac{1}{6}\); confusing with pyramid volume formula

\(\vec{a} \times (\vec{b} \times \vec{c}) + \vec{b} \times (\vec{c} \times \vec{a}) + \vec{c} \times (\vec{a} \times \vec{b}) = \vec{0}\) (Jacobi identity)

Symbols: Fundamental identity for vector triple products

Unit: N/A

When to use: Proving vector identities; advanced JEE-level manipulation problems

Conditions: Holds for any three vectors in \(\mathbb{R}^3\)

Common mistake: Trying to use this identity when a simpler bac-cab expansion would suffice

\(\vec{a} \cdot (\vec{b} \times \vec{c}) = \det(\vec{a}, \vec{b}, \vec{c})\) (determinant form of scalar triple product)

Symbols: Determinant of the 3×3 matrix whose rows (or columns) are the components of \(\vec{a}, \vec{b}, \vec{c}\)

Unit: Volume unit

When to use: Computing scalar triple product quickly; testing linear independence

Conditions: Zero determinant ↔ vectors are linearly dependent (coplanar)

Common mistake: Mixing up row and column placement; the determinant value is the same either way

\(|\vec{a} \times \vec{b}|^2 + (\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b})^2 = |\vec{a}|^2 |\vec{b}|^2\) (Lagrange's identity)

Symbols: Connects cross product magnitude and dot product

Unit: Length⁴

When to use: Proof-based JEE questions; relating two fundamental products

Conditions: Valid for any two vectors in any dimension (in \(\mathbb{R}^2\), cross product is interpreted as scalar magnitude)

Common mistake: Attempting to derive it from scratch instead of recognizing it as a known identity

\(\text{If }\vec{a} \times \vec{r} = \vec{b} \times \vec{r},\text{ then }\vec{r} = \dfrac{\vec{a} \times \vec{b}}{|\vec{a} - \vec{b}|^2} \times (\vec{a} - \vec{b}) + t(\vec{a} - \vec{b})\) for some scalar \(t\)

Symbols: \(\vec{r}\) lies on a line parallel to \(\vec{a} - \vec{b}\)

Unit: Same as \(\vec{a}, \vec{b}\)

When to use: Solving vector equations of the form \(\vec{a} \times \vec{r} = \vec{c}\)

Conditions: Requires \(\vec{a} \neq \vec{b}\); if \(\vec{a} \parallel \vec{b}\), special handling needed

Common mistake: Assuming a unique solution when the solution is actually a line (one free parameter)

\(\text{Coplanarity condition: }[\vec{a}\,\vec{b}\,\vec{c}] = 0\)

Symbols: Scalar triple product equals zero

Unit: N/A

When to use: Determining whether three vectors lie in the same plane; four points are coplanar iff \([\vec{b}-\vec{a},\,\vec{c}-\vec{a},\,\vec{d}-\vec{a}] = 0\)

Conditions: Applies to position vectors from any common origin, or edge vectors from one vertex

Common mistake: Using four position vectors directly without forming differences from one common point
