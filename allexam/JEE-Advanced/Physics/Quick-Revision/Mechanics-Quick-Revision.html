JEE Advanced Physics Mechanics — Quick Revision

JEE AdvancedEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Mechanics: Branch of physics dealing with motion of bodies and forces acting on them. Divided into kinematics (description of motion), dynamics (causes of motion), and statics (equilibrium).
  • Displacement: Vector change in position; magnitude equals shortest distance between initial and final points. Units: metre (m).
  • Velocity: Rate of change of displacement; vector quantity. Instantaneous velocity = derivative of position with respect to time. Units: m/s.
  • Acceleration: Rate of change of velocity; vector quantity. In uniformly accelerated motion, acceleration is constant. Units: m/s².
  • Force: Push or pull that changes or tends to change state of rest or uniform motion of a body. Net force causes acceleration per Newton’s second law. Units: newton (N) = kg·m/s².
  • Work: Scalar product of force and displacement; W = F·s·cosθ. Measured in joule (J).
  • Energy: Capacity to do work. Kinetic energy KE = ½mv²; gravitational PE = mgh; elastic PE = ½kx².
  • Power: Rate of doing work; P = dW/dt. Average power = total work / total time. Units: watt (W).
  • Momentum: Product of mass and velocity; p = mv. Vector quantity. Conserved in isolated system.
  • Impulse: Change in momentum; I = ∫F dt = Δp. Units: N·s or kg·m/s.
  • Center of Mass: Point where total mass of system may be considered concentrated for translational motion. R_cm = (Σ m_i r_i)/M_total.
  • Moment of Inertia: Resistance of rotational motion; I = Σ m_i r_i². Depends on axis and mass distribution. Units: kg·m².
  • Torque: Rotational analogue of force; τ = r × F. Magnitude τ = rF sinφ. Units: N·m.
  • Angular Momentum: L = Iω = r × p. Conserved when net external torque is zero.
  • Fluid Pressure: Force per unit area exerted by fluid. Pascal’s law: pressure applied to enclosed fluid transmitted equally in all directions.

Key concepts

  • Kinematics with constant acceleration: v = u + at, s = ut + ½at², v² = u² + 2as. Apply component-wise for 2D/3D motion. Sign convention essential.
  • Relative motion: v_AB = v_A − v_B. Use when two bodies move; relative acceleration remains same if both have same acceleration.
  • Projectile motion: Time of flight T = 2u sinα/g, range R = u² sin2α/g, max height H = u² sin²α/(2g). Range maximum at α = 45°. Symmetry about highest point if same level.
  • Newton’s first law: Body remains at rest or uniform motion unless net external force acts. Defines inertial frame.
  • Newton’s second law: F_net = ma. Vector equation; resolve components. Free-body diagrams crucial.
  • Friction: Static friction f_s ≤ μ_s N adjusts to prevent sliding; kinetic friction f_k = μ_k N opposes motion. Transition at f_s,max = μ_s N.
  • Circular motion: Centripetal acceleration a_c = v²/r = ω²r directed toward centre. For banked curve without friction: tanθ = v²/(rg). With friction, range of speeds depends on μ.
  • Work-energy theorem: W_net = ΔKE. Conservative forces have path-independent work; non-conservative (friction, air drag) dissipate energy.
  • Conservation of mechanical energy: If only conservative forces do work, KE + PE = constant.
  • Power–velocity relation: P = F·v (when force and velocity are collinear). Instantaneous power = F v cosθ.
  • Collision types: Elastic — both momentum and KE conserved. Perfectly inelastic — bodies stick together, maximum KE loss. Coefficient of restitution e = (v₂−v₁)/(u₁−u₂); e = 1 elastic, e = 0 perfectly inelastic.
  • Center of mass motion: External force on system = M a_cm. Internal forces cancel. CM moves as if all mass concentrated there.
  • Conservation of linear momentum: In absence of external force, total momentum constant. Applies to collisions, explosions, recoil.
  • Variable mass systems: Rocket equation dv = u dm/m (u exhaust speed relative rocket). Treat carefully; use momentum conservation over dt.
  • Rigid body rotation: τ_net = I α. For pure rolling: v_cm = ωR, a_cm = αR, friction provides necessary torque. Condition for no slipping: μ ≥ a_cm/(g cosθ) on incline.
  • Moment of inertia parallel-axis theorem: I = I_cm + Md². Perpendicular-axis theorem for lamina: I_z = I_x + I_y.
  • Angular momentum conservation: If τ_ext = 0, L constant. Applies to planetary motion, spinning ice skater, collision of rotating bodies.
  • Torque and equilibrium: ΣF = 0 and Στ = 0 for static equilibrium. Choose convenient pivot to eliminate unknown forces.
  • Fluid statics: Pressure variation P = P₀ + ρgh. Archimedes’ principle: buoyant force = weight of displaced fluid. Condition for floating/sinking depends on densities.
  • Bernoulli’s equation: P + ½ρv² + ρgh = constant along streamline for ideal steady incompressible flow. Applications: venturimeter, airplane wing, spray pump.
  • Viscosity: Resistive force between layers moving relative; F = ηA (dv/dx). Stokes’ law: F_drag = 6π η r v. Terminal velocity when drag + buoyancy = weight.

Important facts

  • Standard values used in JEE Advanced problems: g = 9.8 m/s² (or 10 m/s² if specified), ρ_water = 1000 kg/m³, atmospheric pressure P₀ = 1.013 × 10⁵ Pa.
  • Common moments of inertia: solid sphere I = (2/5)MR², hollow sphere I = (2/3)MR², solid cylinder I = (1/2)MR², thin ring I = MR², rod about centre I = (1/12)ML², rod about end I = (1/3)ML².
  • Collision formulas for 1D elastic collision: v₁ = ((m₁−m₂)u₁ + 2m₂u₂)/(m₁+m₂), v₂ = ((m₂−m₁)u₂ + 2m₁u₁)/(m₁+m₂). Special case: equal masses exchange velocities if one initially at rest.
  • Coefficient of restitution definition e = relative separation speed / relative approach speed along line of impact.
  • For projectile on inclined plane: time of flight, range up/down incline derived using coordinates along and perpendicular to incline.
  • Banking angle without friction: θ = arctan(v²/(rg)). With friction μ, maximum speed v_max = sqrt(rg (μ + tanθ)/(1 − μ tanθ)), minimum v_min = sqrt(rg (tanθ − μ)/(1 + μ tanθ)).
  • Pure rolling condition on incline: acceleration a = g sinθ/(1 + I_cm/(MR²)). Friction direction depends on whether force is applied at centre or elsewhere.
  • Energy loss in perfectly inelastic collision: ΔKE = ½ μ_reduced (u₁−u₂)² (1−e²), with e=0 gives maximum loss.
  • Centre of mass of uniform triangular lamina is at intersection of medians (centroid), located at average of vertex coordinates.
  • Torque τ = r F sinφ; maximum when φ = 90°, zero when force passes through pivot (φ = 0 or 180°).
  • Angular momentum L = Iω = mvr sinφ for particle. Conservation leads to Kepler’s second law (areal velocity constant).
  • Buoyancy: Upthrust = ρ_fluid V_submerged g. Object floats if weight = upthrust; sinks if weight > upthrust when fully immersed.
  • Terminal velocity derivation: mg − 6πηrv − B = 0 ⇒ v_T = (mg − B)/(6πηr) for sphere.
  • Pascal’s law applications: hydraulic lift, brakes. Pressure transmission unchanged in all directions.
  • Continuity equation: A₁v₁ = A₂v₂ for incompressible steady flow. Used with Bernoulli for velocity-pressure relations.

Memory aids

  • KNOW = Keep Not Overworking Variables — mnemonic for kinematic equations under constant acceleration.
  • F = ma → Force equals mass times acceleration; think ‘Frank May Accelerates’. Reverse for a = F/m.
  • Projectile symmetry: ‘Time up equals time down; range peaks at forty-five degrees on flat ground.’
  • Conservation laws: ‘Momentum never sleeps if no external force; Energy conserved if only conservative forces.’
  • Rolling without slipping: ‘Velocity of contact point zero; a_cm = αR, v_cm = ωR.’
  • Circular motion: ‘Centripetal always points to centre; not a separate force but resultant of real forces.’
  • Friction: ‘Static grips until limit; kinetic slides opposite motion; μ_s usually greater than μ_k.’
  • Moments of inertia: ‘Mass far from axis increases I dramatically — square dependence.’ Remember ‘solid sphere easier to roll than hollow’ because (2/5 < 2/3).
  • Bernoulli: ‘Fast fluid → low pressure; slow fluid → high pressure.’ Vinegar bottle analogy: squeezing increases speed, pressure drops.
  • Rocket: ‘Exhaust backward pushes rocket forward; thrust = u dm/dt.’

Exam tips

  • JEE Advanced frequently tests combination of concepts: e.g., block on wedge with friction, charged particle in combined fields with mechanics, variable mass with relative velocity.
  • Multi-correct questions often have subtle traps: e.g., statement ‘work done by centripetal force is zero’ is true, but ‘work done by friction is always negative’ is false.
  • Numerical answer type: expect answers in SI units, often requiring conversion from km/h to m/s or cm to m. Check significant figures; usually 2-3 digits acceptable.
  • When problem involves inclined plane with friction, resolve forces parallel and perpendicular to plane; normal reaction N = mg cosθ only if no other vertical forces.
  • For collision on smooth surface, conserve momentum in both x and y directions if 2D. Use restitution only along line of impact.
  • Rotational dynamics: identify axis of rotation carefully. For pure rolling, instantaneous axis at contact point can simplify energy calculations.
  • Fluid mechanics questions may combine Bernoulli with continuity; first find velocities from areas, then pressures.
  • Center of mass problems with removal: treat removed part as negative mass. Position of remaining COM shifts away from removed portion.
  • In variable mass (rocket), write momentum of system at t and t+dt including ejected mass. Avoid naive F = ma with changing mass.
  • Always draw diagram for mechanics problems — visualising forces, velocities, angles prevents misinterpretation.
  • Time management: mechanics carries heavy weightage; allocate proportionate time. If stuck >3 minutes, skip and return later.
  • Practice previous years’ JEE Advanced mechanics problems to recognize recurring themes: projectile on incline, collision with spring, rolling on rough surface, banking with friction.

Common traps

  • Sign errors in kinematics: taking upward positive then forgetting gravity is downward. Always fix sign convention before writing equations.
  • Confusing distance with displacement: distance is scalar path length, displacement vector between endpoints. Average speed ≠ magnitude of average velocity unless unidirectional motion.
  • Projectile range formula assumes launch and landing at same vertical level. If different heights, use general equations.
  • Forces in free-body diagram must act ON the body, not BY the body. Action-reaction pairs act on different bodies; never add them on same FBD.
  • Friction direction: kinetic friction opposes relative motion between surfaces. Static friction opposes impending motion; direction determined by attempting to move body.
  • Circular motion centripetal force is not an extra force; it’s the net radial component. Identify actual forces (tension, gravity, normal, friction) that provide it.
  • Work done by friction always negative? Not always — friction can do positive work when it accelerates a body (e.g., block on accelerating truck).
  • Energy conservation misuse: only mechanical energy conserved if non-conservative forces do zero work. Include thermal energy loss if friction present.
  • Collision problems: momentum conserved always if no external impulse; KE conserved only if elastic. Don’t assume elastic unless stated or e=1 given.
  • Inelastic collision final velocity: bodies stick only if perfectly inelastic. General inelastic use coefficient of restitution.
  • Center of mass vs centre of gravity: same only in uniform gravity field. For extended bodies in non-uniform field, they differ.
  • Moment of inertia calculation: forget perpendicular-axis theorem applies only to planar laminas. Parallel-axis theorem requires I_cm known.
  • Rolling friction: often neglected; but rolling resistance differs from static friction needed for rolling without slipping.
  • Bernoulli misuse: valid only for steady, incompressible, inviscid flow along a streamline. Don’t apply across streamlines or with viscosity effects.
  • Terminal velocity: reached when net force zero; acceleration becomes zero, not velocity. Constant velocity thereafter.
