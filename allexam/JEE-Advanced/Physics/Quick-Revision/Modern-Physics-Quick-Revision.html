Definitions

  • Photoelectric Effect: Emission of electrons from a metal surface when electromagnetic radiation of sufficient frequency strikes it. The emitted electrons are called photoelectrons.
  • Work Function (φ₀): Minimum energy required by an electron at the surface of a metal to escape into vacuum. It is material-specific and measured in eV. φ₀ = hν₀ where ν₀ is the threshold frequency.
  • Stopping Potential (V₀): The minimum negative potential applied to the anode that stops even the most energetic photoelectrons from reaching it. eV₀ = K.E._max = hν − φ₀.
  • Threshold Frequency (ν₀): The minimum frequency of incident radiation below which no photoelectrons are emitted, regardless of intensity. ν₀ = φ₀/h.
  • Bohr Radius (a₀): The radius of the first orbit of hydrogen atom in Bohr's model. a₀ = ε₀h²/(πme²) = 0.529 Å.
  • De Broglie Wavelength (λ): The wavelength associated with a moving particle. λ = h/p = h/(mv). For a charged particle accelerated through potential V: λ = h/√(2mqV).
  • Binding Energy (BE): The energy equivalent of the mass defect in a nucleus. BE = [Zm_p + Nm_n − M_nucleus]c². Higher BE per nucleon means more stable nucleus.
  • Half-life (T_{1/2}): The time after which half of the radioactive nuclei in a sample decay. T_{1/2} = ln2/λ where λ is the decay constant.
  • Activity (A): The rate of disintegration of a radioactive sample. A = λN = A₀e^{-λt}. Unit: Becquerel (Bq) = 1 decay/s; 1 Curie = 3.7 × 10¹⁰ Bq.
  • Mass Defect (Δm): The difference between the sum of masses of individual nucleons and the actual mass of the nucleus. Δm = Zm_p + Nm_n − M.

Important facts

  • h = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s = 4.136 × 10⁻¹⁵ eV·s. c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s. e = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C. m_e = 9.1 × 10⁻³¹ kg. m_p = 1.673 × 10⁻²⁷ kg. m_n = 1.675 × 10⁻²⁷ kg.
  • 1 amu = 931.5 MeV/c². hc = 1240 eV·nm = 12400 eV·Å.
  • Photoelectric effect Nobel: Einstein (1921). Compton effect Nobel: Compton (1927). Davisson-Germer Nobel: Davisson & Thomson (1937). Bohr model Nobel: Bohr (1922).
  • Hydrogen atom ground state: E₁ = −13.6 eV, r₁ = 0.529 Å, v₁ = 2.18 × 10⁶ m/s.
  • First excitation potential of hydrogen (n=1→2): 10.2 eV. Ionization potential: 13.6 eV.
  • Spectral line wavelengths (hydrogen): Lyman alpha (n=2→1): 1216 Å. Balmer alpha (n=3→2): 6563 Å (red Hα line).
  • Rydberg constant: R = 1.097 × 10⁷ m⁻¹. 1/R = 911.6 Å.
  • Maximum number of spectral lines in transition from nth level: n(n−1)/2. Number of lines in Lyman series from level n: n−1.
  • 1 Curie (Ci) = 3.7 × 10¹⁰ Bq. 1 Rutherford (Rd) = 10⁶ Bq.
  • For electron: λ(Å) = √(150/V) ≈ 12.27/√V where V is in volts. For proton: λ ≈ 0.286/√V Å. For alpha: λ ≈ 0.143/√V Å.
  • Nuclear density is nearly constant for all nuclei: ρ ≈ 2.3 × 10¹⁷ kg/m³. Nuclear radius: R = R₀A^{1/3} where R₀ ≈ 1.2 fm.
  • Carbon-14 dating: T_{1/2} = 5730 years. Used for organic materials up to ~50,000 years.
  • In beta decay, the number of protons changes but mass number remains same. In alpha decay, both change: Z→Z−2, A→A−4.
  • Fusion requires temperatures of order 10⁷ to 10⁸ K. Sun's core temperature ≈ 1.5 × 10⁷ K. Primary solar reaction: p+p→d+e⁺+ν_e, ultimately 4p → He⁴ + 2e⁺ + 2ν_e + 26.7 MeV.

Memory aids

  • Bohr energy formula mnemonic: 'Energy goes as minus Z-squared over n-squared, thirteen-point-six electron-volts is the way.' So E_n = −13.6 Z²/n² eV.
  • Radius relationship: 'Radius grows with n squared and shrinks with Z' — r_n ∝ n²/Z.
  • Velocity relationship: 'Velocity drops as n grows and climbs with Z' — v_n ∝ Z/n.
  • Spectral series order mnemonic (increasing wavelength): 'Lyman is ultra, Balmer's visible, Paschen, Brackett, Pfund — infrared.' Or simply: 'Lucky Boris Plays Basketball Partly In Rain.'
  • de Broglie shortcut: 'Lambda equals twelve-point-two-seven over root V' for electrons in Angstroms.
  • KE-PE-TE relationship in Bohr atom: 'Kinetic is Positive, Potential is Double and Negative, Total equals Negative Kinetic' → KE : PE : TE = 1 : −2 : −1.
  • Decay formula: 'Remaining is original times e to the minus lambda t' → N = N₀e^{-λt}. Half-life: 'Half-life is natural log of two divided by lambda.'
  • Moseley's law: 'Square root of frequency equals a times Z minus b' — for Kα series b=1 (shielding by one K-shell electron).
  • Uncertainty principle: 'Position times momentum is at least h-bar over two' — Δx·Δp ≥ ℏ/2.
  • Binding energy curve story: 'Light nuclei fuse to climb the hill, heavy nuclei split to roll down — iron sits at the peak, most stable of all.'
  • Photoelectric equation: 'Photon energy minus work function equals max kinetic energy' — hν = φ₀ + K.E._max. Think: 'Pay the fee (work function) then keep the rest (kinetic energy).'

Exam tips

  • JEE Advanced frequently combines photoelectric effect with Bohr model in a single problem — e.g., find the wavelength of light that just causes photoemission from a metal, then use that wavelength in a Bohr transition problem.
  • Be prepared for comparative de Broglie wavelength questions: rank electrons, protons, alphas, and neutrons by wavelength for same V, same K, or same momentum.
  • Nuclear reaction balancing: always conserve both A (mass number) and Z (atomic number). Check both sides add up.
  • For Bohr model numericals involving transitions: memorize that E_n = −13.6Z²/n² eV and 1/λ = RZ²(1/n₁² − 1/n₂²). Practice computing these quickly without calculator.
  • Radioactivity problems often involve mixed decay or daughter products — draw a decay chain diagram to track Nuclide → Nuclide → Nuclide and label each step's emission.
  • When asked about spectral lines from excited state n: total possible lines = n(n−1)/2. Lines in a particular series (ending at n₁): count all transitions from n₂ > n₁ down to n₁.
  • For JEE Advanced, expect integer-type questions on photoelectric effect (finding stopping potential, work function, or threshold wavelength from given data) and on de Broglie wavelength calculations.
  • Matrix-match type questions commonly pair concepts: Bohr model postulates with their consequences, decay types with their characteristics, semiconductor devices with their applications.
  • In semiconductors, remember: conductivity of intrinsic semiconductor increases with temperature (unlike metals). Don't confuse the two behaviors.
  • For Multi-Correct MCQs in Modern Physics, careful reading is essential — options may differ by only one word (e.g., 'proportional to' vs 'inversely proportional to', 'increases' vs 'decreases').

Common traps

  • Using λ = h/√(2mK) for photons — PHOTONS are massless, use λ = hc/E instead. This formula applies only to massive particles.
  • Confusing threshold frequency with threshold wavelength — ν₀ = φ₀/h but λ₀ = hc/φ₀. Longer wavelength means LOWER frequency. If wavelength > λ₀, NO emission occurs.
  • Assuming stopping potential depends on intensity — It does NOT. Stopping potential depends ONLY on frequency of incident light (and work function of metal).
  • Forgetting that in alpha decay, A decreases by 4 AND Z decreases by 2. In beta-minus decay, A stays same but Z increases by 1. In beta-plus decay, A stays same but Z decreases by 1.
  • Using E_n = −13.6/n² for hydrogen-like ions without multiplying by Z². Must write E_n = −13.6 Z²/n² for any hydrogen-like species.
  • Thinking that increased light intensity increases photoelectron energy — Intensity only increases the NUMBER of photoelectrons, not their maximum kinetic energy.
  • Forgetting the factor of (Z−1) in Moseley's law for Kα lines — The effective nuclear charge is (Z−1) due to shielding by the remaining K-shell electron.
  • Calculating de Broglie wavelength using classical KE = ½mv² for relativistic particles — When KE is comparable to rest mass energy (511 keV for electron), must use relativistic relation.
  • Assuming all nuclear reactions conserve mass — Mass is NOT conserved; mass-energy is conserved. The mass defect appears as energy (Q-value).
  • Forgetting that in β⁺ decay, the mass condition requires M(parent) − M(daughter) > 2m_e (not just > 0) because a positron AND an electron mass must be created.
  • Confusing mean life with half-life — τ = 1/λ = T_{1/2}/0.693 ≈ 1.44 T_{1/2}. Mean life is ALWAYS longer than half-life.
  • For mixing parallel decay: taking arithmetic mean of half-lives — Correct: 1/T_eff = 1/T₁ + 1/T₂, so T_eff = T₁T₂/(T₁+T₂).
  • In transistor circuits: confusing common emitter with common base configurations — In CE, β = I_C/I_B (typically 50-200). In CB, α = I_C/I_E (typically 0.95-0.99). Relationship: β = α/(1−α).
  • Applying Bohr model to multi-electron atoms — Bohr model works ONLY for hydrogen-like (single electron) systems: H, He⁺, Li²⁺, etc.
