Electricity and Magnetism — Quick Revision for JEE Advanced

JEE AdvancedEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Electric charge: A fundamental property of matter that causes it to experience a force in an electromagnetic field. Measured in coulombs (C). Charges are conserved and quantized (q = ne).
  • Coulomb's law: The electrostatic force between two point charges q1 and q2 separated by distance r in vacuum is F = (1/4πε₀)(q1q2/r²), directed along the line joining them.
  • Electric field E: Force per unit positive test charge at a point. E = F/q₀. Unit N/C or V/m. It is a vector quantity.
  • Electric flux Φ_E: The dot product integral of E over a surface, Φ_E = ∮ E · dA. Unit N·m²/C or V·m.
  • Gauss's law: The net electric flux through any closed surface equals the enclosed charge divided by ε₀. ∮ E · dA = Q_enc/ε₀.
  • Electric dipole: Two equal and opposite charges ±q separated by distance 2a. Dipole moment p = q·(2a), directed from − to +.
  • Electric potential V: Work done per unit positive charge in bringing it from infinity to a point against the electric field. V = W/q₀. Unit volt (V). Scalar quantity.
  • Potential energy of a system: U = qV for a charge in a potential; for two point charges U = (1/4πε₀)(q1q2/r).
  • Conductor in electrostatic equilibrium: The electric field inside a conductor is zero; excess charge resides entirely on the outer surface; the surface is an equipotential.
  • Capacitance C: Ratio of charge on one plate to the potential difference between plates, C = Q/V. Unit farad (F). Depends only on geometry and dielectric medium.
  • Current I: Rate of flow of charge, I = dq/dt. Unit ampere (A). By convention, current flows in the direction of positive charge motion.
  • Ohm's law: For an ohmic conductor at constant temperature, V = IR. Resistance R is a property of the conductor.
  • Resistivity ρ: Intrinsic property of material, R = ρl/A. Units Ω·m. Temperature dependence: ρ = ρ₀[1 + α(T − T₀)].
  • Magnetic field B: Force per unit moving charge; defined via Lorentz force F = q(v × B). Unit tesla (T).
  • Magnetic dipole moment μ: For a current loop, μ = NI·A, direction given by right-hand rule (curled fingers in current direction, thumb gives μ direction). Unit A·m².
  • Magnetic flux Φ_B: Φ_B = ∫ B · dA. Unit weber (Wb) = T·m².
  • Faraday's law of induction: An emf is induced in a circuit when the magnetic flux through it changes; ε = −dΦ_B/dt for a single loop; for N loops ε = −N(dΦ_B/dt).
  • Lenz's law: The direction of induced emf (and hence induced current) opposes the change in flux that produces it. It is a consequence of conservation of energy.
  • Self-inductance L: Ratio of linked flux to current, L = NΦ/I. Unit henry (H). Energy stored in an inductor: U = ½LI².
  • Mutual inductance M: Emf induced in one coil due to changing current in another, |ε₂| = M|dI₁/dt|. Unit henry (H). M₁₂ = M₂₁.
  • Displacement current I_d: ε₀(dΦ_E/dt); added by Maxwell to make Ampere's law consistent with time-varying fields. Has same unit as conduction current.

Key concepts

  • Electric field of continuous charge distributions (superposition principle):
  • • Line charge λ: E = λ/(2πε₀r) radially outward (infinite wire)
  • • Infinite plane sheet σ: E = σ/(2ε₀) (uniform, independent of distance)
  • • Spherical shell radius R, total charge Q:
  • - Outside (r ≥ R): E = Q/(4πε₀r²), same as point charge
  • - Inside (r < R): E = 0
  • • Solid uniformly charged sphere: Outside same as point charge; Inside E = Qr/(4πε₀R³) (linear in r)
  • Dipole field (on-axis at distance x >> a): E = (1/4πε₀)(2p/x³); (equatorial): E = (1/4πε₀)(p/x³) opposite to p direction. General torque τ = p × E.
  • Electric potential due to point charge: V = q/(4πε₀r). Potential due to dipole (far field): V = (1/4πε₀)(p cos θ/r²). Equipotential surfaces are perpendicular to field lines everywhere.
  • Relation between E and V: E = −dV/dr (in one dimension); more generally E = −∇V. Field points in the direction of steepest decrease of potential.
  • Gauss's law application strategy: Choose a Gaussian surface exploiting symmetry (spherical, cylindrical, planar) so that E is either constant in magnitude and normal to the surface or zero on parts of the surface. Common symmetric configurations: point charge, infinite line, infinite plane, infinite cylinder, spherical shell/solid sphere.
  • Capacitors in series and parallel: Series — 1/C_eq = Σ1/C_i, charge same on each; Parallel — C_eq = ΣC_i, voltage same across each.
  • Parallel plate capacitor (no dielectric): C = ε₀A/d. With dielectric of constant K filling the gap: C = Kε₀A/d. If partially filled (slab of thickness t), effective capacitance C = ε₀A/(d − t + t/K). Energy stored: U = ½CV² = Q²/(2C) = ½QV.
  • Energy density of electric field: u = ½ε₀E² (in vacuum). In dielectric: u = ½εE² = ½Kε₀E².
  • Current, drift velocity and mobility: I = nAev_d. Drift velocity v_d = eEτ/m = μE, where μ is electron mobility (μ = v_d/E) and τ is relaxation time. Current density J = nqv_d = σE, where σ = 1/ρ is conductivity.
  • Electrical power and energy: P = VI = I²R = V²/R. Electrical energy in time t: H = I²Rt (Joule heating). Power supplied by a cell of emf ε and internal resistance r delivering current I: P_source = εI, power dissipated internally = I²r.
  • Kirchhoff's laws: Junction rule (ΣI_in = ΣI_out) follows from charge conservation. Loop rule (ΣΔV = 0 around a closed loop) follows from conservative nature of electrostatic field. Sign convention: traverse loop; potential rises across cell (+ε when going from − to + terminal); drops across resistor (−IR in direction of current).
  • Potentiometer principle: V ∝ l (length of wire) when uniform wire carries steady current. Used to compare emfs: ε₁/ε₂ = l₁/l₂ (null-point lengths). Also measures internal resistance: r = R(l₁ − l₂)/l₂.
  • Lorentz force on moving charge: F = q(E + v × B). In pure magnetic field, magnetic force does no work (always perpendicular to v). Charged particle in uniform B performs circular motion with radius r = mv/(qB) and cyclotron frequency f = qB/(2πm).
  • Biot–Savart law: dB = (μ₀/4π)(I dl × r̂)/r². For infinite straight wire: B = μ₀I/(2πr). For circular arc subtending angle θ at centre: B = (μ₀Iθ)/(4πR). On axis of circular loop radius R at distance x: B = μ₀IR²/(2(R²+x²)^(3/2)). At centre of loop (x = 0): B = μ₀I/(2R).
  • Ampere's circuital law: ∮ B · dl = μ₀I_enc (for steady currents). Apply to highly symmetric current distributions: infinite straight wire, infinite solenoid (B = μ₀nI inside, zero outside ideal), toroid (B = μ₀NI/(2πr) inside), infinite current sheet (B = μ₀K/2 on each side).
  • Force between parallel current-carrying conductors: F/L = μ₀I₁I₂/(2πd). Parallel currents attract; antiparallel repel. Definition of ampere relies on this force.
  • Force/torque on a current loop in uniform B: Net force is zero but torque τ = μ × B = NIAB sinθ. This is the working principle of a moving-coil galvanometer.
  • Motional emf: When a conductor of length l moves with velocity v perpendicular to B, ε = Blv. General form ε = ∫(v × B) · dl. Example: rod sliding on rails in B field — current I = Blv/R (if total resistance R), retarding force F = BIl opposing motion.
  • Self-induction: Changing current in a coil induces emf in itself: ε = −L(dI/dt). Build-up in RL circuit (series): I(t) = (ε/R)(1 − e^(−t/τ)) with τ = L/R. Decay: I(t) = I₀e^(−t/τ). Energy dissipated in resistor during decay equals initial stored energy ½LI₀².
  • Mutual induction: ε₂ = −M(dI₁/dt). Coefficient M depends on geometry, number of turns, separation, and medium. Maximum coupling when coils are coaxial and closely placed.
  • AC basics and impedance: For sinusoidal voltage V = V₀ sin ωt applied to a circuit element:
  • • Resistor: V_R and I_R are in phase. Impedance Z = R.
  • • Inductor: I_L lags V_L by π/2. Inductive reactance X_L = ωL. Z = X_L.
  • • Capacitor: I_C leads V_C by π/2. Capacitive reactance X_C = 1/(ωC). Z = X_C.
  • LCR series circuit: Impedance Z = √(R² + (X_L − X_C)²). Resonance when X_L = X_C → ω₀ = 1/√(LC). At resonance, Z is minimum (= R), current is maximum, and circuit is purely resistive (phase angle φ = 0). Quality factor Q = ω₀L/R = 1/(ω₀CR) = (1/R)√(L/C).
  • Power in AC circuits: Average power P_avg = V_rms I_rms cosφ, where φ is phase angle between V and I. Power factor = cosφ. At resonance φ = 0, power factor = 1 (maximum power transfer). Wattless current occurs when cosφ = 0 (purely inductive or capacitive circuit); average power consumed is zero.
  • Transformers: Based on mutual induction. V_s/V_p = N_s/N_p = I_p/I_s (ideal, no losses). Step-up transformer increases voltage; step-down decreases. Since V increases, I decreases proportionally, conserving power (ideal). Frequency remains unchanged.

Important facts

  • ε₀ = 8.854 × 10⁻¹² C²/(N·m²); μ₀ = 4π × 10⁻⁷ T·m/A; 1/(4πε₀) = 9 × 10⁹ N·m²/C²; c = 1/√(μ₀ε₀) ≈ 3 × 10⁸ m/s.
  • Dielectric constant K (relative permittivity ε_r) of a material: K ≥ 1 always. For vacuum K = 1. K is dimensionless.
  • Breaking strength of dielectric = dielectric strength = maximum field before breakdown. For air at STP: ~3 × 10⁶ V/m.
  • Inside a conductor in electrostatic equilibrium, the field is zero but potential is NOT necessarily zero — it is constant throughout the conductor.
  • For a hollow charged spherical conductor, potential everywhere inside equals the surface potential V = Q/(4πε₀R); the field is zero inside.
  • Equilibrium of a dipole in a uniform electric field: stable when p is parallel to E (θ = 0); unstable when anti-parallel (θ = π). Work done rotating dipole from θ₁ to θ₂: W = pE(cos θ₂ − cos θ₁).
  • When a charged drop merges with another identical uncharged drop: final potential V_f = V_i/2^(2/3) (charge halves but volume doubles so radius increases by 2^(1/3)).
  • Gauss's law is useful for calculating E only when high symmetry exists (spherical, cylindrical, planar). For asymmetric charge distributions, use superposition or direct integration.
  • Real batteries have internal resistance r. Terminal voltage V_terminal = ε − Ir. Short-circuit current I_sc = ε/r (theoretical maximum).
  • In a potentiometer, the 'balance length' does NOT depend on the galvanometer resistance (since no current flows through it at null point), but does depend on the driver cell voltage and wire resistance per unit length.
  • Cyclotron frequency f = qB/(2πm) is independent of particle speed and orbit radius (in non-relativistic regime). This makes the cyclotron a useful particle accelerator.
  • For a solenoid of n turns per unit length carrying current I: B_inside = μ₀nI (uniform field). Field outside an infinitely long ideal solenoid is zero.
  • Toroid: B = μ₀NI/(2πr) inside the coil (r measured from centre). B = 0 outside and in the hollow region. Net magnetic moment of a toroid is zero.
  • In EMI, induced emf depends ONLY on rate of change of flux, not on the flux value itself. A large constant flux produces zero emf.
  • Direction rules to remember: Right-hand grip rule for B around a wire; Fleming's right-hand rule for induced current direction (motion → thumb, field → forefinger, induced current → middle finger); Lenz's law gives opposition direction directly.
  • In LCR resonance, voltage across L and C can EACH be much larger than the source voltage (Q times larger); they cancel in series but individually may exceed breakdown limits of components.
  • Phase relationships (mnemonic): 'ELI the ICE man' — E leads I in an Inductor (ELI); I leads E in a Capacitor (ICE).
  • Transformer cannot work on DC because constant current produces constant flux → no change in flux → no induced emf in secondary.
  • Power loss in transmission lines: P_loss = I²R_line. To minimize loss, transmit at high voltage (low current) using step-up transformers.
  • Ferroelectric materials (like BaTiO₃) have spontaneous polarization; pyroelectric materials generate surface charge on heating. Not all ferroelectrics are pyroelectric but all pyroelectrics are piezoelectric.

Memory aids

  • Coulomb vs Gravity analogy: Both follow inverse-square law; both are central forces. But gravity is always attractive and much weaker; electrostatic force can be attractive or repulsive.
  • 'Gauss needs symmetry': Memorize four go-to shapes for Gauss's law — sphere, cylinder, plane, and shell. For anything else, revert to direct integration or superposition.
  • 'Field points downhill': Electric field always points from higher to lower potential. Equipotentials are always ⊥ to field lines. Field lines never cross (otherwise two directions at one point).
  • 'RC time constant = R × C': Dimensional check — [Ω][F] = (V/A)(C/V) = C/A = s. In RC charging, after one τ, charge reaches (1 − 1/e) ≈ 63% of max. After 5τ ≈ 99.3%, considered fully charged.
  • 'LR time constant = L/R': Dimensional check — [H]/[Ω] = (V·s/A)/(V/A) = s. After one τ, current reaches (1 − 1/e) ≈ 63% of final steady value.
  • 'RLC series resonance': Remember the resonance condition X_L = X_C → ω₀L = 1/(ω₀C) → ω₀ = 1/√(LC). At this point the circuit behaves as a pure resistor.
  • 'Transformers conserve power (ideally)': V_pI_p = V_sI_s. High voltage means low current, which minimizes I²R losses in transmission.
  • 'Right-hand thumb rule for B': Thumb → current direction, curled fingers → direction of magnetic field circles around the wire.
  • 'Lenz's law is energy conservation in disguise': If induced current reinforced the change instead of opposing it, you would get energy from nothing — perpetual motion.
  • 'Motional emf formula ε = Blv': Dimensional check — [T][m][m/s] = (N/(A·m))[m²/s] = (N·m)/(A·s) = J/C = V. Works when B, l, and v are mutually perpendicular.

Exam tips

  • JEE Advanced frequently combines electrostatics with mechanics: charged particle projective motion in uniform E field, oscillation of a charged pendulum, or finding equilibrium positions using force balance. Practice multi-concept numericals.
  • Assertion–reason questions on Lenz's law and conservation of energy are common: the induced effect ALWAYS opposes the cause; if it aided the cause, energy would be created — violating conservation.
  • In Gauss's law numericals, first identify the symmetry before writing the integral. For a point charge off-centre inside a sphere, the flux through the sphere still equals q_enc/ε₀ regardless of position — this is a favourite trick question.
  • For circuits with capacitors and resistors in DC steady state: treat capacitors as open circuits (no current through them) and inductors as short circuits (zero voltage across them). Transient analysis requires solving differential equations with time constants τ = RC or L/R.
  • In AC LCR resonance questions, watch for 'voltages across individual elements' being asked: V_L and V_C can individually exceed the supply voltage. The QUESTION may ask which statement is correct among options about individual voltages versus source voltage.
  • Transformer questions often combine power loss in transmission lines with ideal transformer relations: calculate power loss at low voltage, then show improvement when stepping up voltage. Keep efficiency η = P_out/P_in in mind.
  • Induction problems involving moving rods on rails: draw a clear free-body diagram showing the magnetic force opposing the motion (Lenz's law). Terminal velocity is reached when magnetic force balances external driving force: v_term = F_ext·R/(B²l²).
  • For multi-correct MCQs in Electricity & Magnetism, carefully check each option independently. Questions on superposition of fields (multiple wires, multiple charges) commonly have two or three correct statements.
  • Numerical answer type questions in this chapter: practice computing with SI units only. Convert cm to m, μF to F, mT to T, etc. Watch for powers of 10 errors. Answers are typically given to 2 decimal places or as integers.
  • Memorize the standard field and potential results for frequently recurring geometries: point charge, infinite line, infinite plane, spherical shell, solid sphere, circular loop (axis and centre), solenoid (inside), toroid. These save time in both direct and indirect application problems.

Common traps

  • Confusing electric potential V with electric field E: V can be zero where E is non-zero (e.g., midpoint on equatorial line of dipole, V = 0 but E ≠ 0). Conversely, E can be zero where V is non-zero (inside a charged spherical shell, E = 0 but V ≠ 0).
  • Assuming Gauss's law gives the field everywhere on a Gaussian surface: Gauss's law gives total flux; to extract E at a point you need the surface chosen so that E · dA simplifies (E constant and perpendicular to surface on the relevant portion).
  • Using V = IR for non-ohmic devices (diodes, filaments): Ohm's law is an approximation valid only for ohmic conductors at constant temperature.
  • Forgetting sign convention in Kirchhoff's loop rule: If you traverse a resistor in the same direction as assumed current, the drop is −IR; if opposite, +IR. Consistency matters — an arbitrary choice is fine as long as you're consistent throughout the loop.
  • Mixing up self-inductance and mutual inductance: Self-inductance relates flux linkage to the current in THE SAME coil; mutual inductance relates flux linkage in one coil to current in ANOTHER coil.
  • Thinking motional emf requires a complete circuit: The emf is generated in the moving conductor regardless. Current flows only if a closed path exists, but the potential difference exists even across an open rod.
  • Applying LC resonance formulas to LR or CR circuits: Resonance (sharp current maximum) occurs ONLY in LCR circuits (or LC circuits). Pure RL or RC circuits have no resonance.
  • Confusing RMS and average values in AC: For a sinusoid, V_rms = V₀/√2 but V_avg over a full cycle is zero. Average power uses V_rms and I_rms, NOT peak values.
  • Treating a toroid like a solenoid: Inside a solenoid, B is uniform and ≈ μ₀nI. Inside a toroid, B varies as 1/r and is only approximately uniform if the cross-section is small compared to the mean radius.
  • Assuming internal resistance of ammeter is zero and voltmeter is infinite: Real ammeters have small but non-zero resistance (shunt); real voltmeters have large but finite resistance (multiplier). In precise JEE problems, instrument resistances matter.
  • Wrong radius in cyclotron formula: r = mv/(qB) — momentum appears, not kinetic energy directly. For relativistic particles, use p = γmv and the frequency is no longer constant (cyclotron becomes invalid).
  • Energy stored in capacitor with dielectric: If battery remains connected while dielectric is inserted, V stays constant but C increases, so U = ½CV² increases — the extra energy comes from the battery AND mechanical work done inserting the dielectric. If battery is disconnected, Q is constant and U decreases (mechanical work is done BY the system).
