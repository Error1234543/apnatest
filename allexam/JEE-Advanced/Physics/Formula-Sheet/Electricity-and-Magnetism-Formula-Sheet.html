Electricity and Magnetism — Formula Sheet (JEE Advanced)

JEE AdvancedEnglishFormula Sheet
F = (1/4π∈₀) · (q₁q₂/r²)

Symbols: F = electrostatic force between two point charges; q₁, q₂ = magnitudes of charges; r = distance between them; ∈₀ = permittivity of free space = 8.854 × 10⁻¹² C²/N·m²

Unit: Newton (N)

When to use: Force between two stationary point charges in vacuum or air

Conditions: Charges must be point charges or spherically symmetric charge distributions; valid in vacuum/air unless a dielectric constant K is introduced

Common mistake: Forgetting that the force is attractive for opposite charges and repulsive for like charges; using diameter instead of distance r

F_med = F_vac / K = (1/4π∈) · (q₁q₂/r²), where ∈ = K∈₀

Symbols: K = dielectric constant (relative permittivity) of the medium; ∈ = absolute permittivity

Unit: Newton (N)

When to use: Electrostatic force between charges when a dielectric medium fills the space between them

Conditions: Dielectric must completely fill the space; for partial filling use equivalent capacitance or separation arguments

Common mistake: Assuming K > 1 always reduces force — it does, but students often forget to replace ∈₀ with K∈₀ in Coulomb's law

E = F/q₀ = (1/4π∈₀) · (Q/r²) · r̂

Symbols: E = electric field intensity; q₀ = test charge; Q = source charge; r̂ = unit vector from source to point

Unit: N/C or V/m

When to use: Electric field due to a point charge at distance r

Conditions: Valid only outside or at the surface of a spherically symmetric charge distribution; inside a uniformly charged sphere E ∝ r

Common mistake: Direction error — E points away from positive charge, toward negative charge; confusing source charge Q with test charge q₀

E_line = λ/(2π∈₀r) (infinite line charge)

Symbols: λ = linear charge density (C/m); σ = surface charge density (C/m²); r = perpendicular distance from line

Unit: N/C or V/m

When to use: Field due to infinite line charge and infinite charged plane (both standard JEE results)

Conditions: Infinite extent assumed; for finite sheets/lines use integration

Common mistake: Using σ/∈₀ instead of σ/(2∈₀) for a single plane — the factor 2 comes from one-sided flux; confusing field between two oppositely charged plates (σ/∈₀) with single-plate field

E_on_axis_ring = (1/4π∈₀) · (Qx/(x²+R²)^(3/2))

Symbols: Q = total charge on ring; R = radius of ring; x = distance from center along axis

Unit: N/C or V/m

When to use: Electric field on the axis of a uniformly charged ring

Conditions: Point must lie on the central axis; at center (x=0), E = 0

Common mistake: Forgetting the (x²+R²)^(3/2) denominator; confusing this with field on axis of disc or solid sphere

dV = -E·dr → V = -(1/4π∈₀)∫(E·dr) from ref. to point

Symbols: V = electric potential (scalar); E = electric field; r = distance from source

Unit: Volt (V) or J/C

When to use: Potential due to a point charge; general relation between E and V

Conditions: Potential taken zero at infinity for isolated charges; superposition applies for multiple charges

Common mistake: Sign errors in V = -∫E·dr; treating potential as vector; confusing V (potential) with U (potential energy = qV)

V_on_axis_ring = (1/4π∈₀) · (Q/√(x²+R²))

Symbols: Same as above; V is scalar so no directional cancellation

Unit: Volt (V)

When to use: Potential on axis of charged ring; derive E by differentiating V with respect to x

Conditions: x measured from center along axis

Common mistake: Differentiating incorrectly — forgetting chain rule on (x²+R²)^(-1/2)

Φ_E = ∮ E·dA = Q_enclosed/∈₀

Symbols: Φ_E = electric flux through closed surface; Q_enclosed = net charge inside Gaussian surface

Unit: N·m²/C or V·m

When to use: Gauss's law — calculate E for highly symmetric charge distributions (sphere, cylinder, plane)

Conditions: Only useful when symmetry allows E to be factored out of the integral; valid in vacuum

Common mistake: Including external charges in Q_enclosed; applying to asymmetric surfaces without justification; forgetting ∈₀ in denominator

U = qV = (1/4π∈₀)(qQ/r) (point charges)

Symbols: U = electrostatic potential energy; q, Q = charges; r = separation

Unit: Joule (J)

When to use: Energy of a charge configuration; work needed to assemble charges

Conditions: Reference at infinity; only valid for static charges

Common mistake: Double-counting pairs; sign errors when charges have opposite signs (U < 0 means bound system)

W = ΔU = q(V_B - V_A) = qΔV (work to move charge q from A to B)

Symbols: W = work done by external agent; ΔV = potential difference

Unit: Joule (J)

When to use: Work done moving a charge through a potential difference

Conditions: Charge moved slowly (no kinetic energy change); conservative field

Common mistake: Confusing work done BY field (−qΔV) with work done BY external agent (+qΔV)

C = Q/V = ∈₀A/d (parallel plate capacitor, vacuum)

Symbols: C = capacitance (F); Q = charge on each plate; V = potential difference; A = plate area; d = separation; K = dielectric constant

Unit: Farad (F) = C/V

When to use: Capacitance of parallel plate capacitor; storing charge

Conditions: d << √A (neglect edge effects); K = 1 for vacuum/air

Common mistake: Using total charge (Q₁+Q₂) instead of magnitude Q on one plate; forgetting K multiplies C not divides

Series: 1/C_eq = 1/C₁ + 1/C₂ + ... ; Parallel: C_eq = C₁ + C₂ + ...

Symbols: C_eq = equivalent capacitance of combination

Unit: Farad (F)

When to use: Combining capacitors in series or parallel networks

Conditions: Series: same charge on each; Parallel: same voltage across each

Common mistake: Reversing series/parallel formulas (inverse of resistors); in series with dielectric insertion, capacitances add differently

U_cap = ½CV² = Q²/(2C) = ½QV

Symbols: U_cap = stored energy; u_E = energy per unit volume; E = electric field between plates

Unit: Joule (J); u_E in J/m³

When to use: Energy stored in a capacitor; field energy density

Conditions: Capacitor charged to potential V or charge Q; u_E valid anywhere E exists

Common mistake: Using U = QV instead of ½QV; forgetting the ½ factor

I = dQ/dt = nAv_d e (current from drift velocity)

Symbols: I = current; n = charge carrier density; A = cross-sectional area; v_d = drift velocity; e = electron charge; J = current density

Unit: Ampere (A) = C/s; J in A/m²

When to use: Relating macroscopic current to microscopic drift motion

Conditions: Steady current in a conductor with uniform cross-section

Common mistake: Using total number of electrons instead of number density n; confusing drift velocity with thermal velocity (~10⁶ m/s vs ~10⁻⁴ m/s)

v_d = eEdτ/m = eE/(m·n_coll_rate) = μE, μ = eτ/m

Symbols: v_d = drift velocity; τ = relaxation time (mean free time); μ = mobility (m²/V·s); R = resistance; ρ = resistivity; L = length; A = area

Unit: v_d in m/s; μ in m²/V·s; R in Ω; ρ in Ω·m

When to use: Drift velocity, mobility, and resistance in terms of microscopic parameters

Conditions: Ohmic material; constant τ at fixed temperature

Common mistake: Thinking τ increases with temperature — actually τ decreases as T rises (ρ increases); confusing conductivity σ = 1/ρ with conductance G = 1/R

V = IR (Ohm's law)

Symbols: V = potential difference across resistor; I = current through it; R = resistance; P = power

Unit: V in Volt; I in A; R in Ω; P in Watt (W)

When to use: Fundamental circuit relations for ohmic conductors

Conditions: Constant temperature; ohmic device (V-I graph is linear)

Common mistake: Applying V=IR to non-ohmic devices like diodes; using P = VI with wrong V or I (must be same element)

Resistivity temperature dependence: ρ = ρ₀(1 + αΔT)

Symbols: ρ₀, R₀ = values at reference temperature; α = temperature coefficient of resistivity/resistance; ΔT = temperature change

Unit: α in °C⁻¹ or K⁻¹

When to use: How resistance changes with temperature for metals (α > 0) and semiconductors (α < 0)

Conditions: Small ΔT range where α is approximately constant

Common mistake: Using same α for all materials; confusing α (positive for metals) with negative α for semiconductors/insulators

EMF: ε = ∮ (F_non-elec/q)·dl = W_non-elec/Q

Symbols: ε = emf of cell; r = internal resistance; I = current; V = terminal potential difference

Unit: ε and V in Volt; r in Ω; I in A

When to use: Cell behavior under load; charging vs discharging

Conditions: Constant current during discharge/charge; ir drop represents energy lost as heat inside cell

Common mistake: Forgetting sign change between charging and discharging; treating ε as always equal to terminal voltage

Cells in series: ε_eq = ε₁ + ε₂ + ..., r_eq = r₁ + r₂ + ...

Symbols: n = number of identical cells in parallel

Unit: ε in V; r in Ω

When to use: Combining cells (batteries) in series and parallel

Conditions: Parallel combination requires same emf; otherwise circulating currents arise

Common mistake: Connecting cells of different emf in parallel without calculating circulation current; maximum current condition: R = nr for n identical cells in series-parallel

Junction rule (KCL): Σ I_in = Σ I_out at any node

Symbols: KCL = Kirchhoff's Current Law; KVL = Kirchhoff's Voltage Law

Unit: Dimensionless equation

When to use: Solving complex circuits that cannot be reduced by simple series/parallel combinations

Conditions: Conservation of charge (KCL); conservation of energy (KVL); valid for lumped parameter circuits

Common mistake: Sign convention errors in KVL — must consistently take + when going from - to + terminal, - when + to -; assigning arbitrary current directions and being consistent

Wheatstone bridge balance: R₁/R₂ = R₃/R₄ (no current through galvanometer branch)

Symbols: R, S = unknown and known resistances; l = length from one end of 1m wire

Unit: l in cm; R, S in Ω

When to use: Measuring unknown resistance; null detection principle

Conditions: Bridge must be balanced (galvanometer shows zero deflection)

Common mistake: Confusing which arm is which in metre bridge formula; swapping l and (100-l); not checking if bridge is balanced before applying formula

Potentiometer: V_proportional_to_l, ε₁/ε₂ = l₁/l₂ (null deflection)

Symbols: ε₁, ε₂ = emfs of cells; l₁, l₂ = balancing lengths; R = shunt resistance; r = internal resistance

Unit: Length in cm or m; R, r in Ω

When to use: Comparing emfs; measuring internal resistance of a cell; potentiometer preferred over voltmeter because it draws no current at null

Conditions: Driver cell emf must exceed cell emf being measured; first balance point must be obtained

Common mistake: Using voltmeter reading instead of balancing length; not ensuring driver cell voltage > test cell emf; reversed polarity gives no null point

Biot-Savart Law: dB = (μ₀/4π) · (Idl×r̂)/r²

Symbols: dB = magnetic field element; I = current; dl = length element vector; r = distance from element to point; μ₀ = 4π×10⁻⁷ T·m/A; R = loop radius; x = axial distance

Unit: B in Tesla (T); μ₀ in T·m/A

When to use: Magnetic field due to any current element; standard results for loops and wires

Conditions: Steady current; vacuum/air medium

Common mistake: Forgetting the cross product direction (right-hand rule); using diameter instead of radius; sign errors in axial field formula

Long straight wire: B = μ₀I/(2πr)

Symbols: r = perpendicular distance from wire; k = current per unit width

Unit: B in Tesla (T)

When to use: Magnetic field near long straight conductor and current sheet

Conditions: Infinitely long wire assumed; field circles the wire (right-hand thumb rule)

Common mistake: Using μ₀I/(4πr) instead of μ₀I/(2πr) — factor of 2 error; confusing direction given by right-hand rule

Ampere's Circuital Law: ∮ B·dl = μ₀I_enclosed

Symbols: n = N/L turns per meter; I = current through solenoid

Unit: B in Tesla; μ₀ = 4π×10⁻⁷ T·m/A

When to use: Finding B for highly symmetric current distributions; toroid, solenoid, coaxial cable

Conditions: Amperian loop chosen along symmetry; outside ideal solenoid B ≈ 0; toroid: B = μ₀NI/(2πr) inside, 0 outside

Common mistake: Applying Ampere's law without symmetry; including current outside the Amperian loop; forgetting that n must be in m⁻¹ for SI units

Force on moving charge: F = q(v × B), magnitude = qvBsinθ

Symbols: q = charge; v = velocity; B = magnetic field; θ = angle between v and B; I = current; l = length vector (direction of current)

Unit: F in Newton (N)

When to use: Lorentz force; force on conductor in magnetic field

Conditions: Uniform B for simple forms; for non-uniform B, integrate

Common mistake: Forgetting sinθ factor (force zero when v ∥ B, max when ⊥); using dot product instead of cross product; right-hand rule errors for negative charges (force opposite to RH rule)

Cyclotron radius: r = mv/(qB) = p/(qB)

Symbols: m = mass of particle; v = speed perpendicular to B; p = momentum; q = charge; B = magnetic field

Unit: r in m; f in Hz; ω in rad/s

When to use: Motion of charged particle in uniform B field (circular path); cyclotron operation

Conditions: v perpendicular to B for pure circular motion; if v has parallel component, helical path with pitch = v_parallel × T

Common mistake: Forgetting that frequency is independent of speed; mixing up angular frequency ω with orbital frequency f; not resolving velocity into parallel and perpendicular components for helical motion

Force per unit length between parallel wires: F/l = μ₀I₁I₂/(2πd)

Symbols: d = separation between wires; I₁, I₂ = currents

Unit: F/l in N/m

When to use: Defining ampere; force between parallel current-carrying conductors

Conditions: Long straight parallel wires; vacuum/air

Common mistake: Wrong direction assignment (same direction attracts!); confusing with Coulomb's law form

Torque on current loop: τ = NIABsinθ, μ = NIA (magnetic dipole moment)

Symbols: N = number of turns; I = current; A = area of loop; B = magnetic field; θ = angle between normal to loop and B; μ = magnetic dipole moment

Unit: τ in N·m; μ in A·m² or J/T

When to use: Moving coil galvanometer principle; magnetic dipole in uniform field

Conditions: Uniform B field; rectangular/circular/sinusoidal loop area A

Common mistake: Taking θ as angle between plane of loop and B (should be between normal and B); forgetting N factor; confusing τ_max = NIAB with τ = 0 when μ ⊥ B (actually τ = 0 when μ ∥ B)

Galvanometer: Cθ = NIAB (C = torsion constant), I = Cθ/(NAB)

Symbols: θ = deflection angle; C = restoring torque per unit twist; N, A, B as before

Unit: Sensitivity in rad/A or div/A

When to use: Conversion of galvanometer to ammeter/voltmeter; understanding galvanometer operation

Conditions: Spring-controlled suspension; steady current

Common mistake: Confusing current sensitivity with voltage sensitivity (V/θ = NAB/(CR_g)); not accounting for shunt resistance reduction

Ammeter: R_shunt = (G·I_g)/(I - I_g), where I_g = full-scale deflection current, G = galvanometer resistance

Symbols: I = desired ammeter range; V = desired voltmeter range; I_g = galvanometer full-scale current; G = galvanometer resistance

Unit: R in Ω

When to use: Converting galvanometer to ammeter (shunt) or voltmeter (series resistor)

Conditions: Galvanometer parameters G and I_g known

Common mistake: Forgetting that ammeter must have low resistance (shunt in parallel); voltmeter must have high resistance (series resistor); sign error in R_shunt when I < I_g (impossible range)

Faraday's Law: ε = -dΦ_B/dt

Symbols: Φ_B = magnetic flux = ∫B·dA; ε = induced EMF

Unit: ε in Volt; Φ_B in Weber (Wb) = T·m²

When to use: Induced EMF in a loop due to changing magnetic flux

Conditions: Flux change can be due to changing B, changing area, or changing orientation; N turns: ε = -N(dΦ/dt)

Common mistake: Ignoring the minus sign (Lenz's law); computing flux with wrong angle (θ between B and area normal, not plane); for motional EMF ε = Blv, direction via Fleming's right-hand rule

Motional EMF: ε = Blv (conductor of length l moving perpendicular to B with speed v)

Symbols: B = magnetic field; l = length of conductor; v = velocity; θ = angle between v and B

Unit: ε in Volt

When to use: EMF induced in a moving conductor; rail-gun problems; rotating rod

Conditions: B, l, v mutually perpendicular for simplest form; conducting回路 completed

Common mistake: Using ε = BLv when B varies along length; forgetting that the conductor must be part of a closed回路 for current to flow

Self-inductance: ε = -L(dI/dt), Φ = LI

Symbols: L = self-inductance (Henry, H = Wb/A = V·s/A); I = current; Φ = flux linkage

Unit: H (Henry) = T·m²/A = V·s/A

When to use: Inductor behavior in circuits; energy storage in magnetic field

Conditions: L depends on geometry and core material; solenoid: L = μ₀n²A·l = μ₀N²A/l

Common mistake: Forgetting energy is ½LI² not LI²; confusing self-inductance with mutual inductance; using solenoid formula for toroid without adjustment

Mutual inductance: ε₂ = -M(dI₁/dt), M = N₂Φ₂₁/I₁

Symbols: M = mutual inductance (H); N₁, N₂ = turns; A = common area; l = length

Unit: H (Henry)

When to use: Transformer action; coupling between circuits

Conditions: Coupling coefficient k = M/√(L₁L₂), 0 ≤ k ≤ 1

Common mistake: Assuming M is always max (k=1); confusing primary and secondary flux linkage

LR circuit growth: I(t) = (ε/R)(1 - e^(-Rt/L)) = I₀(1 - e^(-t/τ_L))

Symbols: τ_L = L/R = time constant; I₀ = maximum/steady-state current; t = time

Unit: τ_L in seconds; I in A

When to use: Current buildup and decay in LR circuits

Conditions: Step input (switch closed/open at t=0); L and R constant

Common mistake: Writing wrong exponent sign; confusing τ_L with τ_C = RC; at t = τ_L, I = 0.632I₀ (growth) or I = 0.368I₀ (decay), not 0.5

LC oscillation: ω₀ = 1/√(LC), q(t) = q₀cos(ω₀t + φ), I(t) = -ω₀q₀sin(ω₀t + φ)

Symbols: ω₀ = natural angular frequency; L = inductance; C = capacitance; q₀ = max charge; I₀ = max current

Unit: ω₀ in rad/s; f₀ = 1/(2π√(LC)) in Hz

When to use: Oscillations in ideal LC circuit; electromagnetic oscillations analogy with spring-mass

Conditions: No resistance (ideal); initial charge on capacitor or initial current in inductor

Common mistake: Using f₀ = 1/√(LC) without 2π; confusing LC with LR time constant behavior

RLC series: Impedance Z = √(R² + (X_L - X_C)²)

Symbols: Z = impedance (Ω); X_L = inductive reactance (Ω); X_C = capacitive reactance (Ω); φ = phase angle between V and I

Unit: Z, X_L, X_C in Ω

When to use: AC circuit analysis — finding current amplitude and phase

Conditions: Sinusoidal source V = V₀sin(ωt); steady-state response

Common mistake: Adding reactances algebraically instead of vectorially; sign error in φ (positive when V leads I, i.e., inductive); resonance at X_L = X_C not at ω = 0

Resonance: ω₀ = 1/√(LC), f₀ = 1/(2π√(LC))

Symbols: Q = quality factor; ω₀ = resonant angular frequency

Unit: Q is dimensionless

When to use: Series resonance — max current, min impedance; applications in tuning circuits

Conditions: Series RLC circuit; steady-state AC driving

Common mistake: Forgetting Q is dimensionless; confusing series and parallel resonance conditions; at resonance X_L ≠ 0, they cancel each other

Average power in AC: P_avg = V_rms · I_rms · cosφ = I_rms²R

Symbols: φ = phase angle between V and I; cosφ = power factor; V_rms, I_rms = root mean square values

Unit: P_avg in Watt (W)

When to use: Real power dissipated in AC circuit; only resistive component dissipates power

Conditions: Sinusoidal voltages/currents; R is the only power-dissipating element (L and C store/return energy)

Common mistake: Using peak values instead of RMS in power formula; multiplying by total Z instead of just R; forgetting cosφ for reactive circuits

Inductor: V_L = I_L · X_L = I_L · ωL, V_L leads I_L by 90°

Symbols: X_L = ωL (inductive reactance); X_C = 1/(ωC) (capacitive reactance); phase relationships are key

Unit: Reactance in Ω

When to use: Individual element behavior in AC; building up phasor diagrams

Conditions: Sinusoidal steady state

Common mistake: Swapping lead/lag relationships; for inductor V leads I (ELI); for capacitor I leads V (ICE) — mnemonic helps

Transformer: V_s/V_p = N_s/N_p = I_p/I_s (ideal, no losses)

Symbols: V_p, V_s = primary and secondary voltages; N_p, N_s = number of turns; I_p, I_s = currents

Unit: Voltage ratio equals turn ratio; step-up: N_s > N_p; step-down: N_s < N_p

When to use: Voltage transformation in AC power transmission

Conditions: Ideal transformer (no flux leakage, no resistance, no core losses); only works with AC

Common mistake: Applying transformer equation to DC; confusing step-up (high V, low I) with power increase (power conserved); ignoring that I ratio is inverse of V ratio

Displacement current: I_d = ∈₀(dΦ_E/dt)

Symbols: I_d = displacement current; Φ_E = electric flux; ∈₀ = permittivity; μ₀ = permeability

Unit: I_d in Ampere (A)

When to use: Completing Ampere's law for capacitor charging; electromagnetic wave foundation

Conditions: Between capacitor plates where no conduction current flows; time-varying electric flux

Common mistake: Thinking displacement current is real charge flow; confusing Φ_E with Φ_B; forgetting factor ∈₀

Maxwell's equations (integral form):

Symbols: Four fundamental equations: Gauss's law for E, Gauss's law for B, Faraday's law, Ampere-Maxwell law

Unit: All SI consistent

When to use: Complete classical EM description; foundation for EM waves

Conditions: Classical regime; not quantum

Common mistake: Misremembering signs or missing displacement current term in Ampere-Maxwell

EM wave: c = 1/√(μ₀∈₀) ≈ 3×10⁸ m/s

Symbols: c = speed of light in vacuum; E₀, B₀ = amplitude of electric and magnetic fields

Unit: c in m/s; u in J/m³

When to use: Properties of electromagnetic waves; energy carried by EM waves

Conditions: Plane EM wave in vacuum; E and B perpendicular to each other and to propagation direction

Common mistake: Forgetting E₀/B₀ = c (not E₀/B₀ = 1/c); energy equally split between E and B fields; confusing peak and RMS energy density

Poynting vector: S = (1/μ₀)(E × B), magnitude S = EB/μ₀

Symbols: S = energy flow per unit area per unit time (W/m²); I = intensity (time-averaged)

Unit: S, I in W/m²

When to use: Energy transport direction and rate in EM waves; radiation pressure

Conditions: Plane wave; time-averaged for sinusoidal waves

Common mistake: Using peak S instead of average S; forgetting factor ½ in intensity for sinusoidal waves

Radiation pressure: P_rad = I/c (perfect absorption), P_rad = 2I/c (perfect reflection)

Symbols: P_rad = radiation pressure; I = intensity; c = speed of light; A = area; U = total power

Unit: P_rad in Pa (N/m²); F in N

When to use: Solar sails; light pressure effects

Conditions: Normal incidence assumed; perpendicular to surface

Common mistake: Forgetting factor of 2 for reflection; using I instead of total power U for finite beams

Electric field inside conductor (electrostatics): E = 0, V = constant throughout

Symbols: σ = surface charge density; E_normal = field just outside conductor perpendicular to surface

Unit: σ in C/m²; E in N/C

When to use: Properties of conductors in electrostatic equilibrium

Conditions: Electrostatic conditions (no current flow); charges reside on surface only

Common mistake: Thinking E inside is zero because no charge — actually because charges rearrange to cancel any internal field; E just outside is σ/∈₀ not σ/(2∈₀)

Field inside uniformly charged solid sphere (radius R): E = (1/4π∈₀)(Qr/R³) for r < R

Symbols: r = distance from center; Q = total charge; R = sphere/shell radius

Unit: E in N/C; V in V

When to use: Standard Gauss's law applications for JEE

Conditions: Uniform volume/surface charge distribution; spherical symmetry

Common mistake: Using point charge formula inside sphere; confusing V inside shell (constant, non-zero) with E inside shell (zero)

Dipole: p = qd (magnitude), τ = p × B (in uniform B field)

Symbols: p = dipole moment (C·m); d = separation between ±q; τ = torque; E = field

Unit: p in C·m; E in N/C

When to use: Electric dipole field and torque in uniform field; magnetic dipole analogies

Conditions: Far-field approximation (r >> d); uniform B for torque formula

Common mistake: Using 2p/r³ on equator instead of p/r³; confusing electric dipole with magnetic dipole formulas; sign conventions in τ = p×B

RC circuit charging: q(t) = CV(1 - e^(-t/RC))), I(t) = (V/R)e^(-t/RC))

Symbols: τ_C = RC = time constant; V = battery emf; q₀ = CV = max charge

Unit: τ_C in seconds

When to use: Charging and discharging of capacitor through resistor

Conditions: Simple series RC circuit with step input

Common mistake: Wrong sign in exponent; confusing τ_C = RC with τ_L = L/R; at t=τ_C, q = 0.632CV not 0.5CV

Energy in LR circuit growth: I = I₀(1 - e^(-t/τ_L)), Energy in inductor: U_L = ½LI²

Symbols: U_L = magnetic energy stored; I₀ = ε/R = steady-state current; τ_L = L/R

Unit: U_L in J; dU/dt in W

When to use: Energy bookkeeping in LR circuit — battery supplies, inductor stores, resistor dissipates

Conditions: Series LR with DC source switched on at t=0

Common mistake: Not applying energy conservation: εI = I²R + d(½LI²)/dt; forgetting that at t=0, inductor acts as open circuit (I=0)
