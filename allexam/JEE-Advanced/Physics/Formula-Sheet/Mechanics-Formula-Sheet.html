v = u + at

Symbols: v = final velocity, u = initial velocity, a = acceleration, t = time

Unit: v, u: m/s; a: m/s²; t: s

When to use: First equation of motion for constant acceleration in one dimension

Conditions: Acceleration must be constant; valid only along a straight line

Common mistake: Applying this with variable acceleration or treating it as vector equation without components

s = ut + ½at²

Symbols: s = displacement, u = initial velocity, a = acceleration, t = time

Unit: s: m; u: m/s; a: m/s²; t: s

When to use: Second equation of motion — gives displacement under constant acceleration

Conditions: Constant acceleration; origin at starting point if s is measured from rest position

Common mistake: Confusing displacement s with distance traveled (they differ when direction changes)

v² = u² + 2as

Symbols: v = final velocity, u = initial velocity, a = acceleration, s = displacement

Unit: All SI units as above

When to use: Third equation of motion — relates velocities to displacement without time

Conditions: Constant acceleration; independent of time

Common mistake: Forgetting that v² gives two possible signs for v (direction ambiguity)

s_n = u + a/2(2n - 1)

Symbols: s_n = distance traveled in nth second, u = initial velocity, a = acceleration, n = second number

Unit: s_n: m

When to use: Distance covered specifically during the nth second of motion

Conditions: Constant acceleration only

Common mistake: Using this for non-constant acceleration or confusing s_n with total displacement after n seconds

x(t) = x₀ + v₀t cos α, y(t) = y₀ + v₀t sin α - ½gt²

Symbols: x, y = position coordinates, x₀, y₀ = initial positions, v₀ = initial speed, α = projection angle, g = gravitational acceleration

Unit: x, y: m; v₀: m/s; g: m/s²; t: s

When to use: Parametric equations of projectile motion in a vertical plane

Conditions: No air resistance; uniform gravity g acting downward; flat horizontal ground assumed unless stated otherwise

Common mistake: Using angle with horizontal incorrectly; forgetting y becomes negative below launch level

R = u² sin 2α / g

Symbols: R = horizontal range, u = initial speed, α = angle of projection with horizontal, g = acceleration due to gravity

Unit: R: m; u: m/s; g: m/s²

When to use: Horizontal range of a projectile on level ground

Conditions: Level ground; no air resistance; launch and landing at same height

Common mistake: Assuming max range always at 45° even on inclined planes — on incline θ, optimum angle is π/4 - θ/2

H = u² sin² α / (2g)

Symbols: H = maximum height, u = initial speed, α = angle of projection, g = acceleration due to gravity

Unit: H: m

When to use: Maximum vertical height reached by a projectile

Conditions: Level ground; no air resistance

Common mistake: Using full speed u instead of vertical component u sin α

T = 2u sin α / g

Symbols: T = time of flight, u = initial speed, α = projection angle, g = gravitational acceleration

Unit: T: s

When to use: Total time the projectile remains in the air (level ground)

Conditions: Launch and landing at same height; no air resistance

Common mistake: Using u instead of u sin α for vertical component

y = x tan α - gx² / (2u² cos² α)

Symbols: y = vertical position, x = horizontal position, α = projection angle, u = initial speed, g = gravity

Unit: y, x: m

When to use: Trajectory equation of a projectile — shows path is parabolic

Conditions: No air resistance; uniform gravity

Common mistake: Not recognizing this as a parabola y = ax - bx² form; sign errors with g

v = ω × r

Symbols: v = linear/tangential velocity, ω = angular velocity vector, r = position vector from axis

Unit: v: m/s; ω: rad/s; r: m

When to use: Relation between linear and angular velocity in rotational/circular motion

Conditions: ω perpendicular to r for scalar form v = ωr; valid for rigid body rotation

Common mistake: Treating v = ωr as always true without checking perpendicularity; forgetting cross-product direction

a_c = v²/r = ω²r

Symbols: a_c = centripetal acceleration, v = tangential speed, ω = angular speed, r = radius of curvature

Unit: a_c: m/s²

When to use: Centripetal (radial) acceleration in uniform circular motion

Conditions: Instantaneous radius of curvature r; for uniform circular motion a_tangential = 0

Common mistake: Calling it 'centrifugal force' as a real force in inertial frames; confusing with tangential acceleration

a_total = √(a_c² + a_t²)

Symbols: a_total = net acceleration, a_c = centripetal acceleration, a_t = tangential acceleration

Unit: m/s²

When to use: Net acceleration in non-uniform circular motion where both radial and tangential components exist

Conditions: Both a_c and a_t present; they are perpendicular to each other

Common mistake: Adding a_c and a_t algebraically instead of vectorially (they are perpendicular)

F_net = ma

Symbols: F_net = net external force, m = mass, a = acceleration

Unit: F: N (kg·m/s²); m: kg; a: m/s²

When to use: Newton's Second Law — fundamental equation of dynamics

Conditions: Inertial reference frame; mass constant (non-relativistic)

Common mistake: Using F = ma with only one force instead of NET force; applying in non-inertial frames without pseudo force

f ≤ μN

Symbols: f = friction force, μ = coefficient of friction, N = normal reaction

Unit: f, N: N

When to use: Friction force magnitude (static friction self-adjusts up to limiting value)

Conditions: Static: f_s ≤ μ_s N (self-adjusting); Kinetic: f_k = μ_k N (constant)

Common mistake: Writing f = μN always — static friction equals applied force up to limit, not necessarily μN

μ_s ≥ tan θ (for block on inclined plane just about to slip)

Symbols: μ_s = coefficient of static friction, θ = angle of inclination

Unit: Dimensionless

When to use: Angle of repose relation — minimum μ_s needed to prevent slipping on incline

Conditions: Block on verge of sliding down rough incline

Common mistake: Confusing angle of repose with angle of friction; using kinetic μ instead of static μ_s

W = F·s = Fs cos θ

Symbols: W = work done, F = force magnitude, s = displacement magnitude, θ = angle between F and s

Unit: W: J (joule = N·m)

When to use: Work done by a constant force

Conditions: Constant force; for variable force use integral form

Common mistake: Taking θ as angle with vertical instead of angle between F and displacement vectors

W = ∫ F·dr

Symbols: W = work done by variable force, F = force vector, dr = displacement element

Unit: W: J

When to use: Work done by a variable force along a path

Conditions: Force may vary in magnitude and/or direction along the path

Common mistake: Forgetting the dot product; integrating magnitude only without considering direction

W_spring = ½k(x_i² - x_f²)

Symbols: W_spring = work done by spring force, k = spring constant, x_i = initial extension/compression, x_f = final extension/compression

Unit: W: J; k: N/m; x: m

When to use: Work done by spring force during extension/compression change

Conditions: Spring obeys Hooke's law F = -kx; x measured from natural length

Common mistake: Sign error — work by spring is positive when spring returns toward natural length; confusing with work done AGAINST spring

K = ½mv²

Symbols: K = kinetic energy, m = mass, v = speed

Unit: K: J

When to use: Kinetic energy of a translating body

Conditions: Non-relativistic speeds (v << c)

Common mistake: Using velocity instead of speed (KE depends on v², direction irrelevant)

U_grav = -GMm/r

Symbols: U_grav = gravitational potential energy, G = universal gravitational constant, M, m = masses, r = separation

Unit: U: J; G: N·m²/kg²; M, m: kg; r: m

When to use: Gravitational PE between two point masses (zero at infinity)

Conditions: Point masses or spherical symmetry; reference U = 0 at r → ∞

Common mistake: Using mgh everywhere — mgh is approximation near Earth's surface only; forgetting negative sign

U = mgh (near Earth's surface)

Symbols: U = gravitational PE, m = mass, g = local gravitational acceleration, h = height above reference

Unit: U: J

U_spring = ½kx²

Unit: P: W (watt = J/s)

KE_f - KE_i = W_net

Unit: Joules

When to use: Work-Energy Theorem — relates net work to change in kinetic energy

Conditions: Valid in inertial frames; applies to particles and rigid bodies (with rotational KE included appropriately)
