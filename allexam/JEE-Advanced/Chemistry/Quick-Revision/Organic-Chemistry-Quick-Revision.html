Organic Chemistry - JEE Advanced Quick Revision

JEE AdvancedEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Organic Chemistry: Branch of chemistry dealing with carbon compounds, their structure, properties, reactions, and synthesis.
  • Homolytic Fission: Equal breaking of covalent bond producing free radicals (neutral species with unpaired electron).
  • Heterolytic Fission: Unequal bond breaking producing ions (carbocation or carbanion).
  • Carbocation: Positively charged carbon species (sp2 hybridized, planar), stability order: tertiary > secondary > primary > methyl.
  • Carbanion: Negatively charged carbon species (sp3 hybridized, pyramidal), stability order opposite to carbocation.
  • Free Radical: Neutral species with unpaired electron, highly reactive intermediate.
  • Inductive Effect: Permanent displacement of sigma electrons along carbon chain due to electronegativity difference; falls off with distance.
  • Resonance Effect: Delocalization of pi electrons or lone pairs across adjacent p-orbitals; stabilizes molecule.
  • Hyperconjugation: Delocalization of sigma electrons of C-H bond into adjacent empty p-orbital (no-bond resonance).
  • Electrophile: Electron-deficient species (Lewis acid) that seeks electrons; examples: H+, NO2+, carbocations, AlCl3.
  • Nucleophile: Electron-rich species (Lewis base) that donates electron pair; examples: OH-, CN-, NH3, carbanions.
  • Leaving Group: Atom/group that departs with electron pair during substitution/elimination; good leaving groups are weak bases (I-, Br-, Cl-, Tosylate).
  • Reaction Intermediate: Short-lived, high-energy species formed during reaction mechanism.

Key concepts

  • NOMENCLATURE: IUPAC rules—select longest carbon chain containing principal functional group; number to give lowest locants to substituents and functional groups; suffixes: -ane, -ene, -yne, -ol, -al, -one, -oic acid, -amine, -nitrile; prefixes for halogens, nitro, alkoxy.
  • ISOMERISM: Structural (chain, position, functional, metamerism, tautomerism); Stereoisomerism includes geometrical (cis-trans/E-Z) and optical (enantiomers, diastereomers, meso compounds, R/S configuration via CIP rules). Tautomerism: keto-enol equilibrium (acetone ↔ propen-2-ol).
  • REACTIVITY OF ALKANES: Combustion, halogenation (free radical mechanism: initiation, propagation, termination). Reactivity order of halogens: F2 > Cl2 > Br2 > I2. Selectivity: I2 > Br2 > Cl2. Bond dissociation energy determines ease of H-abstraction.
  • ALKENES & ALKYNES: Electrophilic addition reactions follow Markovnikov's rule (H adds to carbon with more H) and anti-Markovnikov (peroxide effect only with HBr). Ozonolysis (reductive/oxidative) cleaves double bond. Hydroboration-oxidation gives anti-Markovnikov alcohol. Lindlar catalyst partially reduces alkyne to cis-alkene; Na/NH3(l) gives trans-alkene.
  • ARENES: Aromaticity criteria (Huckel: 4n+2 pi electrons, planar, cyclic, conjugated). Electrophilic aromatic substitution: nitration, sulfonation, halogenation, Friedel-Crafts alkylation/acylation. Directing effects: ortho/para directors (activating: -OH, -NH2, -CH3; deactivating: -Hal) vs meta directors (deactivating: -NO2, -COOH, -CHO).
  • ALKYL HALIDES: SN1 (unimolecular, carbocation intermediate, racemization, favored by polar protic solvent, tertiary substrates) vs SN2 (bimolecular, backside attack, inversion/Walden, favored by primary substrates, polar aprotic solvent). E1 and E2 eliminations compete with substitution.
  • ALCOHOLS, PHENOLS, ETHERS: Alcohol classification (1°, 2°, 3°); Lucas test distinguishes them. Oxidation: PCC stops at aldehyde/ketone; KMnO4/K2Cr2O7 goes to carboxylic acid. Phenol acidity > alcohol due to resonance stabilization of phenoxide; reacts with NaOH but not NaHCO3. Williamson ether synthesis: R-X + R'O-Na.
  • ALDEHYDES & KETONES: Nucleophilic addition reactions: HCN, NaHSO3, Grignard reagent, NH2 derivatives. Aldol condensation (enolate formation, beta-hydroxy aldehyde/ketone → alpha,beta-unsaturated carbonyl). Cannizzaro reaction (no alpha-H, disproportionation). Clemmensen reduction (Zn-Hg/HCl) and Wolff-Kishner (NH2NH2/KOH) reduce C=O to CH2.
  • CARBOXYLIC ACIDS & DERIVATIVES: Acidity affected by electron-withdrawing/donating groups. esterification (Fischer), hydrolysis of esters/amides/nitriles. Amides: Hofmann bromamide degradation gives primary amine with one carbon less. Carboxylic acids → acid chlorides (SOCl2) → anhydrides → esters → amides.
  • AMINES: Basicity order in gas phase: 3° > 2° > 1° > NH3; in aqueous phase: 2° > 1° > 3° > NH3 (due to solvation and steric). Hinsberg test distinguishes primary, secondary, tertiary amines. Diazonium salts (ArN2+) from aromatic amines + NaNO2/HCl at 0-5°C; used in coupling reactions and substitution.
  • BIOMOLECULES: Carbohydrates: monosaccharides (aldose/ketose), glycosidic linkage, starch (alpha-1,4), cellulose (beta-1,4), glycogen. Amino acids: zwitterion, isoelectric point, peptide bond. Proteins: primary, secondary (alpha-helix, beta-sheet), tertiary, quaternary structures. Enzymes are protein catalysts.
  • POLYMERS: Addition polymers: polyethylene, PVC, Teflon, polystyrene. Condensation polymers: Nylon-6,6, Bakelite, Dacron. Biodegradable polymers: PHBV, Nylon-2-nylon-6. VULCANIZATION: rubber + sulfur cross-linking.

Important facts

  • Bond strengths: C-C > C=C > C≡C in terms of bond energy per bond, but C≡C has shortest bond length.
  • Stability of carbocations: 3° > 2° > 1° > CH3+; allylic/benzylic carbocations are exceptionally stable due to resonance.
  • Acidity order: carboxylic acid > phenol > water > alcohol > terminal alkyne > ammonia > alkane.
  • Basicity order (gas phase): 3° amine > 2° > 1° > NH3; aqueous: 2° > 1° > 3° > NH3 for alkyl amines.
  • Leaving group ability: I- > Br- > Cl- > F-; Tosylate (OTs) and Mesylate (OMs) are excellent leaving groups.
  • Reactivity of carbonyl compounds toward nucleophilic addition: aldehydes > ketones (steric and electronic reasons).
  • Aromaticity: benzene (6 pi e-), cyclopentadienyl anion (6 pi e-), cycloheptatrienyl cation/tropylium (6 pi e-), pyridine (6 pi e- in ring), pyrrole (6 pi e- including lone pair).
  • Ozonolysis products: reductive (Zn/H2O) gives aldehydes/ketones; oxidative (H2O2) gives carboxylic acids.
  • Iodoform test: positive for compounds containing CH3CO- or CH3CH(OH)- group; gives yellow precipitate of CHI3.
  • Victor Meyer test: distinguishes 1°, 2°, 3° alcohols via nitrolic acid (red) for 1°, false nitrolic acid (blue) for 2°, no color for 3°.

Memory aids

  • Markovnikov's rule: 'Rich get richer'—hydrogen adds to carbon with more hydrogens already.
  • Peroxide effect: Only with HBr (not HCl/HI) due to bond energy considerations; anti-Markovnikov addition.
  • SN1 vs SN2 mnemonic: SN1 = Substitution Nucleophilic Unimolecular (one molecule, rate depends only on substrate); SN2 = bimolecular (both substrate and nucleophile).
  • EAS directing groups: o/p directors activate ring except halogens (which are o/p but deactivating); m directors deactivate ring.
  • Huckel's rule: 4n+2 where n = 0,1,2,... giving 2,6,10,14 pi electrons for aromaticity.
  • CIP rules for R/S: priority based on atomic number; lowest priority away from viewer; clockwise = R, anticlockwise = S.
  • Tautomerism shortcut: keto form has C=O, enol form has C=C-OH; equilibrium favors keto for simple ketones.

Exam tips

  • Practice named reactions and mechanisms extensively—JEE Advanced frequently asks multi-step synthesis pathways.
  • Master spectral interpretation (IR, NMR, MS) as combined questions with organic reasoning appear regularly.
  • Memorize typical reagents and their specific transformations (e.g., PCC vs KMnO4, Lindlar vs Na/NH3).
  • Pay attention to stereochemistry in reaction mechanisms—JEE Advanced tests R/S, E/Z, and optical activity concepts.
  • Be careful with aromaticity exceptions (anti-aromatic compounds like cyclobutadiene are highly reactive).
  • For numerical problems involving organic compounds, use mole concept and stoichiometry correctly with balanced equations.
  • Always check for possible rearrangements (1,2-hydride/methyl shifts) in carbocation intermediates.
  • In multi-correct MCQs, eliminate clearly wrong options first; many organic questions have one obviously incorrect statement.

Common traps

  • Assuming all electrophilic additions follow Markovnikov—peroxide effect is exception only for HBr.
  • Confusing SN1 and SN2 stereochemistry: SN1 gives racemization (partial), SN2 gives complete inversion.
  • Thinking all aromatic compounds undergo addition reactions—benzene prefers substitution to retain aromaticity.
  • Mistaking phenol acidity: phenol is acidic but weaker than carboxylic acids; does not evolve CO2 with NaHCO3.
  • Incorrectly applying aldol condensation to aldehydes/ketones without alpha-hydrogen (they undergo Cannizzaro instead).
  • Forgetting that diazonium salts are unstable above 5°C and decompose to phenols/arenium ions.
  • Assuming all amines are basic—amide nitrogens are non-basic due to resonance delocalization of lone pair.
  • Wrong choice of reducing agent: LiAlH4 reduces all carbonyls, NaBH4 reduces only aldehydes/ketones, not esters/acids.
