Statement-Conclusion — रेल्वे / RRB (Hindi)

Railway / RRBHindiNotes

कहानी और परिचय

Statement-Conclusion एक such logical reasoning topic hai jisme ek ya adhik statements diye jaate hain aur candidates ko ye identify karna hota hai ki kaun si conclusion un statements se logically follow karta hai. Isme factual knowledge nahi, balki inferential thinking aur logical deduction ki capacity test ki jaati hai. RRB jaise exams mein typically 2-5 questions aate hain aur ye section relatively high-scoring maana jaata hai kyunki agar aap rules samajh gaye toh galtiyan kam hoti hain.

Is topic ka core idea ye hai: di gayi statement sach mana jaata hai chahe wo reality se mismatch kyun na kare. Aapko bas itna dekhna hai ki kaun sa conclusion uss statement ke premises se logically arise karta hai. Multiple-choice format mein options A/B/C/D ya phir (1), (2), (3), (4) tarah ke combination codes diye jaate hain.

Statement aur Conclusion ki Basic Definitions

['Statement — Ye wo declaration ya claim hota hai jise diya gaya hai. RRB exams mein statement usually ek paragraph, do-teen lines, ya ek single sentence ho sakti hai.', 'Conclusion — Ye wo inference hota hai jo statement ke logical implications par based hota hai. Ek valid conclusion wo hota hai jo statement se bina kisi extra assumption ke necessarily follow kare.', 'Valid vs Invalid Conclusion — Valid conclusion wo hai jo statement ke truth conditions ko violate na kare. Invalid conclusion wo hai jo ya toh statement ke bahar ka kuch bolti hai, ya phir statement ke directly opposite hai, ya fir ek uncertain premise ko certain result bana deta hai.']

Rules of Logical Inference for Statement-Conclusion

['Rule 1 — Statement ko apne terms mein accept karo: Koi bhi bahari knowledge ya personal opinion apply mat karo. Statement sach maano aur sirf usi se conclusion nikalo.', 'Rule 2 — Extreme words par dhyan do: Always, only, never, must, entirely, none, all jaise absolute words wale conclusions generally invalid hote hain jab tak statement explicitly unki guarantee na de.', "Rule 3 — Possibility vs Certainty: Agar statement mein 'some' ya 'many' jaisa tentative word hai, toh definite conclusion nikaalna galat hoga. Possibility ke liye 'may/could' sahi hai, lekin certainty ke liye nahi.", "Rule 4 — Causal relationship check karo: Statement mein cause mention ho, toh effect logically follow kar sakta hai lekin reverse nahi. 'All A are B' means B ho sakta hai A nahi hona chahiye.", 'Rule 5 — Syllogism-type statements: Agar statement categorical proposition jaisa hai (All, Some, No), toh standard syllogism rules follow karo. Example: All cats are dogs → Some dogs are cats (valid), All dogs are cats (invalid).', 'Rule 6 — Irrelevant information reject karo: Jo conclusion statement ke kisi bhi part se relate na kare, use turant eliminate karo.']

Statement ke Types aur Unke Logical Implications

["Universal Affirmative (All): 'Saare X Y hain.' Iska valid conclusion: 'Kuch Y, X hain' (conversion). Reverse 'Saare Y, X hain' invalid hoga.", "Particular Affirmative (Some): 'Kuch X Y hain.' Ye dono directions mein partial overlap dikhata hai. Koi universal conclusion impossible hai.", "Universal Negative (No): 'Koi X Y nahi hai.' Dono sets mutually exclusive. Isse koi bhi overlap wala conclusion galat hoga.", "Particular Negative (Some...not): 'Kuch X, Y nahi hain.' Ye sirf ek partial rejection deta hai, pure class ke baare mein kuch nahi bolta.", "Conditional (If-then): 'Agar P tabhi Q.' Forward direction valid: P hua → Q hua. Reverse (Q → P) invalid unless stated as biconditional.", "Comparative/Quantitative: 'A, B se zyada tall hai.' Isse comparative order milta hai, par koi absolute value nahi."]

Common Conclusion Traps aur unse bachne ke tareeke

["Trap 1 — Over-generalization: 'Kuch log honest hain' statement se nikalna ki 'Sabhi log dishonest hain' — ye invalid hai kyunki kuch Honest hone se baaki ke baare mein kuch nahi pata.", "Trap 2 — Reverse causation: Statement bole 'Sadak ki buri quality se accidents badhe' aur conclusion bole 'Accidents badhne se sadak buri ho gayi'. Ye reverse causality error hai.", "Trap 3 — Absolute leap from partial data: '50% students pass hue' → 'Aadhe students ne padhai nahi ki' — ye assumption hai, inference nahi.", "Trap 4 — Either-or confusion: Kabhi-kabhi do conclusions one another ko contradict karte hain aur dono separately false ho sakte hain, par option 'either follows' ka upyog hota hai jab exactly do complementary pairs hoon.", 'Trap 5 — Irrelevant extension: Statement medical cost barhne ke baare mein hai, par conclusion education budget par hai — ye logical connection nahi rakhta.', 'Prevention tip: Har conclusion ko ek ek karke statement ke har clause se match karo. Agar koi bhi part statement se support nahi milta, us conclusion ko reject kar do.']

RRB-Style Solved Examples

["Example 1 — Statement: Sabhi engineers technicians hain. Kuch technicians graduates hain. Conclusion I: Sabhi engineers graduates hain. Conclusion II: Kuch graduates engineers ho sakte hain. Solution: Statement ke anusar engineers ⊂ technicians aur technicians ∩ graduates ≠ ∅. Isliye engineers ka directly graduates ke saath relation nahi milta. Conclusion I invalid. Conclusion II possible hai kyunki kuch technicians graduates hain aur sab engineers technicians hain — toh kuch engineers graduates ho sakte hain. Valid: Sirf II possible nature se logical hai, lekin strictly 'follow' ke liye II bhi questionable hai kyunki 'possible' aur 'definitely follow' alag hain. RRB convention ke hisaab se Generally II ko 'follows' maanta hai kyunki statement contradict nahi karti.", "Example 2 — Statement: Government ne naye railway tickets ke liye online booking scheme shuru ki hai taaki passengers ko suvidha mile. Conclusion I: Online booking se tickets easy milenge. Conclusion II: Saare passengers online booking karenge. Solution: Scheme ka uddeshya suvidha dena hai, toh I reasonably follow karta hai. II extreme hai kyunki 'saare' ka guarantee statement nahi deti. Valid: Sirf I follows.", 'Example 3 — Statement: All circles are triangles. No triangle is a square. Conclusion I: No circle is a square. Conclusion II: Some squares are circles. Solution: Circles ⊂ Triangles, Triangles ∩ Squares = ∅. Therefore Circles ∩ Squares = ∅. Conclusion I valid (No circle is a square). Conclusion II directly contradicts I, thus invalid. Valid: Sirf I follows.']

Important facts

  • Statement ko hamesha sach maano chahe wo reality se match na kare.
  • Extreme words (always, only, all, never, must) wale conclusions generally invalid hote hain jab tak statement explicitly guarantee na kare.
  • Venn diagram categorical statements (all/some/no) ko visualise karne mein madad karta hai.
  • Reverse causation aur over-generalization do sabse common traps hain.
  • Possible conclusions strictly 'follows' nahi maane jaate jab tak option code mein explicit allowance na ho.
  • Either-or case tabhi applicable hota hai jab exactly do conclusions complementary hon aur dono individually false hoon.
