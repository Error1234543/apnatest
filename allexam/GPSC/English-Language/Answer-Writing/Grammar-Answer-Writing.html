Write a 150-word descriptive answer on 'The role of the judiciary in protecting fundamental rights in India' ensuring correct use of articles, subject-verb agreement, and appropriate tense throughout.

Suggested points

  • Use definite article 'the' before unique institutions: the Supreme Court, the High Courts, the judiciary.
  • Maintain present perfect tense where relevant: has played, have ensured, has upheld.
  • Subject-verb agreement: 'The judiciary plays', 'Judges deliver', 'A citizen seeks' — match singular/plural carefully.
  • Avoid missing articles with countable nouns: file a petition, approach the court, under Article 32.
  • Common error to avoid: 'The judiciary have the power' → 'The judiciary has the power'.
  • Use precise legal phrasing: writ jurisdiction, public interest litigation, judicial review, constitutional remedy.

Compose a 200-word answer on 'Gujarat's contribution to India's economic growth since the reforms of 1991' using complex and compound-complex sentences correctly, with accurate punctuation.

Suggested points

  • Use a relative clause correctly: 'Gujarat, which contributes nearly 15 per cent to India's GDP, has been a manufacturing hub.' — not 'Gujarat, that contributes...' as non-restrictive.
  • Compound-complex example: 'The state attracted foreign investment after the reforms, and many industrial corridors emerged, which boosted employment significantly.'
  • Avoid comma splices: do not join two independent clauses with only a comma — use a conjunction or semicolon.
  • Semicolon use: 'Ahmedabad is a financial hub; Gandhinagar serves as the administrative capital.'
  • Ensure parallel structure in lists: 'It developed infrastructure, attracted industries, and created jobs' — all gerunds or all base verbs.
  • Punctuation around non-essential clauses: set off extra information with commas correctly.

Identify and explain five common grammatical mistakes that GPSC Mains candidates make in their English answers, with corrected examples for each.

Suggested points

  • Mistake 1 — Subject-verb disagreement: 'The data shows are reliable' → 'The data show are reliable' or 'The data is reliable' depending on context; note 'data' can be plural or singular.
  • Mistake 2 — Wrong article usage: 'He is an CEO' → 'He is a CEO' (sound matters, not spelling).
  • Mistake 3 — Confusing 'its' and 'it's': 'Its a mistake' → 'It's a mistake'; possessive 'its' never takes an apostrophe.
  • Mistake 4 — Misused preposition after adjective: 'different than' in formal Indian English accepted but 'different from' is preferred in competitive exams.
  • Mistake 5 — Dangling modifier: 'Walking to the office, the rain started' → 'Walking to the office, I noticed the rain start'.

Transform the following active voice sentences into passive voice, ensuring the tense is preserved and the agent is included only when necessary for clarity.

Suggested points

  • Rule: object of active becomes subject of passive; main verb takes appropriate form of 'be' + past participle.
  • Present simple: 'The government implements the scheme' → 'The scheme is implemented by the government.'
  • Past continuous: 'The governor was inaugurating the bridge' → 'The bridge was being inaugurated by the governor.'
  • Modal verb: 'One should respect the Constitution' → 'The Constitution should be respected (by one).' — drop 'by one' as it is obvious.
  • When the agent is unknown or unimportant: omit 'by...' phrase — e.g., 'Mistakes were made.'
  • Never change the tense during transformation — check each sentence against the original tense.

Write a 150-word note on 'The significance of the Panchayati Raj system in rural governance' using reported speech correctly for at least three statements.

Suggested points

  • Reporting verb selection: 'stated', 'asserted', 'emphasized', 'noted' — vary to avoid repetition.
  • Tense backshift in reported speech: direct 'I will empower villages' → reported 'He said that he would empower villages.'
  • Present tense can remain unchanged if the statement is a general truth: 'The Minister stated that Panchayati Raj ensures grassroots democracy.'
  • Change pronouns appropriately: 'my' → 'his/her', 'we' → 'they', depending on context.
  • Time expression changes: 'today' → 'that day', 'here' → 'there', 'tomorrow' → 'the next day'.
  • Use reporting clause placement variation: at beginning, middle, or end for stylistic diversity in answers.

Correct the following sentences, identifying the specific grammatical error in each and explaining why the correction is necessary.

Suggested points

  • Sentence A — 'Neither the ministers nor the president were present': error is subject-verb agreement with neither/nor — verb agrees with nearest subject: 'was present'.
  • Sentence B — 'The police has arrested the accused': 'police' is always plural → 'have arrested'.
  • Sentence C — 'He is more intelligent than I am': correct; 'than me' is informal and discouraged in formal exams.
  • Sentence D — 'Each of the students have submitted': 'each' is singular → 'has submitted'.
  • Sentence E — 'She asked me where was I going': indirect question word order → 'where I was going'.
  • Always articulate the rule name and the corrected version separately for maximum clarity in exam answers.

Draft a 200-word answer on 'India's foreign policy principles since independence' using transition words and cohesive devices effectively to ensure logical flow between ideas.

Suggested points

  • Addition transitions: 'furthermore', 'moreover', 'additionally' — use to layer supporting points.
  • Contrast transitions: 'however', 'nevertheless', 'on the other hand' — use when presenting different perspectives.
  • Cause-effect transitions: 'consequently', 'therefore', 'as a result' — link policies to outcomes.
  • Sequencing: 'firstly', 'subsequently', 'finally' — organize chronological or logical progression.
  • Example transitions: 'for instance', 'such as', 'namely' — introduce illustrations without breaking flow.
  • Avoid overusing the same transition; vary vocabulary to demonstrate linguistic range, which is rewarded in descriptive marking.
