Write a précis of the following passage in about one-third of its length. State the main idea in a title. Passage: The Indian administrative system, inherited from the British colonial era, is often criticized for being bureaucratic, rigid, and slow. However, it has also been credited with maintaining unity in diversity, ensuring continuity in governance, and providing a framework for equitable development across regions. Over the decades, various reforms such as the introduction of e-governance, social audit mechanisms, and right to information have attempted to make this system more responsive. Yet, the fundamental structure remains largely unchanged, raising questions about whether incremental reforms are sufficient or whether a complete overhaul is necessary. Critics argue that the colonial mindset persists in administrative behaviour, while supporters contend that the system has adapted well enough to handle India's complex federal structure.

Suggested points

  • Title should capture the central tension between criticism and defence of the Indian administrative system, e.g., 'Indian Administration: Reform or Reconstruction?'; identify that the passage discusses both strengths and weaknesses of the colonial-era system; mention key reform measures cited (e-governance, social audit, RTI); note the central debate — incremental reform versus structural change; identify the two contrasting viewpoints (colonial mindset persists vs. system has adapted); précis must be self-contained and roughly one-third the original length; avoid adding personal opinion outside the passage.

Write an essay on the topic: 'Gujarat's Industrial Growth and Environmental Sustainability — Can They Coexist?'

Suggested points

  • Introduction should frame Gujarat as India's leading industrial state while acknowledging its environmental challenges; body paragraphs must address major industrial corridors and sectors (petrochemicals, pharmaceuticals, textiles, renewable energy); discuss specific environmental concerns — air and water pollution, industrial waste, coastal ecology; present government initiatives (solar parks, green industry policies, EIA compliance); cite case studies where industry and environment have been balanced or conflicted; analyse the role of community participation and regulatory enforcement; conclude with a balanced position supported by evidence rather than vague idealism.

Write a précis of the following passage in about one-third of its length. State the main idea in a title. Passage: Digital literacy has emerged as one of the most critical determinants of social inclusion in twenty-first-century India. While urban centres have seen a rapid proliferation of digital platforms and online services, rural populations continue to face significant barriers including lack of infrastructure, low levels of formal education, and limited awareness about digital tools. The COVID-19 pandemic further exposed this digital divide, as students from disadvantaged backgrounds struggled to access online education and marginalized communities lost touch with essential government services that moved online. Experts suggest that bridging this gap requires not only hardware and connectivity but also context-sensitive training programmes that address local languages, cultural contexts, and varying levels of prior knowledge. Without such targeted intervention, digital India risks becoming a project that leaves behind those it intends to serve.

Suggested points

  • Title should reflect the core issue — digital literacy as a factor in social inclusion and the risks of the digital divide; identify the contrast between urban digital adoption and rural exclusion; note the pandemic as an amplifier of existing inequalities; mention the multidimensional nature of the solution — infrastructure plus training plus localization; capture the warning about digital India excluding its intended beneficiaries; précis should not introduce external examples or data absent from the passage.

Write an essay on the topic: 'Panchayati Raj Institutions and Grassroots Democracy — Promise and Performance'

Suggested points

  • Open with the constitutional basis of Panchayati Raj (73rd Amendment) and its democratic rationale; outline the three-tier structure and its intended empowerment of local self-government; discuss achievements — women's representation, social justice through reservation, local accountability; examine structural and functional challenges — financial dependence, bureaucratic interference, inadequate devolution of functions; reference Gujarat-specific developments if known (Gram Sabhas, functionaries, convergence initiatives); evaluate whether Panchayati Raj has achieved transformative change or remains ceremonial; conclude with reform recommendations grounded in the analysis presented.

Write a précis of the following passage in about one-third of its length. State the main idea in a title. Passage: The concept of good governance has gained prominence in development discourse over the past three decades. It encompasses transparency, accountability, rule of law, participation, responsiveness, and efficiency in public administration. Many developing countries have adopted governance reforms driven by international agencies and domestic pressure for greater state performance. India has made legislative strides such as the Right to Information Act,Whistle Blowers Protection, and the establishment of Lok Pal and Lok Ayuktas. Nevertheless, implementation remains uneven. Corruption continues to affect service delivery at multiple levels, and citizen participation in governance processes is often tokenistic rather than substantive. The challenge, therefore, lies not in the absence of frameworks but in their consistent and sincere application, which demands political will and an informed and empowered citizenry.

Suggested points

  • Title should capture governance reform and implementation gaps, e.g., 'Good Governance: Frameworks and Implementation Gaps'; identify the six pillars of good governance mentioned; note India's legislative measures (RTI, Whistle Blowers Act, Lok Pal/Lok Ayuktas); highlight the central problem — uneven implementation despite institutional frameworks; mention corruption and tokenistic participation as key obstacles; note the concluding argument about political will and citizen empowerment; keep the précis within one-third of the original.

Write an essay on the topic: 'India's Renewable Energy Transition — Opportunities and Challenges for a Developing Economy'

Suggested points

  • Introduction should situate India among global renewable energy leaders while noting per-capita energy poverty; discuss India's renewable targets and solar mission achievements; analyse opportunities — energy security, job creation, climate commitments, reduced import dependence; examine challenges — grid integration, storage technology, land acquisition, financing, inter-state transmission; reference international cooperation and technology transfer dimensions; consider implications for industrial policy and rural development; conclude with a realistic assessment of pace and scale required to meet targets.

Write a précis of the following passage in about one-third of its length. State the main idea in a title. Passage: The Gujarati literary tradition spans over eight centuries and encompasses diverse forms — from the spiritual poetry of Premanand and Meerabai to the modernist experimentation of Narsinh Mehta's successors and contemporary prose writers. What distinguishes Gujarati literature is its continuous engagement with social reform. The nineteenth and twentieth centuries saw writers like Karsondas Multani, Pannalal Patel, and Mamubhai Patel using literature as a vehicle for critiquing caste oppression, gender inequality, and feudal structures. In recent decades, Gujarati fiction has expanded to address urbanization, migration, identity crises, and globalization. Despite this rich heritage, the teaching of Gujarati literature in schools and colleges has declined, and readership among younger generations appears to be shrinking. Revival efforts through digital platforms and literary festivals exist but remain insufficient to reverse the overall trend.

Suggested points

  • Title should reflect both the richness and the crisis of Gujarati literature; identify the historical span and major figures (Premanand, Meerabai, Pannalal Patel, etc.); note the defining characteristic — literature as a tool for social reform; mention thematic evolution from caste and gender critique to urban and global themes; capture the central concern — decline in academic teaching and youth readership; note remedial measures mentioned (digital platforms, literary festivals); keep the précis focused and within the prescribed length.

Write an essay on the topic: 'One Nation One Election — Federalism, Administrative Efficiency, and Democratic Representation'

Suggested points

  • Introduce the proposal and its historical context in India; outline the arguments in favour — administrative cost savings, policy continuity, reduced model code disruptions, improved governance efficiency; present the constitutional and federal objections — erosion of state sovereignty, mismatch between central and state electoral cycles, impact on regional parties and issues; discuss the Role of the Prime Minister's High-Level Committee report and its recommendations; examine comparative perspectives briefly if relevant; evaluate whether federalism can accommodate this reform without damage; conclude with a measured assessment weighing democratic principles against administrative pragmatism.

Write a précis of the following passage in about one-third of its length. State the main idea in a title. Passage: India's demographic dividend is frequently cited as a cornerstone of its economic future, yet the term itself often obscures more than it reveals. A large young population can indeed provide a competitive advantage in labour-intensive industries and innovation-driven sectors, but only if that population is healthy, educated, and employable. Current data suggests that while India has the youngest workforce among major economies, skill levels remain alarmingly low, with a significant proportion of graduates lacking employable competencies. Furthermore, the informal sector continues to absorb the majority of new entrants into the labour market, offering little job security or social protection. Without substantial investment in education quality, vocational training, and healthcare, the demographic dividend may well transform into a demographic disaster, with millions of restless, underemployed youth becoming a source of social instability rather than economic growth.

Suggested points

  • Title should capture the conditional nature of the demographic dividend, e.g., 'Demographic Dividend: Potential and Peril'; identify the thesis — raw population numbers do not guarantee economic benefit; note the three prerequisites: health, education, employability; mention the skill gap among graduates and dominance of the informal sector; capture the warning about potential demographic disaster; précis must avoid adding statistics or examples beyond the passage; maintain one-third length.
