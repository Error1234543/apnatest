GPSC English Language — Precis and Essay Writing: Quick Revision

GPSCEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Precis: A concise, coherent summary of a given passage that captures its essential meaning in approximately one-third of the original word count, written entirely in your own words without adding personal opinion.
  • Essay: A structured piece of writing on a given topic that presents a clear thesis, supports it with logical arguments and relevant examples, and concludes with a balanced synthesis or forward-looking statement.
  • Topic: The subject heading assigned for an essay; it may be abstract (e.g., 'Freedom'), factual (e.g., 'Water Scarcity'), or argumentative (e.g., 'Is Technology a Boon or Bane?').
  • Outline: A brief preliminary plan listing the main points and sub-points to be covered in an essay before writing the full version.

Key concepts

  • Précis Writing Core Principles: Faithfulness (accuracy to the original), Completeness (no omitted key idea), Conciseness (roughly one-third length), Coherence (logical flow), Independence (reads as a standalone piece). Never quote directly; never add your own views; never use abbreviations or bullet points in the final précis.
  • Précis Sub-title & Word Count: Write the原标题 in inverted commas at the top with a suitable title you create; mention the original word count and your précis word count (e.g., Original: 300 words | Précis: ~100 words). Label the section 'Précis:' clearly.
  • Précis Drafting Method: Stage 1 — Read the passage at least twice. Stage 2 — Break the passage into logical units (paragraphs or thought-groups). Stage 3 — Note the key idea of each unit in the margin. Stage 4 — Eliminate examples, illustrations, repetitions, and ornamental language. Stage 5 — Combine the key ideas into flowing prose using conjunctions and transitions. Stage 6 — Check against the original for accuracy and word count.
  • Essay Structure — Standard Four-Part Format: Introduction (hook + context + thesis statement), Body Paragraphs (2–5 paragraphs each developing one main argument with evidence/examples), Conclusion (restatement of thesis in fresh words + final thought/recommendation/call to action). Every body paragraph must begin with a clear topic sentence.
  • Essay Types and Their Approach: Expository essays explain or inform; Argumentative/persuasive essays take a stance and defend it; Narrative essays tell a story with a point; Descriptive essays paint a picture with words. GPSC typically expects expository and argumentative styles on current, social, or governance topics.
  • Essay Planning — The PREP Framework: Point (state your position or main idea), Reason (explain why), Example (give a concrete illustration — data, fact, historical reference, real-life case), Point (reiterate the link back to your thesis). Use PREP for each body paragraph to ensure depth.
  • Essay Outline Format: List Roman-numeral main headings and alphabetical sub-headings. For example: I. Introduction — A. Hook, B. Thesis. II. Historical Context — A. Key events, B. Milestones. III. Current Scenario — A. Data/facts, B. Challenges. IV. Government Initiatives — A. Schemes, B. Impact. V. Way Forward — A. Suggestions, B. Conclusion.

Important facts

  • Précis word limit rule of thumb: if the original is 300 words, target 90–100 words for the précis; if 200 words, target 60–70 words. The passage is usually prescribed to reduce to exactly one-third — verify the question's specific instruction.
  • Common GPSC précis passages draw from editorials, socio-economic reports, government publications, and writings by Indian thinkers on themes like democracy, environment, education, governance, and science.
  • Essays in GPSC often focus on Gujarat-relevant and national-relevant themes: water management in Gujarat, renewable energy, digital India, women empowerment, Indian Constitution, Gandhian philosophy, industrialization vs environment, tourism in Gujarat, unemployment, agricultural reforms.
  • Key essay vocabulary to master: mitigate, exacerbate, sustainable, holistic, paradigm shift, inclusive growth, grassroots, accountability, transparency, equitable, multifaceted, pragmatic, imperative, deleterious, auspicious, seminal, burgeoning.
  • Précis writing avoids: first-person pronouns (I, we) unless the original uses them; direct quotations; trivial details; personal examples; rhetorical questions; headline-style telegraphic sentences.
  • Essay writing avoids: sweeping generalizations without support; emotional ranting; irrelevant personal anecdotes; memorized template intros that do not connect to the topic; last-minute topic changes mid-essay.
  • Important essay openings — stock phrases to adapt: 'The issue of X has assuming centrestage in contemporary discourse,' 'Decades after independence, the quest for X remains unfinished,' 'As Mahatma Gandhi once observed…', 'According to the latest NITI Aayog report…'. Always customize to the specific topic.
  • Essays should ideally be 500–700 words for a 15–20 mark question in GPSC Mains. Allocate roughly: 80–100 words for introduction, 300–450 for body (3–4 paragraphs), and 60–80 for conclusion.

Memory aids

  • PRECIS mnemonic: P — Paraphrase (never copy); R — Remove fluff (cut examples/ornamentation); E — Every idea matters (faithful coverage); C — Coherent flow (read as one piece); I — In your own voice (third person, formal tone); S — Short and sharp (one-third length).
  • ESSAY mnemonic: E — Every paragraph a Point; S — Support with Evidence; S — Stay on Subject; A — Alternate analysis and example.
  • For essay structure recall: I-B-C = Introduction, Body, Conclusion — the universal skeleton. Under Body, think R-E-A-D: Reason, Example, Analysis, Development for each paragraph.
  • Link-word bank for précis: however, therefore, consequently, although, whereas, since, thus, notwithstanding, meanwhile, subsequently. These replace listed ideas smoothly.
  • Transition words for essays by function: Addition (furthermore, moreover, additionally); Contrast (however, on the contrary, despite this); Cause-effect (consequently, as a result, hence); Example (for instance, notably, specifically); Conclusion (in conclusion, ultimately, finally).

Exam tips

  • Time management for GPSC English descriptive papers: allocate roughly 25–30 minutes for précis (including reading and drafting) and 40–45 minutes for the essay. Leave 5 minutes for review and correction in each.
  • Précis tips: read the passage three times — once for gist, once for structure, once for details. Cover the original while writing your draft. After writing, check: does it stand alone? Is every key idea included? Is the word count correct?
  • Essay tips: choose a topic you can approach from multiple angles (historical, contemporary, governmental, ethical). If two topics are given, pick the one you can support with more specific examples — not necessarily the one you know most about.
  • Use Gujarat-specific examples where relevant: e.g., for water management essays mention Sardar Sarovar Dam, Canal networks, Jyotigram Yojana, Amul model for cooperative agriculture, GIFT City for economic development. This shows local awareness valued in GPSC.
  • Quote strategically but accurately: Gandhi, Ambedkar, Nehru, APJ Abdul Kalam, and former Chief Ministers of Gujarat are fair sources. If unsure of the exact wording, paraphrase attributively ('As Gandhi emphasized…') rather than risking a misquote.
  • Handwriting and presentation matter in GPSC Mains. Underline key terms in essays, leave a line between paragraphs, and avoid cross-outs. A neat script can influence marking positively.
  • Practice writing one précis and one essay daily for 30 days before the exam. Use past GPSC question papers and sample papers from the official website. Review model answers to understand the expected standard.
  • For the precis title: create a title that captures the essence, not just a repetition of the opening phrase. Good titles are noun phrases: 'The Challenge of Digital Divide,' 'Sustainable Development in Practice,' 'Women's Empowerment: Progress and Peril.'

Common traps

  • Trap 1: Adding personal opinion in a précis. A précis is purely representative — never write 'I believe' or 'In my view.' The writer's view replaces yours entirely.
  • Trap 2: Ignoring the word limit and writing a précis that is too long or too short. Being 20% over or under costs marks. Practice counting words carefully.
  • Trap 3: Copying sentences verbatim from the passage. Even one copied sentence can lead to heavy deduction. Paraphrase every line.
  • Trap 4: Writing an essay without an outline. This leads to disorganized content, repetition, and running out of time. Spend 5 minutes planning; it saves 15 minutes of rewriting.
  • Trap 5: Beginning every essay with 'Since dawn of time…' or 'Ever since man walked on earth…' — these are overused clichés that evaluators mark down. Start with a relevant fact, quote, statistic, or direct contextual statement.
  • Trap 6: Ending an essay with 'Thus I have tried to write about…' — this is weak. End with a forward-looking recommendation, a quote, a data point, or a synthesizing observation.
  • Trap 7: Using informal contractions (don't, can't, won't) or slang in either précis or essay. Maintain formal academic register throughout.
  • Trap 8: Writing irrelevant material to fill space. Quality of argument always beats quantity of words. One well-supported paragraph beats three vague ones.
