Discuss India's recent achievements in space technology under the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO). How have these contributions strengthened India's strategic and scientific standing?

Suggested points

  • Mangalyaan (Mars Orbiter Mission) — first Asian nation to reach Mars orbit; cost-effective mission demonstrating technological self-reliance
  • Chandrayaan-2 and Chandrayaan-3 — landing at lunar south pole; Vikram lander and Pragyan rover; India as fourth nation to soft-land on Moon
  • Aditya-L1 — India's first solar observatory mission placed at Lagrange point 1 for continuous solar monitoring
  • Gaganyaan — human spaceflight programme; demonstration flights; building indigenous crew escape system and life-support systems
  • PSLV and GSLV launch vehicle family — reliable payload delivery; record payloads in single launch (e.g., PSLV-C37 with 104 satellites)
  • NavIC (Navigation with Indian Constellation) — regional navigation satellite system; strategic autonomy in positioning
  • Strategic significance: dual-use technology, defence applications, disaster management support, reduced dependence on foreign systems
  • Scientific standing: collaboration with NASA, ESA, JAXA; technology export potential; inspiration for young scientists
  • Gujarat connection: Vikram Sarabhai Space Centre links, SDSC SHAR coordination, Gujarat's role in satellite ground stations and manufacturing

Evaluate the role of nanotechnology in transforming healthcare and agriculture in India. What are the challenges and the way forward?

Suggested points

  • Definition and scope: nanotechnology involves manipulation of matter at 1–100 nanometre scale; properties differ from bulk material
  • Healthcare applications: targeted drug delivery reducing side effects; nano-based diagnostic kits for early disease detection; nano-surface implants and tissue engineering
  • Agriculture applications: nano-fertilizers and nano-pesticides improving nutrient use efficiency; nano-sensors for soil and crop monitoring; nano-encapsulated bio-pesticides
  • Indian initiatives: Nano Mission by DST; National Nano Science and Technology Centre; IISc, IITs, and CSIR laboratories driving research
  • Challenges: high cost of research and development; lack of standardized testing protocols; regulatory vacuum for nano-products
  • Safety concerns: potential toxicity of nanoparticles to humans and ecosystems; inadequate long-term studies on environmental impact
  • Intellectual property issues: patenting challenges in a country with large generic pharmaceutical base
  • Way forward: strengthen public-private partnerships; create nano-specific regulatory framework; invest in safe-by-design research; build manufacturing capacity in Gujarat and other states
  • Gujarat as nano-hub: GIFT City potential, pharmaceutical industry linkage, Gujarat Nano Mission initiatives

Examine the importance of biotechnology in addressing India's food security and sustainable development. Highlight government initiatives and their outcomes.

Suggested points

  • Biotechnology defined: use of living systems and organisms to develop products; four traditional branches — red (medical), green (agricultural), white (industrial), blue (marine)
  • Food security contributions: Bt cotton and Bt brinjal increasing yield and reducing pesticide dependency; bio-fortified crops like iron-rich rice and zinc-enriched wheat
  • Sustainable development linkages: bio-pesticides replacing chemical inputs; bio-fertilizers improving soil health; waste-to-energy biotechnology
  • Diagnostic biotechnology: rapid test kits for diseases like dengue, malaria, and COVID-19 developed indigenously reducing import dependence
  • Government initiatives: National Biopharma Mission for MSMEs in biopharmaceuticals; BIO India 2023 for industry-academia convergence; DBT star network for biotech clusters
  • Regulatory body: Genetic Engineering Appraisal Committee (GEAC) under Ministry of Environment granting approvals for GM crops and products
  • Outcomes and criticism: Bt cotton success but ongoing debate on Bt brinjal and food crops; need for transparent risk assessment; farmer dependency on seed companies
  • Way forward: promote CRISPR and gene-editing research with clear regulatory pathways; strengthen public sector biotech institutions; focus on crops relevant to western and southern India including Gujarat

Critically analyse India's progress in defence technology. How has indigenisation strengthened national security, and what gaps remain?

Suggested points

  • Indigenisation drive: Atmanirbhar Bharat in defence; positive defence list restricting imports of 101+ items; make in India for defence equipment
  • Major achievements: Tejas LCA by HAL achieving operational capability; Arjun Main Battle Tank; Kalkan missile system; Pinaka multi-barrel rocket launcher
  • Submarine programme: Arihant-class nuclear submarines under Project 75 and Project 75I; Kalvari-class Scorpene submarines from Mazagon Dock
  • Missile development: Agni series (Agni-5 with MIRV capability); Prithvi, BrahMos supersonic cruise missile with Russia; Nag anti-tank guided missile
  • Air defence: Akash surface-to-air missile system; DRDO's integration of radar and missile networks
  • Strategic impact: reduced dependence on foreign suppliers; improved delivery timelines; enhanced strategic autonomy in critical situations
  • Gaps remaining: reliance on Russian engines for Tejas (now being addressed with GE F414); gap in aircraft carrier construction expertise; limited semi-conductor base for advanced electronics
  • Institutional framework: Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO) with over 50 laboratories; iDEX for innovation by startups; Defence Innovation Organisation
  • Recommendations: increase R&D budget allocation; foster civilian-military technology transfer; develop microelectronics and semiconductor manufacturing in India

Discuss the role of Information Technology and artificial intelligence in governance. How can these technologies improve public service delivery in India?

Suggested points

  • Digital India initiative: flagship programme connecting rural areas with high-speed internet; infrastructure, governance, and services as three pillars
  • e-Governance platforms: Common Services Centres (CSCs) delivering 2000+ services in villages; e-Sanjeevani for telemedicine; DigiLocker for digital documents
  • AI in governance: NITI Aayog's National Strategy for AI; IVAM framework for responsible AI deployment; AI applications in agriculture advisory and disease prediction
  • Improved service delivery: real-time grievance redressal through CPGRAMS; AI-based cheque processing in banks; automated tax filing and verification reducing corruption
  • Jan Dhan-Aadhaar-Mobile trinity:Direct Benefit Transfer eliminating leakages; estimated savings of over ₹3 lakh crore in subsidy leakage
  • Gujarat e-governance: GVAN project for secure communication; Gujarat Single Window Investment Clearance System (GSWICS); CM e-Darshan for live public grievance redressal
  • Challenges: digital divide between urban and rural populations; data privacy concerns under DPDP Act 2023; algorithmic bias and lack of transparency in AI decisions
  • Way forward: invest in digital literacy; strengthen cybersecurity infrastructure; create AI ethics guidelines; ensure inclusive design reaching last-mile beneficiaries

Write an essay on emerging technologies and their implications for society. Focus on blockchain, quantum computing, and 5G/6G communications.

Suggested points

  • Blockchain: distributed ledger technology ensuring transparency and immutability; applications in supply chain tracking, land records, voting systems, and digital currency
  • India's blockchain steps: Reserve Bank of India pilot for tokenised commercial paper; Gujarat using blockchain for land record integrity in pilot projects; Indian Blockchain Council under MeitY
  • Quantum computing: leveraging quantum mechanics (superposition and entanglement) for exponential computational power; applications in cryptography, drug discovery, and climate modelling
  • India's quantum mission: National Quantum Mission approved with ₹6,003 crore outlay; focus on quantum computing, communication, sensing, and materials
  • 5G and 6G: 5G enabling ultra-low latency for IoT, autonomous vehicles, and smart cities; Bharat 6G Vision released outlining India's roadmap for 6G by 2030
  • Societal implications positive: enhanced connectivity in remote areas; faster medical procedures through telesurgery; efficient resource management
  • Societal concerns: job displacement due to automation; surveillance capabilities raising civil liberty issues; digital divide widening between nations and within society
  • India's policy response: India Mobile Congress for technology showcase; spectrum auctions accelerating 5G rollout; need for comprehensive technology ethics framework

Highlight the contributions of Gujarat in the field of science and technology. How has the state contributed to India's scientific and industrial growth?

Suggested points

  • Anchorage of atomic energy: Trombay established India's nuclear programme; Gujarat home to Tarapur (nearby), and future nuclear projects; Naroda Heavy Water Plant supporting nuclear reactors
  • Pharmaceutical capital: Gujarat contributes over 30 per cent of India's generic drug output; clusters in Vadodara, Ankleshwar, and Sanand forming pharma corridors
  • Space and defence: ISRO components manufactured in Gujarat; Astral Aerospace and other private players contributing to satellite components; defence manufacturing in Sanand and Dahej
  • Renewable energy leadership: largest installed wind and solar capacity; International Solar Alliance hub; Bhadla and Dwarka offshore wind projects; green hydrogen mission alignment
  • GIFT City: Gujarat International Finance Tec-City as India's first operational international financial and technology hub; smart city infrastructure and fintech adoption
  • Research institutions: PC Science College, GC Roshnara, Sardar Patel University, and GSFC's research initiatives; National Institute of Industrial Engineering (NITIE) now IIM Mumbai campus influence
  • Chemical and petrochemical industry: largest cluster in India at Dahej, Ankleshwar, and Kandla; innovation in specialty chemicals and polymer technology
  • Sarkari schemes and support: Gujarat Science and Technology Policy; startup ecosystem through GEEM and GIDC incentives; technology parks in Ahmedabad and Gandhinagar

Discuss the role of science and technology in environmental conservation and climate change mitigation. What technologies can India adopt for sustainable growth?

Suggested points

  • Climate change context: India committed to 500 GW non-fossil fuel capacity by 2030 and net-zero by 2070; science and technology central to achieving these targets
  • Renewable energy technologies: solar photovoltaic efficiency improvements; floating solar plants on reservoirs saving land and reducing evaporation; green hydrogen from water electrolysis
  • Carbon capture and storage: emerging technology for trapping CO2 emissions from thermal plants; India's first CCS demo project planned at Talcher, Odisha
  • Environmental monitoring: satellite-based remote sensing for deforestation tracking (NASA-ISRO NRSC partnerships); air quality monitoring stations with sensor networks across cities
  • Water conservation tech: membrane desalination plants in coastal Gujarat; precision irrigation using IoT sensors; rainwater harvesting with smart management systems
  • Waste management: waste-to-energy plants; plasma gasification for municipal solid waste; circular economy approaches in industrial clusters
  • Early warning systems: Cyclone Warning DART system; flood forecasting using numerical weather prediction models; drought monitoring through satellite data
  • Gujarat examples: Sardar Sarovar project combining hydro power and irrigation; Bhavnagar solar park; Gir forest monitoring using camera traps and drone surveillance for biodiversity conservation
