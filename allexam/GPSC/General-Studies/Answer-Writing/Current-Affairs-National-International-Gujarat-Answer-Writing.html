Critically examine the impact of recent Union Budget allocations on inclusive growth in India. Highlight key initiatives aimed at rural development, health, and education.

Suggested points

  • Definition of inclusive growth and its relevance to India's developmental goals
  • Budget allocation trends for key sectors: agriculture, health, education, housing, and employment schemes
  • Analysis of schemes like PM-KISAN, Ayushman Bharat, NEP 2020 implementation funding, and PM Awas Yojana
  • Role of fiscal deficit management and revenue mobilisation in sustaining inclusive spending
  • Criticisms: inadequate capital expenditure, regional disparities, and gap between allocation and ground-level delivery
  • Gujarat-specific dimension: state-level budget alignment with central schemes and Gujarat's performance in human development indicators
  • Way forward: need for outcome-based budgeting, convergence of schemes, and stronger monitoring mechanisms

Discuss the significance of India's strategic partnerships in the Indo-Pacific region. How do they contribute to India's security and economic interests?

Suggested points

  • Concept of Indo-Pacific and its evolution as a strategic framework
  • Key partnerships: Quad (India-US-Australia-Japan), India-Middle East-Europe Corridor (IMEC), and bilateral ties with France, Vietnam, and Indonesia
  • Security dimensions: maritime security, freedom of navigation, counter-balancing China's influence, naval exercises
  • Economic dimensions: trade route protection, energy security, supply chain resilience, investment flows
  • India's Act East Policy and SAGAR (Security and Growth for All in the Region) doctrine
  • Challenges: balancing non-alignment legacy, intra-Quad disagreements, China's diplomatic pushback
  • Conclusion: Indo-Pacific partnerships as a cornerstone of India's multi-dimensional foreign policy

Evaluate the implementation and outcomes of the Jal Jeevan Mission in Gujarat. What challenges remain in achieving household tap water connectivity across the state?

Suggested points

  • Overview of Jal Jeevan Mission (JJM): objectives, target of Functional Household Tap Connections (FHTC), and central-state funding pattern
  • Gujarat's progress: coverage achievements, district-wise status, and unique geographic challenges (arid western region vs. coastal south)
  • Key initiatives in Gujarat: underground piping network expansion, watershed convergence, and use of technology for leak detection
  • Water source sustainability: groundwater depletion concerns, dependence on Narmada canal system, and rainwater harvesting integration
  • Operational challenges: quality of water (fluoride, salinity in Kutch and Somnath districts), rural awareness, O&M costs
  • Gujarat's model of community participation through Jan Andolan and water user associations
  • Way forward: ensuring functional sustainability beyond connectivity targets, integrating with PMKSY, and addressing seasonal water scarcity

Analyse the implications of Artificial Intelligence regulation frameworks globally and their potential impact on India's digital economy. Discuss India's approach to AI governance.

Suggested points

  • Rise of AI and its transformative potential across sectors: healthcare, agriculture, governance, education
  • Global regulatory landscape: EU AI Act (risk-based classification), US Executive Order on AI, China's generative AI regulations
  • India's approach: National Strategy for AI (NITI Aayog), Responsible AI frameworks, Bhashini for multilingual AI, and the Digital India Act consultations
  • Opportunities for India's digital economy: AI-driven MSME empowerment, governance efficiency, startup ecosystem growth
  • Concerns: data privacy, algorithmic bias, job displacement, surveillance risks, and the lack of enforceable legal framework post-Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023
  • India's balanced stance: promoting innovation while ensuring ethical guardrails; role of international forums like GPAI
  • Conclusion: India needs a robust but flexible regulatory architecture that fosters innovation while protecting citizens' rights

Discuss the role of Gujarat in India's energy transition. Highlight the state's contributions to renewable energy and the challenges in achieving net-zero emissions.

Suggested points

  • India's climate commitments: Panchamrit under Paris Agreement, net-zero by 2070 target
  • Gujarat's renewable energy profile: leading installed capacity in solar and wind, Gujarat Energy Transmission Corporation (GETCO) infrastructure
  • Key projects: Rewa Solar Park, Dholera Renewable Energy Park, offshore wind potential in Gulf of Kutch, green hydrogen mission alignment
  • Gujarat as a green fuel hub: Gati Shakti corridor for EV manufacturing, Reliance and Adani investments in green hydrogen and green ammonia
  • Challenges: land acquisition, grid integration of intermittent sources, storage technology gaps, and the continued dependence on thermal power
  • Social and environmental dimensions: impact on coastal ecosystems, displacement concerns, and just transition for fossil-fuel-dependent communities
  • Way forward: integrated energy planning, public-private partnership models, and aligning industrial policy with decarbonisation goals

Examine the causes and consequences of recent global supply chain disruptions. How is India positioning itself as an alternative manufacturing hub under the Production Linked Incentive (PLI) Scheme?

Suggested points

  • Nature of recent disruptions: COVID-19 fallout, Ukraine war, Red Sea crisis, US-China trade tensions, and semiconductor shortages
  • Concept of 'China Plus One' strategy and its implications for global supply chain realignment
  • India's PLI Scheme: sectors covered (electronics, pharmaceuticals, automobiles, textiles, semiconductors), budgetary outlay, and expected output generation
  • Achievements: Apple's iPhone manufacturing expansion in Tamil Nadu and Gujarat, pharmaceutical exports surge, solar module manufacturing growth
  • Challenges: infrastructure bottlenecks, logistics costs still higher than China, skill gap, land and labour law complexities
  • Gujarat's advantage: port infrastructure (Deendayal, Pipavav), industrial corridors, established manufacturing base, and ease of doing business reforms
  • Conclusion: India's potential is significant but requires sustained structural reforms and infrastructure investment to fully capitalize on the shift

Analyse the impact of climate change on Gujarat's coastal and arid regions. Discuss the adaptation and mitigation measures undertaken by the state government.

Suggested points

  • Climate change vulnerability index and Gujarat's exposure: coastal erosion in Kutch and Saurashtra, declining rainfall in semi-arid regions, rising temperatures
  • Impacts: salinity ingress in groundwater, destruction of fishing livelihoods, damage to mangrove ecosystems, heat stress on agriculture and human health
  • Adaptation measures: coastal regulation zone enforcement, afforestation drives, promotion of drought-resistant crops, early warning systems for cyclones
  • Mitigation efforts: solar energy expansion, Gujarat State Climate Change Action Plan, sustainable urban planning in Ahmedabad and Surat
  • Community-level initiatives: traditional water conservation revival, self-help groups in climate-vulnerable districts, insurance schemes for farmers
  • Gaps: inadequate institutional coordination, limited financial resources for adaptation, need for climate-resilient infrastructure in rapidly urbanising areas
  • Way forward: integrating climate action into developmental planning, leveraging GST compensation and CAMPA funds, and strengthening disaster management capacity

    Discuss the significance of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023. How does it impact citizens' right to privacy and the growth of India's digital economy?

    Suggested points

    • Background: Puttaswamy judgment (2017) establishing privacy as a fundamental right, subsequent legislative gaps
    • Salient features: definition of personal data, consent framework, data fiduciary obligations, rights of data principals
    • Data Protection Board of India: composition, functions, and adjudicatory role
    • Exemptions granted to government agencies and their implications for civil liberties and transparency
    • Impact on businesses: compliance burden on startups and MSMEs, data localisation requirements, penalties for violations
    • Positive aspects: legal backbone for digital governance, trustran infrastructure, UPI and India Stack expansion, confidence for global tech companies
    • Critical concerns: insufficient safeguards against state surveillance, weak enforcement mechanism, overlapping jurisdictions with sectoral regulators
    • Way forward: framing rule-based regulations, strengthening the Board's independence, and ensuring a balance between privacy and national security

      Discuss the importance of the National Education Policy 2020 in reshaping India's education landscape. Highlight its key provisions and implementation challenges.

      Suggested points

      • Historical context: need for reform after successive education policies since 8th Five Year Plan, outdated curriculum and examination systems
      • Key provisions: 5+3+3+4 pedagogical structure replacing 10+2, mother tongue as medium of instruction till grade 5, multidisciplinary approach
      • Higher education reforms: undergraduate four-year degree with multiple exit options, Academic Bank of Credits, single regulator (HECI)
      • Vocational education integration, removal of rigid stream separation, emphasis on critical thinking over rote learning
      • Implementation challenges: resistance from stakeholders, teacher training requirements, infrastructure gaps in schools, language standardisation issues for NEP languages
      • Funding constraints and the need for increased public expenditure toward 6% of GDP target for education
      • Gujarat's response: state action plan, teacher recruitment reforms, Gujarati medium school upgrades, and digital infrastructure rollout
      • Conclusion: NEP 2020 represents a paradigm shift but requires sustained political will, adequate funding, and stakeholder consensus for successful implementation
