Critically examine the role of the Indian National Congress in shaping the freedom struggle from 1885 to 1947. How did its ideological and tactical shifts influence the mass movements?

Suggested points

  • Formation in 1885 under A.O. Hume; moderate phase (1885-1905) with petitions and constitutional methods
  • Rise of extremism under Tilak, Bipin Chandra Pal, Lala Lajpat Rai; Swadeshi and Boycott movements post-1905
  • Gandhian era (1915 onwards): Non-Cooperation Movement (1920-22), Civil Disobedience (1930-34), Quit India (1942)
  • Revolutionary activities alongside Congress methods — Bhagat Singh, Chandrashekhar Azad, INA
  • Partition and independence in 1947 as outcome of combined mass pressure and British wartime exhaustion
  • Critique: limitations of Congress approach, communal divides, delayed radical agrarian reforms

Analyze the impact of the Revolt of 1857 on the administrative and political structure of British India. Did it fundamentally change the colonial policy?

Suggested points

  • Immediate causes: Doctrine of Lapse, greased cartridges, annexation policies under Dalhousie
  • Nature of the revolt: regional variations, participation of sepoys, peasants, dispossessed rulers
  • Administrative changes: Government of India Act 1858 — transfer from Company to Crown
  • Policy shifts: divide and rule formalised, princely states preserved as bulwarks, army reorganisation
  • Military reorganisation: 'martial races' theory, reduction of Indian troops, European dominance
  • Long-term impact: seeds of nationalism sown despite British suppression of the narrative

Discuss the significance of the Ghadar Movement in the Indian freedom struggle. How did it differ from the mainstream nationalist movement?

Suggested points

  • Formation in 1913 at San Francisco by Indian immigrants; radical anti-colonial stance
  • Role of Lala Har Dayal and Sohan Singh Bhakna in organising exiled Indians
  • 1915 mutiny plan — aimed at simultaneous sepoy uprisings across India
  • British crackdown: Lahore Conspiracy Case, executions, suppression of Ghadar literature
  • Differences from Congress: extra-parliamentary, revolutionary, diaspora-driven, less emphasis on dialogue
  • Legacy: inspired later revolutionary groups and shaped British counter-insurgency strategies

Evaluate the contribution of Subhas Chandra Bose to the Indian freedom struggle. Was the INA a decisive factor in Britain's decision to leave India?

Suggested points

  • Young Congress leader, break with Gandhian method, founded Forward Bloc in 1939
  • Escape from India, alliance with Axis powers for pragmatic anti-British strategy
  • Formation of Azad Hind Fauj and Provisional Government of Free India in 1943
  • Jail Sharat Chandra Bose and Mohan Singh factors in restructuring the INA
  • INA trials at Red Fort (1945-46) sparked massive public sympathy and military mutinies
  • Debate: INA's military impact vs. broader political and economic factors in British withdrawal

Examine the role of press and literature in awakening national consciousness during the Indian freedom struggle. Give examples from the late 19th and early 20th centuries.

Suggested points

  • Young Bengal and early reformist writings — Raja Ram Mohan Roy's Brahmo Samaj publications
  • Bengal Gazette, Sudharak, Kesari, Mumbai Samachar as vehicles of nationalist thought
  • Arya Samaj's Pratinidhi and Swarajya literature spreading reformist ideas across regions
  • Tilak's Kesari and Maratha in mobilising Marathi-speaking masses through accessible prose
  • Sister Nivedita and other writers bridging Indian and Western audiences
  • Censorship laws like Vernacular Press Act 1878 and their paradoxical effect of fuelling resistance

What were the causes and consequences of the Partition of Bengal (1905)? How did it mark a turning point in the freedom struggle?

Suggested points

  • Curzon's justification: administrative efficiency of unduly large Bengal province
  • Actual motive: divide Hindus and Muslims, weaken nationalist strongholds in East Bengal
  • Massive protest: Swadeshi Movement, Boycott of British goods, education reforms (National Council of Education)
  • Rise of revolutionary terrorism as a parallel current alongside moderate politics
  • Annulment in 1911: restoration of Bengal, shift of capital to Delhi, concessions to moderates
  • Long-term significance: first all-India mass movement, birth of extremist nationalism, beginning of organised revolutionary underground

Assess the importance of the Jallianwala Bagh massacre (1919) in transforming the trajectory of the Indian freedom struggle.

Suggested points

  • Context: Rowlatt Act protests, arrest of Dastur Kitchlew and Satyapal, tension in Amritsar
  • Event details: General Dyer's orders on Baisakhi day, barricading exits, casualty figures
  • Hunter Committee findings and British parliamentary debate on the incident
  • Gandhi's transformation: shift from loyalist to satyagrahi after the massacre
  • Non-Cooperation Movement as direct consequence, widespread disillusionment with British justice
  • Long-term impact: loss of faith in British fairness, radicalisation of a generation of Indians

Compare and contrast the ideologies of the Moderates and Extremists within the Indian National Congress before 1909. What led to the Surat Split?

Suggested points

  • Moderates: Gokhale, Dadabhai Naoroji, Pherozeshah Mehta — petitions, prayers, constitutional reform
  • Extremists: Tilak, Pal, Lajpat Rai — swaraj as right, boycott, national education, self-reliance
  • Ideological differences: methods, scope of demands, view of British goodwill
  • Congress sessions: Madras 1905 (moderate victory), Calcutta 1906 (compromise with Simla Deputation backdrop)
  • Surat Session 1907: physical confrontation between factions, immediate split, organisational weakness
  • Aftermath: British exploited division until Gandhi reunified Congress at Lucknow 1916

Describe the contributions of Gujarat and Gujaratis to the Indian freedom struggle with specific reference to the Kheda Satyagraha and Champaran.

Suggested points

  • Patel's leadership in Kheda Satyagraha (1918): peasant resistance against revenue suspension, successful non-payment campaign
  • Organisational genius: Patel built a disciplined volunteer network, managed funds, negotiated with authorities
  • Champaran (1917): first satyagraha in India, indigo peasants vs. British planters, Gandhi's investigative method
  • Tilak's role in Maharashtra and wider India, his imprisonment and publication reach
  • Vallabhbhai Patel's later role: integration of princely states post-independence as culmination
  • Gujarat's unique contribution: bridge between peasant unrest and organised nationalist politics

Critically analyse the role of the Simon Commission (1927) and the subsequent Nehru Report in shaping India's constitutional future.

Suggested points

  • All-white Simon Commission announced in 1927, no Indian representation — 'Simon Go Back' unified all parties
  • Boycott by Congress and Muslim League initially; some leaders still engaged substantively
  • Nehru Report (1928): Motilal Nehru's draft constitution — dominion status, federal structure, joint electorates
  • Muslim League's objections: separate electorates, weightage in Punjab and Bengal, Srinivas Iyengar committee
  • Gandhi's response: rejected dominion status demand, called for complete independence at Lahore 1929
  • Significance: last major Hindu-Leader constitutional effort before Gandhi took full political leadership

Examine the significance of the Civil Disobedience Movement (1930-34) in the context of India's path to independence.

Suggested points

  • Launch: Dandi March (March-April 1930), salt satyagraha as symbol of economic self-reliance
  • Mass participation: workers, students, women, tribals — broader base than Non-Cooperation
  • Gandhi-Irwin Pact (1931): suspension of movement, Congress participation in Round Table Conference
  • Failure of Round Table Conference: communal deadlock, lack of British commitment to dominion status
  • Resume of movement: failed civil disobedience, Gandhi's arrest, provincial elections 1934
  • Long-term impact: normalised defiance of unjust laws, established Congress as mass organisation, exposed British inability to govern by consent

Trace the evolution of the Quit India Movement (1942) and assess its effectiveness as a tool for achieving independence.

Suggested points

  • Context: Cripps Mission failure (1942), British attempt to secure Indian cooperation in WWII
  • Gandhi's 'Do or Die' call at August Kranti Maidan, Mumbai — clear demand for immediate British exit
  • Immediate mass response: strikes, protests, parallel governments in parts of India (Tikra, Ballia, Kisan Sabha)
  • Brutal repression: arrests of all top leaders, shooting on sight, collective fines, press censorship
  • Regional variations: intense in Bengal, Bihar, Maharashtra; limited in South and Northeast
  • Effectiveness: shattered any illusion of British moral authority, set irreversibility of independence in motion

Discuss the role of tribal and peasant movements in the Indian freedom struggle. How did they differ from urban-led nationalist movements?

Suggested points

  • Tribal movements: Birsa Munda (Bhagalpur), Santhal Hool, Rampa Rebellion, Eani Udaya in Andhra
  • Peasant movements: Indigo revolt, Awadh Kisan Sabha, Bihar peasant agitation, Kisan Sabhas under Congress linkage
  • Distinct features: anti-land revenue, anti-forest laws, religious-millenarian elements, local leadership
  • Difference from urban movements: less organised, more spontaneous, rooted in immediate material grievances
  • Congress engagement: Kisan Sabha formation, limited programme integration until late 1930s
  • Importance: demonstrated that freedom struggle was not exclusively urban elite phenomenon; revealed colonial exploitation's rural face

Analyse the significance of the Rowland Act (1919) and the Rowlatt Satyagraha in the context of Gandhian political strategy.

Suggested points

  • Rowlatt Act provisions: detention without trial, evidence without hearing, extended wartime emergency powers
  • Gandhi's innovation: first all-India satyagraha, combining political and moral resistance
  • Methods: hartals, fasting, voluntary arrest, civil disobedience across multiple provinces
  • Jallianwala Bagh as tragic outcome of suppressed lawful protest under Rowlatt framework
  • Strategic significance: proved Gandhi could mobilise nationally, established satyagraha as valid political tool
  • Limitations: movement called off after violence in several cities, revealed challenges of mass non-violent discipline

Evaluate the contributions of Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev, and Rajguru to the Indian freedom struggle. How did their legacy influence subsequent generations?

Suggested points

  • Background:青年 nationalist radicals inspired by 1919 events and Ghadar legacy
  • Kashmiri Gate assassination of Saunders; Lahori conspiracy and bomb payload in Central Legislative Assembly
  • Ideological foundation: socialist orientation, rejection of purely religious nationalism, class analysis of imperialism
  • Trials as platform: 'Inquilab Zindabad', hunger strike for prisoner rights, printed pamphlets from jail
  • Execution: 23 March 1931, martyrdom that galvanized youth across India and abroad
  • Legacy: inspired Azad, Bhaktavatsalam, numerous underground organisations; cultural iconography in films, songs, literature
