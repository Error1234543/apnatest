1. Scientific Method and Core Terminology

["Science is a systematic enterprise that builds and organises knowledge through testable explanations and predictions about the natural world. The scientific method proceeds through observation, formulation of a hypothesis, experimentation, analysis, and conclusion. In GPSC, candidates must be familiar with the distinction between a hypothesis (a tentative explanation that can be tested), a theory (a well-substantiated explanation supported by extensive evidence — e.g., Cell Theory, Germ Theory, Evolutionary Theory), and a law (a statement describing an observed phenomenon without explaining why, e.g., Newton's Laws of Motion, Law of Conservation of Mass).", 'Key terminology frequently tested: Isotope = atoms of same element with different neutron numbers; Isobar = atoms of different elements with same mass number; Isotone = atoms with same neutron count but different proton count; Allotrope = different structural forms of the same element (e.g., diamond and graphite are allotropes of carbon); Isomer = molecules with same molecular formula but different structural arrangements; Radioactivity = spontaneous emission of radiation from unstable nuclei.', 'SI Units and their associated scientists (high-yield for matching questions): Meter (m) — speed of light; Kilogram (kg) — Planck constant; Second (s) — caesium-133 transition frequency; Ampere (A) — elementary charge; Kelvin (K) — Boltzmann constant; Mole (mol) — Avogadro constant; Candela (cd) — luminous efficacy. Derived units to remember: Hertz (Hz) for frequency, Pascal (Pa) for pressure, Joule (J) for energy, Watt (W) for power, Newton (N) for force, Volt (V) for electric potential, Ohm (Ω) for resistance, Tesla (T) for magnetic field.']

2. Physics and Fundamental Concepts

["Mechanics: Newton's Three Laws form the backbone — First Law (Law of Inertia, objects remain at rest or uniform motion unless acted upon by external force), Second Law (F = ma; force equals mass times acceleration), Third Law (every action has equal and opposite reaction). Pressure in fluids follows Pascal's Law (pressure applied at any point in enclosed fluid transmits equally in all directions). Bernoulli's Principle states that increased fluid speed leads to decreased pressure, foundational for aerodynamics.", "Optics: Refraction is governed by Snell's Law (n₁sinθ₁ = n₂sinθ₂). Total internal reflection occurs when light travels from denser to rarer medium at an angle greater than the critical angle — this principle underlies fibre optics. Mirages, rainbow formation (dispersion + refraction + internal reflection in water droplets), and the blue colour of the sky (Rayleigh scattering) are common application areas.", "Electrical and Magnetic Phenomena: Ohm's Law (V = IR) connects voltage, current, and resistance. Electrical power P = VI = I²R = V²/R. In AC circuits, impedance includes resistance, inductive reactance, and capacitive reactance. Faraday's Law of Electromagnetic Induction states that a changing magnetic flux through a conductor induces an electromotive force — the principle behind generators and transformers. Semiconductors: Silicon (Si) and Germanium (Ge) are intrinsic; doping creates N-type (extra electrons) and P-type (holes) semiconductors. PN junction forms the basis of diodes and transistors.", 'Modern Physics essentials: Photoelectric effect (Einstein, 1921 Nobel) demonstrated the particle nature of light — photons of energy E = hν eject electrons from metal surfaces when photon energy exceeds the work function. Nuclear fission (splitting heavy nucleus, used in reactors and atomic bombs) vs. nuclear fusion (combining light nuclei, powers the sun) must be clearly distinguished. The hydrogen bomb uses fusion triggered by a fission explosion. Nuclear reactors use controlled fission; moderators (heavy water, graphite) slow neutrons; control rods (cadmium/boron) absorb excess neutrons.']

3. Chemistry in Daily Life and Industry

['Polymers: Natural polymers include cellulose, proteins, rubber; synthetic polymers include polyethylene (PE), polypropylene (PP), PVC (polyvinyl chloride), Teflon (PTFE), Nylon, Bakelite. Thermoplastics soften on heating and harden on cooling (reversible); thermosetting plastics undergo irreversible curing (Bakelite, epoxy resins). Nylon was the first fully synthetic fibre. Rayon is called artificial silk but is semi-synthetic (regenerated cellulose).', 'Acids, Bases, and Salts: Strong acids include HCl, H₂SO₄, HNO₃; weak acids include CH₃COOH (acetic acid), H₂CO₃ (carbonic acid). Strong bases include NaOH, KOH; weak bases include NH₄OH. pH scale ranges 0–14; pH < 7 acidic, pH > 7 basic, pH = 7 neutral. Common salts: NaCl (table salt), NaHCO₃ (baking soda, sodium bicarbonate), Na₂CO₃·10H₂O (washing soda), CaOCl₂ (bleaching powder), plaster of Paris (CaSO₄·½H₂O), gypsum (CaSO₄·2H₂O). Baking soda + vinegar produces CO₂ gas (used in fire extinguishers).', 'Environmental Chemistry: Greenhouse gases include CO₂, CH₄ (methane), N₂O (nitrous oxide), CFCs (chlorofluorocarbons), and water vapour. CO₂ concentration currently ~420 ppm. Acid rain is caused by SO₂ and NOₓ reacting with atmospheric water to form H₂SO₄ and HNO₃. Ozone layer depletion is primarily due to CFCs releasing chlorine radicals that catalytically destroy ozone. Photochemical smog contains O₃ and PAN (peroxyacyl nitrate); classical smog contains SO₂, particulates, and fog (London-type).', 'Materials Science: Steel is an alloy of iron and carbon (0.02–2.1%); stainless steel adds chromium (~10.5%) and nickel. Brass = copper + zinc; Bronze = copper + tin; Solder = lead + tin (low melting point); Amalgam = mercury alloy. Glass composition roughly Na₂SiO₃ + CaSiO₃ + SiO₂; borosilicate glass (Pyrex) contains B₂O₃ for thermal resistance. Cement ingredients: limestone, clay, gypsum; setting time is controlled by adding gypsum. Fertilizers: NPK (nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium); Urea (NH₂CONH₂, 46% nitrogen); DAP (diammonium phosphate); TNT is both an explosive and an industrial chemical.']

4. Biology and Medical Sciences

['Cell Biology: The cell is the basic unit of life (Schleiden & Schwann, 1838). Prokaryotic cells lack a true nucleus and membrane-bound organelles (bacteria, archaea); eukaryotic cells have them (plants, animals, fungi, protists). Mitochondria are the powerhouses producing ATP via oxidative phosphorylation. Ribosomes synthesise proteins. Lysosomes contain hydrolytic enzymes for waste digestion. The cell membrane follows the Fluid Mosaic Model (Singer & Nicolson, 1972). DNA structure (double helix) was elucidated by Watson, Crick, and Franklin in 1953.', "Genetics and Biotechnology: Mendel's Laws of Inheritance — Law of Dominance, Law of Segregation, Law of Independent Assortment. Genotype refers to genetic makeup; phenotype refers to observable traits. Mutation is a permanent change in DNA sequence. Down syndrome = Trisomy 21; Turner syndrome = 45,X; Klinefelter syndrome = 47,XXY. CRISPR-Cas9 is a revolutionary gene-editing tool derived from bacterial immune systems. GMO (Genetically Modified Organism) in India includes Bt cotton (Bacillus thuringiensis toxin gene inserted for pest resistance). DNA fingerprinting was developed by Alec Jeffreys (1985).", 'Immunology and Medicine: Antigen triggers immune response; antibody is the protein produced in response. Active immunity develops after exposure to pathogen/vaccine; passive immunity is temporarily acquired (maternal antibodies, antivenom injection). Live attenuated vaccines (BCG, oral polio) vs. inactivated/killed vaccines (Salk polio, hepatitis A). mRNA vaccines (COVID-19 — Pfizer, Moderna) represent a newer platform. Monoclonal antibodies are laboratory-produced molecules mimicking immune system antibodies. Antibiotic resistance arises from overuse/misuse and horizontal gene transfer among bacteria.', 'Human Physiology Highlights: Blood groups (ABO system by Landsteiner, Rh factor discovered later). Normal blood pH 7.35–7.45. Hemoglobin carries oxygen; CO₂ is transported as bicarbonate. Neuron structure: dendrites receive signals, axon transmits, synapse is the junction. Hormones from endocrine glands: insulin (pancreas, beta cells), thyroxine (thyroid), cortisol (adrenal cortex), adrenaline (adrenal medulla), growth hormone (pituitary). Vitamins: A (night blindness), B-complex (beri-beri, pellagra), C (scurvy), D (rickets), K (blood clotting).']

5. Information Technology and Digital Revolution

['Computer Fundamentals: A computer comprises hardware (CPU, memory, input/output devices, storage) and software (system software like OS, application software). CPU (Central Processing Unit) is the brain — consists of ALU (Arithmetic Logic Unit), CU (Control Unit), and registers. Generation-wise: 1st (vacuum tubes, ENIAC), 2nd (transistors), 3rd (ICs), 4th (microprocessors), 5th (AI/Ultra-large-scale integration). Storage hierarchy: registers → cache → RAM (volatile) → SSD/HDD (non-volatile). 1 byte = 8 bits; 1 KB ≈ 1024 bytes.', 'Networking and Internet: LAN (Local Area Network), MAN (Metropolitan), WAN (Wide Area Network). IP address (IPv4: 32-bit dotted decimal; IPv6: 128-bit hexadecimal to address exhaustion). DNS translates domain names to IP addresses. HTTP (port 80), HTTPS (port 443, secure), FTP (port 21). Wi-Fi based on IEEE 802.11 standards. Bluetooth for short-range wireless communication (2.4 GHz). Router directs traffic between networks; switch directs within a network. Cloud computing models: IaaS, PaaS, SaaS.', 'Emerging Technologies — AI and ML: Artificial Intelligence encompasses machines performing tasks requiring human intelligence (reasoning, learning, perception, language). Machine Learning is a subset where models learn from data without explicit programming. Deep Learning uses neural networks with many layers. NLP (Natural Language Processing) enables understanding of human language. Generative AI (like large language models) produces new content. IoT (Internet of Things) connects everyday devices to the internet for data exchange. Blockchain is a decentralised, immutable distributed ledger — underlying cryptocurrencies. Quantum Computing uses qubits (superposition, entanglement) promising exponential speedup for specific problems.', "Cybersecurity and Data Privacy: Key threats include phishing, malware/ransomware, DDoS attacks, man-in-the-middle, SQL injection. Encryption transforms readable data (plaintext) into unreadable form (ciphertext). Symmetric encryption uses one key; asymmetric uses public-private key pairs. India's digital infrastructure: Aadhaar (biometric ID), UPI (Unified Payments Interface), ONDC (Open Network for Digital Commerce), DigiLocker. Data protection framework moves toward the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023."]

6. Space Technology and ISRO Missions

["Space Programme Overview: India's space programme is managed by ISRO (Indian Space Research Organisation), headquartered in Bengaluru. The programme began under Vikram Sarabhai (father of Indian space programme); A.P.J. Abdul Kalam played a pivotal role in missile and space technology. First satellite: Aryabhata (1975, launched by Soviet rocket). First indigenous launch vehicle: SLV-3 (1980, deployed Rohini satellite). First successful Mars mission (Mangalyaan): 2013 launch, entered Mars orbit 2014 — made India the first Asian nation to reach Martian orbit and the first in the world to do so on its maiden attempt.", "Launch Vehicles: PSLV (Polar Satellite Launch Vehicle) — workhorse for sun-synchronous and polar orbits; GSLV (Geosynchronous Satellite Launch Vehicle) — uses cryogenic stage for heavier geostationary payloads; LVM3 (Launch Vehicle Mark-3, formerly GSLV Mk-III) — heavy-lift vehicle carrying Chandrayaan and Gaganyaan. AGILE, Astrosat, Aditya-L1 are important Indian space observatories. Gaganyaan is India's human spaceflight programme targeting crewed orbital flights.", "Key ISRO Missions (high-yield for GPSC): Chandrayaan-1 (2008) — discovered water molecules on lunar surface via MOon Impact Probe and M3 instrument; Chandrayaan-2 (2019) — orbiter still operational, Vikram lander crashed; Chandrayaan-3 (2023) — successfully landed near lunar south pole (Vikram lander, Pragyan rover), making India the fourth country to soft-land on Moon and first to land near the south pole. Aditya-L1 (2023 launch) — India's first solar observatory placed at Lagrange point L1 (~1.5 million km from Earth) to study the Sun. XPoSat (2024) — X-ray polarimetry mission. NISAR (with NASA) — Earth observation using synthetic aperture radar (launch targeted 2024-25)."]

7. Defence Technology and Advanced Materials

['Missile Systems (Agni & Prithvi series under IGMDP — Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme, Dr. APJ Abdul Kalam as key architect): Prithvi (short-range tactical ballistic missile, ~150 km range); Agni-I to Agni-V (series of MRBM and ICBM range missiles); Agni-V has intercontinental range (>5,000 km). Agni-P (Prithvi Advanced Prototype) is a lightweight, two-stage solid-fuelled missile. Other notable systems: BrahMos (supersonic cruise missile, joint Indo-Russian venture), Akash (surface-to-air missile), Trishul (short-range SAM), Pinaka (multi-barrel rocket launcher), Nag (anti-tank guided missile). K-15 and K-4 are submarine-launched ballistic missiles (SLBMs).', 'Defence Platforms: INS Vikrant (indigenous aircraft carrier, commissioned December 2022); Tejas fighter (LCA — Light Combat Aircraft, HAL); Arjun main battle tank; Scorpene-class submarines (Kalvari class, built with French collaboration); P-8I Poseidon maritime patrol aircraft (imported). Indigenous combat aircraft pipeline: AMCA (Advanced Medium Combat Aircraft) under development. DRDO is the primary defence research agency.', 'Advanced Materials and Nanotechnology: Nanotechnology involves manipulating matter at 1–100 nm scale where novel properties emerge. Carbon nanotubes, graphene, quantum dots, nanowires are key materials. Nanomedicine applies nanotechnology to medical diagnosis and treatment. Graphene (single layer of carbon atoms in hexagonal lattice) is the strongest known material, highly conductive, and nearly transparent. Shape-memory alloys (Nitinol — NiTi) return to original shape upon heating. Aerogel is the lightest solid, excellent insulator. Metamaterials have engineered properties not found in nature (negative refractive index). High-entropy alloys combine multiple principal elements for exceptional strength and thermal resistance.']

8. Science in Gujarat and Applied Technology

["Gujarat's Space and Scientific Infrastructure: Gujarat hosts several important scientific installations. The Space Applications Centre (SAC) of ISRO is located in Ahmedabad — it designs and builds satellites, payload systems, and ground stations. VSSC's satellite control centre is at SAC. Liquid Propulsion Systems Centre (LPSC) has a facility in Valiamala (though primarily in Thiruvananthapuram). IOT (Indian Institute of Occupational Therapy) and other institutes operate in the state. The Guptkasmi underground facility (near Gandhinagar) hosts ISRO's testing infrastructure. The Gujarat State Resource Centre for Skill Development collaborates with ISRO on geospatial applications.", "Gujarat's Solar and Renewable Energy Leadership: Gujarat is India's leading state in installed solar capacity. The Khavda Renewable Energy Park (in Rann of Kutch, Bhuj region) is one of the world's largest proposed solar-wind hybrid parks (~60 GW target). Adani Green Energy and Tata Power Renewable Energy have major operations here. The Dholera Solar Park and Vadhwan Solar Park are significant installations. Gujarat has also invested in green hydrogen production, with the Gujarat Green Hydrogen Mission targeting 1.5 MTPA by 2030.", "Pharmaceutical and Chemical Industry: Gujarat is known as the 'Pharma Capital of India' — Surat, Ankleshwar, and Dahej host thousands of pharmaceutical and fine-chemical companies. Aarti Industries, Shilpa Pharmaceuticals, Micron Pharmaceuticals, and numerous generics manufacturers operate here. The Hazira and Dahej petrochemical clusters are among the largest in Asia. The GIFT City (Gandhinagar International Financial Technology City) in Gandhinagar is emerging as a global fintech hub with cutting-edge infrastructure and regulatory sandbox facilities.", "Marine and Ocean Technology: The National Institute of Ocean Technology (NIOT, Chennai) operates in partnership with Gujarat's coastal regions for ocean mining and marine resource exploration. The Gulf of Kutch and Gulf of Khambhat have unique marine ecosystems studied by the Central Marine Fisheries Research Institute (CMFRI) and regional institutes. Marine bioresources, offshore wind potential (Gujarat's long coastline offers ~40 GW offshore wind potential), and desalination technology are areas of active research."]

9. Recent Scientific Developments and Awards

["Nobel Prizes in Science (recent years of exam relevance): Physics — 2022 (Bell inequality experiments/quantum information, Aspect, Clauser, Zeilinger); 2023 (attosecond physics pulse generation, Agostini, L'Huillier, Krausz); 2024 (artificial neural networks/AI, Hopfield, Hinton); Chemistry — 2024 (protein structure prediction with AlphaFold, Baker; algorithmic protein design, Jumper & Hassabis); 2023 (quantum dots, Bawendi, Brus, Ekimov); Medicine — 2024 (microRNA discovery, Ambros & Ruvkun); 2023 (mRNA vaccine technology, Karikó & Weismann); 2022 (DNA sequencing methods, Gilbert & Sanger posthumously recognised). These are directly relevant for current-affairs-linked GS questions.", "Bharat Science and Technology Awards: Bharat Ratna recipients in science include C.V. Raman (physics — Raman Effect), Homi J. Bhabha (nuclear physics), Vikram Sarabhai (space programme), APJ Abdul Kalam (missile/space technology), CV Raman, M.S. Swaminathan (Green Revolution). National Science Day is celebrated on February 28 every year marking Raman's discovery of the Raman Effect (1928). National Technology Day is May 11 (successful Pokhran-II nuclear tests, 1998). Science Congress is held annually in January. Rashtriya Vigyan Sanmand is awarded for popularisation of science.", "Important Scientific Milestones and Instruments: Raman Spectroscopy (C.V. Raman, 1928 Nobel) detects vibrational modes of molecules — widely used in chemistry and materials science. PET scan uses positron emission; MRI uses magnetic fields and radio waves; CT scan uses X-rays; Ultrasound uses sound waves. LASER (Light Amplification by Stimulated Emission of Radiation) — invented by Theodore Maiman (1960), based on Einstein's 1917 theoretical work. Fibre optics transmit data via total internal reflection. Electron microscope (Ernst Ruska, 1931 Nobel) achieves far higher resolution than light microscopes. Cryo-EM (2017 Nobel, Dubochet, Frank, Henderson) enabled near-atomic resolution of biomolecules."]

Important facts

  • Vikram Sarabhai is regarded as the Father of the Indian Space Programme; ISRO headquarters is in Bengaluru; SAC is in Ahmedabad, Gujarat
  • Aryabhata (1975) was India's first satellite; SLV-3 succeeded in deploying Rohini in 1980 — India's first indigenous orbital launch
  • Mangalyaan (Mars Orbiter Mission) launched 2013, entered Mars orbit September 2014 — India became the first nation to succeed on its first Mars attempt
  • Chandrayaan-3 (2023) made India the first country to land near the lunar south pole and the fourth to soft-land on the Moon
  • Aditya-L1 (2023) is India's first dedicated solar observatory positioned at the Sun-Earth L1 Lagrange point (~1.5 crore km from Earth)
  • INS Vikrant (IAC-1) is India's first indigenous aircraft carrier, commissioned December 3, 2022
  • Prithvi and Agni missiles were developed under IGMDP with A.P.J. Abdul Kalam as project director; BrahMos is Indo-Russian supersonic cruise missile
  • National Science Day is celebrated on February 28 (Raman Effect discovery anniversary); National Technology Day is May 11 (Pokhran-II)
  • C.V. Raman won the 1930 Nobel Prize in Physics for the Raman Effect; M.S. Swaminathan is the Father of India's Green Revolution
  • Khavda Renewable Energy Park in Rann of Kutch (Gujarat) targets ~60 GW solar-wind hybrid capacity — among the world's largest
  • Gujarat hosts ISRO's Space Applications Centre (Ahmedabad), a critical node for satellite design and ground station operations
  • CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing is derived from bacterial adaptive immune systems; AlphaFold (DeepMind) solved the protein folding problem with transformative impact
  • Graphene is a single-atom-thick layer of carbon in hexagonal lattice — strongest known material, excellent conductor; discovered/isolated by Geim and Novoselov (2010 Nobel)
  • UVID (Unified Virtual Identification) concept and DigiLocker are part of India's Digital India infrastructure; UPI revolutionised digital payments in India
  • GIFT City in Gandhinagar is India's first operational international financial and technology hub with regulated crypto/digital asset framework
