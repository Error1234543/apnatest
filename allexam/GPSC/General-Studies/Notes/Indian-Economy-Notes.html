1. Concept of Economic Development and Growth

['Economic growth refers to the increase in real output of goods and services over time, typically measured by the growth rate of Real GDP.', 'Economic development is a broader concept that includes growth plus structural changes like improvement in per capita income, human development indices (literacy, life expectancy), reduction in poverty and inequality.', 'GDP (Gross Domestic Product): Total market value of all final goods and services produced within the domestic territory of a country in a financial year.', 'GNP (Gross National Product): GDP + Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA). GNP measures the total production by residents of a country including those abroad.', 'NDP (Net Domestic Product): GDP - Depreciation (consumption of fixed capital).', 'NNP (Net National Product): GNP - Depreciation. NNP at factor cost is the standard measure of national income.', 'Per Capita Income: NNP at factor cost divided by total population. It gives average income per person but does not reflect income distribution.', 'Real GDP vs Nominal GDP: Nominal GDP is measured at current prices; Real GDP is measured at constant base-year prices, adjusting for inflation.', 'HDI (Human Development Index): Composed of three dimensions - Health (life expectancy at birth), Education (mean years of schooling and expected years of schooling), and Standard of Living (GNI per capita PPP $). Published by UNDP.', 'Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI): Measures acute poverty across health, education, and living standards. India launched its MPI in 2023 using NFSA-2011 NFHS data.', 'Gross Value Added (GVA): Value of output minus value of intermediate consumption. GVA by industry = sector-wise contribution; GVA by use = GDP.']

2. Indian Economy Before and After Independence

['Pre-Independence Indian economy was characterized by deindustrialization under British rule — India became a supplier of raw materials and a consumer of finished British goods.', "Dr. Romesh Dutt and Dadabhai Naoroji propounded the 'Drain of Wealth Theory' — Britain extracted surplus from India without adequate return.", 'The 1943 Bengal Famine killed ~3 million people, exposing the fragility of agrarian economy.', 'Income per capita in 1947 was extremely low; ~70% population dependent on agriculture; literacy rate around 16%; industrial base was minimal.', 'Agricultural productivity was stagnant; land tenure systems (Zamindari, Ryotwari, Mahalwari) created exploitative relations.', 'Post-independence leadership adopted mixed-economy model with significant public sector role, inspired by Fabian socialism and Soviet planning experience.', 'The Sir M. Visvesvaraya Report (1945) recommended rapid industrialization and five-year plans.', 'Planning Commission was set up in 1950 (not by Act of Parliament — executive body); first chairman: Jawaharlal Nehru; key architect: PC Mahalanobis.', 'Nehru-Mahalanobis model emphasized heavy industries as the engine of growth (Second Five-Year Plan, 1956-61).', 'Industrial Policy Resolution 1948 and 1956 classified industries into three schedules: Schedule A (state monopoly), B (public sector主导), C (private sector).']

3. Planning in India — Five-Year Plans and Their Outcomes

['First Five-Year Plan (1951-56): Focus on agriculture ( Irrigation, dams like Bhakra Nangal). Based on Harrod-Domar model. Growth rate: ~3.6%. Success in stabilizing post-independence economy.', 'Second Five-Year Plan (1956-61): Mahalanobis model; emphasis on rapid industrialization especially heavy industries (steel, power, machines). Set up Bokaro, Rourkela, Durgapur steel plants. Growth rate: ~4%.', 'Third Five-Year Plan (1961-66): Aimed at self-reliant economy; failed due to China war 1962, Pakistan war 1965, droughts. Plans were overlapped.', 'Plan Holiday (1966-69): Three Annual Plans introduced. Focus on agriculture (Green Revolution) and food self-sufficiency.', "Fourth Five-Year Plan (1969-74): 'Growth with Stability' and self-reliance. Green Revolution expanded. Bank nationalization (1969), GIC nationalization (1972).", "Fifth Five-Year Plan (1974-78): 'Garibi Hatao' (Remove Poverty) as the central theme. Focus on agricultural productivity and employment. Completed prematurely due to Janata Party government.", 'Sixth Five-Year Plan (1980-85): Introduced by Congress(I). Focus on productivity, self-reliance, modernization. Growth rate: ~5.1%. Family planning and poverty alleviation programs expanded.', "Seventh Five-Year Plan (1985-90): 'Food, Work, Productivity'. Emphasis on human resources development. Growth rate: ~5.8%. Rajiv Gandhi's technology mission started.", 'Eighth Five-Year Plan (1992-97): Post-LPG reforms focus on human capital — education, health, women empowerment. Growth impacted by 1991 crisis recovery.', "Ninth Five-Year Plan (1997-2002): 'Growth with Social Justice and Equity'. Focus on agriculture revival, infrastructure, education.", "Tenth Five-Year Plan (2002-07): 'Accelerated and Inclusive Growth'. Target 8% GDP growth. Reduccd to 7.7%.", "Eleventh Five-Year Plan (2007-12): 'Faster and Sustainable Growth'. Target 10% GDP growth; achieved 7.8%. Millennium Development Goals alignment.", "Twelfth Five-Year Plan (2012-17): 'Faster, Sustainable and More Inclusive Growth'. Target 8.2%; achieved 7.2%. Planning Commission replaced by NITI Aayog in 2015.", 'Key achievements of planning era: Industrial base creation, Green Revolution, expansion of education and health infrastructure, establishment of PSUs, space and nuclear programs.', "Criticisms: Low growth rates pre-1991 ('Hindu rate of growth'), license-permit raj inefficiencies, neglect of agriculture in later plans, rising fiscal deficits."]

4. Liberalisation, Privatisation and Globalisation (LPG Reforms 1991)

['The 1991 economic crisis was triggered by: huge fiscal deficit, rising external debt, falling foreign exchange reserves (enough for only 2-3 weeks of imports), twin deficit problem, balance of payments crisis, and inflation.', 'Rajiv Gandhi and VP Singh governments had initiated partial reforms; Narasimha Rao (PM) and Manmohan Singh (Finance Minister) launched comprehensive reforms.', 'LIBERALISATION: Abolition of industrial licensing (except a few sectors), dismantling of MRTP Act restrictions, FDI liberalisation, disinvestment of PSUs, deregulation of trade and prices.', 'PRIVATISATION: Disinvestment programme started; sale of equity in PSUs to private investors; strategic sale of identified PSUs; closure of sick units;', 'GLOBALISATION: Trade liberalisation — reduction of tariffs and quantitative restrictions, rupee devaluation and convertibility (current account convertibility via Rogri Commission, 1993), integration with world economy, WTO membership (1995).', 'NKM Singh Committee (2015) and Nachiket Mor Committee reviewed financial sector reforms.', 'Key outcomes: GDP growth acceleration (avg ~6-7% post-reforms vs ~3-4% earlier), rise in FDI and FX reserves, growth of IT/ITES sector, expansion of middle class, but also increased regional and sectoral inequalities.', 'Criticism: Deindustrialisation in some sectors, jobless growth, agrarian distress continued, benefits skewed toward urban and service sectors.', 'Key reform milestones: New Industrial Policy 1991, Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA) 1999 replacing FERA, GST implementation 2017, Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code 2016, Demonetisation 2016.', 'NITI Aayog (2015) replaced Planning Commission as the policy think tank with cooperative and competitive federalism approach.']

5. Structure of Indian Economy — Sectors and Employment

['Indian economy has three sectors: Primary (agriculture, forestry, fishing), Secondary (manufacturing, construction), Tertiary (services).', 'India is unique globally: Services sector contributes ~55% of GDP but employs only ~25% of workforce; Agriculture contributes ~18-20% of GDP but employs ~45-50% of workforce — indicating disguised unemployment and low productivity in agriculture.', 'Manufacturing sector contribution is ~28-30% of GDP; India aims to raise it to 25%+ of GDP through Make in India, PLI schemes.', 'Population-based sectoral employment share (PLFS data): Agriculture ~45%, Industry ~25%, Services ~30%.', 'Unorganised sector accounts for about 90% of workforce — lacks social security, formal contracts, and labour protections.', 'Disguised unemployment in agriculture: more workers employed than needed; marginal productivity of additional workers is zero or near zero.', 'Cultural and non-cultivation activities include forestry, fishing, hunting.', 'Sectoral interlinkages: Agriculture supplies raw materials to industry and food to urban workers; Industry provides inputs (fertilisers, machinery) to agriculture; Services support both.', 'Regional disparity: Southern and western states (Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Maharashtra, Gujarat) have higher per capita income and lower dependence on agriculture compared to eastern and northern states (Bihar, Jharkhand, UP).', 'Gender disparity in workforce participation: Female LFPR has been declining paradoxically even as economy grows; recent data shows slight improvement but remains very low (~20-25%).']

6. Agricultural Sector — Issues and Policies

['Agriculture remains the backbone of Indian economy employing nearly half the population and contributing ~18-20% to GDP.', 'Green Revolution (1960s-70s): Introduced high-yielding variety seeds, chemical fertilisers, irrigation, and modern techniques primarily in Punjab, Haryana, and Western UP. Achieved food grain self-sufficiency but caused regional and crop imbalance (rice-wheat monoculture).', 'Current issues: Small and fragmented landholdings (average holding size ~1.08 hectares — marginal and small holders constitute ~86% of total holdings), low productivity, dependence on monsoon (only ~53% of net sown area is irrigated), lack of credit and marketing facilities, soil degradation, water table depletion.', 'Subsidies: Fertilizer subsidy (domestic and imported), irrigation subsidy (power and water), credit subsidy (agricultural loans at concessional rates), MSP procurement operations.', 'Minimum Support Price (MSP): Announced by CACP (Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices) based on C2+50% formula recommended by Swaminathan Committee. Covered 23 crops currently.', 'Agricultural Marketing reforms: e-NAM (National Agriculture Market) — online trading platform for agricultural commodities; Model APMC Act 2003 by GoI.', 'Land reforms: Abolition of Zamindari, tenancy reforms, land ceiling laws — partial success; land records modernisation ongoing.', 'Kissan Credit Card (KCC) scheme provides timely credit to farmers.', 'Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY) provides crop insurance to farmers.', "Micro-irrigation: PMKSY (Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchayee Yojana) — 'Har Khet Ko Paani' and 'Per Drop More Crop'.", 'Recent farm laws (2020-2021) on contract farming, trade outside APMC mandis, and batch selling were repealed after farmer protests in 2021.', 'Agriculture Export Policy 2018 aimed at enhancing agricultural exports.', 'Soil Health Card Scheme: Provides card to farmers with soil nutrient status and fertilizer recommendation.']

7. Industrial Sector — Policies, MSMEs and Key Schemes

['Industrial policy framework governed by the Industrial Policy Resolution 1956 (historic) and now by the Industrial Licensing Policy informed by the Negative List for FDI (2020).', 'Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs): Currently around 260+ CPSEs; ranking system (Maharatna, Navratna, Miniratna I & II) based on performance. Disinvestment target enhanced over time.', 'MSMEs (Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises): Define as per investment and turnover criteria revised in 2020 (combined criteria now). Contribute ~30% of GDP, 45% of exports, and are the largest employer after agriculture.', 'Make in India (2014): Launched to transform India into global manufacturing hub; focus on 25 sectors including automobiles, pharmaceuticals, textiles, food processing, renewable energy.', 'Production Linked Incentive (PLI) Scheme: Launched in 2020 for 14 key sectors — electronics, pharma, automobiles, steel, textiles, telecom, renewable energy, etc. Budget outlay ~₹1.97 lakh crore.', 'Stand Up India Scheme: Facilitates bank loans to SC/ST and women entrepreneurs for setting up greenfield enterprises.', 'MUDRA Yojana: Shishu, Kishore, Tarun categories of loans up to ₹10 lakhs to non-corporate small businesses.', 'Startup India: Launched 2016; provides tax benefits, easy compliance, fund of funds, incubation support.', 'Industrial corridors: Delhi-Mumbai Industrial Corridor (DMIC), Chennai-Bengaluru Industrial Corridor, etc.', 'Key challenges: Land acquisition difficulties, labor law complexities, infrastructure bottlenecks, ease of doing business improvements needed, skill gap, credit access.', 'Labor Codes (2020): Four labor codes consolidating 29 laws — Code on Wages, Industrial Relations, Social Security, and Occupational Safety. Implementation ongoing.']

8. Banking and Financial System

['Reserve Bank of India (RBI): Established 1935, nationalized 1949. Central banking institution; regulates monetary policy, banking system, currency issuance, foreign exchange management (FEMA). Governor is appointed by Central Government.', 'Monetary Policy Committee (MPC): Established 2016 under amended RBI Act 1934. Six members (3 from RBI, 3 from GOI). Determines repo rate to achieve inflation target (4% ± 2%).', 'Commercial Banks: Public sector banks (12 after merger wave 2019-2020), Private sector banks (21 large private + several smaller), Foreign banks, Regional Rural Banks (RRBs), Payments Banks, Small Finance Banks.', 'Bank Nationalization: 1969 (14 banks), 1980 (6 banks). Aimed at social control, branch expansion in rural areas, priority sector lending.', 'Priority Sector Lending (PSL): Banks must lend 40% of ANBC to priority sectors — agriculture, MSEs, education, housing, renewable energy, etc.', 'NPA Crisis: Non-Performing Assets rose sharply post-2015; addressed through IBC (2016), SARFAESI Act (2002), Debt Recovery Tribunals, and bank mergers.', 'Payment Banks (PBs): Accepted deposits up to ₹2 lakh per customer; cannot lend. Examples: Airtel Payments Bank, India Post Payments Bank.', 'Small Finance Banks (SFBs): Provide basic banking and credit to unserved sections. Examples: AU Small Finance Bank, Equitas SFB.', 'Digital Payment Initiatives: UPI (Unified Payments Interface) by NPCI; BHIM app; Aadhaar-enabled Payment System (AePS); Account Aggregator framework.', 'Financial Inclusion: Jan Dhan-Yojana (PMJDY) — over 50 crore accounts opened since 2014; linked to Aadhaar, DigiLockers, direct benefit transfer.', 'NBFCs: Non-Banking Financial Companies regulated by RBI; provide banking without being a bank. HUDCO, NHFC, SIDBI are specialized NBFCs.', 'Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI): Regulates capital markets; established 1988, statutory status 1992.']

9. Fiscal Policy, Budget and Taxation

["Fiscal Policy involves government's revenue (taxation) and expenditure decisions to influence economic conditions.", "Fiscal Deficit: Total expenditure minus total receipts excluding borrowings. Represents the government's total borrowing requirement from the market. Target under FRBM Act: reduced to below 4.5% of GDP by FY 2025-26 (path revised during pandemic).", 'Primary Deficit: Fiscal deficit minus interest payments. Shows borrowings for purposes other than servicing old debt.', 'Revenue Deficit: Revenue expenditure minus revenue receipts. Indicates government is borrowing to meet current expenses.', 'FRBM Act 2003: Fiscal Responsibility and Budget Management Act aims to eliminate revenue deficit and contain fiscal deficit. State governments also required to comply under their own FRBM laws.', 'GST (Goods and Services Tax): Introduced July 1, 2017 via 101st Constitutional Amendment. Replaced multiple indirect taxes (VAT, excise, service tax, octroi, etc.). Dual GST structure — CGST (centre) + SGST (state) for intra-state; IGST (IGST Act) for inter-state. GST Council chaired by Union Finance Minister determines rates.', 'Tax Structure: Direct taxes (Income Tax, Corporate Tax, GST component) vs Indirect taxes. Corporate tax rates were significantly reduced in 2019 (new regime: 22% for existing companies, 15% for new manufacturing companies).', 'Budget Documents: Union Budget includes Appropriation Bill, Finance Bill, Fiscal Policy Statement, macroeconomic framework statement, and sectoral allocation details.', 'Zero-Based Budgeting: Introduced in 2017-18 for all ministries — every expenditure to be justified from zero base each year.', 'Market borrowing: Government raises funds through Treasury Bills (91-day, 182-day, 364-day) and Bonds (via RBI as agent). Public Debt as % of GDP is a key sustainability indicator.']

10. External Sector — Trade, Balance of Payments and FEMA

['Balance of Payments (BoP): Records all economic transactions between residents of a country and the rest of the world in a given period.', 'Current Account: Trade in goods (visible trade) and services (invisible trade), unilateral transfers (remittances, gifts), and income (interest, dividends). Current Account Deficit (CAD) is a key concern.', 'Capital Account: Foreign direct investment (FDI), portfolio investment, external commercial borrowings, NRI deposits, sovereign borrowings.', "India's BoP position: Generally maintained through strong remittance inflows ($111 billion in 2024-25), FDI inflows, and robust FX reserves (~$600+ billion). CAD is manageable but vulnerable to oil price shocks.", 'Trade Policy: Export promotion through FE (Foreign Trade) Policy; Import substitution reduced significantly post-1991. Reduced custom duties on many items.', 'WTO Membership: India is a founding member (1995); actively participates in Doha Round; protects agricultural interests through Special and Differential Treatment provisions.', 'FTA/CEPA Agreements: India has FTAs with several countries including Japan, South Korea, ASEAN countries, Singapore, and recently with UAE, Australia (ECEPA), and is negotiating with UK, EU, Canada.', 'Rupee Trade Settlement: Permitted for cross-border trade in INR under specific bilateral arrangements with designated banks.', 'FEMA 1999: Replaced FERA 1974; liberalized foreign exchange regulations; treats foreign exchange as a commodity rather than contraband.', "Foreign Trade Volume: India's exports ~$450+ billion; imports ~$700+ billion (rough figures for 2024-25); key export items: petroleum products, gems and jewellery, pharmaceuticals, engineering goods, IT services; key imports: crude oil, gold, electronics, machinery."]

11. Inflation and Monetary Policy Tools

['Inflation: Sustained increase in general price level leading to fall in purchasing power of money.', 'Measures of Inflation: CPI (Consumer Price Index) — retail inflation (base 2012=100, released by MoSPI); WPI (Wholesale Price Index) — wholesale inflation (base 2011-12=100, released by DPCI); GDP Deflator — broadest measure.', "CPI is the primary anchor for RBI's monetary policy with target of 4% (+/- 2%). WPI is still useful for tracking wholesale price trends.", 'Types of Inflation: Demand-pull (excess demand), Cost-push (increased input costs), Structural (bottlenecks in supply), Imported (rise in global prices, especially oil).', 'Monetary Policy Tools — Repo Rate: The rate at which RBI lends to commercial banks against securities. Key policy rate; changes directly affect lending rates.', 'Reverse Repo Rate: Rate at which RBI borrows from banks; acts as a floor for short-term rates.', 'Bank Rate: Rate at which RBI lends on long-term basis; mainly used for penal rates.', 'Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR): Percentage of deposits banks must maintain with RBI; affects liquidity.', 'Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR): Percentage of deposits banks must maintain in liquid assets (cash, gold, govt securities); affects credit creation capacity.', 'MSF (Marginal Standing Facility): Rate at which banks can borrow overnight from RBI beyond their CRR quota; acts as a ceiling for short-term rates.', 'Open Market Operations (OMO): Sale/purchase of government securities by RBI to absorb/infuse liquidity.', 'Variable Rate Reverse Repo Auction (VRRRA), LAF (Liquidity Adjustment Facility), and moral suasion are additional instruments.', 'Quantitative Easing / Tightening: Unconventional tools used during crises (like COVID-19) — RBI engaged in OMO purchase programmes.', 'Inflation targeting framework formalized in 2015 via tripartite agreement between Govt, RBI, and GoI.']

Important facts

  • Planning Commission was set up in March 1950; replaced by NITI Aayog on January 1, 2015.
  • Economic reforms of 1991 were led by PM Narasimha Rao and FM Dr. Manmohan Singh.
  • RBI was established on April 1, 1935; nationalized in 1949.
  • GST was implemented from July 1, 2017 via 101st Constitutional Amendment.
  • MGNREGA was enacted in 2005 and came into force in 2006; covers all India except J&K initially, now pan-India.
  • National Food Security Act (NFSA) 2013 provides subsidized food grains to approximately 80 crore people.
  • FEMA replaced FERA in 1999.
  • Seventh Five-Year Plan (1985-90) is considered one of the most successful plans with ~5.8% growth.
  • Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) was established in 2016; has 6 members.
  • India's target for non-fossil energy capacity: 500 GW by 2030; Net zero by 2070.
  • CAGR of GDP growth was ~3.5% during 1950-90 and ~6% during 1991-2020.
  • Dr. BR Ambedkar was the first Chairman of the Drafting Committee of the Constitution; also instrumental in establishing RBI's framework.
  • NITI Aayog has no executive powers — it is a think tank, unlike the Planning Commission which allocated funds.
  • Maharatna status allows PSUs greater autonomy in decision-making and foreign collaborations; currently 10 PSUs hold Maharatna status.
  • C2 formula (Swaminathan Committee) recommends MSP at 50% above weighted average cost of production.
  • PMJDY launched in 2014; over 50 crore accounts opened as of 2024.
  • India's FX reserves have crossed $600 billion in recent years.
  • PLI scheme covers 14 sectors with an outlay of approximately ₹1.97 lakh crore.
  • The Human Development Index (HDI) is published by UNDP; India's rank improved but remains in medium human development category.

Formulas and relationships

GDP at Market Price = C + I + G + (X - M) where C=Consumption, I=Investment, G=Government spending, X=Exports, M=Imports
GNP = GDP + Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA)
NDP = GDP - Depreciation
NNP = GNP - Depreciation (NNP at factor cost = National Income)
Per Capita Income = National Income / Total Population
Real GDP = (Nominal GDP / GDP Deflator) × 100
GVA at basic prices = GVA at factor cost + Indirect Taxes - Subsidies
Inflation Rate = ((CPI_current - CPI_base) / CPI_base) × 100
Fiscal Deficit = Total Expenditure - Total Receipts (excluding borrowings)
Primary Deficit = Fiscal Deficit - Interest Payments
Revenue Deficit = Revenue Expenditure - Revenue Receipts
CAD (Current Account Deficit) = (Imports - Exports) + Net Income from Abroad + Net Transfers
Repo Rate Effect: Higher repo rate → higher lending rates → lower credit → lower demand-pull inflation

Common mistakes

  • Confusing GDP with GNP — remember: GNP adds Net Factor Income from Abroad to GDP; GDP is territorial while GNP is citizenship-based.
  • Thinking Planning Commission was a constitutional body — it was an executive body set up by a resolution; NITI Aayog is also non-constitutional.
  • Confusing FRBM Act with the Constitution — FRBM is an Act of Parliament (2003), not a constitutional provision.
  • Mistaking CPI and WPI — CPI is for retail/inflation targeting; WPI is for wholesale prices. RBI targets CPI inflation, not WPI.
  • Thinking GST abolished all indirect taxes — it replaced many but not all ( Customs duty, Stamp duty, State-level taxes like stamp duty on property remain).
  • Assuming MGNREGA is a welfare scheme — it is a right-based employment guarantee scheme; workers have a legal right to demand work.
  • Confusing Repo Rate with Bank Rate — Repo Rate is for overnight lending against securities; Bank Rate is for long-term lending without collateral.
  • Thinking FDI and FII are the same — FDI involves lasting interest and control in enterprise; FII is portfolio investment without control.
  • Assuming economic reforms of 1991 ended all controls — industrial licensing was abolished for most sectors but some remain; FDI still has negative list.
  • Forgetting that NITI Aayog does not allocate funds — unlike Planning Commission, it has no financial allocation powers.
  • Confusing Disguised Unemployment with Open Unemployment — Disguised means more people employed than needed (marginal product = 0); Open means actively seeking work but unemployed.
  • Thinking HDI is calculated by World Bank — it is published by UNDP; World Bank publishes GDP per capita and other indicators.
  • Mistaking CPI formula — it measures price changes of a basket of goods; base year changes affect comparison; do not confuse with GDP deflator which covers all domestically produced goods.
  • Assuming all subsidies are removed post-1991 — fertilizer, food (PDS), and fuel subsidies continue; only some industrial subsidies were removed.
  • Confusing Primary Deficit with Revenue Deficit — Primary Deficit = Fiscal Deficit minus interest payments; Revenue Deficit = Revenue Exp minus Revenue Receipts.
