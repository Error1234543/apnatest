Quick Revision: Indian History and Freedom Struggle — GPSC General Studies

GPSCEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Swaraj: Self-rule; popularized by Bal Gangadhar Tilak as 'Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it.' Later redefined by Gandhi as complete independence and self-governance.
  • Satyagraha: Coined by Gandhi in 1906 during the South Africa struggle; means 'truth-force' or 'soul-force,' a method of non-violent resistance.
  • Non-Cooperation Movement: Launched by Gandhi in 1920; citizens withdrew cooperation from British institutions — schools, courts, legislative councils, foreign goods.
  • Civil Disobedience Movement: Started with the Dandi March (1930); involved open defiance of specific laws, unlike Non-Cooperation which was withdrawal-based.
  • Quit India Movement: Launched by Gandhi on 8 August 1942; demand was 'Do or Die' — immediate British withdrawal from India.
  • Moderates (1885–1905): Early Congress leaders like Dadabhai Naoroji, G.K. Gokhale, Surendranath Banerjea; believed in petitions, prayers, and constitutional methods.
  • Extremists (1905–1919): Leaders like Tilak, Bipin Chandra Pal, Lala Lajpat Rai; advocated Swaraj, boycott, and national education.
  • Swadeshi Movement: Boycott of British goods and promotion of indigenous products, launched after the 1905 Partition of Bengal.
  • Revolutionary Terrorism: Armed revolutionary groups like Bhagat Singh, Chandrashekhar Azad, Rajguru, Sukhdev who believed in violent overthrow of British rule.
  • Mountbatten Plan: Announced on 3 June 1947; provided for the partition of India into two dominions — India and Pakistan.
  • Acts as Constitutional Milestones: Regulating Act 1773, Pitt's India Act 1784, Charter Acts (1833, 1853), Indian Councils Act 1861/1909/1919, Government of India Act 1935 — each progressively expanded Indian participation in governance.

Important facts

  • First Nawab of Awadh: Saadat Ali Khan (1722) — Context for later revolts
  • Battle of Plassey: 23 June 1757 — Foundation of British political power in India
  • Battle of Buxar: 22 October 1764 — Consolidated British control over Bengal
  • Regulating Act 1773: First step by British Parliament to control EIC — Established Governor-General of Bengal (Warren Hastings)
  • Pitt's India Act 1784: Created Board of Control to supervise EIC — Dual control system begun
  • Permanent Settlement: Introduced by Lord Cornwallis in 1793 (Bengal, Bihar, Odisha) — Zamindari system established
  • Dalhousie's Doctrine of Lapse applied to: Satara (1848), Jhansi (1854), Nagpur (1854), Sambalpur (1849) — Major cause of 1857 Revolt
  • Treaty of Sugauli (1816): With Nepal; fixed boundary — British expansion context
  • Indian Councils Act 1861: Expanded GG's executive council; introduced portfolio system — Beginning of Indian legislative participation
  • Indian National Congress: Founded 28 December 1885, Bombay; first session president: W.C. Bonnerjee — Foundation of organized national movement
  • Partition of Bengal: 16 October 1905, Lord Curzon — Triggered Swadeshi Movement
  • Jallianwala Bagh Massacre: 13 April 1919, General Dyer, Amritsar — Turning point in freedom struggle
  • Non-Cooperation Movement: 1920–1922, called off after Chauri Chaura (4 Feb 1922) — First mass movement under Gandhi
  • Dandi March: 12 March – 6 April 1930; 241 miles; symbolically broke salt law on 6 April — Start of Civil Disobedience Movement
  • Gandhi-Irwin Pact: 5 March 1931; Gandhi agreed to attend Second Round Table Conference — Temporary truce in Civil Disobedience
  • Poona Pact: 24 September 1932; between Gandhi and Ambedkar; reserved seats for depressed classes within general electorate — Important constitutional development
  • Quit India resolution: 8 August 1942, Bombay session; Gandhi's 'Do or Die' — Final major Congress movement
  • Royal Indian Navy Mutiny: 18–23 February 1946, Bombay — Showed British couldn't rely on armed forces
  • Indian Independence Act: Passed by British Parliament, July 1947; came into effect 15 August 1947 — Legal basis for partition and independence
  • Mountbatten Plan announced: 3 June 1947 — Immediate steps toward partition
  • First President of Independent India: Dr. Rajendra Prasad (Constituent Assembly, 1946) — Constitution-making context
  • Integration of Princely States: Completed by September 1947 (Junagadh accessoin to Pakistan first, then ruled out by plebiscite); Hyderabad operation (Police Action, 1948); Junagadh acceded to India after plebiscite in 1948 — Patel's achievement, highly relevant for GPSC

Memory aids

  • INC Presidents chronological order for quick recall: 1885 W.C. Bonnerjee, 1886 Dadabhai Naoroji, 1887 Badruddin Tyabji, 1888 Sir William Wedderburn (first European), 1889 S.N. Banerjee, 1890 Madam Bhagwati Charan Verma (first woman, actually Annie Besant was first woman president in 1917), 1896 Dadabhai Naoroji (third time), 1906 Dadabhai Naoroji (fourth time) — remember 'Naoroji four times, Besant first woman, Tyabji Muslim, Wedderburn European'
  • Lal-Bal-Pal: Lajpat Rai (Punjab), Bal Gangadhar Tilak (Maharashtra), Bipin Chandra Pal (Bengal) — extreme trio of early 20th century
  • Gandhi's movements sequence: Champaran (1917, first) → Ahmedabad Strike (1918) → Kheda (1918) → Rowlatt/Satyagraha (1919) → Non-Cooperation (1920–22) → Swaraj Party (1923–29) → Civil Disobedience (1930–34) → Quit India (1942)
  • Acts memory chain: 1773 Regulating → 1784 Pitt's → 1833 Charter (India became possession of Crown) → 1858 Government of India Act (Company ended, Crown began) → 1861 Indian Councils (expansion) → 1909 Morley-Minto (separate electorates) → 1919 Montagu-Chelmsford (dyarchy) → 1935 GOI Act (provincial autonomy)
  • Revolt leaders quick map: North-Central = 1857 leaders; Punjab = Akali movement later; Bengal = Swadeshi + revolutionaries; South = no major 1857 participation (except some parts)
  • Gujarat connection: Sardar Patel (Bardoli Satyagraha 1928) → 'Sardar' title; Mahatma Gandhi (born Porbandar, educated Rajkot); VP Menon (administrative integration); K.M. Munshi (Gujarat's own contribution); note these for GPSC

Exam tips

  • GPSC focuses on chronology and cause-effect. When asked about any revolt/movement, expect questions on: What caused it? Who led it? What was the outcome? What came after?
  • Gujarat-specific facts carry extra weight: Sardar Patel's role (Bardoli, integration of states), Gandhi's early life in Gujarat (Porbandar birth, Rajkot education, Ahmedabad strikes), Gujarat's participation in 1857 (limited but exists in some regions).
  • For Acts, remember the KEY FEATURE of each rather than all details. 1858 = Crown rule; 1909 = Separate Electorate; 1919 = Dyarchy; 1935 = Provincial Autonomy + Federal court.
  • Questions on 1857 Revolt often ask why it is called 'First War of Independence' (Vinayak Damodar Savarkar's term) vs 'Sepoy Mutiny' (British view). Be prepared for both perspectives.
  • GPSC may ask matching questions: Leader ↔ Movement (e.g., Tilak ↔ Swadeshi/Home Rule; Bose ↔ INA; Patel ↔ Bardoli/Integration; Gandhi ↔ Salt March).
  • Chronology questions are common: Arrange movements/events in order. Practice sequencing: 1857 → 1885 → 1905 → 1919 → 1920 → 1930 → 1942 → 1947.
  • Don't ignore constitutional milestones: 1909, 1919, 1935 are high-yield. Know what each introduced.
  • The question 'Who gave the slogan?' type is frequent: 'Do or Die' (Gandhi), 'Swaraj is my birthright' (Tilak), 'Give me blood and I will give you freedom' (Bose), 'Jai Hind' (Bose/INA), 'Quit India' (Gandhi).
  • Be careful with dates — especially years of movements. If unsure of exact date, eliminate options using process of elimination based on known sequences.
  • For the freedom struggle post-1940, focus on:INA trials (1945), Royal Indian Navy Mutiny (1946), Cabinet Mission (1946), Direct Action Day (1946), Mountbatten Plan (1947), Independence Act (1947) — this sequence is a frequent question area.

Common traps

  • Confusing the year of Jallianwala Bagh (1919) with Rowlatt Act (also 1919) — both same year, but Jallianwala was April, Rowlatt Act was March. Confusion arises because both relate to same period.
  • Thinking Non-Cooperation Movement was started in 1921 — it was actually launched in August 1920 and called off in February 1922.
  • Mixing up Gandhi-Irwin Pact (1931) with Lincolnshire Declaration (1934) — the Pact ended the first phase of Civil Disobedience; the Declaration was about future constitutional settlement.
  • Assuming 1935 Act introduced Dominion Status — it did NOT. It introduced Provincial Autonomy. Dominion Status came only in 1947.
  • Attributing the Permanent Settlement to Dalhousie instead of Cornwallis (1793).
  • Thinking the first President of Independent India was Sardar Patel — he was the first Home Minister. Dr. Rajendra Prasad was President of Constituent Assembly.
  • Confusing Nehru Report (1928) with Langley Committee (1929) or Simon Commission (1927) — different things, different purposes.
  • Attributing INA to Subhash Bose alone without acknowledging that the original INA was formed in 1943 in Southeast Asia with Japanese help, while the Azad Hind Fauj had earlier roots (1942, Mohan Singh).
  • Thinking the Quit India Movement was a Gandhi-Nehru consensus — actually, many senior Congress leaders were hesitant; it was largely Gandhi's initiative.
  • Forgetting that the Cabinet Mission (1946) came BEFORE the Mountbatten Plan (1947) — chronological confusion.
  • Confusing the partition of Bengal (1905) with the partition of India (1947) — different events, different people (Curzon vs Mountbatten).
