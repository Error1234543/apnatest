History and Culture of Gujarat — Quick Revision

GPSCEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Gujarat refers to the westernmost state of India, historically known as 'Saurashtra' and 'Gurjaratra', with its cultural identity shaped by the Indus Valley Civilization, ancient trade routes, Hindu and Jain traditions, Islamic sultanates, and British colonial influence.
  • The term 'Gujarati' encompasses both the language (Indo-Aryan branch, closely related to Hindi and Rajasthani with significant Sanskrit and Persian loanwords) and the cultural practices of the region's people.
  • Indus Valley Civilization (IVC) sites in Gujarat represent the earliest known urban culture in the region, dating to c. 3300–1300 BCE, with Lothal being the most commercially significant site.
  • Gurjar-Pratihara dynasty (8th–11th century CE) was a major Rajput dynasty that ruled large parts of northwestern India, including Gujarat, and played a key role in halting Arab advances into India.
  • Sultanate of Gujarat (1407–1573 CE) was an independent Islamic kingdom based in Ahmadabad, known for its architectural synthesis, maritime trade, and patronage of literature and arts.
  • Presidency of Bombay (1818–1947) included Gujarat as part of the larger Bombay Presidency under British administration until reorganization on linguistic lines in 1960.

Key concepts

  • Ancient Gujarat — Indus Valley Sites: Gujarat contains some of the most important IVC sites: Dholavira (Kutch) — a large walled city with advanced water management and a signboard; Lothal (Ahmedabad district) — a major port city with a dockyard, bead factory, and warehouse; Bet Dwarka (off Dwarka coast) — submerged settlement; Rangpur and Surkotada — fortified settlements with horse remains. Dholavira is inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site (2021).
  • Ancient Gujarat — Historical Periods: Post-IVC: Ahar-Banas culture, Painted Grey Ware. Mauryan period: Ashoka's edicts at Sopara and Girnar (Rock Edict XIII). Shunga-Kushana period: Bactrian Greek influence. Gupta period: relative peace and temple building begins. Post-Gupta: Maitraka dynasty of Valabhi (5th–8th century) made Valabhi a renowned centre of learning rivaling Nalanda. Pala dynasty briefly followed.
  • Chaulukya (Solanki) Dynasty (c. 960–1244 CE): The greatest Hindu dynasty of medieval Gujarat. Capital: Anhilwad Patan. Key rulers: Mularaja I (founder), Bhima I, Karnadeva II (victorious over Ghazni invasions), Kumarapala (converted to Jainism, patron of hemachandra), Siddharaja Jaisinhji (conquered Malwa, built Modhera Sun Temple). Known for exceptional temple architecture — Modhera Sun Temple, Sarangapanath Mahadev, Rani ki Vav at Patan (UNESCO). Hemachandra authored 'Dvyashraya Kavya', 'Yogaashastra', and 'Siddhashasthra'.
  • Sultanate of Gujarat (1407–1573 CE): Founded by Zafat Khan (Alauddin Muzaffar Shah). Ahmedabad became capital in 1411. Notable rulers: Ahmad Shah I (founded Ahmedabad), Mahmud Begada (conquered Gujarat fully, defeated Mewar at Dediala), Bahadur Shah (faced Portuguese, died fighting Marathas). Architectural achievements: Sidi Sayyid Mosque (jali screens), Jama Masjid Ahmedabad, Sat Manzil, Jami Masjid Junagadh. End: conquered by Akbar in 1573 after defend of Gujarat.
  • Mughal Period in Gujarat: Gujarat became a Mughal subah (province). Akbar integrated it administratively. Jahangir's wife Nur Jahan had ties to Gujarat. Aurangzeb's policies led to local unrest. Maratha interference increased in 18th century. Treaty of Surat (1775) between British and Raghunathrao. British control consolidated after Second Anglo-Maratha War (1803).
  • Freedom Struggle in Gujarat: Early: 1857 participation in Kheda, Surat, Ahmedabad. Bhil uprisings against British. 20th century: Narayan Mehta (social reformer), Thakkar Bapa (tribal welfare), Vallabhbhai Patel ('Iron Man of India', led Bardoli Satyagraha 1928 — earned title 'Sardar'), Mahatma Gandhi (started satyagraha movement at Champaran 1917, then Ahmedabad textile mill strike 1918, Kheda 1918). Gandhi Ashram at Sabarmati (1915). Dandi March 1930 started from Sabarmati Ashram.Quit India Movement (1942): strong participation from Surat, Bardoli, Vijapur. Post-independence: integration of princely states (Junagadh accession crisis 1947, Hyderabad movement). Patel played pivotal role in merger of ~170 princely states.
  • Bhakti and Saint Tradition: Gujarat produced many influential saints: Kabir (born in Varanasi but influenced Gujarat deeply through Pushti Marg), Vallabhacharya (1479–1531, founder of Pushti Marg/Vishishtadvaita, born in Konark but active in Gujarat), Narsi Mehta (15th century devotional poet, composed padas in praise of Krishna), Tukaram (Marathi but influenced Gujarat), Basava (Kannada, but Virashaiva tradition influenced Gujarat). Jain acharyas: Hemachandra, Jinapragya, Yogendra. Sufi saints: Mu'in ud Din Chishti (Ajmer but influence spread), various Sufi orders active in Gujarat.
  • Art and Architecture: Temple architecture: Solanki style (Modhera Sun Temple, Ranakpur Jain Temple — though Ranakpur is in Rajasthan, closely linked), stepwells (Rani ki Vav, Dada Hari ni Vav, Saraswati Vav). Islamic architecture: Sidi Sayyid Mosque (famous for stone lace jalis), Ahmadabad Friday Mosque, Jami Masjid Junagadh, Qutb Shahi structures in Junagadh. Colonial: Gujarat Vidhyapeeth, Government Museum Ahmedabad, Rajmandir Palace. Modern: CEPT University campus, Gujarat High Court (Gandhinagar).
  • Literature and Language: Classical: 'Prabandhakosh' by Merutunga (14th century), 'Razmnama' translations. Medieval: 'Leela Purushcharana' by Haribhadra Suri, 'Yogashastra' by Hemachandra, 'Gita Govinda' influence on Gujarati poetry. Modern: Narmad (Narsinh Mehta — 19th century literary figure, not to be confused with saint), Mahadev Desai (Gandhi's secretary and translator), Jhaverchand Meghani (folklore collector, 'Kanth Aako'), Umashankar Joshi (Jnanpith winner, 'Pushp Chori'), Kakasaheb Kalelkar, Shivdatarya. Gujarati literature has rich tradition of 'pad', 'abhanga', 'stotra', 'precha', and modern genres.
  • Folk Culture and Performing Arts: Dances: Garba (circular folk dance around Goddess Durga, especially during Navratri), Raas (mimics Krishna's Raas Leela), Bhavai (acrobatic folk theatre with performers balancing pots on head), Terah Taali (13 claps — one on each finger plus chest), Dandia Raas (stick dance). Music: Ajeeb (devotional), Kalariya (folk ballads), Lopanto (funeral songs), Bhangra influence. Puppetry: Kathputli (Rajasthani influence), Patsan (string puppets). Theatre: Jatra (folk drama), Tamasha (Marathi influence).
  • Festivals of Gujarat: Navratri (9 nights, celebrated with Garba and Dandia — largest in the world in Gujarat); Diwali (celebrated with lights, sweets, business new year); Holi (Phagwah); Uttarayana/Makar Sankranti (kite flying festival, International Kite Festival in Ahmedabad); Rathyatra (Puri style celebrated in Gujarat); Tarnetar Fair (trade fair, marriage market); Palitana Bhawaneshwar Fair; Saputara Tribal Festival; Chhath Puja (growing popularity); Ganesh Chaturthi; Janmashtami; Mahavir Jayanti; Bakrid; Eid al-Fitr; Christmas (Goa-Gujarat connection).
  • Crafts and Handicrafts: Bandhani (tie-dye textile, especially from Jetpur and Khavda); Patola silk (double ikat, exclusively from Patan, extremely expensive); Kutchi embroidery (soof, rabri, chenille work — UNESCO recognised); Ajrakh block printing (fromJamnagar and Kutch); Rogan art (red/black wax resist from Nirona); Blue pottery (from Jaipur but practiced in Gujarat); Brassware (from Morvi and Wankaner); Woodwork (from Junagadh and Somnath); Ivory carving (historical, now banned); Bead making (Lothal tradition continues in Gujarat).
  • Tribal Culture of Gujarat: Gujarat has significant tribal population (scheduled tribes include Bhil, Rabari, Charan, Anavil, Dhodia, Kokna, Warshangli, etc.). Rabari community known for cattle rearing and distinctive embroidery. Bhil community primarily in Botad, Jamnagar, Devbhumi Dwarka districts. Garasia tribe in Eastern Gujarat. Each tribe has distinct dress, jewellery, dance, and social organisation. Tribal art includes Gotapujaa (dance-drama), Bhungpuj (art on walls), and intricate bead work.
  • Important Historical Places/Heritage Sites: Dholavira (UNESCO, Kutch) — IVC city; Lothal (UNESCO tentative, Ahmedabad dist.) — IVC port; Rani ki Vav (UNESCO, Patan) — stepwell; Modhera Sun Temple (Sun temple, Mehsana dist.) — Solanki architecture; Somnath Temple (Prabhas Patan, Gir) — one of twelve Jyotirlingas, rebuilt multiple times; Palitana (Shatrunjaya Hills) — 900+ Jain temples, sacred to Svetambara Jains; Girnar (Junagadh) — rocks with Ashokan edicts, Jain temples; Uparkot Fort (Junagadh); Sidi Sayyid Mosque (Ahmedabad); Adalaj Stepwell (Vadodara dist.); Bhimkund (Rajkot dist.); Bhavnagar Palace; Laxmi Vilas Palace ( Vadodara); Chamba Lake (Chamba). The Rann of Kutch hosts the annual Rann Utsav.
  • Notable Historical Figures: Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel (1875–1950): Iron Man of India, first Deputy PM, integrated princely states; Mahatma Gandhi (1869–1948): Father of Nation, born Porbandar, activist from Ahmedabad; Thakkar Bapa (1882–1998): Tribal welfare pioneer, 'Father of Tribal Welfare'; Narayan Mehta (1918–2016): Social reformer, founded Navneeti and Pratinidhi Mandal; Dr. Rajendra Singh (1930–2020): Water conservationist, Right Livelihood Award winner; Hariprasad Chaurasia (b. 1938): Bansuri maestro from Indore but associated with Gujarat; Pt. Vishnu Narayan Bhatkhande influenced Gujarat music; Jhaverchand Meghani (1882–1947): Folklore scholar, poet; Umashankar Joshi (1911–1987): Jnanpith laureate poet.
  • Integration of Princely States (1947–1950): After independence, Gujarat region consisted of: Bombay Presidency (British), plus ~170 princely states including Saurashtra States Union (integrated 1948), Kutch State (merged 1948), Travancore-Cochin border areas. Sardar Patel and V.P. Menon negotiated integration. Junagadh crisis (1947) — Nawab acceded to Pakistan, popular revolt led to accession to India after plebiscite. Bombay State formed on 1 May 1960 by reorganisation on linguistic basis, splitting into Maharashtra and Gujarat.

Important facts

  • Dholavira (Kutch) is the only IVC site with a three-part division (citadel, middle town, lower town) and an elaborate water harvesting system.
  • Rani ki Vav at Patan was built by Queen Udayamati in memory of her husband Bhimdev I (Chaulukya period) and is a UNESCO World Heritage Site (2014).
  • Modhera Sun Temple was built by Siddharaja Jaisinhji in 1026 CE, oriented so that no shadow of the main structure falls on the platform during equinoxes.
  • The Gujarat Sultanate lasted 166 years (1407–1573), founded by Muzaffar Shah I.
  • Bardoli Satyagraha (1928) was led by Sardar Patel; peasants refused to pay taxes until grievances were addressed — Patel earned title 'Sardar'.
  • Mahatma Gandhi started his first satyagraha in India at Champaran (1917), then Ahmedabad Textile Mill Strike (1918), Kheda Satyagraha (1918), and Dandi March (1930).
  • Sabarmati Ashram was established by Gandhi in 1915; Dandi March departed on 12 March 1930.
  • The Rann of Kutch covers approximately 7,500 sq km and is a salt desert wetland of international importance (Ramsar site).
  • Somnath Temple is the first among twelve Jyotirlingas; currently stands in its seventh reconstruction (original destroyed by Mahmud Ghazni in 1024 CE).
  • Patola silk is a double ikat weave technique producing incredibly complex patterns; authentic Patan Patolas can cost lakhs of rupees per set.
  • Gujarat has 4 UNESCO World Heritage Sites: Champaner-Pavagadh Archaeological Park (2004), Rani ki Vav (2014), Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Terminus (shared, Mumbai — not Gujarat), and Dholavira: A Harappan City (2021). Correction: actually Champaner-Pavagadh (2004), Rani ki Vav (2014), and Dholavira (2021) — total 3 in Gujarat proper.
  • Gujarat became a separate state on 1 May 1960 under the Bombay Reorganisation Act, 1960.
  • The Gir Forest in Gujarat is the only natural habitat of Asiatic lions (Panthera leo persica), with approximately 600+ lions as per recent census.
  • Kutch is the largest district of Gujarat by area, covering approximately 45,652 sq km.
  • The Sapta Kushavarta (seven sacred pilgrimage sites) of Gujarat includes: Prabhas Patan, Somnath, Gopi Talav, Dwaraka, Shoolarany, Gomti Ghat, and Triveni Sangam near Veraval.

Memory aids

  • mnemonic for important IVC sites in Gujarat: 'Dhol-Lothal-Bet-Sur' → think 'Dollar Beth Sure' — Dholavira, Lothal, Bet Dwarka, Surkotada.
  • Chaulukya dynasty timeline: remember 'Bhima-Karna-Siddha' (Bhima I → Karnadeva II → Siddharaja Jaisinhji) as the golden era rulers.
  • For freedom struggle chronology in Gujarat: Champaran(1917) → Ahmedabad Mill Strike(1918) → Kheda(1918) → Non-Cooperation(1920-22) → Bardoli(1928) → Civil Disobedience/Dandi(1930) → Quit India(1942).
  • Stepwells (Vavs) of Gujarat: Rani ki Vav (Patan), Dada Hari ni Vav (Gandhinagar), Saraswati Vav (Thol), Bhimal Vav (Gondal), Mithal Vav (Palitana) — remember 'RDSA' for top 4.
  • To remember festivals in order: Navratri(Oct) → Diwali(Oct/Nov) → Uttarayana(Jan 14-15) → Holi(March) → Tarnetar(every year, during Sharad Purnima).
  • Art forms memory chain: Garba(Raas) → Bhavai(trick) → Terah Taali(clap) → Lopanto(funnel) → Kalariya(ballad) → Ajeeb(devotion).
  • Tribal communities quick recall: 'Rabari-Rafting-Rajasthani border', 'Bhil-Botad-Jamnagar-Devbhumi', 'Garasia-Eastern Gujarat forest', 'Charan-Charan-writers-historians', 'Anavil-Agrarian-servants'.

Exam tips

  • For GPSC, expect questions on IVC sites in Gujarat (Dholavira and Lothal are highest priority), freedom struggle events specific to Gujarat (Champaran, Ahmedabad Mill Strike, Kheda, Bardoli, Dandi March), and cultural aspects (festivals, arts, crafts).
  • Memorise dates carefully: 1407 (Gujarat Sultanate founded), 1573 (Sultanate fell to Mughals), 1928 (Bardoli), 1930 (Dandi March), 1960 (Gujarat formation), 2014 (Rani ki Vav UNESCO), 2021 (Dholavira UNESCO).
  • Understand the significance, not just the name: why is Rani ki Vav UNESCO? Why is Dholavira unique among IVC sites? Why is Bardoli important? GPSC tests conceptual clarity.
  • For Art & Culture questions, focus on 'first', 'only', 'largest', 'rare' type facts — e.g., Gir is the only home of Asiatic lions, Patola is the rarest silk, Rann of Kutch is the largest salt desert.
  • When asked about saints, link them to their sect/movement: Vallabhacharya (Pushti Marg), Narsi Mehta (Krishna bhakti), Hemachandra (Jainism). This helps distinguish between similar-looking options.
  • Practice map-based questions: locate Dholavira (Kutch), Lothal (Ahmedabad dist.), Somnath (Prabhas Patan, dist. Junagadh), Palitana (dist. Rajkot), Modhera (dist. Mehsana), Junagadh, Bhuj (Kutch).
  • Read current affairs related to Gujarat heritage: any new UNESCO nominations, archaeological findings, temple restoration news, or cultural festivals covered in newspapers.
  • Differentiate between 'Gujarat' as a geographical/cultural entity and 'Gujarat State' as a political entity formed in 1960. Questions may refer to either context.
  • Be prepared for assertion-reason type questions linking historical events (e.g., 'Sardar Patel led Bardoli Satyagraha' — reason: it was a successful tax resistance movement).'
  • Revision strategy: make flashcards for dates+events, locations+significance pairs, and artisan-craft matchings. Quick revision means focused, high-yield recall rather than passive reading.

Common traps

  • Confusing Loktak Lake (Manipur) with the Rann of Kutch — Loktak is a freshwater lake with floating islands, while Rann of Kutch is a seasonal salt desert in Gujarat.
  • Mixing up dates: Sardar Patel's Bardoli Satyagraha was 1928, NOT 1930 (which was Dandi March). Both are separate events led by Patel and Gandhi respectively.
  • Assuming Somnath Temple was built only once — it was destroyed and rebuilt at least 5 times (by Aluptya, Mularaja, Bhima I, Muzaffar Shah, and post-independence government). The current structure was completed in 1951.
  • Thinking Dholavira is in Sindh (Pakistan) — it is in Kutch district, Gujarat, India. Some IVC sites like Mohenjo-Daro and Harappa are in Pakistan, but Dholavira and Lothal are definitively in Gujarat.
  • Confusing the location of Rani ki Vav: it is at Patan (Anhilwad Patan), not at Ahmedabad or Somnath.
  • Thinking Patola is single ikat — it is double ikat (both warp and weft are tie-dyed before weaving), making it extraordinarily difficult and rare.
  • Mixing up Gujarat Sultanate dates: 1407–1573, not 1500s-only or earlier. Muzaffar Shah I founded it in 1407.
  • Attributing the construction of Lothal dockyard to the Chalukyas — it was built during the IVC period (c. 2400 BCE), not medieval period.
  • Forgetting that the Asiatic lion is found ONLY in Gujarat's Gir Forest — not in any other Indian state or wild location globally.
  • Confusing Mahatma Gandhi's birthplace (Porbandar) with his ashram location (Ahmedabad/Sabarmati) — both are in Gujarat but different places.
  • Thinking Gujarat became a separate state in 1947 — it was formed on 1 May 1960, 13 years after independence, under the Bombay Reorganisation Act.
  • Mistaking Kutch for a city — Kutch (or Cutch) is a region/district, not a single city; its administrative headquarters is Bhuj.
