Definitions

  • Current Affairs refers to recent and ongoing events of significance in the national, international, and state (Gujarat) spheres, including government policies, appointments, schemes, scientific developments, sports achievements, awards, indices/reports, and major summits/summits.
  • PYQ-STYLE PRACTICE note: In GPSC Prelims and Mains, current affairs are tested through direct factual recall, statement-based MCQs, matching pairs, and analytical application — especially for topics intersecting with Economy, Polity, Science & Technology, and Environment.
  • GPSC focuses on events from approximately the last 12–18 months before the exam notification date, with higher weightage given to Gujarat-specific developments and national/international events that have policy or economic implications for India and Gujarat.

Important facts

Gujarat played a role through the Vibrant Gujarat connect and GIFT City's integration into global financial networks.
  • BRICS expanded in 2024 to include Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran, and the UAE (Saudi Arabia's participation remains under discussion); this reshapes India's multilateral engagement strategy with consequences for energy trade, rupee-rial settlement discussions, and Gujarat's port-centric trade patterns.
  • India's Gaganyaan human spaceflight programme advanced with successful crew module atmospheric re-entry vehicle (CARAV) tests and impact pad landing trials; the first uncrewed test flight and subsequent crewed mission timelines remain central to science-and-technology questions.
  • ISRO's commercial arm Antrix Corporation continues to drive satellite-launch services and technology-transfer agreements; Gujarat hosts significant space-sector downstream industries near Gandhinagar and Ahmedabad clusters.
  • The India-Middle East-Europe Economic Corridor (IMEC) agreement signed at the G20 New Delhi Summit aims to connect India to the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Jordan, Israel, and Europe via rail and ship routes — Gujarat's ports and the Delhi-Mumbai Industrial Corridor (DMIC) are strategic nodes.
  • GIFT City (Gandhinagar International Finance Technology City) received significant regulatory concessions under the International Financial Services Centres Authority (IFSCA), enabling trading in foreign currencies, time-zone-aligned global markets, and eased regulatory frameworks attracting multinational firms.
  • Gujarat's green hydrogen mission targets positioning the state as a global hub; the Gujarat Green Hydrogen Policy 2023 outlines incentives for electrolyser manufacturing, green fuel production, and export-oriented projects at Koyna and Kandla special economic zones.
  • The National Logistics Policy (2022) and its state-level implementation, including Gujarat's logistics parks and multi-modal connectivity projects under the Dedicated Freight Corridor, continue to reduce India's logistics cost from approximately 13-14% of GDP toward the targeted 8%.
  • India's digital public infrastructure — Unified Payments Interface (UPI), Account Aggregator framework, On-Demand Service Platform, and the Bhartiya Bitcoin Protocol — saw transaction volumes crossing several trillion monthly UPI transactions, reinforcing India's fintech leadership position.
  • Gujarat's coastal regulation and marine biodiversity initiatives gained attention following cyclone preparedness upgrades; the state's disaster management infrastructure and early-warning systems became case studies in national and international forums.
  • The Prime Minister's Vishwakarma Scheme (2023) provides recognition, skill upgradation, and collateral-free credit to traditional artisans and craftspeople — relevant to Gujarat's rich artisanal heritage including Patola weavers, Kutch embroidery artists, and brass craftsmen.
  • Indigenous defence indigenisation lists and Make in India defence manufacturing have intensified joint ventures between Indian PSUs and private firms; Gujarat has become a significant hub for defence ancillary manufacturing, particularly near Sanand and Halol industrial areas.
  • India's space and satellite-based applications expanded with navigation (NavIC), earth-observation (Cartosat, RISAT series), and communication satellite constellations supporting agriculture monitoring, disaster management, and rural connectivity — all directly useful for Gujarat's agricultural and disaster-management planning.
  • New Education Policy (NEP) 2020 implementation milestones and adult-education/enrollment-data releases from the Ministry of Education and NIEPA remained key governance-indicator topics across states including Gujarat.
  • Gujarat Legislative Assembly elections, by-election results, and changes in the state's political representation were significant current-affairs anchors affecting policy continuity debates.

Memory aids

  • Use the acronym 'AIS' for current-affairs triad: A = Appointments and Institutional Heads, I = International Summits and Treaties, S = Schemes and Statistical Indices — this helps you systematically check whether a question tests any of these three dimensions.
  • For Gujarat schemes, create a table mapping: Scheme Name → Launch Year → Ministry/Department → Key Benefit → Eligibility → Gujarat Implementation Status; revisiting this table before the exam covers maximum scheme-related questions.
  • For international summits, use a chronological timeline anchored to G20 2023 (New Delhi), BRICS 2024 expansion, COP climate conferences, and India-hosted多边 summits; remember the host city and one signature outcome from each.
  • For awards, group them by category: National (Bharat Ratna, Padma, Gallantry, Khel Ratna, Arjuna, Dronacharya), International (Nobel, Oscar, Pulitzer, Booker, UNESCO), Sports (Olympic medals, World Cup performances, Asian Games); memorize the top three recipients per category per year rather than exhaustive lists.
  • For indices, remember the publisher first: UNDP → HDI, World Bank → Ease of Doing Business/BRAI, WHO → health indices, NSO → GDP/inflation data, IFPRI → Global Hunger Index, Transparency International → CPI; the publisher determines the methodological angle and which states rank prominently.
  • For ISRO and defence milestones, anchor dates to notable events: Chandrayaan-3着陆 (August 2023), Gaganyaan test vehicle trials, Agni/Prahaar/Astra test firings, INS Vikrant commissioning (September 2022) and subsequent naval exercises, Tejas LCA delivery numbers; these form the temporal skeleton for science-and-technology questions.
  • Link every national scheme to Gujarat's administrative geography: for example, PM Vishwakarma schemes reach Kutch and Saurashtra artisan clusters; Jal Jeevan Mission coverage figures include Gujarat rural household percentages; understanding district-level rollout strengthens answer-writing for Mains.

Exam tips

  • Read every MCQ carefully for temporal markers ('recently', 'currently', 'as of 2024') and geographic scope ('nationwide', 'state-specific', 'Gujarat') before eliminating options; the trap often lies in the qualifier, not the fact itself.
  • For Mains, structure answers using the triple-layer format: context/fact (what happened), significance/relevance (why it matters for India/Gujarat), and critical analysis (challenges, gaps, recommendations) — this distinguishes high-scoring answers from mere listing.
  • When answering Gujarat-current-affairs questions, explicitly connect central policies to Gujarat's implementation reality; examiners reward answers that demonstrate awareness of state-level administrative dynamics, district-level pilots, and local industry linkages.
  • Practice rewriting headline news in one sentence that captures: who (government/agency), what (scheme/event/appointment), when (year/month), where (national/Gujarat/global), and why it matters; this habit speeds up both Prelims elimination and Mains structuring.
  • Maintain a running monthly note sheet organized by theme (Schemes, International, Science & Tech, Economy, Gujarat, Sports, Awards, Appointments); revise it weekly in the final month before the exam instead of rereading daily newspapers, which becomes inefficient close to the test date.
  • In Prelims, treat statement-based current-affairs questions as evidence-testing: identify the anchor claim, then evaluate each statement against your revised notes rather than relying on vague newspaper memory.
  • For match-the-following questions, eliminate pairs you know are incorrect first; current-affairs matching questions commonly pair a scheme with the wrong ministry or an index with the wrong publisher — spotting one mismatch eliminates entire options.
  • Stay alert to policy reversals or scheme mergers: sometimes a scheme is renamed, merged into a larger mission, or discontinued; choosing an option referencing a superseded name is a frequent distractor in GPSC papers.
  • Do not speculate numerical answers for scheme-coverage figures or ranking positions unless you have concrete revision notes; when uncertain, choose the option consistent with the most recently reviewed authoritative source rather than guessing.
  • Link current-affairs topics to static syllabus chapters: a question about a water-conservation scheme connects to Geography (water resources), a new industrial policy links to Economy (industrialization), and a scientific mission relates to Science & Technology — integrating static and dynamic knowledge improves both speed and accuracy.

Common traps

  • Confusing similar-sounding scheme names: e.g., PM Vishwakarma vs. PM Vishwakarma Yojana vs. previous artisan schemes; PM Awas Yojana-Urban vs. PM Awas Yojana-Gramin; Ayushman Bharat Health Wellness Centres vs. AB-PMJAY. Read the exact official name before selecting an option.
  • Attributing a central-scheme figure to Gujarat without verification: many national scheme statistics differ from Gujarat's state-wise implementation numbers; when a question asks Gujarat's share or specific state data, choose only if you can confirm the state-level figure separately.
  • Mixing up international organization acronyms and their headquarters: e.g., IAEA (Vienna) vs. WTO (Geneva) vs. IMF (Washington DC); also confuse chairmanships rotating annually versus fixed-term leaders.
  • Assuming a newly launched scheme applies statewide immediately: central schemes typically begin with pilot states/districts; Gujarat's adoption timeline may lag the national launch date, and exam questions often test this distinction.
  • Overlooking that some indices use reverse scoring (higher rank number = worse performance): in indices like Corruption Perceptions Index or Press Freedom Index, a lower numerical rank is better; in HDI/GHI, higher scores are better — misinterpreting direction leads to wrong conclusions.
  • Conflating recent policy announcements with enacted legislation: many budget provisions and policy proposals remain in draft or implementation-phase; treat announced targets as 'proposed' unless officially notified.
  • Ignoring temporal qualifiers: questions may specify 'as of 2024' or 'launched during 2023-24'; selecting an option based on an older scheme iteration or superseded figure is a common error.
  • Assuming every international agreement directly impacts Gujarat: while Gujarat is strategically positioned, not all India-level MOUs involve Gujarat stakeholders; distinguish pan-India agreements from those with explicit Gujarat or western-coast provisions.
