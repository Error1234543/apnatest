Indian Economy — Quick Revision

GPSCEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • GDP (Gross Domestic Product): Total market value of all final goods and services produced within the domestic territory of a country during an accounting year.
  • GVA (Gross Value Added): The value of output minus the value of intermediate consumption at basic prices; GDP at market prices = Sum of GVA by resident institutional units + Taxes on products - Subsidies on products.
  • NDP (Net Domestic Product): GDP minus consumption of fixed capital (depreciation). NDP = GDP - Depreciation.
  • GNP (Gross National Product): GDP plus net factor income from abroad (NFIA). GNP = GDP + NFIA.
  • HDI (Human Development Index): Composite index measuring average achievement in three dimensions: health (life expectancy), education (mean years of schooling + expected years), and standard of living (GNI per capita PPP). Published by UNDP.
  • FDP (Financial Density Parameter): Ratio of total deposits of scheduled commercial banks to GDP; used as an indicator of financial inclusion depth in an economy.
  • MSME: Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises as defined under the MSMED Act, 2006 — classified on investment and turnover thresholds, not just employment.
  • PLI Scheme: Production Linked Incentive scheme — a government initiative offering financial incentives on incremental sales of goods manufactured domestically in specified sectors.
  • Fiscal Deficit: Total expenditure minus total receipts excluding borrowings; indicates the government's total borrowing requirement from the market.
  • Revenue Deficit: Revenue expenditure minus revenue receipts; shows how much of the government's borrowing is being used to finance current-day spending rather than capital creation.
  • CAGR: Compound Annual Growth Rate — the mean annual growth rate of an investment over a period longer than one year.
  • SEZ: Special Economic Zone — designated areas with special fiscal and regulatory provisions to promote exports and investment, governed by the SEZ Act, 2005.

Key concepts

  • Sectors of the Indian Economy: Primary (agriculture, forestry, fishing), Secondary (manufacturing, construction), Tertiary (services). India follows a mixed economy model with both public and private sectors coexisting. The tertiary sector now contributes over 50% to GDP but employs a large share of the workforce in the primary sector — a structural anomaly compared to developed economies.
  • National Income Accounting: GDP is the most commonly cited measure; GDP at constant prices shows real growth, while GDP at current (nominal) prices includes inflation effects. GDP deflator = (Nominal GDP / Real GDP) × 100. India uses 2011-12 as the base year for constant-price GDP calculations.
  • Economic Planning in India: The Planning Commission was established on 15 March 1950 and functioned through twelve five-year plans until its abolition in 2014, replaced by NITI Aayog (National Institution for Transforming India) on 1 January 2015. NITI Aayog works as a think tank promoting cooperative federalism through bottom-up planning.
  • Five Year Plan Highlights: First Plan (1951-56) — agrarian focus, Harrod-Domar model. Second Plan (1956-61) — Mahalanobis model, heavy industry emphasis, Iron Steel Plants established. Third Plan (1961-66) — disrupted by wars and droughts. Rolling Plan (1966-69) — brief experiment. Green Revolution era — 1960s onwards, self-sufficiency in food grains achieved by early 1970s. Seven Plan (1987-92) — last full plan before 1991 reforms.
  • 1991 Economic Reforms (LPG): Triggered by balance of payments crisis (foreign reserves dropped to cover only two weeks of imports). Key measures: Industrial licensing abolished for most sectors (discontinuous license raj), FDI liberalization, Rupee devaluation and subsequent partial convertibility, Tax reforms, Public sector disinvestment began, Trade liberalization (reduction in import duties and quantitative restrictions). Named after Liberalsation, Privatisation, Globalisation.
  • Agriculture: Core challenges include small and fragmented landholdings, dependence on monsoon, low irrigation coverage (~52% of net sown area), post-hapost harvest losses, and inadequate credit access. Schemes: PM-KISAN (₹6,000 per year direct income support), PMFBY (Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana — crop insurance), Agri Infrastructure Fund, ONE Nation One Ration Card, e-NAM (national agricultural market). Green Revolution centres Punjab, Haryana, Western UP.
  • Industry: Make in India launched in 2014 to boost manufacturing share of GDP to 25%. Production Linked Incentive (PLI) scheme covers electronics, pharma, automobiles, textiles, food processing, telecom, and more. MSMEs contribute approximately 30% to GDP and employ over 11 crore people. Infrastructure bottlenecks and ease of doing business reforms have been key focus areas.
  • Banking and Financial System: RBI (established 1 April 1935, nationalised 1949) is the central banking institution. Schedule Banks include Commercial Banks (Public Sector, Private Sector, Foreign) and Cooperative Banks (RBI Regulation of Cooperative Societies Act, 2012). Key RBI tools: CRR (Cash Reserve Ratio), SLR (Statutory Liquidity Ratio), Bank Rate, Repo Rate, Reverse Repo Rate, MSF (Marginal Standing Facility). NPA (Non-Performing Asset) classification: account becomes NPA if interest or principal remains overdue for more than 90 days.
  • Budget and Fiscal Policy: Union Budget presented on 1 February (since 2017). Components: Revenue Budget (receipts and expenditure) and Capital Budget. Key fiscal indicators monitored: Fiscal Deficit (% of GDP), Revenue Deficit (% of GDP), Fiscal Responsibility and Budget Management (FRBM) Act, 2003. State Finance Commissions recommend devolution of taxes to local bodies.
  • Monetary Policy: Conducted by RBI's Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) constituted under RBI Act amendment 2016. Six-member MPC: 3 from RBI (Governor, Deputy Governor, one executive), 3 appointed by Government. Mandate: maintain inflation within 4±2% target ( CPI-based). Repo rate is the primary policy rate. Inflation targeting replaced multiple objectives as the sole mandate.
  • Poverty and Unemployment: Poverty line estimated by Tendulkar Committee (2009) and Rangarajan Committee (2014). Antyodaya Anna Yojana (AAY) targets the poorest of poor. Employment schemes: MGNREGA (guarantees 100 days wage employment per rural household), PM RVY (Rohigar Shiksha evam Vikas Yojana — skilling). Types of unemployment: disguised (agriculture), seasonal, structural, frictional, cyclic.
  • Foreign Trade: Major exports — gems & jewellery, petroleum products, pharmaceuticals, engineering goods, IT services. Major imports — crude oil, gold, electronics, machinery. FEMA (Foreign Exchange Management Act), 1999 governs external transactions. India is a member of WTO, IMF, World Bank, BRICS, G20, AIIB. Balance of Payments records all economic transactions between residents and non-residents.
  • Recent Developments: Digital Public Infrastructure (UPI, Aadhaar, ONDC), India's rank improvement in Ease of Doing Business, PLI driving domestic manufacturing, Atmanirbhar Bharat Abhiyan (self-reliant India, announced May 2020), G20 Presidency 2023, India becoming third largest economy by nominal GDP, green hydrogen mission, semi-conductor ecosystem development.

Important facts

  • India's GDP (nominal) exceeded USD 3.7 trillion by FY 2023-24, making it the fifth-largest economy globally.
  • Planning Commission was set up on 15 March 1950 via an executive resolution of the Union Cabinet. NITI Aayog replaced it on 1 January 2015.
  • First Five-Year Plan adopted the Harrod-Domar model; Second Five-Year Plan adopted the Mahalanobis model.
  • Severe balance of payments crisis occurred in June 1991; India's foreign exchange reserves fell to nearly USD 1.2 billion — sufficient for only two weeks of imports.
  • Manmohan Singh, as Finance Minister, presented the landmark 1991 Union Budget introducing comprehensive economic liberalization.
  • Green Revolution in India began in the mid-1960s, primarily in Punjab, Haryana, and western Uttar Pradesh, driven by high-yielding variety seeds.
  • RBI was nationalised on 1 January 1949; prior to that it was a shareholder-owned central bank since its establishment in 1935.
  • Public Sector Banks were nationalised in two phases: 1969 (14 banks) and 1980 (6 banks).
  • MGNREGA was enacted in 2005, guaranteeing 100 days of wage employment to every rural household whose adult members volunteer for unskilled manual work.
  • Tendulkar Committee recommended an urban poverty line of ₹972 per month and a rural poverty line of ₹816 per month (2011-12 prices).
  • Rangarajan Committee (2014) proposed a monthly poverty line of ₹1,407 for rural areas and ₹2,261 for urban areas.
  • e-NAM was launched in 2016, creating a unified national electronic trading platform for agricultural commodities across registered APMCs.
  • India's inflation target of 4% with a tolerance band of ±2% is set by the Central Government on the recommendation of the MPC.
  • NITI Aayog's key documents include the India Innovation Index, SDG India Index, and School Education Quality Index.
  • PLI scheme was initially approved for 13 sectors and has since been expanded to additional sectors including advanced chemistry cell (ACC) battery storage.
  • Cooperative Banks in India are regulated by both RBI and the respective State Governments under the dual regulation framework established by the RBI Act and the Cooperative Societies Acts of states.
  • Villanwal Committee (2023) submitted its report on strengthening the cooperative sector, recommending autonomous regulatory authority for cooperatives.
  • India became the President of G20 in 2023, hosting the leaders' summit in New Delhi in September 2023.

Memory aids

  • LPG Reform Mnemonic: 'Learn Produce Globally' → Liberalisation, Privatisation, Globalisation (1991 reforms sequence).
  • Five Year Plan Models: 'H₂M₂' → Harrod-Domar for First, Mahalanobis for Second Plan. Remember: First has fewer letters (Harrod-Domar shorter conceptually), Second is more complex (Mahalanobis).
  • Banking NPC Mnemonic: 'NPA comes after 90 days' → Think of 'Ninety Plus' sounds like 'No Pay' — bank stops recognizing the loan as income after 90 days of overdue.
  • RBI Tools Ranking: CRR < SLR < Repo < Bank Rate < MSF in terms of cost/severity. 'RBI charges most for Marginal Standing Facility' — highest penalty rate for emergency borrowing.
  • Monetary Policy Committee Mnemonic: '3+3 Rule' → 3 from RBI + 3 from Government = 6 members. 'Six-pack committee makes six decisions'.
  • Planning Timeline: '50 to 15' → Planning Commission 1950 to NITI Aayog 2015. Note the gap: no plan in 1966-67 and 1967-68 due to President's Rule; Rolling Plan 1966-69 inserted.
  • Poverty Committees Order: 'T-R' → Tendulkar first (2009), Rangarajan later (2014). Think of alphabet order: T before R? No — reverse it: Rangarajan came later so he revised Tendulkar.
  • Sector Contribution Sequence: Agriculture < Industry < Services in terms of current GDP contribution. Mnemonic: 'AIS' → Agriculture, Industry, Services — ascending order of GDP share currently.

Exam tips

  • For GPSC GS, focus on numerical data: know key percentages (agriculture's share of GDP ~17%, industry ~28%, services ~55%), population figures, poverty ratios, and scheme allocations — these appear frequently in objective questions.
  • Link Economy with Gujarat-specific content where possible: Gulf stream effect on Gujarat's ports, GIFT City as IFSC, Gujarat's industrial corridors, petrochemical cluster at Jamnagar — these are favourite GPSC combination questions.
  • Current Affairs integration is critical: Any new scheme launched after 2023 that has national economic significance may appear. Focus on budget highlights, five-year targets, and major policy changes.
  • Distinguish between central schemes and state schemes: GPSC sometimes frames questions testing whether you know which schemes are centrally sponsored versus state-specific — especially for Gujarat economy-related schemes.
  • Inequality measures: Know the difference between Gini Coefficient (income inequality, 0 to 1), Human Development Index (0 to 1), and Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI — combines health, education, living standards). India's MPI headcount ratio is around 15-16% as per NITI Aayog's latest report.
  • Assertion-Reason questions are common in GPSC: Ensure your conceptual clarity is sharp — many questions test whether you understand why a reform was needed, not just what it was.
  • Statement-based questions often use 'only one/only two/all three' format: When encountering such questions, evaluate each statement independently before selecting the answer option. Do not assume any statement is correct based on others being wrong.
  • Remember key dates: 1991 reforms, 1969 bank nationalisation, 2005 MGNREGA, 2016 demonetisation, 2017 GST implementation, 2015 NITI Aayog — these dates frequently feature in direct fact questions.
  • For numerical problems related to ratio, percentage change, or growth rate in the Reasoning section applied to economic data, practice basic arithmetic quickly as time management is crucial in the GPSC Prelims.

Common traps

  • Confusing GDP with GNP: GDP counts production within territorial borders regardless of producer nationality; GNP adds net factor income from abroad. An Indian worker earning in USA adds to India's GNP but USA's GDP.
  • Mixing up CRR and SLR: CRR is the percentage of net demand and time liabilities that banks must maintain with RBI in cash form; SLR is the percentage that banks must maintain in liquid assets (cash, gold, govt securities) with themselves, not with RBI.
  • Repo vs Reverse Repo confusion: Repo rate = rate at which RBI lends to banks (banks borrow from RBI); Reverse repo rate = rate at which RBI borrows from banks (banks park money with RBI). 'Repo goes up = banks pay more to borrow' — tightening.
  • Fiscal Deficit vs Revenue Deficit: Fiscal deficit includes capital receipts and expenditure; revenue deficit is purely revenue side mismatch. A government can have zero revenue deficit but positive fiscal deficit if it borrows only for capital formation.
  • Confusing NITI Aayog with Planning Commission: NITI Aayog does not allocate funds to states; it is a policy think tank promoting cooperative federalism. Planning Commission had fund allocation powers.
  • MGNREGA 100 days applies per household, not per individual: A single household can have multiple members working, each counted separately toward the 100-day guarantee per household per financial year.
  • Green Revolution ≠ Blue Revolution: Green Revolution relates to food grain self-sufficiency (wheat, rice); Blue Revolution relates to fisheries and aquaculture development.
  • e-NAM is NOT a new农产品 market: It is an online trading platform that connects existing APMC mandis, not a replacement for APMCs.
  • PLI is incentive on INCREMENTAL sales, not on total sales: Companies receive incentive only on sales exceeding baseline year, not on all domestic production.
  • NPA classification timeline changed: Earlier, agricultural loans became NPA after two crop seasons; now, for short-term agricultural credit, it is based on periodic flow statement — check latest RBI master circular for current norms.
  • Cooperative banks are NOT fully regulated by RBI alone: They operate under dual regulation — RBI for banking operations and State Governments for registration and supervision of societies.
  • GST Council has 31 members: Central Govt (1 votepower) + all States (1 vote each). The Center's vote is 1/3rd of total votes cast, States collectively have 2/3rd — not simple majority.
