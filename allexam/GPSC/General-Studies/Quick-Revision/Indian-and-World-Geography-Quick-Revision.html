Indian and World Geography – Quick Revision for GPSC

GPSCEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Geography is the scientific study of the Earth's physical features, climate, ecosystems, and human activities upon them. In GPSC GS, it is tested through factual knowledge combined with map-based application.
  • Latitude is the angular distance of a place north or south of the Equator, measured in degrees. It determines the climatic zone of a location — tropical, temperate, or polar.
  • Longitude is the angular distance east or west of the Prime Meridian (0° passing through Greenwich). It governs time zones; every 15° of longitude equals one hour of time difference.
  • Equinox occurs twice a year (around 21 March and 23 September) when the Sun is directly above the Equator, resulting in nearly equal day and night across the globe.
  • Solstice occurs twice a year (around 21 June and 22 December) when the Sun is at its northernmost or southernmost point relative to the Equator, producing the longest and shortest days.
  • Drainage pattern refers to the arrangement of rivers and their tributaries in a particular area — dendritic, trellis, radial, deranged, or annular — reflecting underlying geological structure.
  • Oceanic thermohaline circulation is the large-scale movement of ocean water driven by differences in density caused by temperature (thermo) and salinity (haline). It regulates global climate.
  • Biome is a large ecological community characterized by its dominant vegetation and climate — e.g., tundra, taiga, temperate forest, desert, savanna, tropical rainforest.
  • Soil profile is the vertical sequence of soil layers (horizons O, A, B, C, and R) each differing in composition, colour, and texture due to weathering and organic accumulation.
  • Monsoon is a seasonal reversal of wind direction that brings distinct wet and dry periods, especially prominent over the Indian subcontinent from June to September.

Key concepts

  • Earth's Structure: The Earth consists of crust (continental/sial and oceanic/sima), mantle (upper and lower, containing the asthenosphere), outer core (liquid iron-nickel), and inner core (solid iron-nickel). Lithosphere comprises the crust and upper mantle; asthenosphere lies below and allows plate movement.
  • Plate Tectonics: Major plates include Pacific, Eurasian, Indo-Australian, African, North American, South American, and Antarctic. Convergent boundaries form mountains and trenches, divergent boundaries create ridges and rifts, and transform boundaries cause earthquakes. The Himalayas formed by Indo-Australian plate colliding with Eurasian plate.
  • Mineral Resources in India: Coal is primarily found in Jharkhand, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, West Bengal (Gondwana and Tertiary basins). Iron ore in Odisha (Sundargarh, Keonjhar), Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Karnataka (Kudremukh). Bauxite in Odisha, Jharkhand, Gujarat, Maharashtra. Mica in Jharkhand (Singhbhum), Andhra Pradesh, Rajasthan. Copper in Rajasthan (Khetri), Jharkhand, Madhya Pradesh. Oil fields: Mumbai High, Ankleshwar, Numaligarh, Digboi.
  • Soil Types in India: Alluvial soil covers ~43% of India and is the most productive, found in river plains. Black soil (regur) is ideal for cotton, dominates Deccan trap region. Red soil forms from crystalline igneous rocks in peninsular India. Laterite soil develops in high rainfall areas of Western Ghats, NE India. Arid soil is found in Rajasthan and western Gujarat. Saline soil occurs in waterlogged areas.
  • River Systems of India: Peninsular rivers (Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri, Narmada, Tapi, Mahanadi) are older, non-perennial, lack deltas in many cases. Narmada and Tapi flow west through rift valleys forming estuaries. Himalayan rivers (Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra) are perennial, carry heavy silt, form large deltas. Ganga-Brahmaputra delta (Sundarbans) is the world's largest delta.
  • Climate of India: Influenced by latitude, altitude, pressure/wind systems, distance from sea, and monsoon dynamics. Tropical monsoon climate dominates. Western disturbances bring winter rain to NW India. Retreating monsoon brings rain to Tamil Nadu and coastal Andhra. Rain shadow regions exist in interior Deccan and Rajasthan.
  • Natural Vegetation of India: Tropical moist deciduous forests are most widespread (teak, sal, rosewood). Tropical rainforests occur in Western Ghats, NE India, Andaman. Thorn and scrub forests dominate arid western India. Mangrove forests at Sundarbans (Ganga-Brahmaputra delta) and Gujarat coast (Gir). Mountain/temperate forests at higher altitudes with oak, deodar, fir.
  • Major Crops of India: Kharif crops (sown June-July, harvested Oct-Nov) include rice, maize, cotton, sugarcane, groundnut, jowar, bajra. Rabi crops (sown Oct-Nov, harvested April-May) include wheat, barley, oats, mustard, gram. Zaid crops (summer) include cucumber, melon, fodder. Rice dominates in NE and coastal plains; wheat in Indo-Gangetic plain; cotton in Deccan trap; sugarcane in UP, Maharashtra, Punjab.
  • World Geography – Major Physical Features: Mount Everest (8,849 m) is highest peak. Amazon River has greatest discharge. Sahara is largest hot desert. Gobi is largest cold desert. Andes is longest mountain range. Nile and Amazon are longest rivers. Great Barrier Reef is largest coral system. Dead Sea is lowest point on land. Lake Baikal is deepest and oldest freshwater lake.
  • World Climate Zones: Koppen classification divides world into tropical (A), dry (B), temperate (C), continental (D), polar (E). Tropical climates have high year-round temperatures; dry zones feature low precipitation; temperate zones have moderate seasons; continental zones have extreme temperature ranges; polar zones remain cold throughout. Monsoon and humid subtropical types affect large parts of South and East Asia.
  • World Mineral and Energy Resources: Saudi Arabia, Russia, USA lead in oil production. Iron ore leaders: Australia, Brazil, China. Bauxite: Guinea, Australia, China. Coal: USA, China, Russia, India. Uranium: Kazakhstan, Canada, Namibia. Rare earth elements concentrated in China. Petroleum refineries cluster near consuming markets or ports.
  • Agriculture Regions of World: Mediterranean agriculture (citrus, olives, grapes) occurs in California, Mediterranean basin, Chile, South Africa, SW Australia. Plantation agriculture (rubber, tea, coffee, banana) dominates tropical developing regions. Mixed farming combines crops and livestock, common in Europe and NE USA. Nomadic pastoralism persists in arid regions of Africa, Central Asia, and Middle East.
  • Industrial Location Factors: Weber's Least Cost Theory emphasises raw material weight, transport cost, and labour cost. Modern industries locate near markets, ports, or infrastructure hubs. Steel industry historically located near coal or iron ore deposits (Ruhr, Pittsburgh, Jamshedpur). IT industry clusters near universities and quality of life (Bangalore, Silicon Valley). Port cities drive export-oriented industries.
  • Human Geography Indicators: HDI combines life expectancy, education, and per capita income. Population density varies hugely — Monaco is densest, Mongolia sparsest. Urbanisation rate highest in developed countries but growing fastest in developing world. Migration drivers include economic opportunity, conflict, climate change, and political instability.
  • Geography of Gujarat: Western Ghats origin does NOT extend into Gujarat, but Saputara hills are part of the same range in neighbouring Maharashtra. Gujarat has extensive coastal line (~1,600 km) including Rann of Kutch and Gulf of Kutch. Major rivers: Narmada, Tapi, Mahi, Luni. Climate is predominantly arid to semi-arid. mineral resources include natural gas ( KG Basin, Cambay), petroleum, soda ash (Bikaner-Nagaur, Sambhar), limestone. Agricultural products: groundnut, cotton, castor, cumin, amla.

Important facts

  • India covers about 3.287 million sq km — 7th largest country. Latitudinal extent: 8°4′N to 37°6′N. Longitudinal extent: 68°7′E to 97°25′E. Tropic of Cancer passes through 8 states: Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Tripura, Mizoram.
  • Sunderban mangroves (West Bengal) cover ~10,000 sq km and host the Royal Bengal Tiger. They are a UNESCO World Heritage Site and critically important for coastal protection.
  • The Palk Strait and Gulf of Mannar separate India from Sri Lanka. India shares 15 neighbouring countries with a total land boundary of approximately 15,200 km.
  • The Himalayas extend from the Indus to the Brahmaputra over ~2,400 km. They are divided into three parallel ranges: Himadri (Greater Himalaya), Himachal (Lesser Himalaya), and Shiwaliks (Outer Himalaya). Northern plains are formed by alluvial deposition over millennia.
  • Golden Quadrilateral connects Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai, and Kolkata — a 5,700 km highway network. National Highways constitute about 2% of roads but carry 40% of road traffic.
  • Godavari is known as the 'Dakshin Ganga' — longest peninsular river at ~1,465 km. Brahmaputra is called 'Tsangpo' in Tibet and 'Dihang' in Arunachal Pradesh. Narmada originates from Amarkantak plateau in MP and drains into the Arabian Sea through a well-defined rift valley.
  • India's total forest cover as per latest ISFR is approximately 24.6% of geographical area. States with maximum forest cover: Mizoram, Aragongana, Manipur; states with largest absolute area under forest: Madhya Pradesh, Arunachal Pradesh, Chhattisgarh.
  • Green Revolution began in the 1960s in Punjab, Haryana, and western UP with high-yielding varieties of wheat and rice. Red Revolution pertains to tomato cultivation. Golden Revolution relates to horticulture and honey. Yellow Revolution is for oilseeds. White Revolution is Operation Flood (milk). Blue Revolution addresses fisheries.
  • World's major trade routes: Suez Canal (connects Mediterranean to Red Sea), Panama Canal (Atlantic to Pacific), Strait of Malacca (Pacific to Indian Ocean), Strait of Hormuz (Persian Gulf outlet), Bosporus (Black Sea outlet). These are strategic chokepoints for global shipping.
  • Important Indian soil-b crop pairings: Black soil → cotton (black cotton soil of Deccan); Alluvial soil → rice, wheat, sugarcane; Laterite soil → tea, coffee, cashew; Red soil → millets, pulses, oilseeds; Arid soil → date palm, guar, bajra.
  • Gujarat's largest district by area is Kutch (~45,652 sq km). Smallest is Dahod (~902 sq km) in Madhya Pradesh ( nationally; Gujarat's smallest is Gandhinagar). Rann of Kutch is a seasonal salt marsh — white desert visible during summer. Mundra Port in Kutch is India's largest private-sector port.
  • Earth's atmosphere composition by volume: Nitrogen ~78%, Oxygen ~21%, Argon ~0.93%, Carbon dioxide ~0.04%, trace gases including neon, helium, methane, ozone.
  • Major earthquakes in India: 2001 Bhuj (Gujarat, magnitude 7.7), 2005 Kashmir (7.6), 2011 Sikkim (6.9), 2023 Turkey-Syria (outside India but instructive). India falls in seismic zones IV and V (about 60% of land area).
  • El Niño-Southern Oscillation (ENSO) affects Indian monsoon significantly. El Niño typically weakens monsoon causing drought conditions; La Niña strengthens it bringing above-normal rainfall. Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD) is another important modulator.
  • Mumbai High is India's largest offshore oil field, discovered in 1974, operated by ONGC in the Bombay High basin. Gujarat's offshore areas also contribute significant natural gas production.
  • The Indian Standard Time (IST) is based on the meridian 82°30′E passing through Mirzapur near Allahabad. IST is UTC+5:30 with no daylight saving.
  • India's coastline is 7,516.6 km long, distributed among 9 states and 3 union territories. Gujarat has the longest coastline among Indian states at approximately 1,600 km.
  • World population crosses 8 billion in 2022. China and India are the two most populous countries. UN projects India will surpass China as the most populous by 2024.
  • The ozone layer is located in the stratosphere (15–35 km altitude). Montreal Protocol (1987) phased out CFCs to protect the ozone layer. Hole in ozone is observed mainly over Antarctica.

Memory aids

  • Tropic of Cancer states mnemonic: Gaj Rao Mein Chhatra Jawai Bhar Mata Ki Jai → Gujarat, Rajasthan, MP, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, WB, Tripura, Mizoram (8 states).
  • Peninsular rivers flowing west: Narmada, Tapi, Perfetti (small), Bhogavo, Mahi — think 'NaTPM' — these flow through rift valleys and form estuaries unlike eastern rivers.
  • Himalayan ranges order from north to south: Himadri (Greater) → Himachal (Lesser) → Shiwalik (Outer) — remember 'Grand Lesser Small'.
  • Soil-crop pairs: Black = Cotton, Alluvial = Rice/Wheat, Laterite = Tea/Coffee, Red = Pulses/Millets, Arid = Bajra/Date — associate each soil colour and region with its signature crop.
  • Ocean currents around India: Indian Ocean has anti-clockwise circulation in winter and clockwise in summer due to monsoon wind reversal. Warm current flows along East African coast, cold along West Australian coast.
  • Neighbouring countries clockwise from north: Afghanistan, Pakistan, China, Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh, Myanmar — plus maritime neighbours Sri Lanka and Maldives. Use the map visualisation exercise daily.
  • SEZs in India: Special Economic Zones are designated areas for trade promotion. Key ones include Kandla (Gujarat), Naya Raipur (Chhattisgarh), Dholera (Gujarat — SIR), Chennai, Kolkata. Remember Gujarat hosts multiple SEZs and industrial corridors.
  • Monsoon arrival sequence: Kerala coast (early June) → whole of India by mid-July → retreating from NW India (September) → Tamil Nadu coast (October-November).
  • Great Lakes of North America: Huron, Erie, Ontario, Michigan, Superior — 'He Em OMS' or 'Super Man Has No Eye' — connected by St. Lawrence River which empties into Atlantic.
  • Desert locations: Sahara (N Africa), Arabian (W Asia), Thar (NW India/Pakistan), Gobi (N China/Mongolia), Kalahari (S Africa), Atacama (Chile), Namib (SW Africa), Great Victoria (Australia).

Exam tips

  • For GPSC, focus heavily on Indian Geography with emphasis on physical features, drainage, climate, soils, vegetation, and mineral distribution. Map-based questions are frequent — practice locating rivers, mountain passes, national parks, and mineral belts.
  • Gujarat-specific geography carries weight: know the Rann of Kutch, Gulf of Kutch, major ports (Kandla, Mundra, Pipava, Dahej), climate zones, agricultural produce, and industrial regions.
  • Learn to differentiate between similar-looking concepts: sedimentary vs metamorphic vs igneous rocks; fluvial vs glacial vs wind erosion features; anticyclones vs cyclones; insolation vs advection.
  • For world geography, prioritise: major mountain ranges and plateaus, important rivers and their tributaries, deserts and their locations, climate types, and major resources of key countries.
  • Practice statement-based questions: 'Which of the following statements is/are correct?' — these test precision. Eliminate options systematically rather than guessing.
  • Use elimination strategy in matching questions: if you are unsure about one item in a pair, eliminate options containing that item to narrow choices.
  • Revise with maps daily: keep an outline map of India and one of the world. Mark mountains, rivers, passes, ports, mineral areas, national parks, soil types, crop zones, and climate regions.
  • Pay attention to current relevance: geography questions sometimes link to recent events — floods, landslides, cyclones, glacial lake outburst floods, or environmental disasters can appear as contextual questions.
  • Do not over-study world geography at the expense of Indian geography for GPSC — the weightage skews heavily toward India and Gujarat. However, don't ignore world geography entirely as 3-5 questions typically come from it.
  • Time management in GS paper: geography factual questions should take 40-60 seconds each. If stuck, mark for review and move on. Practice with timed mock tests to build speed without sacrificing accuracy.

Common traps

  • Confusing Peninsular rivers with Himalayan rivers: Peninsular rivers are largely non-perennial and do not have extensive delta systems except Godavari and Kaveri; Himalayan rivers are perennial and carry massive sediment loads.
  • Assuming all west-flowing rivers of peninsular India form deltas: Narmada and Tapi form estuaries because they flow through rift valleys with steep gradients. Only Godavari (east-flowing) among the major west-flowing exception does NOT form a significant delta.
  • Mixing up Kharif and Rabi sowing/harvesting times: Kharif is monsoon crop (June sowing, Oct harvest); Rabi is winter crop (Oct sowing, April harvest). Many aspirants reverse this.
  • Attributing all Indian earthquakes to plate boundary activity: Most Indian earthquakes are intra-plate, occurring within the Indian plate due to reactivation of old faults, not solely at the plate margin.
  • Confusing laterite soil with black soil for cotton: Cotton grows best in black (regur) soil, not laterite. Laterite soil is leached and acidic — suitable for cashew, tea, coffee.
  • Thinking monsoon is just rain: Monsoon is a wind system reversal. Rain is a consequence. The Southwest monsoon winds blow from ocean to land (June-Sept); Northeast monsoon blows from land to ocean (Oct-Dec).
  • Overlooking that Narmada and Tapi originate in east but flow west: Narmada originates from Amarkantak (MP) and Tapi from Western Ghats (Maharashtra) near Navagaon — both flow west into Arabian Sea via rift valleys.
  • Incorrectly associating mineral with wrong region: Bauxite is NOT primarily from Jharkhand alone — Guinea, Australia, and Brazil are global leaders. In India, Odisha leads in bauxite, not Jharkhand.
  • Mistaking HDI components: HDI uses life expectancy at birth, mean years of schooling, expected years of schooling, and GNI per capita — NOT literacy rate directly or infant mortality rate directly.
  • Ignoring that Gujarat's coastline is the longest in India: Many candidates mistakenly answer Tamil Nadu or Andhra Pradesh. Gujarat's 1,600 km coastline exceeds any other state.
