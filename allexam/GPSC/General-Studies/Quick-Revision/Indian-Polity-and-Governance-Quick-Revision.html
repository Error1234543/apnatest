Indian Polity and Governance — Quick Revision for GPSC

GPSCEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Constitution: The supreme legal document of India that defines the political framework, fundamental rights, directive principles, duties of citizens, and the structure and powers of institutions at Union, State, and Local levels.
  • Preamble: The introductory statement of the Constitution that declares India to be a Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic and commits to securing Justice, Liberty, Equality, and Fraternity for all citizens.
  • Fundamental Rights (Part III, Articles 12-35): Justiciable rights guaranteed by the Constitution to all citizens (except Article 31 abrogated) protecting civil liberties against arbitrary state action; six categories originally, now five after deletion of Right to Property.
  • Directive Principles of State Policy (Part IV, Articles 36-51): Non-justiciable guidelines to the State for establishing social and economic democracy; the State must apply them in making laws and policies.
  • Fundamental Duties (Part IVA, Article 51A): Moral obligations of citizens added by the 42nd Amendment (1976); nineteen duties covering respect for Constitution, national symbols, unity, environment, scientific temper, etc.
  • Basic Structure Doctrine: Judicially evolved principle holding that Parliament cannot amend the basic/foundational features of the Constitution; first propounded in Kesavananda Bharati (1973).
  • Writs: Judicial remedies issued by the Supreme Court (Article 32) and High Courts (Article 226) — Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Certiorari, and Quo Warranto — to enforce fundamental rights and check arbitrary authority.
  • Constitutional Amendment: A change to the Constitution through Parliament under Article 368;三类 types: simple majority, special majority, and special majority + ratification by half of State legislatures.
  • Emergency Provisions (Part XVIII, Articles 352-360): Three types — National Emergency (Article 352), President's Rule/State Emergency (Article 356), and Financial Emergency (Article 360) — each suspending or altering normal constitutional functioning.
  • Parliamentary Sovereignty vs. Constitutional Supremacy: India follows constitutional supremacy — Parliament derives its authority from the Constitution and cannot legislate beyond its constitutional limits.

Key concepts

  • Structure of the Indian Constitution: Original length 395 articles in 22 parts and 8 schedules; currently over 470 articles in 25 parts and 12 schedules after numerous amendments; it is the longest written constitution of any sovereign country.
  • Sources of the Constitution: Borrowed features from multiple constitutions — Government of India Act 1935 (federal scheme, governor's office, judiciary), UK (parliamentary govt, rule of law, single citizenship), USA (fundamental rights, judicial review, impeachment), Ireland (DPSP), Canada (federalism with strength to centre), Germany/Weimar (suspension of fundamental rights during emergency), Australia ( Concurrent List), Japan (procedure established by law), South Africa (amendment procedure), USSR/Russian Federation (fundamental duties, ideals of justice in Preamble).
  • Preamble — Key Amendments: The Preamble was amended only once by the 42nd Amendment Act, 1976, which inserted the words 'Socialist', 'Secular', and 'Integrity' (unity and integrity of the Nation). It declares India a 'Republic' (head of state elected, not hereditary).
  • Fundamental Rights — Five Categories: (1) Right to Equality (Articles 14-18): equality before law, prohibition of discrimination, abolition of untouchability and titles; (2) Right to Freedom (Articles 19-22): six freedoms under Article 19, protection against conviction, arrest, life/liberty, traffic in human beings; (3) Right against Exploitation (Articles 23-24): prohibition of trafficking, forced labour, child labour; (4) Right to Freedom of Religion (Articles 25-28): freedom of conscience, profession, practice, propagation; (5) Cultural and Educational Rights (Articles 29-30): protection of language, script, culture of minorities; (6) Right to Constitutional Remedies (Article 32): Dr. Ambedkar called it the 'heart and soul' of the Constitution.
  • Fundamental Rights — Key Features: Most are available to citizens only (Articles 15, 19, 29-30); some to all persons including foreigners (Articles 14, 20-22, 23-28, 31); they are not absolute — subject to reasonable restrictions; Parliament can restrict them during National Emergency (Article 358, 359); can be suspended during Emergency but not Articles 20 and 21.
  • Fundamental Rights vs. Directive Principles: FRs are negative in character (restraints on State), justiciable, and aimed at political democracy; DPSPs are positive in character (directives to State), non-justiciable, and aimed at social and economic democracy; conflicts between them are resolved by courts using harmony and balance doctrine (Minerva Mills case).
  • Directive Principles — Classification: Socialistic (Articles 38, 39, 39A, 41, 42, 43), Gandhian (Articles 40, 43, 43B, 46, 47), Liberal-Intellectual (Articles 44, 45, 48, 48A, 49, 50, 51). Important ones: uniform civil code (Art 44), free legal aid (Art 39A), separation of judiciary from executive (Art 50), protection of environment (Art 48A).
  • Fundamental Duties — Complete List (Article 51A): (a) Abide by Constitution and respect its ideals, symbols, and National Flag/Anthem; (b) Cherish ideals of freedom struggle; (c) Protect sovereignty, integrity, and defence of India; (d) Render national service when called; (e) Promote harmony and spirit of common brotherhood transcending religious, linguistic, regional separations; (f) Value and preserve rich heritage of composite culture; (g) Protect and improve natural environment including forests, lakes, wildlife; (h) Develop scientific temper, humanism, spirit of inquiry and reform; (i) Safeguard public property and abjure violence; (j) Strive towards excellence in all fields of individual and collective activity; (k) Provide early opportunity for education to children aged 6-14 (added by 86th Amendment). Total: 11 duties after 86th Amendment (originally 10 after 42nd Amendment, later 11 after 86th Amendment in 2002).
  • Amendment Procedure (Article 368): Three modes — (1) Simple Majority: most constitutional provisions can be amended by simple parliamentary majority (e.g., creation of new states, citizenship, delimitation); (2) Special Majority of Parliament: majority of total membership + 2/3 present and voting + no less than 50% of total strength; (3) Special Majority + Ratification by States: special parliamentary majority plus ratification by at least half of State Legislatures (e.g., federal provisions, election of President, distribution of legislative powers).
  • Union Executive: President (Article 52) — Head of State, elected by Electoral College (elected members of both Houses of Parliament + elected members of State Legislative Assemblies); term 5 years; eligibility: citizen, 35+ years, qualified for Lok Sabha membership; impeachment requires special majority in either House; Vice-President (Article 66) — ex-officio Chairman of Rajya Sabha; Prime Minister (Article 74) — real executive head, appoints Cabinet ministers; Council of Ministers collectively responsible to Lok Sabha.
  • Parliament: Bicameral legislature — Lok Sabha (House of the People, max 552 members: 530 from States, 20 from UTs, 2 Anglo-Indian nominated earlier but 104th Amendment removed this); Rajya Sabha (Council of States, max 250 members: 238 elected by State ASes + 12 nominated by President); Speaker of Lok Sabha (presides over LS, no vote in first instance, casting vote only in case of tie); Chairman of Rajya Sabha (Vice-President), Deputy Chairman presides in his absence.
  • Legislative Process: Ordinary bill can originate in either House (except Money Bill only in Lok Sabha); Money Bill (Article 110) requires President's recommendation for introduction; passed by simple majority in LS; Rajya Sabha can delay 14 days but cannot reject; joint sitting (Article 108) convened by President to break deadlock — first held in 1961 on Dowry Prohibition Bill; Appropriation Bill, Financial Bill I & II also have special procedures.
  • Judiciary: Supreme Court (Articles 124-147) — highest court of appeal and constitutional interpreter; composed of Chief Justice + max 33 judges (currently 34); original jurisdiction in Federal disputes between Centre and States (Article 131); writ jurisdiction under Article 32; appointed by President after consultation with Collegium; retirement age 65 years; High Courts (Articles 214-231) — highest court in each State; composed of Chief Justice + judges as prescribed by President; writ jurisdiction under Article 226 is wider than Article 32.
  • Appointment of Judges — Collegium System: Established through three Judges Cases (First Judges Case 1981, Second Judges Case 1993, Third Judges Case 1998); Supreme Court collegium (CJI + 4 senior-most judges) recommends appointments to SC and HCs; National Judicial Appointments Commission (NJAC) Act, 2014 struck down by Supreme Court in Fourth Judges Case (2015) as violating judicial independence.
  • State Executive: Governor (Article 153) — nominal head of State, appointed by President for 5-year term; holds office during pleasure of President; Real executive is Council of Ministers headed by Chief Minister (Article 163) collectively responsible to State Legislative Assembly; Advocate General (Article 165); State Public Service Commission (Article 315).
  • State Legislature: Unicameral (Legislative Assembly/Vidhan Sabha) only in some States; Bicameral in 6 States: Andhra Pradesh, Bihar, Jammu & Kashmir (now UT with legislature), Karnataka, Maharashtra, Telangana, Uttar Pradesh; Legislative Council (Vidhan Parishad) — max 1/3 of AS strength, not more than 50; Chairman presides over LC; Deputy Chairman elected from among members.
  • Constitutional Bodies: Election Commission of India (Article 324) — superintendence, direction, control of elections; UPSC (Article 315) — recruitment to All-India Services and Group A & B civilian posts; Finance Commission (Article 280) — recommends distribution of tax proceeds between Centre and States every 5 years; CAG (Article 148) — audits all government accounts; National Commission for SCs (Article 338), NC for STs (Article 338A), NC for OBCs (statutory); National Commission for Women (statutory); National Human Rights Commission (statutory under Protection of Human Rights Act 1993).
  • Local Self-Government: Panchayati Raj (73rd Amendment, 1992) — three-tier system: Gram Panchayat at village level, Panchayat Samiti at block level, Zila Parishad at district level; 73rd Schedule lists 29 subjects; Gram Sabha is the foundation; reservation for SC/ST in proportion to population and 1/3 for women; Panchayats have power to prepare plans for economic development and social justice; Municipalities (74th Amendment, 1992) — urban local bodies: Nagar Panchayat, Municipal Council, Municipal Corporation; 74th Schedule lists 18 subjects; Nagar Palikas, Municipal Corporations, District Planning Committees.
  • Emergency Provisions — National Emergency (Article 352): Proclaimed on grounds of war, external aggression, or armed rebellion (original: 'internal disturbance' changed to 'armed rebellion' by 44th Amendment); effect: fundamental rights under Article 19 automatically suspended; remaining FRs can be suspended only during proclamation of Emergency under Article 359; President's Rule (Article 356): if State govt cannot be carried on per Constitutional provisions, President assumes functions; Assembly dissolved/suspended; 35th Amendment made Sikkim association; Judicial review available (S.R. Bommai case, 1994).
  • Financial Emergency (Article 360): Not yet imposed in India; President may reduce salaries of all State servants including Judges of High Court and Supreme Court; State budgets require prior approval of President; All Money Bills passed by State Legislatures require President's recommendation.
  • Citizenship: Part II, Articles 5-11; originally five modes: citizenship at commencement (Art 5), citizenship by birth (Art 6), citizenship by descent (Art 7), citizenship by registration (Art 8), citizenship by domicile (Art 9); later Parliament enacted Citizenship Act 1955 (amended 2003, 2005, 2019) providing acquisition and termination of citizenship; dual citizenship not permitted in India.
  • Special Provisions for Scheduled Tribes: Article 244 and Fifth/Sixth Schedules provide autonomy to tribal areas; Fifth Schedule applies to Scheduled Areas in States (except NE); Sixth Schedule applies to autonomous districts in Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram; Tribal Advisory Council (Article 244(2)); Special officer for SCs and STs — Commissioner for SCs and STs (now bifurcated).
  • Constitutional Amendments — Most Important for GPSC: 1st (1951) — reasonable restrictions on FRs, land reform; 7th (1956) — States Reorganization; 24th & 25th (1971) — FR vs DPSP conflict resolution; 42nd (1976) — 'Mini-Constitution': added Socialist, Secular, Integrity to Preamble, Fundamental Duties, curtailed judicial review; 44th (1978) — rolled back many 42nd amendments, changed 'internal disturbance' to 'armed rebellion'; 52nd (1985) — Anti-Defection Law (10th Schedule); 61st (1988) — lowered voting age from 21 to 18; 73rd & 74th (1992) — Panchayati Raj and Municipalities; 76th (1994) — Tamil Nadu's 69% reservation; 81st (2000) — crease class in backlog reservations; 86th (2002) — Right to Education as Fundamental Right (Art 21A); 101st (2016) — GST introduced; 103rd (2019) — 10% EWS reservation; 104th (2021) — extended SC/ST reservation in LS and State ASes till 2030; 105th (2021) — restored State list for OBC identification.
  • Writs — Detailed: Habeas Corpus (to produce a person unlawfully detained); Mandamus (command to a public authority to perform its duty); Prohibition (Higher court prohibits lower court from exceeding jurisdiction — preventive); Certiorari (Higher court quashes an order of lower court — curative); Quo Warranto (inquiry into the legality of a person's claim to a public office).
  • Ordinance Power: President (Article 123) and Governors (Article 213) can promulgate ordinances when Parliament/State Legislature is not in session; ordinance has same force as Act but must be approved by legislature within 6 weeks; if not approved, it lapses; maximum valid lifespan limited by Supreme Court (D.C. Wadhwa case, 1987).
  • Judicial Review: Power of courts to examine the constitutionality of legislative enactments and executive orders; embodied in Articles 13, 32, 226, 131, 245, 246; Indian judiciary can strike down laws violating Constitution; Supreme Court and High Courts exercise this power; basis of constitutional supremacy.
  • Parliamentary Committees: Select Committees (temporary, specific purpose) and Standing Committees (permanent, ongoing functions); important ones: Estimates Committee (largest committee, 30 LS members, examines economy of public expenditure), Public Accounts Committee (examine audit reports of CAG), Committee on Public Undertakings (examines performance of PSUs), Business Advisory Committee, Rules Committee, Business Advisory Committee of Rajya Sabha, Joint Select Committees for bills.

Important facts

  • Constitution adopted by Constituent Assembly on 26 November 1949; came into effect on 26 January 1950; 26 January chosen because Independence Day was celebrated on 26 January 1930 (Purna Swaraj Day).
  • Dr. B.R. Ambedkar is considered the 'Father of the Indian Constitution'; he was the Chairman of the Drafting Committee.
  • Total time taken to adopt the Constitution: 2 years, 11 months, 18 days; 11 sessions over 166 days; 11 Constituent Assembly sessions.
  • M.N. Roy suggested the name 'Constituent Assembly'.
  • Sachchidananda Sinha was the temporary President of the Constituent Assembly; Dr. Rajendra Prasad became its permanent President.
  • The Constitution originally had 395 articles in 22 parts and 8 schedules; currently over 470 articles, 25 parts, 12 schedules.
  • India is the largest democracy in the world; it has a federal system with unitary bias ('Union of States' — Article 1).
  • The concept of 'Judicial Review' was borrowed from the United States of America.
  • The concept of 'Directive Principles of State Policy' was borrowed from the Irish Constitution.
  • The concept of 'Fundamental Duties' was borrowed from the Constitution of the former USSR (Russian/Soviet Constitution).
  • The President of India is elected by an Electoral College consisting of elected members of both Houses of Parliament and elected members of State Legislative Assemblies — proportional representation by single transferable vote.
  • The Vice-President is also elected by an electoral college but only includes members of Parliament, not State Assembly members.
  • The minimum age for President is 35 years; for Vice-President it is 35 years; for Lok Sabha member it is 25 years; for Rajya Sabha member it is 30 years.
  • The Chief Justice of India is appointed by the President; other judges are appointed after consultation with the CJI.
  • The 44th Amendment (1978) is known as the 'Anti-Indira Gandhi' amendment; it restored several democratic safeguards.
  • Article 17 abolishes Untouchability; Article 18 abolishes Titles; Article 23 prohibits traffic in human beings and forced labour; Article 24 prohibits employment of children below 14 years in factories/mines/hazardous employment.
  • Article 370 (special status to Jammu & Kashmir) was abrogated on 5 August 2019 via Presidential Order and the J&K Reorganisation Act, 2019; reorganised into two Union Territories: J&K and Ladakh.
  • Article 371 — Special provisions for various States: A (Maharashtra/Gujarat), B (Gujarat-specific provisions were initially included but later restructured), C, D, F, G, H, I, J, K, L, M, N, P — these protect regional identities and administrative needs.
  • The 101st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2016 introduced Goods and Services Tax (GST) from 1 July 2017; replaced multiple indirect taxes with a dual GST structure (CGST + SGST/UTGST + IGST).
  • GST Council constituted under Article 279A; chaired by the Union Finance Minister; includes Union Finance Minister and State Finance Ministers.
  • The Ninth Schedule (added by 1st Amendment, 1951) protects laws from judicial review on the ground of violation of Fundamental Rights; however, in I.R. Coelho case (2007), Supreme Court held that laws in the Ninth Schedule are subject to judicial review if they violate basic structure.
  • The Tenth Schedule (Anti-Defection Law) was added by the 52nd Amendment Act, 1985;Speaker of the House/Chairman of the Council decides disputes under this Schedule;

Memory aids

  • Preamble keywords mnemonic: 'SSDIJLEF' — Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic, Justice, Liberty, Equality, Fraternity.
  • Six Fundamental Rights groups: 'E-F-R-C-D-U' — Equality, Freedom, Religion, Culture, Constitutional Remedies, (Exploitation was separate but now subsumed under Freedom).
  • Three types of Emergency: 'N-E-F' — National (Art 352), Emergency/President's Rule (Art 356), Financial (Art 360).
  • Five Writs: 'H-M-P-C-Q' — Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Certiorari, Quo Warranto. Remember 'Have My Pet Cat Quietly' (for the order: HC PC → Habeas Corpus, Certiorari, Prohibition, Mandamus, Quo Warranto).
  • Constitution borrowing mnemonic: 'USA-UK-Ireland-Canada-Australia-Japan-Germany-Russia-France' — FRs(USA), Rule of Law(UK), DPSP(Ireland), Federalism(Canada), Concurrent List(Australia), Procedure(Law Japan), Emergency(Germany), Duties(Russia), Procedure for Amendment(France-approx.).
  • Amendment milestones mnemonic: '1-7-24-42-44-52-61-73-74-86-101-103-104-105' — 1st(Restrictions), 7th(States reorg), 24th/25th(FR vs DPSP), 42th(Mini-Constitution), 44th(Anti-Indira), 52nd(Anti-defection), 61st(Voting age 18), 73rd/74th(Local bodies), 86th(RTE), 101st(GST), 103rd(EWS), 104th(Reservation extension), 105th(OBC identification).
  • Parts of Constitution mnemonics: Group remembering by topic — Articles 1-4 (Union Territory), 5-11 (Citizenship), 12-35 (FR), 36-51 (DPSP), 51A (FD), 52-78 (UE), 79-123 (Parliament), 124-147 (Supreme Court), 148-151 (CAG), 152-237 (State Exec + Legislature + High Court), 243-243O (Panchayats), 243P-243ZG (Municipalities), 244-244A (Scheduled Areas), 245-255 (Legislative Relations), 256-263 (Administration), 264-267 (Finance), 268-279 (Taxation), 279A-290A (GST/Compensation), 290-298 (Territory/States), 299-312 (Services), 315-323 (Tribunals), 324-329E (Elections), 330-342 (Reserved Seats), 343-351 (Official Language), 352-360 (Emergency), 360-395 (Miscellaneous/Transitional), 395+ (Repealed/Amended provisions).

Exam tips

  • For GPSC Prelims, focus on Articles, Amendments, and Key Cases — these are frequently asked directly (e.g., 'Which Article provides for...?', 'When was X amendment enacted?').
  • Pay special attention to articles related to Gujarat-specific provisions (Article 371, special status of Gujarat, specific Panchayati Raj provisions under Gujarat Panchayat Act).
  • Remember landmark cases: Kesavananda Bharati (basic structure), Maneka Gandhi (due process), Minerva Mills (FR vs DPSP), S.R. Bommai (Article 356), NJAC (collegium), I.R. Coelho (Ninth Schedule).
  • Practice numerical-based questions on President's election (electoral college composition), percentage of reservation in Panchayats, strength of Rajya Sabha from each state.
  • For Mains, develop answers with constitutional article references, case law citations, and contemporary examples to strengthen analytical depth.
  • Don't ignore Current Affairs linkages: new Bills, recent Supreme Court judgments on polity, constitutional amendments, and major policy changes related to governance are often tested.
  • Differentiate between 'may be prescribed by law' restrictions on FRs versus 'reasonable restrictions' — wording matters in options.
  • Note the difference between Fundamental Rights enforceable during Emergency: Article 20 and 21 cannot be suspended even during National Emergency (Article 359).
  • Keep a separate short note on each Constitutional Body: function, appointment, tenure, removal, and key powers — these are high-yield for objective questions.
  • Focus on GPSC-specific pattern: Prelims has two objective papers (GS-I and GS-II); GS-I typically carries substantial polity questions; ensure coverage matches GPSC's actual weightage from previous years.
  • When answering assertion-reason type questions on polity, carefully evaluate whether the Reason correctly explains the Assertion — this is a common trap pattern in GPSC.
  • Use elimination strategy for MCQs: eliminate obviously wrong options first, then compare remaining ones based on article numbers and amendment years you have memorised.
  • Time management in Prelims: polity questions are usually quicker to solve than science/gk; allocate time accordingly and return to difficult ones later.
  • Revise Preamble, Fundamental Rights (Articles 12-35), DPSP (36-51), and Fundamental Duties (51A) thoroughly — these form the backbone of every polity question set.
  • Be thorough on local self-government since GPSC emphasizes Gujarat's Panchayati Raj system — know the Gujarat Panchayat Act, 1961 alongside the 73rd Amendment.

Common traps

  • Confusing 'Rule of Law' (UK origin, judicial supremacy) with 'Sovereignty of Parliament' (UK concept). India follows constitutional supremacy, not parliamentary sovereignty.
  • Mixing up Habeas Corpus and Mandamus: Habeas Corpus is about unlawful detention; Mandamus is about compelling performance of public duty.
  • Thinking Prohibition and Certiorari are the same: Prohibition is preventive (issued before order); Certiorari is curative (issued after order to quash).
  • Confusing 42nd Amendment (1976) with 44th Amendment (1978): 42nd added FD, amended Preamble, curtailed judiciary; 44th rolled back many 42nd changes, restored democratic rights.
  • Assuming Fundamental Rights apply to foreigners equally: Most FRs (especially Arts 15, 19, 29-30) are available only to citizens; some (Arts 14, 20-22, 23-28) extend to all persons.
  • Forgetting that the Right to Property (Art 31) was removed as a Fundamental Right by the 44th Amendment (1978) and is now a legal right under Article 300A.
  • Confusing the President's rule under Article 356 with National Emergency under Article 352: 356 is state-level breakdown; 352 is national-level threat.
  • Thinking Rajya Sabha can introduce Money Bills: Only Lok Sabha can introduce Money Bills (Article 110); Rajya Sabha can only recommend changes within 14 days.
  • Believing that Fundamental Duties are justiciable: They are non-justiciable (no direct legal enforcement), though courts can consider them while interpreting laws.
  • Confusing the terms 'Secular' and 'Socialist' added to Preamble: Both were added together by the 42nd Amendment in 1976, not separately.
  • Thinking the Governor holds office during the pleasure of the President indefinitely: Governor holds office during the pleasure of the President but can only be removed by President; there is no impeachment process like the President.
  • Assuming all constitutional amendments require ratification by States: Only amendments affecting federal provisions (Art 368(2)) require State ratification; most amendments need only parliamentary special majority.
  • Confusing the jurisdiction of Supreme Court (Art 32) and High Court (Art 226) for writs: High Court's writ jurisdiction is wider — it can issue writs for enforcement of FRs AND for any other purpose.
  • Forgetting that the 103rd Amendment (EWS reservation) was challenged and upheld by Supreme Court in Janhit Abhiyan v. Union of India (2022) — this is highly relevant for current GPSC questions.
