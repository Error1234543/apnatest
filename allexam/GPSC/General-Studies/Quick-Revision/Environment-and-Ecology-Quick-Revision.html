Quick Revision: Environment and Ecology (GPSC)

GPSCEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Ecology is the scientific study of interactions between organisms and their physical/biological environment.
  • Biodiversity refers to the variety of all life forms on Earth—gene, species, and ecosystem diversity.
  • Ecosystem is a functional unit where living organisms interact with each other and the surrounding abiotic environment.
  • Biome is a large naturally occurring community of flora and fauna occupying a major habitat (e.g., desert, tundra, rainforest).
  • Carrying capacity is the maximum population size of a species that an environment can sustain indefinitely.
  • Biological magnification is the increasing concentration of a pollutant in the tissues of organisms at successively higher levels in a food chain.
  • Conservation is the sustainable use and protection of natural resources and biological diversity.
  • Sustainability means meeting present needs without compromising the ability of future generations to meet theirs.
  • Endangered species is a category of species that is at risk of becoming extinct.
  • Invasive alien species are non-native species that cause ecological or economic harm in a new environment.

Key concepts

  • Trophic levels and energy flow: Only about 10% of energy is transferred from one trophic level to the next; energy pyramids are always upright.
  • Ecological succession: Sequential process of change in species composition over time—primary (bare rock) vs secondary (post-fire/flood).
  • Biogeochemical cycles: Carbon cycle, nitrogen cycle, phosphorus cycle, water cycle. Nitrogen fixation is performed by Rhizobium, cyanobacteria, Azotobacter.
  • Biodiversity hotspots: Regions with high species endemism under threat. India shares four hotspots: Western Ghats, Eastern Himalayas, Indo-Burma, and Sundaland (Lakshadweep islands).
  • Climate change drivers: Greenhouse gases—CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, CFCs. Global warming potential varies; CH₄ is ~28× CO₂ over 100 years.
  • Environmental impact assessment (EIA): Statutory process in India under EIA Notification 2006; mandatory for certain infrastructure/industrial projects.
  • Indian Wildlife Protection Act, 1972: Provides schedules for animal protection (Schedule I most stringent), established sanctuaries/national parks, banned trade in specified animals.
  • Forest Rights Act, 2006: Recognizes rights of forest-dwelling communities over land and resources.
  • Green Revolution impacts: Increased yields but soil degradation, groundwater depletion, pesticide resistance, loss of crop diversity.
  • Gujarat-specific ecology: Great Rann of Kutch (salt desert ecosystem), Gir National Park (Asiatic lion), Gulf of Kutch (marine biodiversity), Bhitarkanika (mangroves, odisha-Gujarat linkage).

Important facts

  • India ratified the UNFCCC in 1994; Paris Agreement adopted in 2015, ratified by India in 2016.
  • Ramkarni Committee (1996) and Misra Commission reviewed Panchayat powers on forest/water resources.
  • CITES (Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species) came into force in 1975; India is a contracting party since 1976.
  • Ramsar Convention on Wetlands adopted in 1971; India has over 80 Ramsar sites including Chilka, Sambhar, Vembanad.
  • National Bamboo Mission launched in 2006-07; merged into Mission for Overall Management of Biomass in later years.
  • Compensatory Afforestation Fund Act (CAFRA) 2016 established National and State Compensatory Afforestation Funds.
  • National Action Plan on Climate Change (NAPCC) launched in 2008 with eight missions: Solar, Mission for Maintaining Himalayan Ecosystem, etc.
  • Project Tiger started in 1973; currently 56 tiger reserves in India.
  • Project Elephant launched in 1992 to protect elephant populations and corridors.
  • Gujarat State Biodiversity Board constituted under Biological Diversity Act, 2002; State Biodiversity Boards manage access and benefit sharing.

Memory aids

  • Biodiversity hotspots mnemonic: 'WES-I' — Western Ghats, Eastern Himalayas, Sundaland, Indo-Burma.
  • Greenhouse gases mnemonic: 'CMNC' — CO₂, Methane, Nitrous oxide, CFCs.
  • Tropical rainforest features: Always hot and wet, layered canopy, high endemism, thin nutrient-poor soils.
  • Schedule-wise protection strength: Schedule I > Schedule II > … > Schedule V (hunting prohibited across all, but penalties differ).
  • IUCN Red List categories mnemonic: 'Extinct → EW → CR → EN → VU → NT → LC → DD' (Never Ever Crash Rockets Near Loud Drones Daily).

Exam tips

  • GPSC frequently asks about Gujarat-specific environmental features: Rann of Kutch, Gir, marine parks, and state policies on solar/wind energy.
  • Link environment questions with economy: renewable energy targets, green hydrogen mission, carbon credit trading scheme.
  • For current-affairs-linked ecology questions, note recent Ramsar site designations, new wildlife sanctuary notifications, and climate summits held in India.
  • Remember constitutional provisions: Article 48A (State duty to protect environment) and Article 51A(g) (fundamental duty to protect environment).
  • Practice statement-based questions: many GPSC prelims use 'Which of the following statements is/are correct?' format for environment-lifestyle linkages.
  • Do not confuse the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change with the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy.
  • Use elimination in biodiversity questions: eliminate options that pair wrong species with wrong location or wrong conservation status.

Common traps

  • Confusing the Wildlife Protection Act, 1972 with the Forest Conservation Act, 1980. First protects fauna, second regulates diversion of forest land.
  • Thinking 'endemic' means 'rare'. Endemic species are restricted to a specific geographic area; rarity is different.
  • Assuming all national parks allow tribal habitation. In India, human habitation inside NP is generally not permitted unless specifically allowed.
  • Mistaking carbon dioxide as the only greenhouse gas. Methane and nitrous oxide have higher global warming potentials.
  • Believing the Biological Diversity Act, 2002 replaced the Wildlife Protection Act. They address different aspects—biodiversity conservation and sustainable use vs species protection.
  • Assuming the Green Climate Fund is an Indian scheme. It is a UN-backed international fund.
  • Confusing COP meetings: COP26 was Glasgow, COP27 was Sharm El-Sheikh, COP28 was Dubai.
  • Thinking all biosphere reserves are also tiger reserves. India has 18 biosphere reserves; not all coincide with tiger reserves.
