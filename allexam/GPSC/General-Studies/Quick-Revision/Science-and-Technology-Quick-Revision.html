Science and Technology — Quick Revision for GPSC General Studies

GPSCEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Science and Technology in the GPSC context refers to the systematic study of natural phenomena (science) and the practical application of scientific knowledge to develop tools, processes, and systems (technology), as relevant to India's development, governance, and global competitiveness.
  • Space Technology: The branch of technology concerned with the design, construction, and operation of spacecraft and satellites for communication, navigation, Earth observation, and scientific exploration.
  • Biotechnology: The use of living systems and organisms — or their derivatives — to develop or make products and processes for specific uses, spanning healthcare, agriculture, industry, and environmental management.
  • Nanotechnology: The manipulation of matter at the atomic, molecular, and supramolecular scale (approximately 1–100 nanometres) to create materials, devices, and systems with novel properties and functions.
  • Renewable Energy: Energy derived from naturally replenishing sources such as solar, wind, tidal, geothermal, and biomass, distinguished from fossil fuels by its sustainability and lower carbon emissions.
  • Information and Communication Technology (ICT): An umbrella term that includes all technical means used to handle information and aid communication, including computers, the Internet, broadcasting networks, and telephony.
  • Artificial Intelligence (AI): A branch of computer science dealing with the simulation of intelligent behaviour in machines, including machine learning, natural language processing, robotics, and expert systems.
  • Defence Technology: Technologies developed primarily for military and security purposes, including missile systems, radar, stealth, naval vessels, aerospace platforms, and network-centric warfare systems.
  • Digital India: A flagship programme launched in 2015 to ensure government services are made available to citizens electronically by improving online infrastructure and by issuing Internet connectivity.
  • Green Technology: Technologies that seek to mitigate negative human impact on the environment through sustainable practices, pollution control, energy efficiency, and conservation of natural resources.

Key concepts

  • Indian Space Programme — ISRO: Founded in 1969; led by Dr. V. Ramakrishna (Chairman, 2023 onwards). Key milestones: Chandrayaan-1 (2008, first Indian Moon mission), Mangalyaan/Mars Orbiter Mission (2013, first Asian nation to reach Mars orbit), Chandrayaan-2 (2019, orbiter + lander Vikram + rover Pragyan), Chandrayaan-3 (2023, successful soft landing near lunar south pole), Aditya-L1 (2023, India's first solar observatory mission placed at Lagrange point L1). PSLV and GSLV are workhorse launch vehicles. Gaganyaan is India's crewed spaceflight programme. NSIL (NewSpace India Limited, est. 2019) commercialises ISRO's technologies.
  • Atomic Energy Programme: Initiated by Homi J. Bhabha. Three-stage nuclear power programme: Stage 1 — Pressurised Heavy Water Reactors (PHWR) using natural uranium and heavy water; Stage 2 — Fast Breeder Test Reactor (FBTR) and Prototype Fast Breeder Reactor (PFBR) using plutonium and uranium-238; Stage 3 — Thorium-based Advanced Heavy Water Reactors (AHWR) exploiting India's large thorium reserves. BARC (Bhabha Atomic Research Centre), Tata Institute of Fundamental Research (TIFR), and DAE (Department of Atomic Energy) are key institutions.
  • Biotechnology in India: DBT (Department of Biotechnology, est. 1986) is the nodal agency. India is a global leader in biosimilars, vaccine production (Serum Institute of India), and agricultural biotech (Bt cotton). Key areas: medical biotech (gene therapy, monoclonal antibodies), agri-biotech (stress-resistant crops, biofertilisers), industrial biotech (enzymes, biofuels), and environmental biotech (bioremediation). Genome Valley (Hyderabad) and Biotech Park (Chennai) are major hubs. The National Biopharma Mission and National Institute of Biologicals are important recent initiatives.
  • Nanotechnology — Indian Initiatives: Mission on Navigating Micro and Nano Electronics (Mi-MeLi) aims to develop indigenous nanoelectronics capabilities. The Nano Mission was launched by DST in 2007. IISc, IITs, and RRI (Raman Research Institute) are key research centres. Applications include drug delivery, water purification, electronics, and advanced materials. CSIR-Nano Science and Technology Initiative promotes translational nanotech research.
  • Renewable Energy in India: India ranks among the top countries in installed renewable capacity. Solar: International Solar Alliance (ISA), co-founded by India and France in 2015, headquartered in Gurugram.PM-Kusum scheme for solar pumps. Wind: Tamil Nadu, Maharashtra, Karnataka lead in installed wind capacity. Green Hydrogen Mission (2023) targets making India a global hub for green hydrogen production and export. Net-zero target by 2070 announced by PM Narendra Modi at COP26 (Glasgow, 2021).
  • Information Technology and Digital Infrastructure: India Stack — a set of open APIs enabling digital public infrastructure (Aadhaar, UPI, ONDC, DigiLocker, CoWIN). UPI (Unified Payments Interface) launched in 2016 by NPCI; now one of the world's largest real-time payment systems. BharatNet aims to connect all Gram Panchayats with high-speed internet. 5G rollout began in October 2022. India's semiconductor mission aims to establish fab capacity. Chaudhary Charan Singh National Institute of Agricultural Marketing (CCS NIAM) and C-DAC are key institutions.
  • Artificial Intelligence and Emerging Technologies: NITI Aayog released the National Strategy for AI (2018) focusing on health, agriculture, education, smart cities, and mobility. AI platforms include IndiaAI Mission (₹10,000 crore allocation announced in Budget 2024-25). Quantum Computing: National Quantum Mission (₹6,003 crore) approved for 2023-2031, targeting interim/early quantum computers, long-distance quantum communication, and indigenous quantum technologies. India's first quantum satellite SREYAS (planned). Blockchain: India has explored blockchain for land records, supply chain, and CBDC (Digital Rupee — e-Rupee, RBI pilot launched in 2022).
  • Defence Technology — Indigenous Programmes: Agni missile series (Agni-I to Agni-V, range up to 5,000+ km) under DRDO. BrahMos supersonic cruise missile (joint Indo-Russian venture). Tejas LCA (Light Combat Aircraft) by HAL. INS Vikrant (indigenous aircraft carrier, commissioned 2022). Arjun main battle tank. Pinaka multi-barrel rocket launcher. Akash surface-to-air missile system. S-400 Triumf air defence system (imported from Russia). Atmanirbhar Bharat initiative has accelerated defence indigenisation. DRDO established in 1958; HQ in New Delhi.
  • ISRO's Earth Observation and Navigation: Cartosat series for high-resolution imaging. IRS (Indian Remote Sensing) satellites for agriculture, forestry, and disaster management. NVS series for navigation augmentation. NavIC (Navigation with Indian Constellation, formerly IRNSS) — operational regional navigation system covering India and 1,360 km around it. INSAT/GSAT series for communications, meteorology, and DTH.
  • Science Policy and Institutions: DST (Department of Science and Technology) promotes basic and applied research. DSIR (Directorate General of Scientific and Industrial Research) coordinates industrial R&D. CSIR (Council of Scientific and Industrial Research) operates 39 laboratories across India. ICMR (Indian Council of Medical Research) for medical research. ICAR (Indian Council of Agricultural Research) for agricultural research. SERB (Science and Engineering Research Board) funds fundamental research via grants. IISc, IITs, and IISERs are premier research institutions. The Ministry of Science & Technology oversees DST and DBT.
  • Notable Indian Scientists and Contributions: CV Raman — discovery of Raman Effect (1928, Nobel Prize 1930); Homi J. Bhabha — father of Indian nuclear programme; APJ Abdul Kalam — missile man, key role in Pokhran-II and SLV/PSLV programmes; Vikram Sarabhai — father of Indian space programme; C.V. Raman — Raman spectroscopy; Srinivasa Ramanujan — contributions to number theory, infinite series; Meghnad Saha — Saha ionization equation; Jagadish Chandra Bose — pioneer in radio/microwave optics; Madhavan Nair — Chandrayaan-1 architect; K. Sivan — Chandrayaan-2/3 and Mangalyaan mission director.
  • International Science Collaborations: India-Japan: JUICE (Jupiter Ice Moons Explorer), GPS Aid; India-France: SSLV collaboration, Chambar/Rosetta participation; India-Russia: S-400, GLONASS integration, Roscosmos cooperation; India-US: NASA partnerships (NISAR mission), iCET (India-US Critical and Emerging Technology Initiative); India-EU: satellite navigation cooperation, climate research. India is a member of the International Thermonuclear Experimental Reactor (ITER) project for fusion energy research.
  • Science in Everyday Governance and Public Policy: SCISAP (Scheme for Promotion of Science, Innovation and Entrepreneurship) bridges grassroots innovators with industry. Rashtriya Vigyan Forum (RVF) promotes science popularisation. Khan Academy and e-Yantra support STEM education. National Science Day is celebrated on 28 February annually (commemorating discovery of Raman Effect). International Science, Technology and Innovation Centre (ISTC) for South-South cooperation was proposed by India at UNESCO.
  • Emerging Topics for GPSC: 6G research begins in India; indigenous chip design initiatives; biotechnology parks in Gujarat (Sanand Biotech Park); Gujarat's renewable energy targets (wind and solar); Gujarat Atomic Power Station (Planned at Palanpur) and DAE's role; Blue Economy initiatives; Ocean Mission; India's role in global science diplomacy including hosting ICSU/ISC events; GSTN digital infrastructure; ONDC as open-source digital commerce platform.

Important facts

  • ISRO was established in 1969; Headquarters at Bengaluru. Chairman: Dr. V. Ramakrishna (as of 2023–24).
  • Chandrayaan-3 successfully landed near the lunar south pole on 23 August 2023, making India the fourth country to achieve a soft lunar landing and the first to land near the south pole.
  • Mangalyaan (Mars Orbiter Mission) was launched on 5 November 2013 and entered Mars orbit on 24 September 2013 — first Asian nation and first in the world to succeed on its maiden attempt.
  • Aditya-L1 was launched on 2 September 2023; it orbits at the Sun–Earth L1 Lagrange point (~1.5 million km from Earth).
  • Gaganyaan: India's human spaceflight programme, targeted for first crewed flight in 2025 (unmanned demo flights completed earlier). Four astronauts selected for training.
  • NSIL (NewSpace India Limited), established in 2019 under DPE, commercialises space technology developed by ISRO and DLT.
  • GSLV Mark III (formerly LVM3) is the heaviest launch vehicle developed by ISRO, capable of placing ~4,000 kg payloads in GEO.
  • NavIC (IRNSS) provides positioning accuracy better than 20 metres in the Indian region; operates in seven frequencies (L5, S-band, and L1 bands).
  • DRDO was formed on 1 January 1958 by merging various defence R&D establishments under DES (Directorate of Defence Research and Development). Headquarters: New Delhi.
  • Agni-V has a range exceeding 5,000 km and is a three-stage solid-fuelled ballistic missile.
  • INS Vikrant (IAC-1) was commissioned on 2 September 2022 at Kochi; displaces approximately 45,000 tonnes.
  • India ratified the Paris Agreement on 2 October 2016; committed to net-zero emissions by 2070 and 500 GW non-fossil fuel capacity by 2030.
  • International Solar Alliance (ISA) was launched in 2015 by India and France; has 119 member nations (as of 2024), mostly between the Tropics of Cancer and Capricorn.
  • UPI was launched on 21 July 2016 by NPCI; crossed 10 billion monthly transactions in early 2023.
  • Digital Rupee (e-Rupee) — RBI pilot launched on 1 November 2022; initially in retail (e-Rupee-W) and wholesale (e-Rupee-W) segments.
  • National Quantum Mission (NQM) approved in December 2023 with outlay of ₹6,003 crore for eight years (2023–2031).
  • India's first semiconductor fabrication plant (foundry) is being set up by Tata Electronics and Micron in Gujarat (Dholera SIR) and Karnataka.
  • Green Hydrogen Mission aims to create a global hub for green hydrogen production with an outlay of ₹19,744 crore (including central financial support).
  • DBT (Department of Biotechnology) falls under the Ministry of Science and Technology; established in 1986.
  • CSIR has 39 labs across India; headquarters in New Delhi. Director-General (as of recent): Dr. Sugata Ray.
  • SERB was established in 2008 under the Science and Engineering Research Board Act, 2008; promotes basic and applied research.
  • National Science Day is celebrated on 28 February every year.
  • India signed a Memorandum of Understanding with NASA for the NISAR (NASA-ISRO Synthetic Aperture Radar) mission, scheduled for launch in 2024–25.
  • Indira Gandhi Centre for Atomic Research (IGCAR) at Kalpakkam focuses on fast reactor technology.
  • PIL (Physics Instrumentation Laboratory) at Trombay and IPR (Institute for Plasma Research) at Gandhinagar are key atomic energy facilities; IPR participates in ITER.
  • Gujarat has significant renewable energy capacity; the state leads in wind power installation and has solar parks at Mandvi (Kutch) and Bhavani Island.
  • India's biofuel blending target is 20% (Ethanol) and 5% (Diesel) by 2025–26; ethanol blending programme extended under PM ETF (Ethanol Blending Target).
  • C-DAC (Centre for Development of Advanced Computing) was established in 1988; developed India's first supercomputer PARAM (1991) and various e-governance solutions.
  • India's first indigenously built ice-breaking research vessel 'MVG Sagar PrahariKA' was commissioned in 2023 for Antarctic missions.
  • The ATAL incubation centres (under MeitY) promote startups in technology and innovation across India.
  • India's Chandrayaan-2 orbiter is still operational and has contributed significantly to lunar science data collection since 2009.

Memory aids

  • Chandrayaan Chronology: 1 → Mars Orbiter/Mangalyaan (2013); 2 → Moon southern polar attempt (2019, orbiter succeeded, lander crashed); 3 → Successful south-pole landing (2023); 4 → (announced, upcoming). Remember as '1 failed landing, 2 came close, 3 succeeded on the pole.'
  • Agni Missiles mnemonic: 'Agni comes in five flames — I, II, III, IV, V — each bigger, reaching further south to north across India.' Agni-I (~700 km), Agni-II (~2,000 km), Agni-III (~3,500 km), Agni-IV (~4,000 km), Agni-V (>5,000 km).
  • ISRO launch vehicles: 'PSLV is the workhorse — dependable, many launches; GSLV is the heavy lifter — needs cryogenic engine; LVM3 is the newest beast — carries Gaganyaan crew modules.' PSLV = Polar Satellite Launch Vehicle; GSLV = Geosynchronous Satellite Launch Vehicle.

Exam tips

  • For GPSC GS Science & Technology questions, expect a mix of factual recall (missions, dates, institutions) and application-based questions linking science to governance, economy, and society.
  • Focus on chronological awareness: know when key missions launched and what they achieved. Timeline-type questions (e.g., 'which mission happened first?') are common.
  • Do not neglect Indian scientists and their contributions. Questions on Raman Effect, Bhabha, Sarabhai, and Kalam appear regularly in state-level exams.
  • Link science and technology with current affairs: the latest ISRO mission, new government scheme (e.g., NQM, Green Hydrogen), and recent defence induction are fair game for both prelims and mains.
  • Practice mapping institutions to their parent ministries and locations. This helps eliminate options in MCQs quickly.
  • When a question asks about 'indigenous' technology, remember the Atmanirbhar Bharat push has made indigenisation a hot topic — distinguish between indigenously developed, jointly developed, and imported systems.
  • In mains answer writing, connect science and technology with developmental outcomes: e.g., how UPI transforms financial inclusion, how remote sensing aids disaster management in Gujarat, how solar energy reduces Gujarat's dependence on coal.
  • Pay attention to numbers: mission years, ranges of missiles, capacity targets (500 GW renewables by 2030), and funding amounts (₹6,003 crore for NQM, ₹19,744 crore for Green Hydrogen Mission). Approximate figures are acceptable in prelims.
  • For Gujarat-specific questions: know about Gujarat Atomic Power Station (proposed at Palanpur/Dahod), DAE's role, renewable energy projects in Kutch and the Gulf of Khambhat, and the Sanand Biotech Park. Gujarat also contributes to India's semiconductor ecosystem (Tata-Micron project in Dholera).
  • Elimination strategy: if two options describe similar space-related facts, the one with the correct year or result is usually right. Cross-check mission names (Chandrayaan vs Mangalyaan vs Aditya) with their objectives (Moon vs Mars vs Sun).

Common traps

  • Confusing ISRO with DRDO: ISRO handles space satellites and launch vehicles; DRDO handles weapons, missiles, and defence systems. Do not attribute missile development to ISRO.
  • Mistaking NavIC for GPS: NavIC is an Indian regional navigation system; GPS is American global. NavIC has limited coverage (~1,360 km from India's borders).
  • Confusing CSIR with CSC: CSIR is Council of Scientific and Industrial Research (research labs); CSC is Common Services Centre (Citizen service delivery under MeitY). Very different mandates.
  • Thinking the Nuclear Three-Stage Programme is complete: Only Stage 1 is fully operational. Stage 2 (PFBR) achieved first criticality in 2023 but is not yet commercial. Stage 3 (AHWR) is still under development.
  • Attributing Agni missiles to ISRO: Agni missiles are developed by DRDO, not ISRO. ISRO's launch vehicles (PSLV, GSLV) are separate.
  • Mixing up ISA headquarters: ISA is headquartered in Gurugram (not New Delhi or Bengaluru). Co-founded by India and France in 2015.
  • Assuming all Indian satellites are launched by PSLV: GSLV and LVM3 also launch satellites; PSLV is the most frequent but not exclusive launch vehicle.
  • Confusing Chandrayaan missions: Chandrayaan-1 (orbiter only, 2008); Chandrayaan-2 (orbiter working, lander crashed, 2019); Chandrayaan-3 (successful lander + rover, 2023). Do not conflate results of different missions.
  • Thinking Black Money or Demonetisation falls under Science & Technology: These are economic/governance topics, not science and technology subjects in the GPSC syllabus.
  • Assuming Digital India equals just Aadhaar: Digital India encompasses broadband, e-sign, DigiLocker, ONDC, CoWIN, UPI, and many more pillars beyond just identity infrastructure.
  • Confusing Green Hydrogen with Blue Hydrogen: Green hydrogen is produced via electrolysis using renewable electricity; blue hydrogen is produced from natural gas with carbon capture. India's mission specifically targets green hydrogen.
  • Assuming NQM (National Quantum Mission) is about quantum entanglement only: It covers computing, communication, sensing, and materials — not just theoretical physics experiments.
  • Mixing up IPR Gandhinagar and IPR Ahmedabad: IPR (Institute for Plasma Research) is located in Gandhinagar, not Ahmedabad city centre. It is a key DAE institution involved in fusion research and ITER.
  • Confusing the roles of SERB and DST: DST is a ministry-level department that formulates policy and funds research broadly; SERB is an autonomous body under DST that specifically funds scientific and engineering research through grants and fellowships.
