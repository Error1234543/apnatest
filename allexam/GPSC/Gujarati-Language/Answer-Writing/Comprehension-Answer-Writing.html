GPSCEnglishAnswer Writing

Read the following Gujarati passage (approx. 150–200 words) on a theme related to Gujarat's cultural heritage — for example, the significance of the Sun Temple at Modhera, the tradition of Ravanhaba, or the role of bhakti saints like Narsinh Mehta in social reform — and write a structured answer covering: (a) main idea of the passage, (b) two key supporting details, (c) one inference you can draw, and (d) a short concluding sentence in your own words. Aim for 120–160 words in Gujarati.

Suggested points

  • Identify the central theme before writing; do not begin listing details without stating the main idea first.
  • Support your summary with direct evidence from the passage — paraphrase accurately rather than copying verbatim.
  • Inference should go beyond what is explicitly stated but must remain grounded in the text; avoid unrelated generalisations.
  • Conclude with a single sentence that reflects the passage's broader relevance to Gujarat's cultural or social context.
  • Maintain formal Gujarati register throughout; avoid mixing colloquial spellings with literary vocabulary.

Given a Gujarati expository passage on a contemporary issue — such as water conservation in Saurashtra, eco-tourism in Gir, or the impact of digital literacy in rural Gujarat — write a paragraph-style answer (100–140 words) that: (a) explains the problem described, (b) outlines the measures suggested or implied in the passage, and (c) evaluates whether those measures are practical for Gujarat's geography and administration. Write in clear, concise Gujarati.

Suggested points

  • Separate description from evaluation: describe only what the passage says before offering your assessment.
  • Link each suggested measure to a specific geographical or administrative feature of Gujarat (e.g., semi-arid climate, panchayat-level implementation, groundwater table decline).
  • Use cause-effect logic when judging practicability — state why a measure may or may not work, not merely that it does or does not.
  • Avoid adding external facts not inferable from the passage; stick to what the text supports.
  • If the passage mentions data (rainfall figures, forest cover %, number of villages), reference it briefly to strengthen credibility.

You are given a short Gujarati narrative or anecdote — for instance, a story about a local farmer adapting to climate change, a teacher's experience in a remote tribal school, or a community-led effort to protect a wetland. Write an answer (130–170 words) in Gujarati that: (a) narrates the sequence of events in chronological order, (b) identifies the protagonist's motivation, (c) states the outcome, and (d) extracts one moral or social lesson applicable to Gujarat today.

Suggested points

  • Use sequencing markers (પહેલાં, ત્યારબાદ, અંતે) to maintain chronological clarity.
  • Motivation should be inferred from the character's actions and dialogue in the passage, not imagined externally.
  • The moral or lesson must connect to a real Gujarati social context (e.g., community solidarity, sustainable livelihood, gender equity in education).
  • Keep narration concise; do not retell every detail — select only what advances the theme.
  • Avoid overly dramatic language; GPSC values measured, analytical tone even in narrative-based answers.

Read a Gujarati passage containing argumentative or persuasive content — for example, an editorial on preserving classical dance forms like Garba and Ras, a debate on mother-tongue education versus English-medium schooling in Gujarat, or an opinion piece on protecting the Arabian Sea coastline. Write a response (140–180 words) that: (a) states the author's position clearly, (b) lists three arguments used, (c) identifies any counter-argument acknowledged and how the author refutes it, and (d) gives your own balanced view in one short paragraph.

Suggested points

  • State the author's stance in the opening sentence using phraseology such as 'લેખકનો મુખ્ય મત છે કે...'
  • Each argument should be distinct in content; do not split one idea into two parts artificially.
  • Refutation, if present, must be explained in terms of logic, not merely labelled as 'dismissed.'
  • Your own view should acknowledge strength in the opposing side before stating agreement or disagreement.
  • Do not introduce new policy proposals unless the passage implicitly invites them; stay within the passage's frame.

Given a Gujarati passage describing a historical event or figure connected to Gujarat — for example, the contributions of Karnavati, the freedom struggle in Gujarat, the integration of princely states, or the legacy of Mahatma Gandhi in Sabarmati Ashram — write an answer (150–200 words) that: (a) places the event/figure in its correct historical timeframe, (b) summarises its significance for Gujarat specifically, (c) connects it to one broader Indian historical theme, and (d) writes a one-line concluding remark on its contemporary relevance.

Suggested points

  • Accuracy of period matters; use correct century or decade terminology rather than vague phrases like 'આજેથી બહુ પહેલાં.'
  • Gujarat-specific significance must be explicit — mention district, city, community, or movement by name where possible.
  • The broader Indian theme should be genuinely linked (e.g., non-cooperation, constitutionalism, federalism), not tacked on as a generic statement.
  • Contemporary relevance must be a concrete connection (institution, law, tradition, place still active today), not a rhetorical flourish.
  • Maintain formal historical register; avoid storytelling tone unless the passage itself is narrative.
