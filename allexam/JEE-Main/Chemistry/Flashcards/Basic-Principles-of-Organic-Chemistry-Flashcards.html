Basic Principles of Organic Chemistry – JEE Main Flashcards (Hindi)

Card 1

आधारभूत सिद्धांत – बंध टूटने के प्रकार

संयुक्त बंध का विखंडन दो प्रकार से होता है: • समरूप विखंडन (Homolytic fission): दोनों परमाणु प्रत्येक एक-एक इलेक्ट्रॉन लेकर जाते हैं। इससे मुक्त मूलक (free radical) बनते हैं। • असमरूप विखंडन (Heterolytic fission): एक परमाणु दोनों इलेक्ट्रॉन ले लेता है। इससे आयन (cation/anion) बनते हैं। → जो परमाणु इलेक्ट्रॉनों की कमी रखे → कैटायन → जो परमाणु अतिरिक्त इलेक्ट्रॉन ग्रहण करे → एनायन

Card 2

मुक्त मूलक (Free Radical) की स्थिरता

मुक्त मूलकों की स्थिरता क्रम: 3° > 2° > 1° > CH₃· > ·CH=CH₂ > ·C≡CH कारण: α-H की संख्या बढ़ने पर हाइपरकम्प्यूगेशन (+R) और इलेक्ट्रॉन-दान करने वाले समूह की उपस्थिति से अयुग्मित इलेक्ट्रॉन का वितरण होता है। हैलोजन मुक्त मूलक: I· > Br· > Cl· > F· (बंदन ऊर्जा ↓ → मूलक अधिक स्थिर)

Card 3

कार्बोकैटायन की स्थिरता – मुख्य कारक

कार्बोकैटायन (C⁺) की स्थिरता: 3° > 2° > 1° > CH₃⁺ कारण: 1. +I प्रभाव (alkan group electrons push towards C⁺) 2. हाइपरकम्प्यूगेशन (α-C–H bonds का σ-electron C⁺ में delocalisation) 3. +R प्रभाव (π-system या lone pair सहलग्नता से स्थिरीकरण) उदाहरण: allyl⁺ ≈ benzyl⁺ > 3° alkyl C⁺

Card 4

कार्बेन (Carbene) – संरचना और विशेषताएँ

कार्बेन :CR₂ एक तटस्थ मध्यस्थ है जिसमें कार्बन के चारों ओर 6e⁻ होते हैं। • सिंगलेट कार्बेन:sp², एक ζ-बंध और एक अयुग्मित युग्म; सभी इलेक्ट्रॉन्स युग्मित होते हैं। стереосिलेक्टिव योग करता है। • ट्रिपलेट कार्बेन:sp, दो अयुग्मित इलेक्ट्रॉन्स (Hund's rule); कम संकीर्ण, कम stereoselective। उदाहरण: :CH₂ (मेथिलीन कार्बेन) – डाइएजोमीथेन की UV अपघटना से।

Card 5

नाइट्रीन (Nitrene) – संक्षिप्त सार

नाइट्रीन :NR एक तटस्थ, अत्यधिक अभिक्रियाशील मध्यस्थ है (6 valence e⁻ on N)। • सिंगलेट नाइट्रीन: sp², एक ζ-बंध + एक युग्मित इलेक्ट्रॉन युग्म। • ट्रिपलेट नाइट्रीन: sp, दो अयुग्मित इलेक्ट्रॉन्स। उत्पादन: ऐज़ो यौगिकों या नाइट्रिल हैलाइड (RN=C=O) के photolysis से। संयोजन: सीधा C–H insertion और alkene addition से carbene की तरह ही cyclic products देता है।

Card 6

बेंजीन (Benzyne) – संरचना एवं अभिक्रिया

बेंजीन (1,2-Didehydrobenzene) एक अत्यंत सक्रिय इंटरमीडिएट है। • निर्माण: strong base (NaNH₂/NH₃) द्वारा Haloarene से β-elimination; • संरचना: benzene ring में तीसरा π bond अतिरिक्त, जिससे triple-bond-like linear geometry होती है; • अभिक्रिया: nucleophile (NH₂⁻, OH⁻) जोड़कर substituted aniline/phenol बनाता है; • meta-chloroaniline जैसे मिश्रित उत्पाद (rearrangement proof) इसी के कारण मिलते हैं।

Card 7

प्रेरण प्रभाव (Inductive Effect, +I / –I)

प्रेरण प्रभाव: sigma बंध के अनुदिश इलेक्ट्रॉन घनत्व का स्थायी स्थानांतरण। +I दाता (electron releasing): –OCH₃, –CH₃, –C₂H₅ आदि –I स्वीकर्ता (electron withdrawing): –NO₂, –CN, –COOH, –F, –Cl आदि → +I समूह C⁺ को स्थिर करते हैं, C⁻ को अस्थिर करते हैं। → –I समूह C⁺ को अस्थिर करते हैं, C⁻ को स्थिर करते हैं। → प्रभाव दूरी के साथ तेज़ी से कम हो जाता है (3-4 बंध के बाद नगण्य)।

Card 8

अनुनाद प्रभाव (Resonance / Mesomeric Effect, +R / –R)

अनुनाद (Mesomeric) प्रभाव: p-π या π-π संयुजकों में इलेक्ट्रॉनों का पूर्ण विस्थानीकरण। +R समूह (इलेक्ट्रॉन दान): –OH, –OR, –NH₂, –NHR, –Cl (lone pair donation) –R समूह (इलेक्ट्रॉन खींचते): –NO₂, –CN, –CHO, –COOH, –SO₃H (π* orbital लव्हीन)→ C⁺ को स्थिर (अनुनादी रूप), C⁻ को अस्थिर करता है। → अनुनाद प्रभाव दूरी के बिना पूरे conjugated system में फैलता है। → अणु की ऊर्जा अनुनादी स्थिरीकरण (resonance energy) से कम होती है।

Card 9

विद्युत-ग्रामी प्रभाव (Electromeric Effect, E-प्रभाव)

विद्युत-ग्रामी प्रभाव: एक आक्रमणकारी अभिकर्मक की उपस्थिति में π-बंध के इलेक्ट्रॉनों का पूर्ण स्थानांतरण होता है। यह अस्थायी प्रभाव है। + E प्रभाव: π-इलेक्ट्रॉन्स आक्रमणकारी की ओर खिंचते हैं (उदा. C=O पर Nu⁻ attack)। – E प्रभाव: π-इलेक्ट्रॉन्स आक्रमणकारी से दूर खिंचते हैं (उदा. C=C पर electrophile attack)। → प्रभाव केवल अभिक्रिया अंतर्गत रहता है, अभिकर्मक हटते ही समाप्त हो जाता है।

Card 10

हाइपरकम्प्यूगेशन (Hyperconjugation)

हाइपरकम्प्यूगेशन: σ(C–H) बंध का adjacent π-प्रणाली (double bond, carbocation, free radical) के साथ अतिव्यापी होकर इलेक्ट्रॉन का आंशिक विस्थानीकरण। इसे no-bond resonance भी कहते हैं। स्थिरता नियम: • alkene stability: trans > cis > monosubstituted; • carbocation: 3° > 2° > 1°; • free radical: 3° > 2° > 1°; → α-C–H बंधों की संख्या जितनी अधिक, उतना ही हाइपरकम्प्यूगेशन अधिक।

Card 11

तौटोमेरिज्म (Tautomerism) – प्रकार एवं उदाहरण

तौटोमेरिज्म: दो संरचनात्मक समघटकों का संतुलन, जहाँ एक हाइड्रोजन परमाणु का स्थानांतरण होता है। 1. Keto-enol tautomerism: CH₃–C(=O)–CH₃ ⇌ CH₂=C(OH)–CH₃ Enol form तब स्थिर जब: conjugated, intramolecular H-bonding, phenolic-like; 2. Nitro-acinitro tautomerism; 3. Imine-enamine tautomerism; 4. Valence tautomerism. → keto form सामान्यतः अधिक स्थिर होता है (C=O बंध ऊर्जा > C=C बंध)।

Card 12

अनुनादी (Resonance) संरचनाएँ – नियम

अनुनादी संरचनाओं के नियम: 1. केवल इलेक्ट्रॉन्स स्थानांतरित होते हैं, नाभिक नहीं। 2. सभी परमाणुओं के लिए octet पूरा होना चाहिए। 3. विपरीत आवेश वाले दो परमाणु निकटवर्ती न हों (स्थिरता बढ़ती है)। 4. अधिक ऋणात्मक आवेश अधिक विद्युतऋणात्मक परमाणु पर होना चाहिए। 5. कम से कम चार्ज पृथक्करण वाली संरचना अधिक स्थिर होती है। 6. अनुनादी संरचनाओं में π-बंदों की संख्या समान रहती है।

Card 13

प्रतिक्रिया के प्रकार – वर्गीकरण

मुख्य प्रतिक्रिया प्रकार: 1. प्रतिस्थापन (Substitution): SN1, SN2, electrophilic aromatic substitution; 2. योगात्मक (Addition): electrophilic, nucleophilic, free-radical addition; 3. विनिर्मुक्त (Elimination): E1, E2, E1cB; 4. पुनर्विन्यास (Rearrangement): hydride shift, methyl shift, Wagner-Meerwein; 5. ऑक्सीकृत-अपचयन (Redox): ऑक्सीजन जुड़ना ya हटना; 6. मुक्त मूलक प्रतिक्रिया: initiation, propagation, termination चरण।

Card 14

SN1 और SN2 की तुलना

SN1: • द्वि-चरणीय, rate = k[substrate]; • racemisation (planar carbocation); • tert-alkyl halide सबसे उपयुक्त; • ध्रुवीय protic solvent अनुकूल; • nucleophile की सांद्रता प्रभाव नहीं डालता। SN2: • एक-चरणीय, rate = k[substrate][Nu]; • inversion of configuration (Walden inversion); • primary alkyl halide सबसे उपयुक्त; • ध्रुवीय aprotic solvent अनुकूल; • sterically unhindered substrate आवश्यक।

Card 15

E1, E2, E1cB की तुलना

E1: • द्वि-चरणीय, base की सांद्रता प्रभाव नहीं; • carbocation intermediate; • Zaitsev product प्राथमिक; • tertiary substrate अनुकूल। E2: • एक-चरणीय, rate = k[substrate][base]; • anti-periplanar geometry आवश्यक; • strong base चाहिए; • Saytzeff या Hofmann product base की प्रकृति पर निर्भर। E1cB: • कार्बनायन intermediate; • poor leaving group + acidic β-H; • weak base, polar aprotic solvent।

Card 16

Zaitsev (Saytzeff) और Hoffman नियम

Zaitsev नियम: • elimination में अधिक substituted alkene प्राथमिक उत्पाद होता है। • strong base (EtO⁻/OH⁻) में यह प्रभावी होता है। Hoffman नियम: • bulky base (t-BuO⁻) या poor leaving group (–NR₃⁺) में कम substituted alkene प्राथमिक बनता है। • कारण: steric hindrance बड़े β-H को preferentially remove कराती है।

Card 17

कार्बोकैटायन पुनर्विन्यास (Rearrangement)

कार्बोकैटायन पुनर्विन्यास: • 1,2-hydride shift: H⁻ का neighboring C⁺ पर स्थानांतरण; • 1,2-methyl shift: CH₃⁻ का C⁺ पर स्थानांतरण; • Wagner-Meerwein rearrangement: carbocation → more stable carbocation; • प्राय: 3° C⁺ बनने की दिशा में; • rearrangement तभी संभव जब adjacent C पर H या alkyl group हो।

Card 18

इलेक्ट्रोफ़िलिक योगात्मक प्रतिक्रिया (Markovnikov), Anti-Markovnikov

Markovnikov नियम: • HX addition में H⁺ पहले उस C पर जुड़ता है जिस पर अधिक H हैं; • carbocation intermediate स्थिरता पर निर्भर। Anti-Markovnikov (peroxide effect): • HBr + peroxide में radical mechanism; • Br· पहले उस C पर जुड़ता है जिस पर कम H हैं; • केवल HBr में संभव (HCl/HI में नहीं); H₂O₂ प्रभाव केवल HBr के साथ; • radical intermediate की स्थिरता (3° > 2° > 1°) पर आधारित।

Card 19

नाभिकस्नेही प्रतिस्थापन (Nucleophilic Aromatic Substitution, NAS)

NAS के तीन मुख्य मार्ग: 1. Addition-elimination (SNAr): strong electron-withdrawing groups (NO₂, CN) ortho/para position पर होने चाहिए; Meisenheimer complex intermediate; 2. Elimination-addition (Benzyne): strong base (NaNH₂) से benzyne बनता है, फिर nucleophile add होता है; 3. Radicaaromatic substitution: aryl radicals के माध्यम से।

Card 20

फ्री-रेडिकल हैलाइडेशन (Alkane halogenation)

फ्री-रेडिकल हैलाइडेशन: 1. Initiation: UV या heat से X₂ → 2X·; 2. Propagation: R–H + X· → R· + HX; R· + X₂ → R–X + X·; 3. Termination: 2R· → R–R, R· + X· → R–X, 2X· → X₂। → Reactivity order: I₂ > Br₂ > Cl₂ > F₂ (bond dissociation energy ↑); → Selectivity: Br· अधिक selective (3° H > 2° H > 1° H), Cl· कम selective।

Card 21

इंडक्टिव और रेजोनेंस प्रभाव की तुलना

इंडक्टिव प्रभाव: • σ-बंध के अनुदिश; • अस्थायी नहीं, स्थायी; • दूरी के साथ तेज़ी से कम; • इलेक्ट्रॉन खींचने/छोड़ने वाले समूहों से; रेजोनेंस प्रभाव: • π-अभिक्रमण के अनुदिश; • पूरे conjugated system में; • दूरी से स्वतंत्र; • lone pair और π-bond के सहयोग से; → दोनों के संयुक्त प्रभाव से acid/base strength, reactivity निर्धारित होती है।

Card 22

ऑर्गेनिक यौगिकों की शुद्धि – विधि चयन

शुद्धि विधियाँ: 1. क्रिस्टलीकरण (Recrystallisation): ठोस यौगिक; растворимость温差 से; 2. आसवन (Distillation): द्रव; boiling point अंतर; 3. आंशिक आसवन: نزدیک boiling points; 4. वाष्प आसवन: steam-volatile, insoluble in water; 5. निर्वात आसवन: high boiling, thermally sensitive; 6. रंग स्पंदन (Chromatography): column, TLC, GC; 7. निक्षालन (Extraction): liquid-liquid; 8. सब्लिमेशन: sublimate able solids (naphthalene, camphor)।

Card 23

Kjeldahl विधि –窒素 की मात्रा ज्ञात करना

Kjeldahl विधि: 1. जलীয় sample को conc. H₂SO₄ के साथ गर्म करके NH₃ उत्पन्न किया जाता है; 2. NH₃ को boric acid में absorb किया जाता है; 3. बोरिक एसिड के अमोनियम लवण को standard acid से titrate किया जाता है। →窒素 % = (V_acid × M_acid × 14.007 × 100) / (mass sample × 1000) → Nitrogen-containing organic compounds (amines, amides) में उपयोग; → NO₂, nitro, azo, azo compounds में प्रत्यक्ष रूप से काम नहीं करती (reduction की आवश्यकता)।

Card 24

Lassaigne's Test – तत्व पहचान

Lassaigne's test (Sodium fusion test): • Na + organic compound → NaCN, Na₂S, NaX; • N检测: NaCN + FeSO₄ → Fe(CN)₆⁴⁻ → Prussian blue precipitate; • S检测: Na₂S + lead acetate → black PbS; • Halogens检测: NaX + AgNO₃ → AgX precipitate (white/s pale yellow/cream); • I detection: AgI bright yellow; → N+S दोनों होने पर FeSCN²⁺ red colour देता है (false positive risk)।

Card 25

प्रतिगामी-आधुनिक पद्धति – qualitative analysis की सीमाएँ

Qualitative analysis की सीमाएँ: • Lassaigne test केवल N, S, halogens दिखा सकता है; • O, P detect नहीं होता (इन्हें combustion के बाद विशिष्ट tests से); • Multiple elements simultaneous detection में interference; •微量 levels पर sensitivity कम; → अतः modern instrumental methods (elemental analyser, ICP-OES, XRF) अधिक सटीक हैं।

Card 26

Organic compound की संरचना निर्धारण – चरण

संरचना निर्धारण के चरण: 1. Purification; 2. Elemental analysis (C, H, N, S, halogens); 3. Molecular mass determination (Mass spec, colligative properties); 4. Empirical formula; 5. Molecular formula; 6. Functional group identification (chemical tests + IR); 7. Structure elucidation (NMR, MS, UV-Vis); 8. Confirmation (melting point, mixed melting point, X-ray)।

Card 27

कार्बोकैटायन में hyperconjugation कैसे काम करता है?

Carbocation में hyperconjugation: • C⁺ के adjacent C–H bond का σ-electron C⁺ की खाली p-orbital में delocalize होता है; • जितने अधिक α-C–H बंध, उतना ही अधिक stabilization; • 3° C⁺ में 9 α-H, 2° में 6, 1° में 3; • CH₃⁺ में कोई α-H नहीं → अत्यंत अस्थिर; → इसीलिए alkyl groups electron-donating लगती हैं।

Card 28

Phenol की Acidity – प्रतिस्पर्धी प्रभाव

Phenol की acid strength: • +I समूह (–CH₃, –OCH₃): acidity घटाते हैं (phenoxide ion को destabilize); • –I समूह (–NO₂, –CN, –F, –Cl): acidity बढ़ाते हैं (phenoxide को stabilize); • Ortho/para NO₂ सबसे प्रभावशाली (–R + –I दोनों); → p-nitrophenol > phenol > o-cresol; → Phenoxide ion resonance-stabilized (O⁻ charge delocalized into ring)।

Card 29

Carboxylic acid की Acidity – प्रभाव

Carboxylic acid (RCOOH) की acidity: • –I समूह (–NO₂, –CN, –F, –Cl): COO⁻ को stabilize → acid stronger; • +I समूह (–CH₃, –OCH₃): COO⁻ destabilize → acid weaker; • Ortho effect: ortho-substituted acids सभी अन्य सब्स्टीट्यूटेड acids से अधिक बलवान; • Resonance stabilized carboxylate ion (COO⁻); → Trichloroacetic > Dichloroacetic > Chloroacetic > Acetic

Card 30

Alcohol की Acidity – तुलना

Alcohol acidity क्रम: • H₂O > methanol > 1° alcohol > 2° alcohol > 3° alcohol; • कारण: alkyl group +I effect, RO⁻ को destabilize करता है; • Larger alkyl chain = weaker acid; • Electron-withdrawing groups (–CF₃, –NO₂) acidity बढ़ाते हैं; → तृतीयक butanol, प्राथमिक ethanol से कम बलवान होता है।

Card 31

SNAr में leaving group ability

SNAr में leaving group ability: • Leaving group strength: F⁻ > Cl⁻ > Br⁻ > I⁻ (electronegativity ↑ → better leaving group in SNAr); • कारण: SNAr में rate-determining step nucleophile addition है, leaving group departure fast होता है; → F सबसे अच्छा leaving group SNAr में क्योंकि strongest C–F bond (high ΔG‡) का transition state stabilization বেশি; → लेकिन general substitution में I⁻ best leaving group होता है (weak base)।

Card 32

Benzene की Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution (EAS)

EAS mechanism: 1. Electrophile generation (E⁺); 2. Formation of arenium ion (σ-complex) – rate-determining; 3. Loss of proton → restoration of aromaticity; Activating groups (ortho/para directors): –OH, –NH₂, –OCH₃, –CH₃; Deactivating groups (meta directors): –NO₂, –CN, –CHO, –COOH; Halogen (–Cl, –Br): deactivating but ortho/para directing (–I dominates, +R directs)।

Card 33

Aromatic vs Antiaromatic vs Non-aromatic

Aromaticity नियम (Hückel): • Aromatic: cyclic, planar, fully conjugated, 4n+2 π electrons; • Antiaromatic: cyclic, planar, fully conjugated, 4n π electrons (unstable); • Non-aromatic: किसी एक condition fail; • Cyclobutadiene (4π e⁻) → antiaromatic; • Benzene (6π e⁻) → aromatic; • Cyclooctatetraene (8π e⁻, non-planar) → non-aromatic।

Card 34

Markovnikov vs Anti-Markovnikov addition – reagents

Markovnikov addition: • HX (X = Cl, Br, I); • H₂O / H⁺ (acid-catalyzed hydration); • H₂SO₄ / H₂O; Anti-Markovnikov addition: • HBr / peroxide (ROOR); • H₂O / BH₃·THF then H₂O₂/OH⁻ (hydroboration-oxidation); → Hydroboration syn addition, stereo-specific भी; → Peroxide effect केवल HBr में, HCl/HI में नहीं।

Card 35

Free radical addition of HBr – mechanism

Free radical addition mechanism: 1. Initiation: ROOR → 2RO·; RO· + HBr → ROH + Br·; 2. Propagation: Br· + CH₂=CH₂ → ·CH₂CH₂Br; ·CH₂CH₂Br + HBr → BrCH₂CH₂Br + Br·; 3. Termination: 2Br· → Br₂, etc. • Br· पहले उस C पर जोड़ता है जहाँ radical más stable बनता है (3° > 2° > 1°); • Yields anti-Markovnikov product; • Cl·, I· इसी तरह नहीं काम करते (Cl बहुत reactive, I too unreactive)।

Card 36

Carbene insertion into C–H bond

Carbene insertion: • Singlet carbene: concerted, stereospecific (retention of configuration); • Triplet carbene: stepwise radical pathway, stereoselective नहीं; • C–H insertion general: :CH₂ + R–H → R–CH₃; • Simmons-Smith reaction (CH₂I₂/Zn-Cu): cyclopropane formation; • Carbene को dichlorocarbene (:CCl₂) भी generate किया जा सकता है (CHCl₃/KOH)।

Card 37

Benzyne intermediate – formation और reactivity

Benzyne formation: • Base (NaNH₂/NH₃) haloarene से β-elimination; • Triple bond benzene ring में; • Highly reactive, short-lived; Reactivity: • Nucleophile (NH₂⁻, OH⁻) जोड़कर substituted product देता है; • Regioselectivity ambiguous → mixture of ortho/meta/para products; • Diels-Alder reaction भी करता है (dienophile के रूप में)।

Card 38

Nitrene insertion vs addition

Nitrene behavior: • Singlet nitrene:插入 into C–H (concerted); • Triplet nitrene: stepwise radical pathway; • Addition to alkene → aziridine (3-membered ring); • Insertion into C–H → amine; • Curtius rearrangement: acyl azide → isocyanate → amine (nitrene-like intermediate); • Hofmann rearrangement: amide → amine (nitrene involved)।

Card 39

Keto-enol tautomerism – conditions for enol stability

Enol stability बढ़ाने वाली शर्तें: 1. Intramolecular H-bonding (β-diketones like acetylacetone); 2. Aromatic character (phenol-type enol); 3. Conjugation with double bond or aromatic ring; 4. Electron-withdrawing groups stabilizing enolate; →Acetylacetone enol content ~80% (H-bond + conjugation); → Cyclohexanone enol ~10⁻⁴ %; → Simple ketones enol बहुत कम।

Card 40

Valence tautomerism – उदाहरण

Valence tautomerism: • Structures differ only in bonding pattern (no atom movement); • उदाहरण: benzene ↔ Dewar benzene ( strained cyclobutene rings); • Prismane ↔ benzene; • Cyclooctatetraene tub shape ↔ flat structure; • Reversible, thermal/photochemical; → यह resonance से अलग है क्योंकि resonance में structures contribute equally, तौटोमेरिज्म में equilibrium shift होता है।

Card 41

Dipole moment vs Inductive effect

डायपोल मोमेंट vs इनडक्टिव इफेक्ट: • +I groups increase dipole moment (electron推向 electronegative atom); • –I groups decrease dipole moment (pull electrons away); • But dipole depends on geometry too; • Example: CH₃Cl (1.9 D), CH₃F (1.8 D) – despite F being more electronegative, C–F bond shorter pero C–Cl more polarizable; → Inductive effect permanent, dipole moment measurable property है।

Card 42

Resonance hybrid – characteristics

Resonance hybrid characteristics: 1. All contributing structures have same nuclear positions; 2. Hybrid has delocalized electrons; 3. Energy lower than any single contributor (resonance stabilization); 4. Bond lengths intermediate between single and double; 5. Charge distributed over multiple atoms; 6. Major contributor: full octets, minimal charge separation, negative charge on more electronegative atom।

Card 43

SN1 vs E1 competition

SN1/E1 competition: • Same carbocation intermediate; • Low temperature favors SN1 (substitution); • High temperature favors E1 (elimination); • Weak nucleophile/strong base → E1; • Good nucleophile/weak base → SN1; • Solvent: polar protic favors both; • Rearrangements common in both; • Products: substitution + alkene mixture।

Card 44

E2 vs SN2 competition

E2/SN2 competition: • Strong base + primary alkyl halide → mostly SN2; • Strong bulky base (t-BuOK) → E2 favored; • Secondary halide → mix of both; • Tertiary halide → E2 only (SN2 blocked by sterics); • Temperature: high → E2; • Solvent: polar aprotic favors SN2; • Steric hindrance pushes toward E2।

Card 45

Wagner-Meerwein rearrangement – classic example

Wagner-Meerwein rearrangement: • 1,2-shift of H or alkyl group in carbocation; • Classic: neopentyl system → rearranged product; • Pinacol rearrangement: diol → ketone via 1,2-shift; • Neuermann rearrangement: amide → amine with R shift; • Key: most stable carbocation forms; • Often observed in solvolysis of secondary/tertiary halides with adjacent quaternary carbon।

Card 46

Charged intermediates vs neutral intermediates

Charged intermediates: • Carbocation (C⁺): electrophilic, Lewis acid; • Carbanion (C⁻): nucleophilic, Lewis base; • Radical cation/anion: single electron species; Neutral intermediates: • Carbene (:CR₂): electrophilic; • Nitrene (:NR): electrophilic; • Benzyne: highly reactive diene/dienophile; • Stability order generally: carbanion > carbocation (in gas phase) but solvation changes things significantly।

Card 47

Stability of carbocations – solvent effects

Solvent effects on carbocation stability: • Polar protic solvents stabilize C⁺ strongly (solvation); • Gas phase: intrinsic stability order 3° > 2° > 1° > CH₃⁺; • Solution phase: same order but magnitude differs; • Solvolysis rates reflect both C⁺ stability AND solvent nucleophilicity; • Ion-pairing effects can reverse expected selectivity in low-dielectric solvents।

Card 48

Carbanion stability – factors

Carbanion stability factors: 1. Hybridization: sp > sp² > sp³ (more s-character = closer to nucleus = more stable); 2. Inductive effect: –I groups stabilize (withdraw electron density); 3. Resonance: adjacent π-systems stabilize (e.g., enolate); 4. Solvation: polar protic solvents stabilize anions; 5. Electronegativity: C bonded to electronegative atoms more stable; → Acetylide (sp C⁻) > vinyl (sp²) > alkyl (sp³) carbanion।

Card 49

Free radical stability – halogen comparison

Halogen radical stability: • I· > Br· > Cl· > F· • Bond dissociation energy: H–F > H–Cl > H–Br > H–I; • Lower BDE → easier homolytic cleavage → more stable radical; • F· extremely reactive (low selectivity), I· relatively stable; • Selectivity: I· most selective (late transition state), F· least selective (early TS)।

Card 50

Benzylic and allylic positions – reactivity

Benzylic/allylic positions: • C–H bond at benzylic position weaker due to resonance stabilization of resulting radical/cation; • Bond dissociation energy: benzyl C–H ~ 90 kcal/mol vs ethyl ~ 101; • Allylic similar: propene allylic C–H weaker; • Both positions undergo easy halogenation (NBS for allylic bromination); • Oxidation also easier at these positions (KMnO₄ → carboxylic acid from benzyl alcohol)।

Card 51

Tollen's reagent vs Fehling's solution – organic distinction

Tollen's vs Fehling's: • Tollen's (Ag(NH₃)₂⁺): oxidizes aldehydes (both aliphatic & aromatic) to carboxylate; silver mirror formed; • Fehling's (Cu²⁺/tartrate): oxidizes only aliphatic aldehydes; red Cu₂O precipitate; • Aromatic aldehydes (benzaldehyde) give Tollen's positive but Fehling's negative; • Ketones do not respond to either (except α-hydroxy ketones)।

Card 52

Lucas test – principle and application

Lucas test: • Reagent: conc. HCl + anhydrous ZnCl₂; • Principle: differentiates 1°, 2°, 3° alcohols by rate of SN1 reaction; • 3° alcohol: immediate turbidity (fastest SN1); • 2° alcohol: turbidity in 5 min; • 1° alcohol: no turbidity at room temp; • Only works for alcohols soluble in Lucas reagent (≤6 carbons)।

Card 53

Iodoform test – principle and scope

Iodoform test: • Reagent: I₂ + NaOH (or NaOI); • Positive for: CH₃CO– group or CH₃CH(OH)– group; • Yellow precipitate of CHI₃ (iodoform); • Alcohols: ethanol, 2° alcohols with CH₃CH(OH)–; • Aldehydes/ketones: acetaldehyde, methyl ketones; • Not given by: propanal, benzophenone, tertiary alcohols (no α-methyl)।

Card 54

Beilstein test – principle

Beilstein test: • Principle: copper wire heated in flame, dipped in sample, re-heated; • Green/blue-green flame indicates halogen presence (copper halide volatile); • Quick screening test for halogens; • False positives: some nitrogen compounds also give green color; • Does NOT identify which halogen (Cl/Br/I all give green)।

Card 55

Nitrogen estimation – Dumas method vs Kjeldahl

Dumas vs Kjeldahl: • Dumas method: – Organic compound burned in excess O₂; – N₂ gas collected and measured; – Works for ALL nitrogen compounds (including nitro, azo); • Kjeldahl method: – Digestion with H₂SO₄ → NH₃; – Titration of NH₃; – Only works for amine/amide type N (not NO₂, –N=N–); • Dumas gives direct N₂ volume; Kjeldahl gives间接 measure।

Card 56

Carius method – halogen estimation

Carius method: • Organic compound heated with conc. HNO₃ in sealed tube; • Halogen converts to halide ion; • AgNO₃ added → AgX precipitate; • Weighed to calculate halogen %; • Works for Cl, Br, I simultaneously; • Cannot detect fluorine (HF soluble, won't precipitate with Ag⁺); • Requires careful handling of sealed tubes (high pressure)।

Card 57

Chromatography – principle and types

Chromatography principles: • Separation based on differential partitioning between stationary and mobile phases; • Types: 1. Adsorption chromatography (silica gel, alumina); 2. Partition chromatography (paper, reverse phase); 3. Ion exchange chromatography; 4. Size exclusion chromatography; • Rf value = distance traveled by compound / distance traveled by solvent front; • Higher polarity → lower Rf on silica (normal phase)।

Card 58

Mass spectrometry – key concepts

Mass spectrometry concepts: • Molecular ion peak (M⁺) gives molecular weight; • Base peak: tallest peak (100% relative abundance); • Isotope patterns: Cl (M, M+2 in 3:1 ratio), Br (M, M+2 in 1:1 ratio); • Fragmentation patterns help identify structure; • Nitrogen rule: odd nominal mass → odd number of N atoms; • McLafferty rearrangement: γ-hydrogen transfer in carbonyl compounds।

Card 59

IR spectroscopy – functional group regions

IR spectral regions: • 3600–3200 cm⁻¹: O–H stretch (alcohol, phenol); • 3400–3200 cm⁻¹: N–H stretch (amine, amide); • 3100–3000 cm⁻¹: =C–H stretch (alkene, arene); • 3000–2850 cm⁻¹: C–H stretch (alkane); • 2200–2100 cm⁻¹: C≡C, C≡N stretch; • 1850–1650 cm⁻¹: C=O stretch (strong); • 1600–1450 cm⁻¹: C=C stretch (aromatic); • Fingerprint region: < 1500 cm⁻¹ (unique pattern)।

Card 60

NMR – ¹H NMR basics

¹H NMR basics: • Chemical shift (δ): position relative to TMS (0 ppm); • Common ranges: – Alkyl H: 0.5–2 ppm; – Allylic H: 2–3 ppm; – Alpha to carbonyl: 2–3 ppm; – Aromatic H: 6.5–8 ppm; – Aldehyde H: 9–10 ppm; – Carboxylic acid H: 10–13 ppm; • Integration: area under peak ∝ number of H; • Splitting: n+1 rule (n neighboring equivalent H)।

Card 61

Isomerism – classification overview

Isomerism classification: 1. Structural isomerism: – Chain (different carbon skeleton); – Position (different position of functional group); – Functional (different functional group); – Metamerism (different alkyl groups on same functional group); – Tautomerism (keto-enol equilibrium); 2. Stereoisomerism: – Geometric (cis-trans/E-Z); – Optical (enantiomers, diastereomers)।

Card 62

Geometric isomerism – E/Z notation

E/Z notation: • Based on Cahn-Ingold-Prelog priority rules; • Z (zusammen): higher priority groups on same side; • E (entgegen): higher priority groups on opposite sides; • Used when cis/trans nomenclature insufficient (three or four different substituents); • Priority: atomic number determines rank; • If same atom, look at next atoms in chain。

Card 63

Optical isomerism – chirality and R/S

Optical isomerism: • Chirality: molecule non-superimposable on its mirror image; • Chiral center: carbon with 4 different groups; • R/S notation: Cahn-Ingold-Prelog priority rules; • R (rectus): clockwise priority sequence; • S (sinister): counterclockwise sequence; • Enantiomers: rotate plane-polarized light equally but opposite directions; • Racemic mixture: 50:50 enantiomers, optically inactive; • Meso compound: has chiral centers but internally compensated (plane of symmetry)।

Card 64

Optical activity – specific rotation

Specific rotation: • [α] = α / (l × c) where α = observed rotation (degrees) l = path length (dm) c = concentration (g/mL) • Temperature and wavelength specified (usually 20°C, sodium D line); • Enantiomers have equal but opposite specific rotations; • Racemic mixture: [α] = 0; • Excess of one enantiomer = optical purity = observed [α] / max [α] × 100%।

Card 65

SN1 reaction stereochemistry

SN1 stereochemistry: • Planar carbocation intermediate allows attack from both sides; • Partial racemization: mainly inversion but some retention; • Ion pairing can cause net inversion if nucleophile attacks from less hindered side; • Complete racemization rare – usually slight excess of inversion product; • Neighboring group participation can lead to net retention (anchimeric assistance)।

Card 66

SN2 reaction stereochemistry

SN2 stereochemistry: • Backside attack required (180° from leaving group); • Walden inversion: complete stereochemical inversion; • R config → S config (if priority unchanged); • Concerted mechanism: bond breaking and forming simultaneous; • No intermediate, single transition state; • Steric hindrance dramatically affects rate (methyl > 1° > 2° >> 3°)।

Card 67

E2 stereochemistry – anti-periplanar requirement

E2 stereochemistry: • Anti-periplanar geometry required (H and leaving group 180° apart); • In cyclic systems: both must be axial (trans-diaxial); • Syn-periplanar possible but much slower; • Stereospecific: specific stereoisomer gives specific alkene product; • Newman projection helpful for visualizing conformation; • Zaitsev vs Hofmann product depends on base size and substrate structure。

Card 68

Arenium ion (sigma complex) – stability

Arenium ion (sigma complex): • Formed when electrophile adds to benzene ring; • Positive charge delocalized over 5 carbons (not all 6); • Resonance structures show charge at ortho and para positions; • Meta director groups destabilize ortho/para attack → meta product favored; • Ortho/para director groups stabilize ortho/para attack → those positions preferred; • Rate-determining step: formation of arenium ion।

Card 69

Electrophile generation – common examples

Common electrophile generation: • NO₂⁺: HNO₃ + H₂SO₄ → NO₂⁺ + HSO₄⁻ + H₂O; • Cl⁺: Cl₂ + FeCl₃ → Cl⁺ + FeCl₄⁻; • Br⁺: Br₂ + FeBr₃ → Br⁺ + FeBr₄⁻; • ⁺CH₃: CH₃Cl + AlCl₃ → ⁺CH₃ + AlCl₄⁻; • SO₃: H₂SO₄ (conc.) → SO₃ + H₂O; • ⁺NO: HNO₃ + H₂SO₄ → ⁺NO₂ + HSO₄⁻ + H₂O (nitrosation); • Carbocations from alkyl halides + Lewis acid。

Card 70

Nucleophile generation – common examples

Common nucleophile generation: • OH⁻: NaOH, KOH (aq); • OR⁻: NaOR (from ROH + Na); • CN⁻: NaCN, KCN; • NH₃/NH₂⁻: ammonia, liquid NH₃ + NaNH₂; • RS⁻: NaSR; • H⁻: NaBH₄, LiAlH₄ (hydride sources); • Carbanions: organolithiums (RLi), Grignard reagents (RMgX); • weak nucleophiles: H₂O, ROH (for SN1 conditions)。

Card 71

Leaving group ability – ranking

Leaving group ability ranking: • Best: Tosylate (OTs), Mesylate (OMs), Triflate (OTf); • Excellent: I⁻, Br⁻; • Good: Cl⁻, H₂O (from protonated alcohol); • Poor: F⁻, OH⁻, OR⁻, NH₂⁻; • Principle: good leaving groups are weak bases (stable after departure); • To make poor LG leave: convert to better LG (e.g., OH → tosylate, or protonate OH → H₂O⁺); • Sulfonate esters (TsCl, MsCl) commonly used to activate alcohols。

Card 72

Solvent effects – protic vs aprotic

Solvent effects: • Polar protic (H₂O, ROH, RCOOH): – Solvate cations and anions well; – Favor SN1/E1 (stabilize carbocation); – Reduce nucleophilicity of anions (H-bonding shell); • Polar aprotic (DMSO, DMF, acetone, acetonitrile): – Solvate cations well, anions poorly; – Increase nucleophilicity of anions; – Favor SN2 (naked nucleophile); • Nonpolar solvents (hexane, benzene): – Poor for ionic reactions; – Used for radical reactions。

Card 73

Reaction rate – factors affecting

Factors affecting reaction rate: 1. Substrate structure (sterics, stability of intermediate); 2. Nucleophile/base strength and concentration; 3. Leaving group ability; 4. Solvent polarity and type; 5. Temperature (Arrhenius equation: k = Ae^(-Ea/RT)); 6. Catalyst presence (lowers activation energy); 7. Concentration of reactants; 8. Light/radiation (for radical reactions); 9. Pressure (for gas-phase reactions)।

Card 74

Hammond postulate – application

Hammond postulate: • For exothermic reactions: transition state resembles reactants (early TS); • For endothermic reactions: transition state resembles products (late TS); • Application to SN1: rate-determining step endothermic (C–LG bond breaking) → TS resembles carbocation; • Application to E2: if highly endothermic, TS resembles alkene product (product-like); • Helps predict selectivity: late TS = more selective (more product-like)。

Card 75

Kinetic isotope effect – primary vs secondary

Kinetic isotope effect (KIE): • Primary KIE: isotope at bond-breaking position (k_H/k_D = 2–7); – Indicates C–H bond breaking in rate-determining step; • Secondary KIE: isotope at position adjacent to reaction center; – Normal: k_H/k_D > 1 (sp³ → sp², cation formation); – Inverse: k_H/k_D < 1 (sp² → sp³, anion formation); • Large KIE confirms bond to isotope broken in RDS; • Used to distinguish SN1 vs SN2 mechanisms。

Card 76

Fischer projection – rules

Fischer projection rules: • Vertical lines: bonds going away from viewer (into page); • Horizontal lines: bonds coming toward viewer (out of page); • Carbon chain vertical, most oxidized carbon at top; • Interchanging any two groups: inverts configuration; • Rotating 180° in plane: same molecule; • Rotating 90°: enantiomer; • Cannot lift out of plane without changing representation。

Card 77

Newman projection – conformations

Newman projection conformations (ethane example): • Staggered: lowest energy, H atoms 60° apart; • Eclipsed: highest energy, H atoms aligned; • Anti: largest groups 180° apart; • Gauche: largest groups 60° apart; • For butane: anti-butane most stable, gauche less stable by ~0.9 kcal/mol; • Eclipsed conformations have torsional strain and steric strain。

Card 78

Chair conformation of cyclohexane

Cyclohexane chair conformation: • Most stable conformation (minimal angle and torsional strain); • Two types of positions: axial (vertical) and equatorial (horizontal); • Ring flip interconverts axial ↔ equatorial; • Larger substituents prefer equatorial position (less 1,3-diaxial interaction); • 1,3-diaxial interactions cause steric strain (~0.9 kcal/mol per CH₃); • Equatorial preference: t-butyl > isopropyl > methyl > H。

Card 79

Steric strain – 1,3-diaxial interaction

1,3-Diaxial interaction: • Axial substituent on C1 interacts with axial H's on C3 and C5; • Each CH₃–H 1,3-diaxial interaction ≈ 0.9 kcal/mol; • A x CH₃ group experiences two such interactions (total ~1.8 kcal/mol); • Diequatorial conformation avoids this strain; • For disubstituted cyclohexanes: diequatorial preferred over diaxial; • Conformational analysis predicts major product distribution。

Card 80

A-value – quantitative measure of steric bulk

A-value concept: • A-value = energy difference between axial and equatorial conformations (kcal/mol); • Measures effective steric bulk of substituent; • Typical values: H = 0, CH₃ = 1.74, i-Pr = 2.15, t-Bu = 4.9, OH = 0.87, F = 0.25; • Large A-value → strong equatorial preference; • Used to predict conformational equilibria; • t-Bu group essentially locks conformation (A-value ~4.9 kcal/mol)。

Card 81

Transannular strain – medium rings

Transannular strain: • Occurs in medium-sized rings (8-11 members); • Atoms across ring interact unfavorably (steric repulsion); • Cyclooctane: twist-boat-chair to minimize strain; • Cyclodecane: boat-chair-chair to reduce transannular contacts; • Strain energy peaks around C₈–C₁₀; • Larger rings (C₁₂+) can adopt chair-like conformations with minimal strain。 • Important in steroid and terpenoid chemistry।

Card 82

Baeyer strain theory – limitations

Baeyer strain theory: • Proposed: ring strain due to deviation from ideal tetrahedral angle (109.5°); • Calculated strain from angle deformation; • Limitations: 1. Assumes planar rings (actually puckered); 2. Doesn't account for torsional strain; 3. Doesn't explain stability of larger rings; 4. Predicts cyclopentane should be strained (actually nearly strain-free); • Modern view: total strain = angle strain + torsional strain + transannular strain。

Card 83

Pitzer strain – torsional strain

Pitzer strain (torsional strain): • Caused by eclipsing bonds; • Ethane: eclipsed conformation ~3 kcal/mol higher than staggered; • Cyclohexane chair: all bonds staggered → zero torsional strain; • Cyclohexane boat: eclipsing interactions cause ~5.5 kcal/mol torsional strain; • Contribution to overall ring strain; • Minimized by puckering/conformational adjustment。

Card 84

Angle strain – definition and measurement

Angle strain: • Deviation of bond angles from ideal tetrahedral angle (109.5°); • Cyclopropane: 60° angles → huge angle strain (~27.5 kcal/mol); • Cyclobutane: ~90° angles → significant strain (~26.3 kcal/mol); • Cyclopentane: ~108° (envelope) → minimal angle strain; • Cyclohexane: 109.5° (chair) → zero angle strain; • Measured by heats of combustion per CH₂ group。

Card 85

Heat of combustion – strain indicator

Heat of combustion as strain indicator: • Each CH₂ group in unstrained chain releases ~157.4 kcal/mol; • Deviation indicates strain energy; • Cyclopropane: 166.6 kcal/mol per CH₂ (strain = 27.5 kcal/mol total); • Cyclobutane: 164.2 (strain = 26.3 total); • Cyclopentane: 158.7 (strain = 6.5 total); • Cyclohexane: 157.4 (strain ≈ 0); • Strain energy = experimental ΔHc – calculated ΔHc for unstrained reference।
