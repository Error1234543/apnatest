बायोमोलीक्यूल — परिचय एवं वर्गीकरण

जীবित प्राणियों के शरीर में पाए जाने वाले एवं उनके जीवन की प्रक्रियाओं (वृद्धि, ऊर्जा उत्पादन, वंशागति आदि) से जुड़े जटिल यौगिकों को बायोमोलीक्यूल कहते हैं। इन्हें मुख्यतः दो श्रेणियों में बाँटा जाता है — (1) प्राथमिक बायोमोलीक्यूल: कार्बोहाइड्रेट, प्रोटीन, लिपिड तथा न्यूक्लिक अम्ल; जो विघटित होने पर छोटे ईंधन अणु देते हैं। (2) द्वितीयक बायोमोलीक्यूल: हार्मोन, एंजाइम, कुछ विटामिन आदि।

• प्राथमिक संरचनात्मक इकाईएँ:
— कार्बोहाइड्रेट → मोनोसेकरीड (ग्लूकोज, फ्रुक्टोज)
— प्रोटीन → अमीनो अम्ल (कुल 20 मानक)
— न्यूक्लिक अम्ल → न्यूक्लियोटाइड (निओबस + पेंटोज़ शर्करा + फॉस्फेट)

• बायोमोलीक्यूल की प्रमुख विशेषताएँ:
— बहुविध जैविक गतिविधि (ऊर्जा, संरचना, नियन्त्रण, परिवहन)
— उच्च आणविक भार (macromolecules)
— विशिष्ट त्रिविम रसायन (stereochemistry) का महत्त्वपूर्ण योगदान
— अक्सर हाइड्रोसोलुबल या लिपोसोलुबल स्वरूप में व्यवहार

JEE Main स्तर पर यह समझना आवश्यक है कि बायोमोलीक्यूल का अध्ययन जैव रसायन का वह भाग है जहाँ कार्बनिक रसायन की प्रतिक्रियाएँ (glycosidic linkage, peptide bond, esterification) जीवित कणों के संदर्भ में देखी जाती हैं।

कार्बोहाइड्रेट — श्रेणी, नामांकन एवं रासायनिक गुण

कार्बोहाइड्रेट वे बहु-हइड्रॉक्सी एल्डिहाइड या कीटोन होते हैं जिन्हें हाइड्रोलाइसिस द्वारा सरल एल्डिहाइड/कीटोन नहीं दिया जा सकता; सामान्य सूत्र Cₙ(H₂O)ₙ होता है। इन्हें तीन मुख्य श्रेणियों में बाँटा जाता है।

(क) मोनोसेकरीड — पुनः वर्गीकरण:
• ऐल्डोस (एल्डिहाइड समूह): ग्लूकोज, गैलेक्टोस, रिवोज़, आर्थोस
• कीटोस (कीटोन समूह): फ्रुक्टोस, राइब्युलोस
ग्लूकोज (C₆H₁₂O₆) एक ऐल्डोहेक्जोस है; फ्रुक्टोज (C₆H₁₂O₆) एक कीटोहेक्जोस है। रिवोज़ (C₅H₁₀O₅) ऐल्डोपेन्टोस है जिसका महत्त्व RNA में है।

(ख) डाइसेकरीड — दो मोनोसेकरीड का glycosidic linkage (−C−O−C−) द्वारा युग्मन:
• मेलूज = ग्लूकोज + फ्रुक्टोज (α−1,2-glycosidic); sucrose है यह
• लैक्टोज़ = ग्लूकोज + गैलेक्टोस (β−1,4-linkage)
• मैल्टोज़ = ग्लूकोज + ग्लूकोज (α−1,4-linkage); reducing sugar है
गুরুत्त्वपूर्ण बिंदु: माल्टोज़ और लैक्टोज़ reducing sugars हैं (मुक्त anomeric carbon), जबकि sucrose non-reducing है क्योंकि इसमें दोनों anomeric carbons linkage में सम्मिलित हैं।

(ग) पॉलीसेकरीड — बहु-मोनोसेकरीड की श्रृंखला:
• स्टार्च = amylose (α-1,4-linked linear) + amylopectin (α-1,4 + α-1,6 branch)
• glycogen = حیوانों का storage polysaccharide; amylopectin जैसा पर अधिक branched (हर 8-12 residues)
• cellulose = β-1,4-linked D-glucose units; रैखिक, hydrogen bonding से fibers बनाती है; human digestion में cellulose digest नहीं होती क्योंकि human body में cellulase enzyme नहीं होती

रासायनिक परीक्षण:
• Benedict's/Fehling's reagent → reducing sugars से red ppt (Cu₂O)
• Molisch test → général carbohydate test (α-naphthol + conc. H₂SO₄ → violet ring)
• Iodine test → starch blue-black complex; cellulose iodine से रंग नहीं देती
• Seliwanoff test → ketoses (fructose) fast red; aldoses slow red
• Bial's test → pentoses (blue-green)

प्रोटीन — अमीनो अम्ल, बंधन तथा संरचनात्मक स्तर

प्रोटीन अमीनो अम्लों की पॉलीपेप्टाइड श्रृंखलाएँ हैं। प्रत्येक अमीनो अम्ल में एक carboxyl group (−COOH), एक amino group (−NH₂), एक H तथा एक side chain (R) एक chirality center से जुड़ा होता है। प्रोटीन में पाए जाने वाले 20 मानक अमीनो अम्लों में सभी (glycine को छोड़कर) L-configuration के होते हैं।

अमीनो अम्लों को R-ग्रुप के आधार पर वर्गीकृत किया जा सकता है:
• Non-polar aliphatic: Gly, Ala, Val, Leu, Ile, Met, Pro
• Aromatic: Phe, Tyr, Trp
• Polar uncharged: Ser, Thr, Cys, Asn, Gln
• Positively charged (basic): Lys, Arg, His
• Negatively charged (acidic): Asp, Glu

प्रमुख बंधन:
• Peptide bond (−CO−NH−): resonance-stabilized; planar; trans usually preferred
• Disulfide bond (−S−S−): Cys-Cys crosslink; stabilizes tertiary/quaternary structure
• Hydrogen bonds: main secondary structure stabilizer (α-helix, β-sheet)
• Ionic (salt bridges), hydrophobic interactions, van der Waals: tertiary stabilizers

संरचना के चार स्तर:
1. Primary — amino acid sequence; determines all higher levels
2. Secondary — regular local folding: α-helix (H-bond every C=O to NH four residues ahead, right-handed); β-pleated sheet (parallel/antiparallel); both stabilized by backbone H-bonds
3. Tertiary — overall 3D shape of single polypeptide; driven by R-group interactions
4. Quaternary — assembly of multiple polypeptide chains (e.g., hemoglobin = 4 subunits)

प्रोटीन के गुण:
• Amphoteric nature: exist as zwitterion at isoelectric point (pI); pI differs by amino acid composition
• Denaturation: loss of secondary/tertiary/quaternary structure due to heat, pH change, heavy metals, urea, detergents; primary structure retained; biological activity lost
• Protein tests: Biuret (peptide bonds → violet), Xanthoproteic (aromatic AAs → yellow, orange with NH₃), Ninhydrin (all AAs → purple/blue)

JEE Main हेतु महत्त्वपूर्ण: peptide bond की planarity, resonance; α-helix vs β-sheet distinction; denaturation factors; hemoglobin/myoglobin structural comparison.

एंजाइम — उद्देश्य, कार्यप्रणाली एवं विशेषताएँ

एंजाइम प्रोटीन प्रकृति के जैव उत्प्रेरक (biological catalysts) हैं जो bioreactions की activation energy घटाकर दर बढ़ाते हैं। अधिकांश एंजाइम globular proteins होते हैं; कुछ RNA भी catalytic होते हैं जिन्हें ribozyme कहते हैं।

एंजाइम वर्गीकरण (EC classification):
• EC 1: Oxidoreductases (redox reactions)
• EC 2: Transferases (group transfer)
• EC 3: Hydrolases (bond cleavage by water)
• EC 4: Lyases (non-hydrolytic cleavage)
• EC 5: Isomerases (isomerization)
• EC 6: Ligases (bond formation with ATP)

कृतिक अभिक्रिया क्रियाविधि:
1. Substrate (S) एंजाइम के active site (substrate-binding cleft) से specificity के साथ bind होता है
2. Enzyme-substrate complex (ES) बनता है — induced-fit model के अनुसार active site conformational change experience करता है
3. Transition state stabilization → activation energy घटती है
4. Product (P) release → enzyme free becomes available again

माइकेलिस-मेंटेन समीकरण:
V = Vmax[S] / (Km + [S])
जहाँ Km (Michaelis constant) substrate concentration है जिस पर reaction rate Vmax का आधा होता है। कम Km = high affinity for substrate.

एंजाइम inhibition प्रकार:
• Competitive: substrate-like inhibitor active site bind करता है; Vmax same, Km बढ़ता है
• Non-competitive: allosteric site पर bind, Vmax घटता है, Km unchanged
• Uncompetitive: ES complex से only bind, both Vmax and Km decrease

Specificity प्रकार:
• Absolute specificity, group specificity, linkage specificity, stereochemical specificity

उदाहरण (JEE प्रसंग):
• Urease — urea → NH₃ + CO₂ (first enzyme isolated & crystallized; Sumner, 1926)
• Zymogen (proenzyme) forms: pepsinogen → pepsin (HCl), trypsinogen → trypsin (enterokinase)
• Coenzymes: NAD⁺, FAD, CoA (organic cofactors, often derived from vitamins)
• Metal ion cofactors: Zn²⁺ (carbonic anhydrase), Fe²⁺/³⁺ (hemoglobin), Mg²⁺ (kinases)

विटामिन — वर्गीकरण, स्रोत, कार्य एवं कमी रोग

विटामिन essential organic compounds हैं जिन्हें शरीर sufficient मात्रा में синтеज़ नहीं कर पाता; इसलिए आहार से ग्रहण करना अनिवार्य है। इन्हें two groups में बाँटा गया है:

(क) Fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K) — lipid-soluble,貯存在 liver/fat, enterohepatic circulation
(ख) Water-soluble vitamins (B-complex, C) — excess excreted in urine,不太易 store

प्रमुख विटामिन: (JEEMain frequently tests deficiency diseases + structures)

• Vitamin A (Retinol): Vision (rhodopsin → retinal), epithelial tissue maintenance. Deficiency: night blindness, xerophthalmia. Sources: carrots (β-carotene precursor), liver, dairy.

• Vitamin B₁ (Thiamine): Coenzyme form TPP (thiamine pyrophosphate); decarboxylation of α-keto acids, transketolase in pentose phosphate pathway. Deficiency: beriberi, Wernicke-Korsakoff syndrome. Sources: whole grains, pork.

• Vitamin B₂ (Riboflavin): Precursor of FAD & FMN (redox carriers). Deficiency: angular cheilitis, glossitis. Sources: milk, eggs, green vegetables.

• Vitamin B₃ / Niacin (B₉): NAD⁺, NADP⁺ precursors. Deficiency: pellagra (3 Ds — dermatitis, diarrhea, dementia).

• Vitamin B₆ (Pyridoxine): Transamination, decarboxylation of amino acids; PLP coenzyme.

• Vitamin B₁₂ (Cobalamin): Methyl transfer (methionine synthase), odd-chain fatty acid oxidation. Deficiency: pernicious anemia, neurological issues. Only vitamin containing metal (Co). Sources: animal products only.

• Vitamin C (Ascorbic acid): Collagen hydroxylation (proline/lysine hydroxylase requires ascorbate), antioxidant, Fe³⁺→Fe²⁺ reduction for absorption. Deficiency: scurvy (bleeding gums, poor wound healing). Human/cricket cannot synthesize L-gulono-γ-lactone oxidase.

• Vitamin D (Calciferol): Ca²⁺/PO₄³⁻ homeostasis, bone mineralization. Deficiency: rickets (children), osteomalacia (adults). Synthesized from 7-dehydrocholesterol on skin exposure to UV.

• Vitamin E (Tocopherol): Antioxidant protecting membranes from peroxidation.

• Vitamin K (Phylloquinone/K₁, Menaquinone/K₂): γ-carboxylation of glutamate residues in clotting factors II, VII, IX, X. Deficiency: bleeding tendency.

JEE Main हेतु याद रखें: structure recognition (especially for B-complex), deficiency names, coenzyme forms, and which vitamins are antioxidants.

न्यूक्लिक अम्ल — DNA व RNA की संरचना, बेस पेयरिंग एवं प्रतिलिपिकरण

न्यूक्लिक अम्ल (DNA व RNA) आनुवंशिक जानकारी के संग्रह, expression व inheritance के molecular basis हैं। Structure consists of nucleotide monomers.

Nucleotide = nitrogenous base + pentose sugar + phosphate group.

Nitrogenous bases:
• Purines (double ring): Adenine (A), Guanine (G)
• Pyrimidines (single ring): Cytosine (C), Thymine (T), Uracil (U)
DNA में A, G, C, T; RNA में A, G, C, U

Pentose sugars:
• DNA: 2-deoxyribose (no OH at C2')
• RNA: ribose (OH at C2')

Phosphodiester bond: between 3'-OH of one sugar and 5'-phosphate of next sugar → sugar-phosphate backbone; directionality 5'→3'

DNA double helix (Watson-Crick model):
• Two antiparallel strands (5'→3' opposite direction)
• Base pairing: A=T (2 H-bonds), G≡C (3 H-bonds)
• Helix parameters: ~10.5 bp/turn; rise ~0.34 nm/bp; diameter ~2 nm
• Major groove (wider, more sequence information accessible to proteins)
• Minor groove (narrower)
• B-DNA most common; A-DNA (dehydrated); Z-DNA (left-handed, alternating purine-pyrimidine)

Chargaff's rules:
• [A]=[T], [G]=[C] in double-stranded DNA
• Base composition varies between species (species-specific)

RNA types:
• mRNA (messenger): carries genetic code from DNA to ribosome; contains codons (triplets)
• tRNA (transfer): cloverleaf secondary structure; L-shaped 3D; anticodon loop pairs with codon; carries specific amino acid at 3' CCA end
• rRNA (ribosomal): structural + catalytic component of ribosomes; peptidyl transferase activity is rRNA-catalyzed (ribozyme)

Key JEE points:
• Meselson-Stahl experiment (1958): proved semiconservative replication using ¹⁵N labeling + density gradient centrifugation
• DNA polymerase: synthesizes 5'→3' only; needs primer; has proofreading (3'→5' exonuclease)
• RNA polymerase: no primer needed; synthesizes 5'→3'; transcription
• Reverse transcriptase: RNA→DNA (Retroviruses)
• Telomerase: adds repetitive sequences to chromosome ends (counteracts shortening)

पॉलिमर — परिचय, वर्गीकरण एवं निर्माण विधियाँ

पॉलिमर large molecules (macromolecules) हैं जो repeated small units (monomers) की covalent bonding से बनते हैं। Word derived from Greek 'poly' (many) + 'meros' (parts).

Classification on the basis of source:
• Natural polymers: starch, cellulose, proteins, wool, silk, natural rubber (cis-1,4-polyisoprene), DNA
• Synthetic polymers: polyethylene, PVC, Nylon-6,6, Terylene, Buna-S, Teflon, Bakelite, etc.
• Semi-synthetic: rayon (regenerated cellulose), cellulose acetate

Classification on the basis of structure:
• Linear: long straight chains; close packing → high density; strong intermolecular forces; e.g., HDPE, nylon, polyester
• Branched: side chains off main backbone; less packing → lower density; e.g., LDPE
• Cross-linked (network): covalent bonds between chains; hard, infusible, insoluble; e.g., Bakelite, melamine-formaldehyde, vulcanized rubber

Classification on the basis of mode of polymerization:
• Addition (chain-growth): monomers add without loss of small molecules; involves initiation, propagation, termination; occurs with unsaturated monomers (C=C, C≡C); free-radical, anionic, cationic mechanisms possible
• Condensation (step-growth): monomers with two+ functional groups react with elimination of small molecule (H₂O, HCl, ROH); proceeds stepwise; high conversion needed for high MW; e.g., polyesters, polyamides, novolac

Classification on the basis of molecular forces:
• Elastomers: weak intermolecular forces; can be stretched & recover; e.g., vulcanized rubber, neoprene
• Fibres: strong H-bonding / dipole-dipole; high tensile strength; e.g., nylon, polyester
• Thermoplastics: soften on heating, harden on cooling; linear/branched; recyclable; e.g., PE, PP, PVC, PS
• Thermosets: irreversibly cured; cross-linked; cannot be remelted; e.g., Bakelite, urea-formaldehyde

Addition polymerization mechanism (free radical):
1. Initiation: radical (R•) adds to C=C → new radical
2. Propagation: radical adds to another monomer → chain grows
3. Termination: two radicals combine (combination) or disproportionate

Initiators: peroxides (benzoyl peroxide, dicumyl peroxide), azo compounds (AIBN);
Chain transfer agents control MW.

Key copolymer concepts:
• Copolymer: two+ different monomers in same chain; e.g., Buna-S (butadiene + styrene); Buna-N (butadiene + acrylonitrile)
• Random, alternating, block, graft copolymers

प्रमुख संश्लेषित पॉलिमर — तैयारी, उपयोग एवं रसायन

Important commercially & exam-wise polymers:

1. Polyethylene (PE):
• Homopolymer of ethylene (CH₂=CH₂)
• LDPE: high pressure (1000-3000 atm), trace O₂ as initiator, 350°C; highly branched; flexible, translucent; bags, bottles, insulation
• HDPE: low pressure, Ziegler-Natta catalyst; linear; rigid, opaque; pipes, toys, crates
• Properties: chemically inert, good electrical insulator, thermoplastic

2. Polypropylene (PP):
• From propene (CH₂=CHCH₃); Ziegler-Natta; isotactic PP widely used
• Tough, resilient, heat-resistant; fibers, packaging, automotive parts

3. Polystyrene (PS):
• From styrene (vinyl benzene); brittle, transparent; foam (thermocol = expanded PS)
• Used: disposable cups, insulation, CD cases

4. PVC (Polyvinyl chloride):
• From vinyl chloride (CH₂=CHCl); free-radical polymerization; rigid (pipes) to flexible (cables, raincoats — plasticizers added)
• Flame-resistant, durable

5. Teflon (PTFE):
• From tetrafluoroethylene (CF₂=CF₂); extremely inert, non-stick, high thermal stability (-200°C to 260°C)
• Used: non-stick cookware, seals, gaskets

6. Buna-S (Styrene-butadiene rubber, SBR):
• Copolymer of 1,3-butadiene + styrene (typically 3:1); emulsion polymerization; first synthetic rubber; automobile tyres, soles

7. Buna-N (Nitrile rubber):
• Copolymer of butadiene + acrylonitrile; oil/resistant; fuel hoses, gaskets

8. Neoprene (Polychloroprene):
• Polymer of chloroprene (2-chloro-1,3-butadiene); excellent chemical/weather/oil resistance; wetsuits, conveyor belts

9. Nylon-6,6 (Polyamide):
• Condensation: hexamethylenediamine + adipic acid (1:1), heat → Nylon-6,6 + H₂O
• Repeating unit: −[NH−(CH₂)₆−NH−CO−(CH₂)₄−CO]−
• Strong fibres; ropes, garments, gears

10. Terylene (Dacron, PET — Polyester):
• Condensation: ethylene glycol + terephthalic acid (or dimethyl terephthalate) → PET + H₂O/MeOH
• Strong, crease-resistant; textiles, beverage bottles

11. Bakelite (Phenol-formaldehyde resin):
• Condensation: phenol + formaldehyde; acid or base catalysed
• Novolac (acid cat., excess phenol) + formaldehyde → cross-linked Bakelite under heat
• First synthetic plastic; electrical switches, handles

12. Melamine-formaldehyde resin:
• Melamine + formaldehyde; hard, thermosetting; laminates, crockery

13. Plexiglas (PMMA):
• Polymer of methyl methacrylate; transparent, shatter-resistant; aircraft windows, lenses

बायोडीग्रेडेबल पॉलिमर तथा विशेष पॉलिमर

Biodegradable polymers degrade naturally by microbial action into harmless products (CO₂, H₂O, biomass). Important for environmental sustainability.

Key biodegradable polymers:

1. PHB (Poly-β-hydroxybutyrate) / PHBV:
• Produced by certain bacteria (e.g., Ralstonia eutropha) as energy reserve
• PHBV = copolymer of 3-hydroxybutanoic acid + 3-hydroxypentanoic acid; better controlled degradation rate
• Applications: orthopaedic devices, drug delivery, disposable tableware

2. Nylon-2-nylon-6 (Polyamide):
• Copolymer of glycine (NH₂CH₂COOH) + aminocaproic acid (NH₂(CH₂)₅COOH)
• Biodegradable polyamide; alternative to conventional nylons

3. Polylactic acid (PLA):
• From lactic acid (derived from fermentation of corn starch, sugarcane)
• Ring-opening polymerization of lactide; biocompatible, biodegradable
• Used: medical implants, food packaging, 3D printing filaments

4. Polycaprolactone (PCL):
• Slow degrading; tissue engineering scaffolds

5. Starch-based plastics:
• Blends of starch with synthetic polymers (PLA, PBAT); partial biodegradability

Non-biodegradable vs Biodegradable comparison:
| Property | Non-biodegradable | Biodegradable |
|----------|------------------|---------------|
| Examples | PE, PP, PS, PVC, Nylon, Teflon | PHB, PLA, Nylon-2-nylon-6, PCL |
| Source | Petroleum-derived | Bio-based or petro-based but degradable |
| Environmental impact | Persistent, accumulates | Break down to benign products |
| Mechanical properties | Generally superior | Varies; improving with technology |
| Applications | Bulk commodities | Niche: medical, eco-packaging |

Specialty polymers worth remembering:
• Conducting polymers: polyaniline, polypyrrole, polythiophene — conduct electricity when doped; organic electronics
• Liquid crystal polymers (LCP): high strength, low thermal expansion; displays, aerospace
• Kevlar (aramid): para-aramid polyamide; exceptionally strong; bulletproof vests
• Nomex: meta-aramid; heat resistant; firefighter gear
• Silicone polymers: Si-O backbone with organic side groups; heat stable, waterproof; sealants, breast implants
• Epoxy resins: excellent adhesion, chemical resistance; coatings, composites

Important facts

  • Carbohydrates: general formula Cₙ(H₂O)ₙ; classified as mono/di/poly-saccharides based on hydrolysis behaviour
  • Glycosidic linkage: C−O−C bond joining two monosaccharides; α or β configuration determined by anomeric carbon stereochemistry
  • Maltose and lactose are reducing sugars; sucrose is non-reducing because both anomeric carbons participate in glycosidic bond
  • Cellulose: β-1,4-glycosidic linkage between D-glucose units; straight chains with extensive inter-chain H-bonding; indigestible by humans
  • Starch: mixture of amylose (α-1,4-linear) and amylopectin (α-1,4 + α-1,6 branched every ~24-30 residues)
  • Glycogen: highly branched animal starch; branch every 8-12 glucose residues
  • Peptide bond: −CO−NH− linkage formed by condensation of amino and carboxyl groups; planar due to resonance; trans configuration dominant
  • Primary protein structure = amino acid sequence; all higher structures depend on it
  • Denaturation: disruption of secondary/tertiary/quaternary structure by heat, pH, chemicals; primary structure intact; biological activity lost
  • Enzyme active site binds substrate specifically; induced-fit model explains specificity better than lock-and-key
  • Km (Michaelis constant) = substrate concentration at half Vmax; inverse measure of enzyme-substrate affinity
  • Competitive inhibition: inhibitor resembles substrate, binds active site; Vmax unchanged, Km increases
  • Non-competitive inhibition: inhibitor binds allosteric site; Vmax decreases, Km unchanged
  • Vitamin B₁ (Thiamine): coenzyme TPP; deficiency → beriberi; source: whole grains
  • Vitamin B₁₂ (Cobalamin): only vitamin containing metal (Co); deficiency → pernicious anemia; found only in animal products
  • Vitamin C: reduces Fe³⁺ to Fe²⁺ aiding absorption; required for collagen hydroxylation; deficiency → scurvy
  • DNA: double helix, antiparallel strands, A=T (2 H-bonds), G≡C (3 H-bonds); deoxyribose sugar; thymine base
  • RNA: usually single-stranded; ribose sugar; uracil replaces thymine; types: mRNA, tRNA, rRNA
  • Chargaff's rules: [A]=[T], [G]=[C] in dsDNA; base composition is species-specific
  • Meselson-Stahl experiment: proved semiconservative DNA replication using ¹⁵N and CsCl density gradient
  • Addition polymerization: no small molecule eliminated; occurs via free-radical/anionic/cationic mechanism
  • Condensation polymerization: small molecule (H₂O/HCl/ROH) eliminated; step-growth mechanism; requires bifunctional monomers
  • Thermoplastics: linear/branched, soften on heating, recyclable (PE, PP, PVC, Nylon)
  • Thermosets: cross-linked, irreversibly cured, do not soften on reheating (Bakelite, melamine)
  • Nylon-6,6: from hexamethylenediamine + adipic acid; 6 carbons each = '6,6'
  • Nylon-6: from ε-caprolactam (ring-opening polymerization); single monomer source
  • Terylene (PET): from ethylene glycol + terephthalic acid; polyester
  • Bakelite: phenol + formaldehyde; acid-catalysed novolac + formaldehyde → cross-linked thermoset
  • Teflon (PTFE): from tetrafluoroethylene; extremely inert, non-stick
  • PHB and PHBV: bacterial polyesters; biodegradable; used in medical devices
  • Nylon-2-nylon-6: copolymer of glycine and aminocaproic acid; biodegradable polyamide
  • Buna-S: styrene-butadiene rubber (copolymer); SBR tyres
  • Natural rubber: cis-1,4-polyisoprene; vulcanization (S cross-links) improves elasticity and strength
  • Iodine test: starch-blue black; cellulose gives no colour
  • Biuret test: peptide bonds give violet colour; xanthoproteic test: aromatic AAs give yellow (nitration)

Formulas and relationships

Carbohydrate general empirical formula: Cₙ(H₂O)ₙ (not strict — exceptions exist, e.g., deoxy sugars)
Michaelis-Menten equation: v = Vmax[S] / (Km + [S])
Lineweaver-Burk double reciprocal: 1/v = (Km/Vmax)(1/[S]) + 1/Vmax
Isotope dilution for MW determination: not directly tested but related to Meselson-Stahl concept
Degree of polymerization (DP): DP = M_n / M₀ where M_n = number-average MW, M₀ = monomer MW
Number average molecular weight: M_n = ΣNᵢMᵢ / ΣNᵢ
Weight average molecular weight: M_w = ΣNᵢMᵢ² / ΣNᵢMᵢ
Polydispersity index (PDI): PDI = M_w / M_n ≥ 1 (PDI = 1 for monodisperse, e.g., natural polymers like proteins)

Common mistakes

  • Calling sucrose a reducing sugar — it is non-reducing because both anomeric carbons are involved in the glycosidic bond, leaving no free hemiacetal/hemiketal group
  • Assuming cellulose is digestible by humans — humans lack cellulase enzyme; only ruminants (with symbiotic microbes) can digest cellulose
  • Confusing α-1,4 vs β-1,4 glycosidic linkages — starch has α-linkages (helical), cellulose has β-linkages (linear sheets)
  • Forgetting that not all enzymes are proteins — ribozymes are catalytic RNA molecules
  • Interpreting Km inversely — low Km means HIGH enzyme-substrate affinity, not low affinity
  • Mixing up competitive vs non-competitive inhibition effects on Vmax and Km
  • Classifying Bakelite as a thermoplastic — it is a thermoset (cross-linked phenol-formaldehyde resin) that cannot be remelted
  • Confusing Nylon-6 with Nylon-6,6 — Nylon-6 comes from caprolactam (one monomer); Nylon-6,6 from diamine + diacid (two monomers)
  • Assuming all synthetic polymers are non-biodegradable — PHB, PHBV, PLA, Nylon-2-nylon-6 are synthetic AND biodegradable
  • Misidentifying vitamin solubility — Vitamins A, D, E, K are fat-soluble; B-complex and C are water-soluble
  • Writing wrong deficiency diseases — beriberi (B₁), pellagra (B₃/niacin), scurvy (C), rickets (D), pernicious anemia (B₁₂), night blindness (A)
  • Forgetting that denaturation does NOT break peptide bonds — primary structure remains intact after denaturation
  • Assuming all polymers with C=C in monomer undergo addition — some condensation polymers may have unsaturation in monomer but still proceed via condensation
  • Incorrectly stating Chargaff's rules apply to RNA — they apply only to double-stranded DNA
  • Forgetting that DNA strands are antiparallel — one strand runs 5'→3', the other 3'→5'
