Important facts

  • Group 17 mein Cl ka electron gain enthalpy sabse zyada negative hai (F nahi, kyunki F ka small size electron-electron repulsion badhata hai)
  • B₂H₆ (diborane) mein 3-center-2-electron (banana) bonds hote hain
  • XeF₄ square planar (sp³d², 2 lone pairs), XeF₂ linear (sp³d, 3 lone pairs)
  • Tl⁺ more stable than Tl³⁺ due to strong inert pair effect
  • Graphite layers ke beech weak van der Waals forces; diamond hara hardest natural material
  • H₂SO₄ contact process mein V₂O₅ catalyst, 720K pressure use hota hai
  • White P (P₄ tetrahedral) highly reactive, stored under water; Red P less reactive polymeric form
  • Rhombic S (α-S) stable below 95.5°C; Monoclinic S (β-S) stable above 95.5°C
  • Cl₂ disproportionation: cold dilute NaOH → NaOCl; hot conc NaOH → NaClO₃
  • N₂O₄ ⇌ 2NO₂ equilibrium: colourless ↔ brown; high pressure favour N₂O₄
  • O₃ ozone bent structure (117.5°), strong oxidising agent, stratospheric UV protection
  • HF glass etch karti hai: SiO₂ + 4HF → SiF₄ + 2H₂O
  • SnCl₂ reducing agent: Fe³⁺→Fe²⁺, Hg²⁺→Hg₂²⁺→Hg
  • XePtF₆ pehla noble gas compound (Neil Bartlett, 1962)
  • Iodine starch test: I₂ ya IO₃⁻+starch blue complex deta hai
  • Pure HNO₃ colourless; yellow colour NO₂ dissolution se hota hai

Formulas and relationships

VSEPR geometry: AX₂E₀=linear, AX₂E₁=bent, AX₃E₀=trigonal planar, AX₃E₁=pyramidal, AX₄= tetrahedral, AX₄E₁=see-saw, AX₃E₂=T-shaped, AX₂E₃=linear, AX₅=trigonal bipyramidal, AX₄E₂=square planar
SP³ hybridization: 4 bond pairs (CH₄), SP²: 3 bond pairs (BF₃), SP: 2 bond pairs (BeCl₂), SP³D: 5 (PCl₅), SP³D²: 6 (SF₆)
Haber process equilibrium: N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃; ΔH = -92 kJ/mol; Le Chatelier: high P, low T favour NH₃
Contact process: 2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃; ΔH = -196 kJ/mol; V₂O₅ catalyst
Cl₂ disproportionation cold: Cl₂ + 2NaOH → NaCl + NaOCl + H₂O
Cl₂ disproportionation hot: 3Cl₂ + 6NaOH → 5NaCl + NaClO₃ + 3H₂O
B₂H₆ + 6H₂O → 2H₃BO₃ + 6H₂ (hydrolysis reaction)
SiCl₄ + 4H₂O → H₄SiO₄ + 4HCl (hydrolysis)
CCl₄ + 2H₂O → CO₂ + 4HCl (but very slow; CCl₄ does not hydrolyse easily)
XeF₂ hydrolysis: XeF₂ + H₂O → Xe + O₂ + 2HF

Common mistakes

  • Students often think F has highest electron gain enthalpy — actually Cl is more negative due to small size causing e-e repulsion in F
  • Confusing XeF₂ (linear) with XeF₄ (square planar) — check lone pairs: XeF₂ has 3 LP, XeF₄ has 2 LP
  • Thinking CCl₄ hydrolyses easily like SiCl₄ — C has no d-orbitals, so CCl₄ is resistant to hydrolysis
  • Confusing white P (P₄, reactive) with red P (polymeric, stable) allotropes
  • Thinking N₂ is reactive — it is extremely inert due to strong triple bond (941 kJ/mol)
  • Mixing up B₂H₆ bonding — it has 4 normal bonds + 2 banana bonds, not 6 conventional bonds
  • Thinking Tl³⁺ is more stable than Tl⁺ — opposite is true due to inert pair effect
  • Forgetting that rhombic S converts to monoclinic S at 95.5°C transition temperature
  • Confusing H₂SO₄ preparation — oleum (H₂S₂O₇) is intermediate, never absorb SO₃ directly in water
  • Thinking noble gases form compounds easily — only Xe forms significant compounds under special conditions
  • Mixing up oxidation states: Cl in HClO₄ is +7 (highest), not +5
  • Not recognizing that NO is a radical (unpaired electron) while NO₂ is also a radical but dimerises to N₂O₄
  • Thinking all group 15 trihalides are stable — NCl₃ is unstable/explosive compared to PCl₃
  • Ignoring that H₂O has higher boiling point than H₂S due to hydrogen bonding, not molecular mass
