Organic Compounds with Oxygen — Complete Notes for JEE Main (हिंदी)

JEE MainHindiNotes

1. अल्कोहल — वर्गीकरण, नामांकन और संश्लेषण विधियाँ

अल्कोहल वे यौगिक हैं जिनमें कार्बन श्रृंखला से जुड़ा एक या अधिक -OH समूह होता है। इनका वर्गीकरण तृतीयक (3°), द्वितीयक (2°) और प्राथमिक (1°) में किया जाता है, जो उस कार्बन पर निर्भर करता है जिससे -OH जुड़ा हो। JEE Main के लिए प्राथमिक, द्वितीयक और तृतीयक अल्कोहल के बीच भेद करना सबसे महत्वपूर्ण है क्योंकि Lucas test और ऑक्सीकरण प्रतिक्रियाएँ इसी आधार पर अलग-अलग होती हैं।

व्यवहारिक नामांकन में 'अल्कोहल' शब्द जोड़कर मातृ हाइड्रोकार्बन का नाम दिया जाता है (जैसे मेथनॉल, इथेनॉल, आइसोप्रोपिल अल्कोहल)। IUPAC नामांकन में -ऑल प्रत्यय का उपयोग किया जाता है; उदाहरण: CH₃CH₂OH → एथेनॉल, CH₃CH(OH)CH₃ → प्रोपेन-2-ऑल। बहुфункционаल समूहों वाले यौगिकों में principal chain चुनने का नियम यह है कि -OH को न्यूनतम स्थान संख्या मिले।

संश्लेषण की प्रमुख विधियाँ:
• हाइड्रोबायोगणेशन (Hydroboration-Oxidation): अल्कीन + BH₃·THF, फिर H₂O₂/OH⁻ → एन्टी-माार्कॉनिकोफ़ नियम के अनुसार अल्कोहल प्राप्त होता है। anti-Markovnikov addition, no rearrangement — यह JEE के लिए बहुत महत्वपूर्ण है।
• एक्वार्किशियन हाइड्रेशन: अल्कीन + H₂O/H⁺ → Markovnikov नियम के अनुसार अल्कोहल, carbocation intermediate होने के कारण rearrangement (1,2-H या 1,2-methyl shift) संभव है।
• एपॉक्साइड का opening: Grignard reagent के साथ एपॉक्साइड का opening — nucleophile कम substituted carbon पर attack करता है।
• Grignard reagent + carbonyl compound: Formaldehyde → 1° alcohol; अन्य aldehydes → 2° alcohol; ketones → 3° alcohol।
• NaBH₄ और LiAlH₄ द्वारा carbonyl compounds की reduction।
• C₆H₅MgBr + formaldehyde → PhCH₂OH (benzyl alcohol) जैसे important conversions याद रखें।

2. अल्कोहल — भौतिक और रासायनिक गुण

भौतिक गुण: Lower members (C₁–C₃) miscible in water due to H-bonding; boiling points increase with molecular mass and branching decreases B.P. (more surface area = more van der Waals). Primary > secondary > tertiary for B.P. (straight chain vs branched). Solubility in water decreases as hydrophobic alkyl group grows larger.

रासायनिक गुण (JEE Main high-yield):
• Acidic nature: रिएक्टिव धातुओं (Na, K) के साथ H₂ gas निकलती है। 1° > 2° > 3° order in acidity (alkyl group electron-donating effect reduces acidity). Methanol is more acidic than ethanol.
• Lucas Test: concentrated HCl + anhydrous ZnCl₂ — 3° alcohol: immediate turbidity; 2° alcohol: turbidity in ~5 min; 1° alcohol: no turbidity at room temperature (needs heating). इस test से अल्कोहल की class पहचानी जाती है।
• Oxidation: Strong oxidizing agents (KMnO₄, K₂Cr₂O₇/H⁺, CrO₃/pyridine) — 1° alcohol → aldehyde → carboxylic acid (PCC पर aldehyde तक रुकता है, further oxidation नहीं होती); 2° alcohol → ketone; 3° alcohol → oxidation नहीं होती (बिना C-H bond के)।
• Dehydration (Elimination): conc. H₂SO₄, Δ → alkene. Reactivity order: 3° > 2° > 1°. Zaitsev's rule: major product वही है जिसमें double bond अधिक substituted हो। E1 mechanism via carbocation — rearrangement हो सकती है।
• Reaction with HX: 3° > 2° > 1° (SN1 pathway for 2°/3°; SN2 for 1°). ZnCl₂ catalyst HCl के लिए।
• Reaction with PX₃/PX₅: R-OH + PCl₃ → R-Cl + H₃PO₃; R-OH + PCl₅ → R-Cl + POCl₃ + HCl.
• Esterification: R-OH + R'-COOH (H⁺ catalyst) → ester + water। Fischer esterification mechanism याद रखें।
• Victor Meyer test: alcohols को iodine + Ag₂O से treat करके iodoalkane बनाकर फिर AgNO₃ + NH₃ — nitrolic acid (red) → 1°; pseudo-nitrolic acid (blue) → 2°; no colour → 3°.

3. फिनॉल — संश्लेषण, गुण और महत्वपूर्ण अभिक्रियाएँ

फिनॉल (C₆H₅OH) एक aromatic alcohol है जिसमें -OH सीधे benzene ring से जुड़ा होता है। ज्ञात सबसे महत्वपूर्ण aromatic organic compound with oxygen है।

संश्लेषण विधियाँ:
• Dow's process: Chlorobenzene + NaOH (623K, 300 atm) → sodium phenoxide → H⁺ → phenol। Benzyne mechanism / addition-elimination।
• Cumene process: सबसे important commercial method — benzene + propene → cumene → O₂ oxidation → cumene hydroperoxide → H⁺ → phenol + acetone। इसमें acetone भी principal product है जो economic aspect देता है।
• Diazotization: Aniline → diazonium salt (NaNO₂/HCl, 0-5°C) → H₂O/warm → phenol।
• Raschig process: Benzene + Cl₂/air (catalyst) → chlorobenzene → hydrolysis → phenol।
• Sodium benzenesulfonate + NaOH (fusion 300°C) → sodium phenoxide → phenol।

भौतिक गुण: Phenol high melting point (43°C) — intermolecular H-bonding के कारण; slightly soluble in water but more soluble than equivalent alkyl alcohol। Phenol weakly acidic — pKa ≈ 10 (ethanol pKa ≈ 16 से much more acidic)।

रासायनिक गुण:
•酸性 character: NaOH में dissolve होती है (sodium phenoxide बनती है) लेकिन NaHCO₃ में नहीं — CO₂ नहीं देती। इससे phenol और carboxylic acid में distinction कर सकते हैं।
• Electrophilic aromatic substitution (EAS): -OH activating, ortho-para directing है।
— Bromination: Br₂/H₂O → 2,4,6-tribromophenol (white precipitate, quantitative test)। Br₂/CS₂ → monobromination (p-bromophenol major)।
— Nitration: dilute HNO₃ → o-nitrophenol + p-nitrophenol; conc. HNO₃ → 2,4,6-trinitrophenol (picric acid)।
— Kolbe-Schmitt reaction: Sodium phenoxide + CO₂ (加压, 125°C) → salicylic acid (o-hydroxybenzoic acid)। This is highly important. Heat controls regioselectivity (higher temp favors para)。
— Reimer-Tiemann reaction: Phenol + CHCl₃ + NaOH → salicylaldehyde (o-hydroxybenzaldehyde) + minor p-isomer। Mechanism: dichlorocarbene (:CCl₂) generated, attacks ortho position।
— Gattermann-Koch: Phenol + CO + HCl (anhyd. AlCl₃) → salicylaldehyde।
— Friedel-Crafts alkylation/acylation possible but less common due to OH coordination with Lewis acid。
• Reaction with FeCl₃: violet colour — characteristic test for phenol (formation of complex [Fe(OC₆H₅)₆]³⁻)।
• Reduction: Zn dust → benzene।

4. इथर — संश्लेषण, गुण और अभिक्रियाएँ

इथर R-O-R' general formula वाले organic compounds हैं। Simmetric (R=R') और asymmetric (R≠R') इथर।

संश्लेषण विधियाँ:
• Williamson synthesis: सबसे important laboratory method — R-OH + Na → R-O⁻Na⁺, फिर R'-X → R-O-R' + NaX। Primary alkyl halide best substrate; if secondary/tertiary used, elimination (alkene) major product बनेगा (E2 competing)।
• Acid-catalyzed dehydration: 2 R-OH (H₂SO₄, 140°C) → R-O-R + H₂O। Only effective for primary alcohols at lower temperatures (140°C)। Higher temps (170°C) → alkene बनेगा।
• Alkoxymercuration-demercuration: alkene + R-OH, Hg(OAc)₂, फिर NaBH₄ → ether (anti-Markovnikov not applicable here, follows Markovnikov)。

भौतिक गुण: इथर low boiling points (alcohol से कम क्योकि H-bonding नहीं करते) — analogous molecular mass वाले alkane के पास boiling point; moderately soluble in water (H-bond acceptor)।

रासायनिक गुण (बहुत important):
• Cleavage by HX (HI, HBr): R-O-R' + HX → R-X + R'-OH (1 equiv.); excess HX → R-X + R'-X (2 equiv.)। Mechanism: 1° alkyl group → SN2 (X⁻ attacks less hindered carbon); 3° alkyl group → SN1 (carbocation forms on more substituted side)। Always remember: I⁻ attacks the smaller (less substituted) carbon first under SN2 conditions।
• Oxidation: slowly by air → peroxides (dangerous!) — diethyl ether forms explosive peroxides on standing। इसीलिए ether store करते समय antioxidant add किया जाता है और fresh distillation करनी पड़ती है before use।
• Reaction with cold conc. HI: CH₃OCH₃ + HI → CH₃OH + CH₃I (SN2 — iodide attacks methyl carbon, smaller one)।
• Ether formation as protecting group: TBS ether, MOM ether etc. advanced level।
• Ether is relatively unreactive — good solvent for Grignard reactions, NaBH₄ reductions।

5. एल्डिहाइड और कीटोन — संश्लेषण और भौतिक गुण

Carbonyl group (C=O) contains sp² hybridized carbon. Aldehydes have formyl group (-CHO) at terminal position (R-CHO); ketones have carbonyl between two carbon atoms (R-CO-R')। Formic acid (HCOOH) और its derivatives इस chapter में शामिल नहीं हैं (those are carboxylic acids)。

संश्लेषण विधियाँ:
• Aldehydes:
— Oxidation of 1° alcohols (PCC — pyridinium chlorochromate in CH₂Cl₂): stop at aldehyde stage, no over-oxidation to acid। TEMPO/BAIB भी modern method।
— Rosenmund reduction: acyl chloride + H₂/Pd-BaSO₄ (quinoline poisoned) → aldehyde।
— Gattermann-Koch: ArH + CO + HCl (AlCl₃/CuCl) → ArCHO。Benzaldehyde synthesis।
— Etard reaction: Toluene + CrO₂Cl₂ (CS₂) → benzaldehydechromium complex → H₂O → benzaldehyde।
— Ozonolysis of alkenes (reductive workup: Zn/H₂O) → aldehydes/ketones mixture।
• Ketones:
— Oxidation of 2° alcohols (KMnO₄/K₂Cr₂O₇)。
— Friedel-Crafts acylation: ArH + RCOCl/AlCl₃ → aryl ketone (ArCOR)。
— Ozonolysis of alkenes (same as above)。
— Hydration of alkynes: terminal alkyne + HgSO₄/H₂SO₄ → methyl ketone; internal alkyne → mixture of ketones。
— From nitriles: RCN + R'MgBr → imine → hydrolysis → ketone。

भौतिक गुण: C≤4 aldehydes/ketones are soluble in water (H-bond acceptor); B.P. higher than comparable hydrocarbons but lower than alcohols (no H-bond donor); aromatic aldehydes (benzaldehyde) are liquids, solids at RT।

6. एल्डिहाइड/कीटोन — Nucleophilic Addition और α-Hydrogen Reactions

Nucleophilic addition mechanism (most tested reaction type):
• CN⁻ addition: HCN → cyanohydrin (α-hydroxynitrile)। Cyanohydrin → hydrolysis → α-hydroxy acid। Important for chain extension (1 carbon added)。
• Grignard addition: R-MgX + R'CHO → 2° alcohol after H₃O⁺ workup; R-MgX + R'COR'' → 3° alcohol। Form aldehydes give 2° alcohols directly; ketones give 3°。
• Alcohol addition (acetal/hemiacetal formation):
— Hemiacetal: RCHO + R'OH ⇌ RCH(OH)(OR')। Acid/base catalyzed reversible。
— Acetal: RCHO + 2R'OH (dry HCl) → RCH(OR')₂ + H₂O। Acetals serve as protecting groups for aldehydes — stable to base, removed by aqueous acid。
— Ketones form hemiketal/ketal similarly but less readily。
• Ammonia derivatives:
— NH₂OH → oxime (R=CH=N-OH)
— 2,4-DNP → 2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazone (bright yellow/orange precipitate — identification test)。
— NH₂NH₂ → hydrazone; PhNHNH₂ → phenylhydrazone।
— NH₂SO₃H → sulfonic acid derivative (crystalline solid, mp helps identify)。

α-Hydrogen reactions (very important for JEE):
• Aldol Condensation: Two molecules of aldehyde/ketone having α-H, dilute NaOH → β-hydroxy aldehyde (aldol) or β-hydroxy ketone (ketol)。Heat → α,β-unsaturated carbonyl (crossed aldol if different aldehydes both have α-H)।
— Crossed aldol: one partner has NO α-H (e.g., benzaldehyde) → directional aldol。Do NOT confuse with Cannizzaro。
• Cannizzaro Reaction: Aldehydes WITHOUT α-hydrogen (HCHO, PhCHO, (CH₃)₃C-CHO) + conc. NaOH → alcohol + carboxylate salt (disproportionation)。
— Crossed Cannizzaro: formaldehyde + another non-α-H aldehyde; formaldehyde oxidizes preferentially → gets oxidized to formate, other reduced to alcohol。
• Haloform Reaction: CH₃CO- group or CH₃CH(OH)- group + X₂/NaOH → CHX₃ (haloform, yellow ppt) + carboxylate。Iodoform test (I₂/NaOH) gives yellow CHI₃ — detects methyl ketone or secondary alcohol with CH₃CH(OH)-group。Ethanol and methanal also give iodoform test।

7. एल्डिहाइड/कीटोन — oxidation, reduction और नामांकित अभिक्रियाएँ

Oxidation:
• Mild oxidizing agents distinguish aldehydes from ketones:
— Tollens' test: [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ (ammoniacal silver nitrate) + aldehyde → silver mirror (Ag deposited) + carboxylate。Ketones do NOT react (except α-hydroxy ketones)。
— Fehling's test: Cu²⁺ (tartrate complex, alkaline) + aliphatic aldehyde → red Cu₂O ppt。Aromatic aldehydes (PhCHO) do NOT reduce Fehling's solution — this is a key distinguishing feature!
— Benedict's test: similar to Fehling's, uses citrate complex。
• Strong oxidation: KMnO₄, K₂Cr₂O₇ → carboxylic acids (aldehydes)।

Reduction:
• Catalytic hydrogenation: H₂/Ni, Pt, Pd → alcohols (aldehydes → 1°; ketones → 2°)。
• Clemmensen reduction: Zn(Hg)/conc. HCl → converts C=O to CH₂ (full reduction to alkane)। Acidic condition, works for ketones and aldehydes not sensitive to acid。
• Wolff-Kishner reduction: NH₂NH₂/KOH (ethylene glycol, 180°C) → C=O to CH₂। Basic condition, acid-sensitive substrates preferred over Clemmensen。
• NaBH₄: selective reduction of aldehydes/ketones only (does not reduce esters, acids, amides)。
• LiAlH₄: stronger, reduces aldehydes, ketones, esters, acids, amides।
• Meerwein-Ponndorf-Verley (MPV) reduction: Al(O-i-Pr)₃ / i-PrOH → ketone/aldehyde → alcohol (reversible, uses isopropanol as hydride source)。

Important named reactions:
• Perkin reaction: ArCHO + (CH₃CO)₂O/sodium acetate → ArCH=CH-COOH (cinnamic acid type)。 Requires α-H on anhydride。
• Knoevenagel condensation: Active methylene compound + aldehyde/ketone (pyridine, mild base) → alkene。Not to confuse with Perkin。
• Benzoin condensation: 2 PhCHO + KCN (alc.) → benzoin (PhCO-CHOH-Ph)。 Cyanide catalyzed umpolung reaction — reverses polarity of carbonyl carbon in second molecule。
• Wittig reaction: R₂C=O + Ph₃P=CR'₂ → R₂C=CR'₂ (alkene) + Ph₃P=O。Very important for bond formation。
• Aldol self-condensation products for JEE: acetaldehyde → 3-hydroxybutanal → crotonaldehyde (but-2-enal)।

8. कार्बोक्सिलिक अम्ल और उनके अवक्षय — संश्लेषण, गुण, अभिक्रियाएँ

Carboxylic acids (RCOOH) — strongest commonly encountered organic acids. The carboxyl group consists of carbonyl + hydroxyl; resonance stabilizes carboxylate anion.

संश्लेषण:
• Oxidation of 1° alcohols and aldehydes (KMnO₄, K₂Cr₂O₇, HNO₃, etc.)。
• Hydrolysis of nitriles: R-CN + H₂O/H⁺ or OH⁻ → RCOOH (adds 1 carbon)。
• Hydrolysis of esters: RCOOR' + H₂O/H⁺ → RCOOH + R'OH। Acid or base hydrolysis (saponification: base hydrolysis → carboxylate salt, then acidify)。
• Hydrolysis of acid chlorides/anhydrides: very vigorous。
• Carboxylation of Grignard: RMgX + CO₂ → RCOOH (after H₃O⁺) — adds one carbon。
• Oxidation of alkylbenzenes: KMnO₄/KOH, heat → benzoic acid (side chain oxidised regardless of length, as long as benzylic H exists)。
• Malonic ester synthesis: diethyl malonate → monoalkylation → hydrolysis + decarboxylation → substituted acetic acid。

भौतिक गुण: Carboxylic acids have highest B.P. among comparable organic compounds (strong dimer H-bonding in vapour phase and non-polar solvents)। Formic acid and acetic acid miscible with water; solubility decreases with increasing chain length。

रासायनिक गुण:
• Acidity: pKa values — Formic (3.75) < Acetic (4.76) < Phenol (10) < Water (15.7) < Ethanol (16)। Electron-withdrawing groups increase acidity (inductive effect); electron-donating decrease acidity。Halogens on α-carbon strongly increase acidity (chloroacetic > acetic; trichloroacetic strongest among simple mono-carboxylic acids)。
• Reaction with NaHCO₃: effervescence (CO₂ release) — positive test for carboxylic acid over phenol (phenol does NOT liberate CO₂ from NaHCO₃)।
• Esterification (Fischer): RCOOH + R'OH (H⁺ cat.) ⇌ RCOOR' + H₂O। Equilibrium reaction — Le Chatelier's principle applies (remove water → drive forward)。
• Decarboxylation: Soda-lime (NaOH+CaO) fusion → RCOONa + NaOH → RH + Na₂CO₃। Lose one carbon as CO₂。
• Kolbe electrolysis: Sodium carboxylate electrolysis → alkane (R-R dimer)。
• Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky (HVZ) reaction: RCOOH + Br₂/P (red) → α-bromo acid → further substitutions possible। Specific to α-bromination of carboxylic acids。
• Reaction with SOCl₂: RCOOH + SOCl₂ → RCOCl + SO₂ + HCl (best method to prepare acid chlorides)。

Derivatives hierarchy (reactivity order for nucleophilic acyl substitution):
Acid chloride (RCOCl) > Acid anhydride ((RCO)₂O) > Ester (RCOOR') > Amide (RCONH₂) > Carboxylate anion (RCOO⁻, least reactive)。
This reactivity trend determines which derivative can be converted to which (high → low yes; low → high needs special reagents like SOCl₂)।

9. अम्ल अवक्षय — संश्लेषण, गुण और महत्वपूर्ण अभिक्रियाएँ

Acid chlorides (RCOCl), anhydrides ((RCO)₂O), esters (RCOOR'), and amides (RCONH₂) — collectively carboxylic acid derivatives.

Acid Chlorides (Acyl chlorides):
• Preparation: RCOOH + SOCl₂ (best) / PCl₅ / PCl₃ → RCOCl + byproducts。
• Hydrolysis: RCOCl + H₂O → RCOOH + HCl (vigorous, fumes) — instantly hydrolysed by moisture。
• Alcoholysis: RCOCl + R'OH → ester + HCl (pyridine often used to scavenge HCl)。
• Aminolysis: RCOCl + 2R'NH₂ → amide + R'NH₃⁺Cl⁻。
• Rosenmund reduction: RCOCl + H₂/Pd-BaSO₄(quinoline) → RCHO (stop at aldehyde)。
• Friedel-Crafts acylation: ArH + RCOCl/AlCl₃ → ArCOR (aryl ketone) — very important for aromatic synthesis。
• Reaction with lithium aluminium hydride → 1° alcohol (through aldehyde intermediate)。

Acid Anhydrides:
• Preparation: 2 RCOOH (heat/dehydration) → anhydride + H₂O; or RCOCl + RCOO⁻ → anhydride + Cl⁻。
• Hydrolysis → 2 molecules carboxylic acid。
• Alcoholysis → ester + acid。
• Aminolysis → amide + acid。
• Acetic anhydride (CH₃CO)₂O most commonly used reagent in lab (safer alternative to acetyl chloride)。

Esters:
• Preparation: Fischer esterification (acid cat.), Schmidt reaction (acid + NaN₃), esterification via acid chloride。
• Saponification: ester + NaOH(aq) → carboxylate salt + alcohol (irreversible, goes to completion)。
• Acid hydrolysis: ester + H₂O/H⁺ → carboxylic acid + alcohol (equilibrium)。
• Ammonolysis: ester + NH₃ → amide + alcohol。
• Claisen condensation: 2 esters with α-H + NaOEt → β-keto ester。Crossed Claisen uses two different esters。
• Reduction: LiAlH₄ → 2 alcohols; DIBAL-H at -78°C → aldehyde (controlled partial reduction)。

Amides:
• Preparation: RCOCl + NH₃ → primary amide; ester + NH₃ → amide。
• Hydrolysis: RCONH₂ + H₂O/H⁺ → RCOOH + NH₄⁺; or RCONH₂ + OH⁻ → RCOO⁻ + NH₃。
• Dehydration: RCONH₂ + P₂O₅ → R-CN (nitrile)。Important conversion。
• Hofmann bromamide degradation: RCONH₂ + Br₂ + 4NaOH → R-NH₂ (primary amine, 1 carbon less) + Na₂CO₃ + 2NaBr + 2H₂O。Mechanism involves isocyanate intermediate; migration of R group to N。Loss of one carbon atom — extremely important reaction。
• Schmidt reaction: RCOOH + HN₃ (H₂SO₄) → R-NH₂ + CO₂ + N₂ (also loss of one carbon)。
• Curtius rearrangement: RCOCl → RCON₃ (via acyl azide) → heat → R-N=C=O (isocyanate) → H₂O → R-NH₂ (loss of one carbon)。Similar to Hofmann — all three (Hofmann, Curtius, Schmidt) give primary amine with one less carbon。

10. नामांकित अभिक्रियाएँ — JEE Main High-Yield Revision

These named reactions are repeatedly asked in JEE Main. Master each one with reagent, mechanism type, and product:

1. Aldol Condensation: 2 RCH₂CHO + dil. NaOH → RCH₂CH(OH)CH(R)CHO → heat → RCH₂CH=CH(R)CHO + H₂O। Requires α-H。Product: β-hydroxy carbonyl → α,β-unsaturated carbonyl。

2. Cannizzaro Reaction: 2 HCHO + conc. NaOH → CH₃OH + HCOONa। No α-H required。Disproportionation (one reduced, one oxidized)。

3. Perkin Reaction: ArCHO + (CH₃CO)₂O/CH₃COONa, heat → ArCH=CH-COOH (cinnamic acid)。α-H on anhydride required。

4. Knoevenagel Condensation: RCHO/R₂CO + CH₂(COOEt)₂/pyridine → alkene with COOEt groups。Active methylene compound。

5. Benzoin Condensation: 2 PhCHO + KCN(alc.) → PhCO-CHOH-Ph (benzoin)。Cyanide-catalyzed umpolung — carbonyl carbon of one molecule acts as nucleophile toward the other。

6. Claisen Condensation: 2 CH₃COOC₂H₅ + NaOEt → CH₃COCH₂COOC₂H₅ + C₂H₅OH。β-keto ester formed。

7._cross_Clacein: Different esters, one must have no α-H (e.g., ethyl benzoate + ethyl acetate with NaOEt) → crossed β-keto ester。

8. Reimer-Tiemann: Phenol + CHCl₃ + NaOH → o-hydroxybenzaldehyde (major) + p-isomer (minor)。DCM → dichlorocarbene intermediate。

9. Kolbe-Schmitt: Sodium phenoxide + CO₂, 125°C/pressure → salicylic acid (ortho)。Regioselectivity depends on cation (Na⁺ → ortho; K⁺ → para)。

10. Friedel-Crafts Acylation: ArH + RCOCl/AlCl₃ → ArCOR + HCl。No rearrangement (unlike FC alkylation)。Important区别。

11. Friedel-Crafts Alkylation: ArH + RCl/AlCl₃ → ArR + HCl。Carbocation rearrangement possible; polyalkylation problem。Less reliable than acylation。

12. Gattermann-Koch: ArH + CO + HCl (AlCl₃/CuCl) → ArCHO。Formylation of aromatics。Different from Reimer-Tiemann — Gattermann-Koch makes ArCHO directly。

13. Etard Reaction: Toluen + CrO₂Cl₂/CS₂ → benzaldehydechromium complex → H₂O → benzaldehyde。Selective oxidation of methyl to CHO。

14. Rosenmund Reduction: RCOCl + H₂/Pd-BaSO₄(quinoline poison) → RCHO。Poisoned catalyst prevents over-reduction。

15. Clemmensen vs Wolff-Kishner: Both reduce C=O to CH₂. Clemmensen: Zn(Hg)/conc.HCl (acidic); Wolff-Kishner: NH₂NH₂/KOH, glycol, 180°C (basic)。Choose based on acid/base sensitivity of substrate。

16. Haloform Reaction: CH₃CO-R or CH₃CH(OH)-R + X₂/NaOH → CHX₃↓(yellow) + RCOONa。Iodoform test specifically uses I₂/NaOH। Ethanol and acetaldehyde also give iodoform (ethanol oxidized to CH₃CHO first)。

17. HVZ (Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky): RCH₂COOH + Br₂/red P → RCHBrCOOH (α-bromination)。Only carboxylic acids with α-H undergo this。Direct bromination without α-H requires different conditions。

18. Hofmann Bromamide: RCONH₂ + Br₂ + 4NaOH → RNH₂ + Na₂CO₃ + 2NaBr + 2H₂O。Primary amine with one carbon less。Mechanism: N-bromoamide → isocyanate → carbamate → amine。

19. Stephen Reaction: R-CN + SnCl₂/HCl → R-CH=NH (imino salt) → H₂O → R-CHO。Alternative to Rosenmund for aldehydes from nitriles。

20. Oppenauer Oxidation: 2° alcohol + ketone (Al(O-i-Pr)₃) → ketone + i-PrOH。Reverse of MPV reduction。Selective for 2° alcohols in presence of other functional groups。

Quick comparison table for differentiation tests:
• 1° alcohol vs 2° vs 3°: Lucas test (time of turbidity)
• Aldehyde vs ketone: Tollens/Fehling (aldehyde positive, ketone negative except α-hydroxy ketones)
• Phenol vs carboxylic acid: NaHCO₃ (acid gives CO₂ bubbles, phenol does not)
• Methyl ketone: Iodoform test (yellow CHI₃)
• Phenol: FeCl₃ (violet colour)
• Aldehyde without α-H: Cannizzaro (conc. NaOH, disproportionation)

Important facts

  • Lucas test: 3° alcohol → immediate turbidity; 2° → 5 min; 1° → no turbidity at RT
  • Kolbe-Schmitt: Na phenoxide + CO₂ → salicylic acid (ortho); K phenoxide → para-hydroxybenzoate
  • Reimer-Tiemann: phenol + CHCl₃ + NaOH → salicylaldehyde (o-hydroxybenzaldehyde) via :CCl₂ intermediate
  • Perkin reaction requires α-hydrogen on the anhydride; Knoevenagel uses active methylene compound
  • Tollens test positive for all aldehydes (including aromatic); Fehling's test positive ONLY for aliphatic aldehydes
  • Acid chloride > anhydride > ester > amide > carboxylate anion in reactivity toward nucleophilic acyl substitution
  • HVZ reaction brominates ONLY at the α-position of carboxylic acids (requires P/red as catalyst)
  • Hofmann bromamide, Curtius, and Schmidt reactions ALL give primary amines with one carbon less than starting material
  • Clemmensen reduction (Zn-Hg/HCl) is acidic; Wolff-Kishner (NH₂NH₂/KOH/glycol, 180°C) is basic — choose based on substrate sensitivity
  • Ethanol and acetaldehyde give positive iodoform test; other alcohols must have CH₃CH(OH)- group
  • Phenol is more acidic than ethanol (pKa 10 vs 16) but less acidic than carboxylic acids (pKa ~4-5); phenol dissolves in NaOH but NOT in NaHCO₃
  • C₆H₅OH + FeCl₃ → violet coloured complex [Fe(OC₆H₅)₆]³⁻; this is the standard confirmatory test for phenol
  • Dow's process uses chlorobenzene + NaOH at 623K/300atm; Cumene process gives phenol + acetone as co-products
  • Williamson synthesis fails with 2°/3° alkyl halides (elimination dominates); use 1° halide always
  • Rosenmund reduction uses Pd/BaSO₄ poisoned with quinoline to prevent over-reduction to alcohol
  • Benzoin condensation uses KCN in alcohol — cyanide acts as nucleophilic catalyst (umpolung mechanism)
  • MPV reduction uses Al(OiPr)₃ in excess isopropanol — 2° alcohol selectively oxidized to ketone while aldehyde/ketone reduced to alcohol
  • Gattermann-Koch directly converts ArH → ArCHO using CO+HCl; Reimer-Tiemann converts phenol → o-hydroxybenzaldehyde

Formulas and relationships

Lucas reagent: conc. HCl + anhydrous ZnCl₂ — distinguishes 1°, 2°, 3° alcohols by turbidity rate
Tollens reagent: [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ — oxidises aldehydes to carboxylates, Ag⁺ reduced to Ag (mirror)
Fehling's solution: Cu²⁺ tartrate complex in alkaline medium — oxidises aliphatic aldehydes to carboxylates, Cu²⁺→Cu₂O (red ppt)
Iodoform test: I₂ + NaOH → generates NaOI in situ; reacts with CH₃CO- or CH₃CH(OH)- groups → yellow CHI₃ ppt
HCN addition: RCHO + HCN → RCH(OH)CN (cyanohydrin); then H₃O⁺ → RCH(OH)COOH (α-hydroxy acid)
Grignard + aldehyde: R'MgX + RCHO → RCH(OMgX)R' → H₃O⁺ → RCH(OH)R' (2° alcohol)
Grignard + ketone: R'MgX + R₂C=O → R₂C(OMgX)R' → H₃O⁺ → R₂R'C-OH (3° alcohol)
Clemmensen: R₂C=O + Zn(Hg)/conc.HCl → R₂CH₂ + H₂O
Wolff-Kishner: R₂C=O + NH₂NH₂/KOH, glycol, 180°C → R₂CH₂ + N₂ + H₂O
Aldol: 2 RCH₂CHO --(dil.NaOH)--> RCH₂CH(OH)CH(R)CHO --(heat)--> RCH₂CH=CH(R)CHO + H₂O
Cannizzaro: 2 RCHO (no α-H) + conc.NaOH → RCH₂OH + RCOONa
Hofmann bromamide: RCONH₂ + Br₂ + 4NaOH → RNH₂ + Na₂CO₃ + 2NaBr + 2H₂O (amine with one carbon less)
HVZ: RCH₂COOH + Br₂/P(red) → RCHBrCOOH (α-bromo acid)
Perkin: ArCHO + (CH₃CO)₂O/CH₃COONa, heat → ArCH=CHCOOH + CH₃COOH
Reimer-Tiemann: C₆H₅OH + CHCl₃ + NaOH → o-HOC₆H₄CHO + p-isomer + NaCl + H₂O
Kolbe-Schmitt: C₆H₅ONa + CO₂ (125°C, pressure) → o-HOC₆H₄COONa → H⁺ → o-HOC₆H₄COOH (salicylic acid)
Esterification: RCOOH + R'OH ⇌ RCOOR' + H₂O (H⁺ catalyst, equilibrium)
Saponification: RCOOR' + NaOH → RCOONa + R'OH (irreversible, goes to completion)
Etard: C₆H₅CH₃ + CrO₂Cl₂/CS₂ → [complex] → H₂O → C₆H₅CHO
Rosenmund: RCOCl + H₂/Pd-BaSO₄(quinoline) → RCHO + HCl
Gattermann-Koch: C₆H₆ + CO + HCl/AlCl₃-CuCl → C₆H₅CHO + HCl
Benzyne mechanism (Dow's): C₆H₅Cl + NaOH (623K, 300atm) → C₆H₅ONa → C₆H₅OH
