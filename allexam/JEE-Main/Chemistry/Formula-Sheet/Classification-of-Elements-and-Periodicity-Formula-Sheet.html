n + l rule (Madelung rule)

Symbols: n = principal quantum number, l = azimuthal quantum number

Unit: N/A

When to use: Used to determine the order of filling of orbitals; lower (n+l) value filled first; if equal, lower n filled first

Conditions: Applies for ground state electronic configurations of neutral atoms

Common mistake: Students often fill higher n first when n+l is same; correct rule is: same n+l → fill lower n first (e.g., 4s before 3d)

Effective nuclear charge (Slater's Rules): σ = Σ(s Screening constant values) → Z_eff = Z - σ

Symbols: Z = actual atomic number, σ = screening constant calculated from electron groups

Unit: No unit (dimensionless)

When to use: Calculates the net positive charge experienced by an electron in multi-electron atoms; explains periodic trends

Conditions: Electrons in same group contribute 0.35 each; electrons in (n-1) shell contribute 0.85; electrons in (n-2) or lower contribute 1.00

Common mistake: Forcing Slater's rules on d/f block electrons incorrectly; remember: for ns/np valence electrons, (n-1)d electrons contribute 1.00

Shielding/Screaning Constants (Slater's Rules Summary): For s/p electrons: same shell(e→0.35), (n-1)(→0.85), (n-2)and below(→1.00); For d/f electrons: same shell(e→0.35), all inner shells(→1.00)

Symbols: σ = total screening constant, Z_eff = Z - σ

Unit: No unit

When to use: Quick reference for Slater's rules application in JEE problems

Conditions: These coefficients are empirical approximations; most accurate for main group elements

Common mistake: Using wrong screening value for d-block elements; for 3d electrons, all electrons in n=1,2 contribute 1.00 and same 3d contributes 0.35

Ionic Radius Trend: Cation radius < Neutral atom radius < Anion radius (for isoelectronic series, higher Z → smaller radius)

Symbols: r_+ = cation radius, r_- = anion radius, r = neutral atomic radius

Unit: pm (picometer) or Å (Angstrom); 1 Å = 100 pm

When to use: Predicting size order of ions; isoelectronic species comparison

Conditions: Isoelectronic: more protons → stronger pull → smaller size

Common mistake: Thinking anions are smaller than cations; anions are always larger than their parent atoms due to increased electron-electron repulsion

Ionisation Enthalpy (IE): IE₁, IE₂, IE₃... successive values always increase: IE₁ < IE₂ < IE₃

Symbols: IE₁ = first ionisation enthalpy, IE₂ = second, etc.

Unit: kJ mol⁻¹ or eV atom⁻¹; 1 eV = 96.485 kJ mol⁻¹

When to use: Understanding difficulty of removing successive electrons; explains why +1, +2 oxidation states exist

Conditions: Gas phase isolated atom; value increases sharply after removal of all valence electrons

Common mistake: Assuming IE₂ ≈ 2×IE₁; actually IE₂ >> IE₁ because removing from a positively charged ion is much harder

Periodic Trend of IE: Increases across a period (left→right); Decreases down a group (top→bottom); Exceptions: Be > B, N > O, Mg > Al, Zn > Ga

Symbols: IE = Ionisation Enthalpy

Unit: kJ mol⁻¹

When to use: Predicting which element has higher/lower IE without memorising exact values

Conditions: Be(2s²) has full subshell → higher IE than B(2s²2p¹); N(2p³) has half-filled → higher IE than O(2p⁴)

Common mistake: Forgetting the exceptions; full/half-full subshells give extra stability, raising IE unexpectedly

Electron Gain Enthalpy (Δ_egH): Becomes more negative across a period; becomes less negative down a group; F has less negative Δ_egH than Cl due to small size and inter-electronic repulsion

Symbols: Δ_egH = Electron gain enthalpy

Unit: kJ mol⁻¹

When to use: Predicting tendency of atom to accept an electron; relates to reactivity of halogens

Conditions: More negative = more energy released = greater tendency to gain electron

Common mistake: Assuming F has highest electron affinity; actually Cl has more negative Δ_egH than F because F's small size causes repulsion

Electronegativity (Pauling Scale): χ = 0.17 + 0.036 × IE(eV) + 0.034 × |Δ_egH|(eV); Increases across period, decreases down group

Symbols: χ = electronegativity, IE = ionisation energy, Δ_egH = electron gain enthalpy

Unit: Pauling scale (dimensionless); F = 4.0 (highest)

When to use: Predicting bond polarity, ionic vs covalent character; determining direction of dipole

Conditions: Pauling scale is most commonly used; Mullikan and Allred-Rochow scales also exist

Common mistake: Confusing electronegativity with electron affinity; EN is bonded-atom property, EA is for isolated atom

Fajan's Rules (Polarising Power & Polarizability): Covalent character ∝ (Cation charge / Cation size) × (Anion size); Small highly charged cation + large anion → more covalent

Symbols: Polarising power ∝ charge/radius of cation; Polarizability ∝ size of anion

Unit: Qualitative rule, no numerical unit

When to use: Predicting ionic vs covalent nature of bonds; thermal stability of carbonates/nitrates

Conditions: Applies to compounds with significant covalent character; better for heavier elements

Common mistake: Forgetting that large anions are more polarizable; I⁻ > Br⁻ > Cl⁻ > F⁻ in polarizability

Lattice Energy (Born-Haber Cycle): U = (Z⁺·Z⁻·e²)/(4πε₀r₀) × (1 - 1/n); U ∝ (Product of ionic charges) / (Sum of ionic radii)

Symbols: U = lattice energy, Z⁺, Z⁻ = ionic charges, r₀ = interionic distance, n = Born exponent

Unit: kJ mol⁻¹

When to use: Comparing stability of ionic compounds; understanding solubility trends

Conditions: Simplified form; full Born-Lande equation includes Madelung constant

Common mistake: Assuming lattice energy depends only on charge; size of ions matters equally — MgO > NaCl despite both being 1:1 and 2:2

Valence Shell Electron Pair Repulsion (VSEPR) — Bond Angle Trends: Lone pair-lone pair > LP-BP > BP-BP repulsion; More lone pairs → smaller bond angle

Symbols: BP = bonding pair, LP = lone pair

Unit: Degrees (°)

When to use: Predicting molecular geometry and bond angles

Conditions: Applies to central atom with steric number 2–6

Common mistake: Forgetting that lone pairs compress bond angles; H₂O (104.5°) < NH₃ (107°) < CH₄ (109.5°) due to increasing LP

Hybridisation from Steric Number: SN = (Number of sigma bonds + Number of lone pairs on central atom); SN=2→sp, SN=3→sp², SN=4→sp³, SN=5→sp³d, SN=6→sp³d²

Symbols: SN = steric number (electron domain count)

Unit: N/A

When to use: Determining hybridisation and molecular shape quickly

Conditions: Only sigma bonds and lone pairs count; pi bonds do not contribute to SN

Common mistake: Counting pi bonds in steric number; double/triple bonds count as ONE sigma domain only

Metallic Character: Increases down a group, decreases across a period; Inversely related to IE and EN

Symbols: Metallic character = tendency to lose electrons

Unit: Qualitative

When to use: Predicting reactivity of metals with water/acid; basicity of oxides

Conditions: Best metallic: Cs/Fr; Best non-metal: F/O

Common mistake: Confusing metallic character with density or melting point; they follow different trends

Non-Metallic Character: Increases across period, decreases down group; Directly related to EN and IE; Inversely related to atomic size

Symbols: Non-metallic character = tendency to gain electrons

Unit: Qualitative

When to use: Predicting acidic/basic nature of oxides; oxidising power

Conditions: Most non-metallic: F; Least: Fr

Common mistake: Assuming noble gases are non-metals in reactivity context; they have very high IE but don't readily gain electrons

Amphoteric Oxide Trend: Metal oxides basic, Non-metal oxides acidic, Intermediate elements form amphoteric oxides; e.g., Al₂O₃, ZnO, SnO, PbO

Symbols: Amphoteric = reacts with both acids and bases

Unit: Qualitative

When to use: Identifying amphoteric behaviour in JEE questions

Conditions: Elements near metal-nonmetal border show amphoterism

Common mistake: Thinking all metal oxides are basic; Al, Zn, Sn, Pb oxides are amphoteric exceptions

Diagonal Relationship (Li↔Mg, Be↔Al, B↔Si): Similarities due to comparable ionic size and charge density; e.g., Li and Mg both form nitrides, both carbonates decompose on heating

Symbols: Diagonal relationship between elements of 2nd period and 3rd period of next group

Unit: N/A

When to use: Quick recall of anomalous similarities in s-block

Conditions: Caused by similar polarising power (charge/radius ratio)

Common mistake: Including Na-K or other pairs; only specific diagonal pairs show this

Atomic Number (Z) = Number of Protons = Number of Electrons (in neutral atom); Mass Number (A) = Z + N (N = neutrons)

Symbols: Z = atomic number, A = mass number, N = number of neutrons

Unit: N/A (whole numbers)

When to use: Fundamental identity of elements; used in all periodic table placement

Conditions: Basis of modern periodic law: properties are periodic function of Z

Common mistake: Using mass number instead of atomic number for position; isotopes have same Z but different A

Block Assignment from Electronic Configuration: s-block: ns¹⁻² (Groups 1,2); p-block: ns²np¹⁻⁶ (Groups 13–18); d-block: (n-1)d¹⁻¹⁰ns⁰⁻² (Groups 3–12); f-block: (n-2)f⁰⁻¹⁴(n-1)d⁰⁻²ns² (Lanthanoids & Actinoids)

Symbols: n = principal quantum number of valence shell

Unit: N/A

When to use: Quickly identifying block and group from configuration

Conditions: Lanthanoids: 4f¹⁻¹⁴; Actinoids: 5f¹⁻¹⁴

Common mistake: Placing He(1s²) in p-block; it is technically s-block but placed in group 18 (noble gases) by convention

Group Number from Valence Configuration: s-block Group = 10 + ns electrons (Group 1,2); p-block Group = 10 + ns + np electrons (Groups 13–18); d-block Group = (n-1)d + ns electrons (Groups 3–12, with exceptions)

Symbols: ns, np, (n-1)d = electrons in valence/sub-valence shells

Unit: N/A

When to use: Finding group number directly from electronic configuration

Conditions: For d-block: if (n-1)d⁵ns¹ or (n-1)d¹⁰ns¹, group = sum of d+ns electrons

Common mistake: For Cr(3d⁵4s¹): group = 6, not 5 or 7; sum d+se electrons

Noble Gas Configuration Stability: ns²np⁶ (octet) is most stable; Half-filled: ns²np³, nd⁵, nf⁷ give extra stability → anomalies in IE and configurations

Symbols: Octet = 8 valence electrons; Half-filled subshells have symmetrical distribution

Unit: Qualitative concept

When to use: Explaining Cr(3d⁵4s¹) not Cr(3d⁴4s²), Cu(3d¹⁰4s¹) not Cu(3d⁹4s²)

Conditions: Half/full filled subshells have extra exchange energy stabilization

Common mistake: Applying half-filled stability to p-block; N(2p³) half-filled is stable but O(2p⁴) not; don't overgeneralise

Oxidation State from Group Number: For main group: Max OS = Group number (for metals/non-metals); Min OS = Group number - 18 (for p-block non-metals); e.g., S in Group 16: +6, -2

Symbols: OS = Oxidation State

Unit: Signed integer

When to use: Predicting possible oxidation states of elements

Conditions: s-block: fixed +1, +2; p-block: variable; d-block: variable, usually +2, +3

Common mistake: Assigning -2 to oxygen in OF₂; F is more EN so O gets +2 here
