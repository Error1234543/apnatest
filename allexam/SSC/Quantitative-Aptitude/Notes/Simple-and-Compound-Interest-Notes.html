Simple and Compound Interest — Complete Notes (SSC Quantitative Aptitude)

SSCHindiNotes

1. Basic Definitions and Core Concept

Simple Interest (SI) और Compound Interest (CI) दोनों ही 'वृद्धि' (growth) से संबंधित अवधारणाएँ हैं। यहाँ कुछ मौलिक शब्दावली को समझना अत्यावश्यक है:

• Principal (P, मूलधन): वह राशि जो आपको ऋण के रूप में दी जाती है या आप किसी निवेश में लगाते हैं। इस पर ब्याज की गणना की जाती है।

• Interest (I, ब्याज):principal पर लगाया गया वह खर्च या लाभ है, जो एक निश्चित समय के लिए लिया/दिया जाता है।

• Amount (A, राशि): Principal + Interest का योग। A = P + I.

• Rate of Interest (R, वार्षिक ब्याज दर): प्रति वर्ष मूलधन पर लगने वाला ब्याज, आमतौर पर प्रतिशत (% में) में दिया जाता है।

• Time (T, काल): जो समय (आमतौर पर वर्षों या माहों में) ब्याज की गणना के लिए लिया गया हो।

Simple Interest में ब्याज हमेशा मूल Principal (P) पर ही लगता है। हर साल ब्याज समान रहता है क्योंकि मूलधन नहीं बदलता।

Compound Interest में हर compounding period के बाद ब्याज को मूलधन में जोड़ दिया जाता है, जिससे अगले अवधि के लिए ब्याज उस नए (बढ़े हुए) principal पर लगता है। इसे 'ब्याज पर ब्याज' (interest on interest) कहा जाता है।

SSC (CGL/CHSL/MTS/GO) में SI और CI के बीच का अंतर, दोनों के सूत्र और उनके उपयोग सीधा तथा बहुत महत्वपूर्ण है। प्रश्नों की कठिनाई मुख्यतः मध्यम स्तर की होती है, लेकिन समय की दृष्टि से तेज़ी आवश्यक है।

2. Simple Interest — Formula, Derivation and Variations

Simple Interest (साधारण ब्याज) का सूत्र:

SI = (P × R × T) / 100

जहाँ P = Principal (₹), R = वार्षिक ब्याज दर (%), T = समय (वर्ष में)

Amount: A = P + SI = P + (P×R×T)/100 = P(1 + RT/100)

Derivation का सरल तर्क: यदि ₹100 की दर 5% प्रतिवर्ष है, तो 1 वर्ष पर ब्याज = ₹5 होगा। इसलिए 1 वर्ष पर किसी भी राशि P पर ब्याज = (P×5)/100। T वर्षों में = (P×R×T)/100।

यदि T माहों में हो तो T को वर्ष में बदलें: T_years = T_months / 12। अतः SI = (P × R × T_months) / (100 × 12)।

यदि R को वार्षिक दर न देकर मासिक/दैनिक/quarterly में दिया हो, तो भी T को उसी इकाई में रखें। उदाहरण: यदि दर मासिक 1% है तो R=1 और T=कुल माह संख्या, तब SI = P×R×T/100 ही लगेगा बशर्ते R और T समान इकाई में हों。

Common variations tested in SSC:
• जब P, R, T और SI सभी ज्ञात हों — सीधे सूत्र में रखें।
• जब SI, P और T दिए जाएँ → R = (100×SI)/(P×T)
• जब SI, R और T दिए जाएँ → P = (100×SI)/(R×T)
• जब SI, P और R दिए जाएँ → T = (100×SI)/(P×R)
• A का मान दिया हो → पहले SI = A − P निकालें।

उदाहरण 1: ₹5000 पर 8% वार्षिक दर से 3 वर्षों का SI और Amount ज्ञात करें।
SI = (5000 × 8 × 3)/100 = ₹1200
Amount = 5000 + 1200 = ₹6200。

उदाहरण 2: ₹7500 पर 10% वार्षिक दर से कितने वर्षों में amount ₹9000 होगी?
SI = 9000 − 7500 = ₹1500
T = (100×1500)/(7500×10) = (150000)/75000 = 2 वर्ष।

3. Compound Interest — Formula, Derivation and Compounding Frequencies

Compound Interest (चक्रवृद्धि ब्याज) का सामान्य सूत्र:

A = P × (1 + R/100)^T

CI = A − P = P × [(1 + R/100)^T − 1]

यहाँ R = वार्षिक ब्याज दर (%), T = वर्षों की संख्या (जो integer होनी चाहिए जब तक compounding frequency बदल न हो)。

Derivation: अगर compounding वार्षिक है, तो 1 वर्ष के बाद A₁ = P(1+R/100); 2 वर्षों के बाद A₂ = P(1+R/100)²; T वर्षों के बाद A_T = P(1+R/100)^T。

When compounding frequency varies (SSC में यह अक्सर पूछा जाता है):

• Half-yearly: Rate becomes R/2 %, Time becomes 2T periods
Formula: A = P × (1 + (R/2)/100)^(2T)

• Quarterly: Rate becomes R/4 %, Time becomes 4T periods
Formula: A = P × (1 + (R/4)/100)^(4T)

• Every N-th month: Adjust rate and time accordingly。

Special Cases (when T is given in years but with different compounding periods):

• Si a particular year has different rate: A = P × (1 + r₁/100) × (1 + r₂/100) × ...
Example: 2 years, first year rate = 8%, second year rate = 10%: A = P × (1.08)(1.10)。

• Continuous compounding (not commonly asked but possible): A = P × e^(RT/100)。SSC में rarely asks this; stick to annual/half-yearly/quarterly।

Example 1: ₹10,000 on CI at 10% p.a. compounded annually for 2 years।
A = 10000 × (1 + 10/100)² = 10000 × (1.1)² = 10000 × 1.21 = ₹12,100
CI = 12100 − 10000 = ₹2100。

Example 2: Same principal, same rate compounded half-yearly for 1 year।
Half-yearly: rate = 10/2 = 5%; periods = 2
A = 10000 × (1 + 5/100)² = 10000 × (1.05)² = 10000 × 1.1025 = ₹11,025
CI = ₹1025。

Notice CI > SI when compounding more frequently (since effectively more periods)。

Example 3: ₹8000 at 5% compounded annually for 3 years。
A = 8000 × (1.05)³ = 8000 × 1.157625 = ₹9261
CI = ₹1261。

4. Difference between SI and CI — Patterns and Formulas

One of the most frequently tested area in SSC is the difference between SI and CI। यह अंतर विशेषतः निम्नलिखित situations में महत्वपूर्ण है:

1. Difference after 1 year:
Difference = CI₁yr − SI₁yr = 0
क्योंकि 1 year में compounding का कोई effect नहीं पड़ता।

2. Difference after 2 years:
Difference after 2 years = P(R/100)²
यह एक बहुत useful shortcut formula है।
Proof:
SI₂ = P × R × 2 / 100 = 2PR/100
CI₂ = P[(1 + R/100)² − 1] = P[1 + 2R/100 + (R/100)² − 1] = P[2R/100 + (R/100)²]
Difference = CI₂ − SI₂ = P × (R/100)²

Example: P = ₹5000, R = 10%, T = 2 years
Difference = 5000 × (10/100)² = 5000 × 0.01 = ₹50
Check: SI = 5000×10×2/100 = 1000; CI = 5000[(1.1)²−1] = 5000×0.21 = 1050; Difference = 50 ✓。

3. Difference after 3 years:
Difference = P × (R/100)² × (3 + R/100)
Proof sketch: CI₃ = P[(1+R/100)³−1]; SI₃ = 3PR/100
Expand (1+r)³ = 1 + 3r + 3r² + r³; subtract 1; difference from SI (3r) gives 3r² + r³ = r²(3 + r)
where r = R/100。

Example: P = ₹4000, R = 5%, T = 3 years
Diff = 4000 × (0.05)² × (3 + 0.05) = 4000 × 0.0025 × 3.05 = 10 × 3.05 = ₹30.50

General Principle: With increasing T, difference increases because CI accumulates interest on previously accumulated interest。

4. Finding P, R, or T given the difference:
If difference after 2 years = D₂ → P = D₂ × (100/R)²
If difference after 3 years = D₃ → P = D₃ / [ (R/100)² × (3 + R/100) ]

This is a powerful SSC shortcut।

5. Installments and Related Problems

Installment problems involve paying back a loan in equal parts at regular intervals, where each installment includes both principal and interest paid up to that point。

Key Concept: The total amount due at the end of the period must equal the sum of all installments calculated with appropriate interest。

For equal annual installments on a loan of P at R% p.a. SI:
Each installment = X; Total amount due at end of T years = Sum of all installments adjusted for time。

Shortcut approach — let's work through the standard formula:

If loan = P and it is repaid in n equal annual installments of amount 'a' each:
a = P × R / [100 × (n − (n(n−1)/2)×R/100)]

But this can get complex; simpler method for SSC is:
Total payment = n × a
Interest saved by paying earlier installments must equal interest accrued on remaining balance।

Example 1: A loan of ₹1000 is to be paid back in 2 equal annual installments at 10% SI per annum। Find each installment。

Method: Let each installment = a
First installment is paid at end of year 1; it saves interest on remaining (1000 − a) for 1 year: Interest saved = (1000−a)×0.10
Second installment paid at end of year 2; no further saving。
Total principal repaid = 2a; but original loan was 1000, and interest on 1000 for 2 years = 200, so total amount due = 1200。
But paying early reduces total: Actually, correct approach uses present value concept: 1000 = a/(1+0.10) + a/(1+0.20) ... This gets complicated。

Better approach for SSC level:
Use: a = [P × 100 × (100 + nR)] / [100n + n(n−1)R/2]
For above: a = [1000 × 100 × (100 + 2×10)] / [100×2 + 2×1×10/2] = [1000×100×120] / [200 + 10] = 12,000,000/210 ≈ 5714.29…

Actually the cleanest exam-friendly formula is:
a = P / [n − n(n−1)R/(2×100)]
Substituting: a = 1000 / [2 − 2×1×10/(200)] = 1000 / [2 − 0.1] = 1000/1.9 ≈ ₹526.32 — this is wrong。

Let's verify with the most reliable standard result:
a = [100×P] / [100n + n(n−1)R/2]
= 100000 / [200 + 10] = 100000/210 ≈ ₹476.19
Check: 2×476.19 = 952.38 — close to 1000 but interest must be accounted।

The right conceptual formula for equal SI installments:
a = P / [n − n(n−1)r/2] where r = R/100
= 1000 / [2 − 2×1×0.10/2] = 1000 / [2 − 0.10] = 1000/1.9 = ₹526.32

Each installment ≈ ₹526.32。

Example 2 (CI installment): Loan of ₹1210 to be paid in 2 equal annual installments at 10% CI। Each installment a:
1210 = a/(1.10) + a/(1.10)² = a[1/1.10 + 1/1.21] = a[0.9091 + 0.8264] = a×1.7355
a = 1210/1.7355 ≈ ₹697.20

Installment problems require careful distinction between SI and CI contexts。**

6. Practical Applications and Word Problem Types in SSC

SSC में Simple/Compound Interest के प्रश्न निम्नलिखित pattern में आते हैं:

Type 1 — Direct Calculation (Most Common):
Given P, R, T; find SI, CI, Amount, or Difference。
Strategy: Apply direct formula। If T is fractional (e.g., 1.5 years), calculate SI for full years then apply proportional interest for fraction, or use compound formula (1+R/100)^T using logarithms if needed—but SSC usually avoids fractional T for CI without simplification।

Type 2 — Finding Missing Variable:
Given any three of P, R, T, I (SI or CI), find the fourth। Use algebraic rearrangement of formulas।
Example: SI = ₹800 on P for 2 years at 8%; find P → P = 100×800/(8×2) = 5000。

Type 3 — Ratio Problems:
If amount doubles in x years at SI, find time to become 4 times। Since doubling means SI = P, so P×R×x/100 = P → Rx/100 = 1। For 4× amount, SI needs to be 3P → T = 3x years。
For CI: if amount doubles in n years, then to become 8 times = 2³ → requires 3n years (because (1+R/100)^(3n) = [(1+R/100)^n]³ = 2³ = 8)。

Type 4 — Comparing Two Schemes:
Two sums invested at different rates/times; compare interests or find break-even point。
Example: Which is better—₹10000 at 8% SI for 3 years or at 7.5% CI for 3 years? Calculate both directly。

Type 5 — Population/Growth Problems Mapped to CI:
Population growth, depreciation of machines, etc., are treated as CI problems।
Population after n years: P(1 + R/100)^n
Depreciation after n years: P(1 − R/100)^n
These are conceptually identical to CI/SI।

Type 6 — Banker's Discount vs True Discount (Advanced but occasionally appears):
Banker's Discount = face value × rate × time; True Discount = (Face Value × Rate × Time)/(100 + Rate × Time)。Banker's Gain = BD − TD = (TD × Rate × Time)/100। SSC sometimes tests this relation。

Each type requires clear identification of which formula applies before calculation。

7. Shortcuts, Tricks and Speed Techniques for SSC

Speed and accuracy are critical in SSC exams। Here are proven shortcuts:

1. Ratio Method for SI:
If P and R are constant, SI is directly proportional to T। If T doubles, SI doubles। If T is tripled, SI triples।
Useful for quick comparison questions。

2. CI Ratio Method:
For CI, if amount after n years is A, then amount after 2n years = A²/P (approximately for small R)। More precisely, amount after kn years = P×(1+R/100)^(kn) = [P×(1+R/100)^n]^k / P^(k−1)。
For simple mental math: if money doubles in 5 years at CI, it becomes 4 times in 10 years, 8 times in 15 years。

3. Rule of 72 (Approximation):
At CI, doubling time ≈ 72/R (%)。At R = 8%, doubling time ≈ 9 years। Useful for estimation only。

4. Quick SI Calculation Using 1% Base:
1% of P = P/100। Then SI for R% for T years = T × R × (P/100)। Do P/100 mentally first, then multiply。

5. Difference Shortcut Recap:
After 2 years: Diff = P(R/100)²। After 3 years: Diff = P(R/100)²(3 + R/100)。
Memorize these to save calculation time。

6. Fraction-to-Percentage Conversion for CI:
Convert R/100 to fraction. E.g., 5% = 1/20, 10% = 1/10, 12.5% = 1/8, 16⅔% = 1/6, 20% = 1/5, 25% = 1/4, 33⅓% = 1/3, 50% = 1/2।
Then (1 + 1/n)^T is easy to compute mentally। E.g., 10% for 2 years: (1 + 1/10)² = 1.21; multiply by P to get Amount。

7. Cross-Multiply Elimination:
In multiple-choice questions, plug each option back to verify which satisfies the condition։ This is faster than solving from scratch when options are given।

8. Unit Digit Check:
For large calculations, check unit digits to eliminate wrong options quickly।

8. Common Mistakes and Confusion Points

Students frequently make these errors in SI/CI problems। Avoid them:

1. Confusing SI and CI formulas:
Always write down which formula you are using before plugging values। Mixing them up gives entirely wrong answers。

2. Forgetting to convert time units:
If rate is annual but time is given in months, always convert T to years (divide by 12) before applying formula। Failure to do so inflates SI/CI by factor of 12。 Similarly, if compounding is half-yearly, halve the rate AND double the time period。

3. Compounding frequency error:
Half-yearly does NOT mean divide time only; both rate AND periods change। Quarter: divide rate by 4, multiply periods by 4। This is the #1 mistake in SSC。

4. Using SI formula for CI problems and vice versa:
Read carefully: if question says "compounded annually"/"half-yearly"/"quarterly", it is definitely CI। If it says simply "at R% per annum" without mentioning compounding, assume SI (unless context suggests otherwise)。

5. Wrong application of Difference formula:
The shortcut Diff = P(R/100)² is ONLY valid for 2 years。 For 3 years, use the 3-year formula। Using 2-year formula for 3 years is a major error。

6. Arithmetic mistakes with fractions:
When converting percentage to decimal or vice versa, keep precision。 E.g., 12.5% = 0.125, not 0.12 or 0.13。 Small rounding errors accumulate。

7. Assuming doubling time is linear for CI:
Doubling time at CI is NOT simply proportional。 If it doubles in n years, it does NOT quadruple in 2n years—it actually quadruples in about 2n years only for small R as approximation, but exact answer requires (1+r)^(2n) = 4।

8. Ignoring unit consistency:
Always ensure P, R, T units match the formula requirements। This is the root cause of most calculation errors。

9. Misreading "amount becomes X times" vs "amount increases BY X times":
"Doubles" means becomes 2P (increase = P)。 "Increases by 3 times" means becomes 4P (increase = 3P)। Careful reading prevents wrong answers。

9. Worked Examples with Full Solutions

Example 1: Find the CI on ₹6400 at 10% p.a. compounded semi-annually for 1½ years।
Rate per half-year = 10/2 = 5%; number of periods = 1.5 × 2 = 3。
A = 6400 × (1 + 5/100)³ = 6400 × (1.05)³
(1.05)³ = 1.157625
A = 6400 × 1.157625 = ₹7408.80
CI = 7408.80 − 6400 = ₹1008.80。

Example 2: The difference between CI and SI on a certain sum for 2 years at 5% p.a. is ₹10। Find the sum。「
Using shortcut: Diff = P(R/100)² → 10 = P × (0.05)² = P × 0.0025
P = 10 / 0.0025 = ₹4000。
Verification: SI = 4000×5×2/100 = 400; CI = 4000[(1.05)²−1] = 4000×0.1025 = 410; Diff = 10 ✓。

Example 3: A sum amounts to ₹1338 after 3 years and ₹2007 after 6 years on CI। Find the principal。「
A₃ = P(1+r)³ = 1338
A₆ = P(1+r)⁶ = 2007
Divide: A₆/A₃ = (1+r)³ = 2007/1338 = 1.5
So (1+r)³ = 1.5
Therefore P = 1338 / 1.5 = ₹892。「

Example 4: At what rate percent per annum will ₹2000 amount to ₹2315 in 3 years at CI?「
A = P(1+r)^T → 2315 = 2000(1+r)³
(1+r)³ = 2315/2000 = 1.1575
Try r = 5%: (1.05)³ = 1.157625 ≈ 1.1575 → So R ≈ 5%。「

Example 5: In how many years will a sum double at 10% CI?「
2P = P(1.10)^T → (1.10)^T = 2
Try T = 7: 1.10⁷ ≈ 1.9487; T = 8: 1.10⁸ ≈ 2.1436
So between 7 and 8 years (approx 7.3 years)。「

Example 6: A person borrows ₹5000 for 2 years at 4% p.a. SI. He lends this amount to another person at 5% p.a. CI compounded annually for the same 2 years। Find his gain。「
Gain = CI earned − SI paid
SI paid = 5000×4×2/100 = ₹400
CI earned = 5000[(1.05)²−1] = 5000×0.1025 = ₹512.50
Gain = 512.50 − 400 = ₹112.50。「

10. Exam Weightage and Preparation Strategy for SSC

SSC exam pattern note: Exact marks, duration, and question counts vary by post (CGL/CHSL/MTS/JE/etc.) and are subject to official notification changes। The information below reflects general trend based on historically available patterns and should be verified against the latest official notification for your specific exam。

Weightage:
• Simple and Compound Interest typically carries 3–6 questions in Tier 1 Quantitative Aptitude section (out of 25 quant questions)。「
• Questions range from direct formula application to moderate word problems。「
• CI with half-yearly/quarterly compounding and SI/CI difference problems are high-yield topics。「

Preparation strategy:
1. Memorize the core formulas: SI = PRT/100; A = P(1+R/100)^T; Difference after 2 yrs = P(R/100)²。「
2. Practice at least 30–40 varied problems covering all types。「
3. Learn to identify compounding frequency within seconds。「
4. Use fraction equivalents for common percentages to speed up CI calculations。「
5. Solve previous year questions (or PYQ-style practice) to understand the actual difficulty level。「
6. Work on calculation speed—use approximation and option elimination techniques。「

Important reminders:
• Always write down known values before substituting。「
• Double-check unit conversions (months→years, half-yearly rate→correct periods)。「
• For CI, prefer calculating A first, then subtracting P to get CI。「
• Keep a formula sheet and revise daily。「

Important facts

  • SI = (P × R × T) / 100; A = P + SI
  • CI Amount: A = P(1 + R/100)^T; CI = A − P
  • Half-yearly compounding: use R/2 as rate and 2T as periods
  • Quarterly compounding: use R/4 as rate and 4T as periods
  • Difference between CI and SI after 2 years = P(R/100)²
  • Difference between CI and SI after 3 years = P(R/100)²(3 + R/100)
  • At SI, time to double is directly proportional; at CI, doubling time approximates 72/R years
  • For CI, if amount doubles in n years, it becomes 4 times in approximately 2n years and 8 times in approximately 3n years
  • Population growth follows CI formula: P(1+R/100)^n; depreciation: P(1−R/100)^n
  • Always ensure time is in years matching the rate frequency; convert months/days accordingly

Formulas and relationships

SI = (P × R × T) / 100
A (SI) = P + SI = P(1 + RT/100)
A (CI, annual) = P(1 + R/100)^T
CI = A − P = P[(1 + R/100)^T − 1]
CI (half-yearly) = P[(1 + R/200)^(2T)] − P
CI (quarterly) = P[(1 + R/400)^(4T)] − P
Difference after 2 years = P × (R/100)²
Difference after 3 years = P × (R/100)² × (3 + R/100)
P = (100 × SI) / (R × T); R = (100 × SI) / (P × T); T = (100 × SI) / (P × R)
Population after n years = P(1 + R/100)^n; Depreciated value = P(1 − R/100)^n
Banker's Discount = Face Value × R × T / 100; True Discount = (BD × 100) / (100 + RT)

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting to halve the rate AND double the periods for half-yearly compounding (only doing one conversion)
  • Using SI formula when question mentions compound interest or vice versa
  • Applying the 2-year difference shortcut P(R/100)² for 3-year problems
  • Not converting months to years when rate is annual ( multiplying by months directly gives 12× overestimated interest)
  • Confusing 'amount becomes X times' with 'amount increases BY X times'
  • Arithmetic errors when squaring or cubing (1+R/100) without calculator
  • Treating fractional time periods for CI without adjusting compounding frequency correctly
  • Mixing up Principal in SI installment problems with Amount
  • Assuming linear doubling at CI (doubling time is not exactly double for quadrupling)
  • Ignoring that SI and CI are equal only for T = 1 year; they diverge for T > 1
