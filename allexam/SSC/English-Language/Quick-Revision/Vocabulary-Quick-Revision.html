Important facts

  • SSC (CGL/CHSL/MTS) English Vocabulary section contains 5–10 questions per paper. Key areas: Synonyms, Antonyms, One-Word Substitution, Idioms & Phrases, Spelling, and Confused Words. — High — vocabulary forms ~10% of the total English section
  • One-Word Substitution is the most consistent topic in SSC exams. Questions like 'Person who loves mankind', 'Study of population', 'Remedy for all diseases' appear in almost every SSC exam. — Very High — almost guaranteed 2-3 questions
  • Idioms in SSC are usually from everyday usage. Very literary or rare idioms are avoided. Focus on common idioms involving body parts, animals, colors, natural phenomena. — High — 2-4 idioms typically asked
  • Antonyms/Synonyms are mostly simple words. Rarely obscure words are asked. The pattern is direct — given one word, choose the correct synonym/antonym from four options. — Moderate — 2-3 questions each
  • Homophones appear in Sentence Improvement and Spotting Errors sections rather than Vocabulary specifically. However, knowing homophones helps in both Vocabulary and Grammar sections. — Moderate — indirectly tested
  • Spelling mistakes in SSC often involve double letters (accommodate, embarrassed, necessary) and i-before-e rules. — Moderate — 1-2 questions per exam
  • For One-Word Substitution, Latin/Greek roots help: 'phil-' means love (philanthropist), 'mis-' means hate (misanthrope), 'demo-' means people (demography). — Useful trick for quick elimination
  • SSC does not ask extremely rare idioms. Focus on idioms that appear in everyday newspapers and common usage rather than Shakespearean or archaic phrases. — Strategy insight

Memory aids

  • For One-Word Substitution: Remember Latin prefixes — Philo (love): philanthropist, philologist, philosopher; Mis (hate): misanthrope, misogynist; Demo (people): democracy, demography; Chron (time): chronology, chronic; Geo (earth): geography, geology.
  • For Homophones: Use context sentences. Accept = take in; Except = exclude. Principal = headmaster/principal rule; Principle = fundamental truth.
  • For Spelling: Remember 'I before E except after C' — receive, perceive, ceiling; but except, species, counterfeit are exceptions.
  • For Idioms: Group idioms by theme — animals (kick the bucket = die; bird in hand; fish out of water), body parts (cold shoulder, bend ear, blood is thicker), colors (blue monday, green with envy, pink slip).
  • For Synonyms/Antonyms: Learn word families. Create: creation, creative, creator, recreate. This helps identify related words quickly.

Exam tips

  • For One-Word Substitution: Read the definition carefully. Sometimes two options seem correct — choose the MOST accurate one based on the exact wording of the definition.
  • For Idioms: If you know the literal meaning doesn't match any option, eliminate those options immediately. Idiom meanings are almost never literal.
  • For Antonyms: If you know the word's meaning but not its antonym, try to think of the opposite concept. If that fails, eliminate obviously wrong options first.
  • For Spelling: When unsure, try writing the word aloud slowly and listen for awkward sounds. Many misspellings sound wrong when spoken.
  • Time management: Vocabulary questions should take 30–45 seconds each. Don't spend more than 1 minute on any single vocabulary question.
  • Practice previous years' vocabulary lists specifically. SSC repeats many words across different exam sessions. A list of top 100 repeated vocabulary words covers ~60% of exam vocabulary.
  • For Confused Words: Make a personal table of commonly confused pairs and review it daily for 10 minutes. This is the fastest way to improve accuracy.

Common traps

  • Confusing 'affect' (verb) with 'effect' (noun). This is the most commonly confused pair in SSC English. Trick: Affect is Action (verb), Effect is End result (noun).
  • Writing 'definitely' as 'definately'. This is one of the most common spelling errors tested in SSC.
  • Confusing 'its' (possessive) with 'it's' (it is). SSC frequently tests this distinction.
  • Thinking 'stationary' means stationery (paper/stationery items). Memory aid: Stationary = stands still (like a train at a station); Stationery = things you find at a stationery shop.
  • Choosing a synonym that is merely related but not truly synonymous. SSC tests precise meanings, not loose associations.
  • Assuming all idioms make logical sense literally. Many idioms have historical or cultural origins unrelated to their literal meaning.
