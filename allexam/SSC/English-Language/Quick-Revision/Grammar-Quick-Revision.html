SSC English - Grammar Quick Revision (Hindi Medium)

SSCHindiQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Tense = वाक्य के समय के अनुसार क्रिया का रूप। तीन मुख्य काल: Present, Past, Future।
  • Subject-Verb Agreement = कर्ता (Subject) और क्रिया (Verb) में सङ्ख्या और पुरुष का मिलान होना चाहिए। Singular subject के साथ singular verb और plural subject के साथ plural verb आता है।
  • Article = संज्ञा से पहले आने वाला शब्द जो बताता है कि संज्ञा किसी विशिष्ट वस्तु की ओर इशारा कर रही है या सामान्य। तीन प्रकार: a, an, the।
  • Preposition = वह शब्द जो संज्ञा/सर्वनाम से पहले आकर उसके स्थान, समय, दिशा आदि को दर्शाता है। जैसे: in, on, at, by, from, to, between, among।
  • Conjunction = दो शब्दों, वाक्यों या वाक्य अंशों को जोड़ने वाला शब्द। जैसे: and, but, or, so, because, although, if, though।
  • Active Voice = जब कर्ता स्वयं क्रिया करता हो। Pass. Voice = जब कर्ता पर क्रिया प्रभाव डाले (क्रिया कर्ता द्वारा नहीं, बल्कि उसे करने वाले द्वारा की जाए)।
  • Direct Speech = बोले गए शब्दों का शब्द:शः उद्धरण। Indirect/Narrated Speech = बोले गए शब्दों का अर्थ बताते हुए वर्णन।
  • Noun = व्यक्ति, वस्तु, स्थान, भाव या विचार का नाम। Types: Proper, Common, Collective, Material, Abstract।
  • Pronoun = Noun के बदले प्रयुक्त शब्द। Types: Personal, Reflexive, Relative, Interrogative, Indefinite।
  • Modal Verb = वह सहायक क्रिया जो ability, possibility, permission, necessity, obligation आदि व्यक्त करे। जैसे: can, could, may, might, will, would, shall, should, must, ought to।
  • Conditionals = such sentences which express a condition and its result. Four types: Type 1 (Real/Possible), Type 2 (Unreal/Present), Type 3 (Unreal/Past), Mixed۔
  • Non-finite Verbs = वे verb forms जो tense, person, number से स्वतन्त्र होते हैं: Infinitive (to + V1), Gerund (V+ing as noun), Participle (V2/V3 present/past participle)।

Key concepts

  • Tenses — Rules & Forms: Twelve tenses total (4 each: Present, Past, Future). Key patterns:
    • Present Indefinite: S1+V1(s/es)+O → habitual/general truth. Example: He plays cricket.
    • Present Continuous: S1+is/am/are+V1+ing+O → happening now. Example: She is reading.
    • Present Perfect: S1+has/have+V3+O → completed action with present relevance. Example: I have finished.
    • Present Perfect Continuous: S1+has/have+been+V1+ing+since/for → started past, continues now. Example: He has been working since morning.
    • Past Indefinite: S2+V2+O → completed past action. Example: She went there.
    • Past Continuous: S2+was/were+V1+ing+O → ongoing past action. Example: They were playing.
    • Past Perfect: S2+had+V3+O → action completed before another past action. Example: I had eaten before he came.
    • Past Perfect Continuous: S2+had+been+V1+ing → ongoing before another past action.
    • Future tenses use will/shall + base form or be going to.
    • Stative verbs (know, believe, like, hate, belong, seem) are NOT used in continuous forms.
  • Subject-Verb Agreement — Core Rules: Rules essential for SSC:
    1. Singular subject → singular verb; Plural subject → plural verb.
    2. 'Each/every/no' + singular noun → singular verb. Example: Each student has a book.
    3. 'Either...either or, Neither...nor, Not only...but also' → verb agrees with nearest subject. Example: Neither the boys nor the girl is coming.
    4. Subjects joined by 'and' → plural verb. Exception: when both refer to same thing/person (Bread and butter is my breakfast.).
    5. 'One of the + plural noun + relative clause' → singular verb. Example: One of the boys is missing.
    6. Collective nouns (team, jury, committee) → singular when acting as unit, plural when members act individually.
    7. Distance, time, money, weight as single amount → singular verb. Example: Ten rupees is enough.
    8. 'There is/are' → verb agrees with following subject. Example: There is a cat. / There are cats.
    9. Indefinite pronouns (everybody, everyone, somebody, anyone, nobody, none) → singular verb.
    10. Many a + singular noun → singular verb. Example: Many a man has tried.
  • Articles — Usage Rules: Definite article 'the' and indefinite articles 'a/an'.
    • 'A' before consonant sound; 'an' before vowel sound. Note: 'a university' (y-sound), 'an hour' (h silent).
    • Generic singular countable nouns need an article: I saw a dog. (not 'I saw dog.')
    • No article before plural/general uncountable nouns: Dogs are loyal. / Water is essential.
    • 'The' for specific/reference: The Ganges flows north to south.
    • 'The' before superlatives and ordinals: the best, the first.
    • 'The' before musical instruments: play the piano.
    • No article before names of persons, most countries, lakes, mountains (singular): India, Mumbai, Ram.
    • 'The' before rivers, seas, deserts, mountain ranges, plural countries: the Himalayas, the Amazon.
    • No article before meals, seasons, months, days, languages: breakfast, summer, Hindi.
    • 'The' before adjectives representing a class: the poor, the rich, the blind.
    • Fixed phrases without article: at home, in hospital, go to school/church/prison, by heart, make use of.
  • Prepositions — High-Yield Patterns: SSC frequently tests preposition collocations:
    • Respect: in respect of, with respect to
    • Depend: depend on/upon
    • Insist: insist on
    • Aim: aim at
    • Apply: apply for (job), apply to (person/institution)
    • Accuse: accuse of
    • Charge: charge with
    • Punish: punish for
    • Thank: thank for
    • Apologize: apologize for/to
    • Suspect: suspect of
    • Capable: capable of
    • Famous: famous for
    • Angry: angry at (person)/about (thing)
    • Afraid: afraid of
    • Good/Bad at (subject/skill), good/bad for (health)
    • Marry: marry (no preposition); be married to
    • Listen: listen to
    • Wait: wait for
    • Agree: agree with (person), agree to (proposal), agree on (date/place)
    • Belong: belong to
    • Compare: compare with (similar), compare to (dissimilar metaphorically)
    • Different: different from/than
    • Proud: proud of
    • Fear: fear of
    • Instead: instead of
    • Except: except for/unless
    • Beside vs. Besides: beside = next to; besides = in addition to
    • Among (3+) vs. Between (2)
    • In (enclosed), On (surface), At (point)
  • Conjunctions — Common Errors: Important conjunction rules:
    • 'Although/Though' cannot be used with 'but' in same sentence. Use either/or.
    • 'Because' cannot be used with 'so'. Example: Because it rained, we stayed home. (NOT: So we stayed home.)
    • 'Unless' = except if; usually followed by positive verb. Unless you work hard, you will fail.
    • 'However' = but (used as adverb); punctuation matters.
    • Correlative pairs: both...and, either...or, neither...nor, not only...but also, whether...or.
    • 'So that' shows purpose/result; 'such that' shows degree.
    • 'As soon as' vs 'the moment': same meaning, both indicate immediate sequence.
    • 'Hardly...when' and 'No sooner...than' — inversion required.
    Hardly had he entered when the phone rang.
    No sooner had he reached home than it started raining.
    • 'Lest...should' — formal usage: He walked softly lest he should be heard.
  • Active & Passive Voice — Conversion Rules: General rules for voice change:
    1. Object of active → Subject of passive.
    2. Main verb changes to appropriate form of 'be' + V3.
    3. Subject of active becomes agent with 'by' (optional if unknown/unimportant).
    4. Tense remains same — be verb carries tense.
    Present Simple: am/is/are + V3 | Present Continuous: am/is/are being + V3 | Present Perfect: has/have been + V3
    Past Simple: was/were + V3 | Past Continuous: was/were being + V3 | Past Perfect: had been + V3
    Future: will be + V3 | Modals: can/must + be + V3
    5. Exceptions — Imperatives: Let ... be + V3 (Let the door be opened). Suggestions: Should be + V3 (It should be done). Existential 'there is': No passive.
    6. Intransitive verbs (sleep, die, come, go) generally have NO passive form.
    7. Two-object verbs (give, tell, offer, show, teach, send, hand, write) allow two passives: 'He gave me a pen' → 'I was given a pen' OR 'A pen was given to me.'
    8. 'Have something done' = causative (Passive sense).
  • Direct & Indirect Speech — Rules: Key reporting changes:
    1. Tense backshift (if reporting verb in past):
    Present Simple → Past Simple | Present Continuous → Past Continuous | Present Perfect → Past Perfect
    Past Simple → Past Perfect | Past Continuous → Past Perfect Continuous | Past Perfect → Past Perfect (unchanged)
    Will → Would | Can → Could | May → Might | Must → Had to
    2. Pronoun changes: I→he/she, You→he/she/they, We→they per context.
    3. Time/Place words: here→there, now→then, today→that day, tomorrow→the next day, yesterday→the previous day, this→that, these→those, ago→before.
    4. Question types:
    - Yes/No questions: use 'if/whether'. Verb-subject order restored. Did he come? → Asked if he had come.
    - WH- questions: keep WH-word. What did you do? → Asked what I had done.
    - Tag questions: use 'whether' or 'or not'.
    - Commands/Requests: infinitive form. 'Close the door' → Told him to close the door.
    - Exclamations: state the feeling indirectly.
    5. Reporting verbs: said→told/aske/said, asked→inquired/requested, ordered/demanded/suggested.
  • Noun & Pronoun — Key Points: Nouns:
    • Countable vs Uncountable: information, advice, furniture, luggage, baggage, scenery, baggage, equipment, evidence, knowledge, fame, wealth → uncountable (no plural, no 'a').
    • Quantity expressions: a piece of advice/information/news/a letter; a cup of tea/coffee.
    • Plural irregulars: child→children, foot→feet, tooth→teeth, mouse→mice, person→people, datum→data, criterion→criteria, phenomenon→phenomena, fungus→fungi, analysis→analyses, diagnosis→diagnoses, basis→bases, thesis→theses, axis→axes.

    Pronouns:
    • Who = subject, Whom = object, Whose = possessive.
    • Who is vs Whom is: choose based on role in clause.
    • Reflexive: myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself, ourselves, yourselves, themselves.
    • Use reflexive for emphasis: I myself saw it. After prepositions: between you and me.
    • Each other (two), One another (more than two).
    • Anyone/any body vs Any body: anyone = any person; any body = any physical body.
    • None = not any (of); Nobody = no person.
  • Adjective & Adverb — Comparison & Usage: Comparative & Superlative forms:
    • One syllable: tall→taller→tallest | fast→faster→fastest
    • Two+ syllables: beautiful→more beautiful→most beautiful | happy→happier→happiest
    • Irregular: good→better→best | bad→worse→worst | much/many→more→most | little→less→least | far→farther/further→farthest/furthest
    • Two-syllable ending in -y: change y to i + er/est.

    Adverbs of frequency: always, usually, often, sometimes, rarely, seldom, hardly, never → position before main verb, after be.
    'Hard' (effort) vs 'Hardly' (almost not); 'Late' (not early) vs 'Lately' (recently); 'Near' vs 'Nearly'; 'Pretty' vs 'Prety' (typo trap).
    'Too' = excess (negative); 'Also' = addition (neutral/positive);
    'Enough' after adjective/adverb: good enough, fast enough.
    'Such...that' vs 'So...that': such + noun phrase; so + adj/adv.
  • Conditional Sentences: Type 1 (Real/Possible): If + Present Simple, Will + V1. Example: If it rains, I will stay home.
    Type 2 (Unreal Present): If + Past Simple, Would + V1. Example: If I were rich, I would travel.
    • 'Were' used for all persons in unreal conditions (were I rich = If I were rich).
    Type 3 (Unreal Past): If + Past Perfect, Would have + V3. Example: If I had studied, I would have passed.
    Mixed conditionals combine types: If + Past Perfect (Type 3) + would + V1 (Type 2 result) → If I had taken the job, I would be living in London now.
    Inversion in conditionals: Should you need help, call me. / Were I you, I would go. / Had I known, I would have acted.
    'Provided that', 'Providing', 'Unless', 'Except that' = conditional meaning.
  • Modals — Nuanced Usage: Can/Could:
    • Can = ability (present), permission (informal), possibility
    • Could = ability (past), polite request, possibility
    May/Might:
    • May = permission (formal), possibility (50%)
    • Might = possibility (less certain)
    Must:
    • Must = obligation, strong certainty, logical deduction
    • Mustn't = prohibition; Don't have to = absence of obligation
    Should/Ought to:
    • Both express advice/recommendation; ought to requires 'to'
    Will/Shall:
    • Will = future, willingness, promise
    • Shall = formal future (I/we), suggestions/offers
    Need/Dare:
    • Need as modal (rare, negative/question): Need I come? Needn't worry.
    • Dare as modal: How dare you say that?
    Had better = strong advice (better than should, implies negative consequence)
    Would rather = preference (Would rather + V1 than + V1).
  • Non-Finite Verbs — Gerund, Infinitive, Participle: Infinitive (to V1):
    • Purpose: I went to the shop to buy milk.
    • After certain verbs: want, hope, decide, plan, refuse, expect, agree, learn, fail, afford, manage, pretend, appear.
    • After adjectives: easy to read, difficult to understand.
    Gerund (V+ing as noun):
    • After certain verbs: enjoy, avoid, finish, mind, suggest, risk, practice, consider, involve, admit, deny, keep, miss, can't help, feel like, quit.
    • After prepositions: interested in, good at, busy with, capable of, succeed in, look forward to (here 'to' is preposition → look forward to seeing you).
    Participle:
    • Present participle (-ing): active meaning, continuous aspect, adjective. The crying baby.
    • Past participle (V3): passive meaning, perfect aspect. The broken glass. Having finished the work, he left.
    • Dangling participle error: The walking down the street, the tree looked beautiful. (Wrong — modifier must attach to correct subject).

Important facts

  • Stative verbs (know, believe, understand, remember, forget, love, hate, like, prefer, belong, own, contain, consist, seem, appear, sound, smell, taste, feel) are not used in continuous/progressive tenses.
  • Each, every, either, neither, one of the, many a → take SINGULAR verb despite plural meaning.
  • No sooner...than | Hardly...when | Scarcely...when → always use inversion and past perfect in first clause.
  • Although/though cannot be paired with but in the same sentence. Because cannot be paired with so.
  • The preposition AFTER 'look forward to' is 'to' (preposition, not infinitive marker) → followed by V+ing.
  • Between = two items; Among = three or more. Though among is sometimes used informally for groups.
  • A/An = one; The = specific/already mentioned/unique. Zero article = general/plural/uncountable.
  • Whom is used after prepositions: To whom, With whom, For whom, Of whom.
  • Some used in offers/requests (Would you like some tea?) even though question form.
  • Few/a few; Little/a little: 'a' = positive meaning (enough); without 'a' = negative (hardly any).
  • More than one + singular noun → singular verb. More than one student has submitted.
  • The number of → singular verb; A number of → plural verb. The number of applicants is large; A number of applicants are waiting.
  • Passive of 'let' = be allowed to. Let him go → He is allowed to go.
  • Suggest + that clause uses subjunctive (base form): I suggest that he go. NOT goes.
  • If clause with were → invert: Were I rich (If I were rich). Should you need help (If you should need). Had I known (If I had known).

Memory aids

  • Tense tense triangle: Past ← Present ← Future. For perfect: use has/have/had + V3. For continuous: use be + V1+ing.
  • SAVA rule for Subject-Verb Agreement: Subject-Agreement-Verb- Agreement. Match number and person.
  • Preposition pairs mnemonic: ACCuse OF, Charge WITH, Punish FOR, Thank FOR, Depend ON, Insist ON, Aim AT, Apply FOR/TO, Marry (no prep)/Be married TO.
  • Correlative conjunctions shortcut: Both-AND (addition), Either-NOR (choice/negation), Not only-BUT ALSO (emphasis/addition), Neither-NOR (double negation).
  • Hard/Hardly, Late/Lately, Near/Nearly, Pretty/Prety — remember: -ly suffix often changes meaning drastically.
  • Gerund verbs mnemonic: FAVORITES — Finish, Avoid, Consider, Admit, Vin, Enjoy, Resist, Involve, Suggest, Try (meaning change).
  • SOONER/HARDLY inversion pattern: No SOONER had + subject + V3 THAN... | HARDLY/SCARCELY had + subject + V3 WHEN...

Exam tips

  • SSC English Grammar carries ~20-25 marks in Tier 1 across Spotting Errors, Sentence Improvement, Replacement, Active-Passive, Direct-Indirect. Practice rules, not just memorisation.
  • In Spotting Errors, read the full sentence twice. Check: subject-verb agreement first, then tense consistency, then preposition/article, then idiom/phrase.
  • For Active-Passive: identify object first. If no object → no passive possible (intransitive verbs). Check tense carefully.
  • For Direct-Indirect: note the question type first (yes/no vs wh). Change tense ONLY if reporting verb is in past. Commands become infinitive.
  • Learn collocations and fixed prepositional phrases — they are heavily tested. Make a personal list of 50 most-used ones.
  • Do NOT change the meaning of the sentence in Error spotting. If no error exists, choose 'No Error'.
  • Time management: Grammar questions are quick to solve if rules are clear. Spend max 30-40 seconds per question. Mark unsure ones and return later.
  • For articles: always check if noun is specific or generic, countable or uncountable, singular or plural.
  • Practice 50+ each of Active-Passive and Direct-Indirect from previous years. These are the highest-yield grammar topics in SSC.
  • When in doubt between 'since' and 'for': since + point of time (2010, Monday); for + duration (10 years, 3 hours).

Common traps

  • I am agree — WRONG. Correct: I agree. ('Agree' is a verb, not adjective.)
  • He is knowing French — WRONG. 'Know' is stative; use He knows French.
  • He don't know — WRONG. Third-person singular: He doesn't know.
  • The police is coming — WRONG. 'Police' is always plural: The police are coming.
  • She is more better — WRONG. 'Better' is already comparative; use 'much better'.
  • I am wanting water — WRONG. 'Want' is stative; use 'I want water'.
  • He said that he is coming yesterday — WRONG. Backshift needed: 'he was coming'.
  • One of my friend is here — WRONG. Should be 'One of my friends IS here' (one is singular).
  • There is many problems — WRONG. Should be 'There are many problems'.
  • Neither of the boys have come — DEBATABLE but preferred: 'has come' (neither = not one = singular).
  • Because of rain so we stayed — WRONG. Use either 'because of rain we stayed' or 'due to rain, we stayed'.
  • Despite of the rain — WRONG. 'Despite' takes no 'of'; use 'In spite of the rain'.
  • He is good in English — WRONG. Use 'good at English'.
  • I am agreeing with you — WRONG. 'Agree' is stative.
  • She sings beautifully song — WRONG. 'Beautifully' modifies verb (sings); 'beautiful' modifies noun (song).
  • The staff are very efficient — CAN BE RIGHT (individual members) but staff as unit is singular. Context decides.
  • His height is more than me — WRONG. 'Higher than mine' or 'taller than me'.
  • Each of the boys have — WRONG. Each → singular verb: has.
