Quick Revision — Corporate and Economic Laws (CMA Final Group 3)

CMAEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Companies Act, 2013: The principal statute governing incorporation, responsibilities, powers, and duties of companies in India, superseding the Companies Act, 1956. It applies to all forms of companies including those incorporated before its commencement.
  • Section: A specific provision within the Companies Act, 2013 identified by its section number, containing substantive or procedural law relevant to corporate governance, compliance, and operations.
  • Company: An incorporated association of persons, whether bodies corporate or individuals, with perpetual succession and a common seal, capable of suing and being sued in its own name.
  • Board of Directors: The elected or appointed body of individuals tasked with the management, governance, and strategic decision-making of a company, acting as agents of the shareholders.
  • Related Party: Any person or entity that has a relationship with the company such that one party may control or influence the other, including promoters, directors, key managerial personnel, and their relatives.
  • Key Managerial Personnel (KMP): Includes Chief Executive Officer, Managing Director, Whole-time Director, Company Secretary, and Chief Financial Officer as defined under Section 2(51) of the Companies Act, 2013.
  • SEBI: Securities and Exchange Board of India, the regulatory authority established under the SEBI Act, 1992, responsible for protecting investors' interests and regulating securities markets.
  • Competition Commission of India (CCI): The statutory body established under the Competition Act, 2002 to prevent practices having an adverse effect on competition, promote and sustain competition, and protect consumer interests.
  • FEMA: Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999, enacted to facilitate external trade and payments and to harmonize foreign exchange laws with broader economic objectives.
  • IBC: Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016, a comprehensive framework for insolvency resolution and liquidation of corporate persons, partnership firms, and individuals, consolidated under a single statute.
  • Liquidation: The process of winding up a company's affairs, realizing assets, paying off creditors, and distributing any surplus among members as per statutory priority.
  • Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP): A time-bound procedure under the IBC for resolving the insolvency of a corporate debtor, led by a Resolution Professional, with a maximum period of 330 days including extensions.

Key concepts

  • Company Formation and Classification: Companies may be formed as Private Limited, Public Limited, or One Person Company (OPC). Classification depends on share transferability, member minimum/maximum limits, and publication requirements. OPC allows a single individual to incorporate a company without a board of partners.
  • Memorandum of Association (MOA): Defines the company's scope of operations through six clauses — Name, Registered Office, Object, Liability, Capital, and Subscription. Ultra vires acts beyond these clauses are void ab initio and cannot be ratified.
  • Articles of Association (AOA): Contains rules and regulations for internal management. Subject to the Companies Act and MOA, it governs conduct of business, director appointment, dividend declaration, and share transfers.
  • Share Capital and Debentures: Equity shares carry voting rights; preference shares carry preferential rights to dividend and capital repayment. Debentures represent debt with fixed interest, secured or unsecured. Calls on shares must follow proper procedures under Section 39.
  • Prospectus and Allotment: A prospectus invites the public to subscribe to securities. Misstatement in a prospectus attracts liability under Sections 34-36. Allotment must be made within 30 days of application money receipt, with refund provisions for failure to allot.
  • Corporate Governance: Mechanisms include independent directors (Section 149), audit committees, related party transactions approval (Section 188), and disclosure requirements. Independent directors must constitute at least one-third of the board for listed companies.
  • Meetings and Resolutions: Statutory meetings, annual general meetings, extraordinary general meetings, and board meetings have specific notice periods, quorum requirements, and procedural rules. Special resolutions require 75% majority; ordinary resolutions require simple majority.
  • Company Incorporation and Registration: Incorporation requires filing SPICe+ form with ROC, obtaining DIN, DPAN, and PAN/TAN. The Certificate of Incorporation conclusively evidences company formation under Section 7.
  • Debenture Redemption: Companies issuing debentures must create a Debenture Redemption Reserve (DRR) unless exempted. Secured debentures require creation of security within 30 days of issue under Rule 18 of Companies (Share Capital and Debentures) Rules, 2014.
  • Merger and Amalgamation: Governed by Sections 230-232, requiring NCLT approval after creditor and member consent. The scheme must be in the public interest, and valuation must be fair. Cross-border mergers permitted under specified conditions.

Important facts

  • Section 2(68): Defines 'Private Company' — minimum 2 members, maximum 200 members; restricts right to transfer shares; prohibits public invitation for subscriptions. Exception: OPC if it meets prescribed turnover and paid-up capital thresholds.
  • Section 3(1): A company may be formed for any lawful purpose by seven or more persons subscribing their names to a memorandum (two or more for private company, one for OPC).
  • Section 4(1)(a)-(f): Six mandatory clauses in MOA — Name clause, Location clause, Object clause, Liability clause, Capital clause, and Association clause.
  • Section 7: Requirements for incorporation include filing Form INC-7 (application for registration), Form INC-8 (declaration), Form INC-9 (consent of promoters/directors), and SPICe+ (Sri Pro).
  • Section 8: Company formed for promoting commerce, art, science, religion, charity, or any other useful object may apply for license. License granted by Central Government with conditions; company enjoys privileges of limited liability and tax benefits.
  • Section 10: Memorandum and Articles may contain regulations consistent with the Act. Alterations require special resolution and compliance with applicable provisions.
  • Section 12: Every company shall keep at its registered office a register of members, charge register, and other statutory registers. Inspection available to members and debenture holders.
  • Section 22: Change of name requires passing special resolution and obtaining Central Government approval. New name must not be identical or resemble existing company/trademark without authorization.
  • Section 39-42: Call on shares must be uniform for each class of shares. Shareholder entitled to notice of call with 14 days' notice. Non-payment attracts penalty and forfeiture proceedings.
  • Section 44: Shares are movable property transferable in manner provided by articles.
  • Section 45-51: Transfer and transmission of shares — instrument of transfer in Form SH-4; stamp duty; registration within 60 days. Transmission occurs by operation of law on death, lunacy, or insolvency.
  • Section 56: Refusal of registration of transfer must be communicated within 60 days with reasons. Disputes adjudicated by Tribunal.
  • Section 61: Increase of authorized share capital requires special resolution.
  • Section 62: Rights issue, bonus issue, and preferential allotment — existing shareholders have pre-emptive rights. Private placement requires board resolution and compliance with Section 42.
  • Section 68-77: Buyback of securities — declared by board or special resolution; maximum 25% of paid-up capital; completed within one year; no buyback in five years for same class of shares; disclosure and file with ROC required.
  • Section 392: SEBI (Issue of Capital and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations govern public issues, rights issues, and preferential allotments with disclosure norms.
  • Section 149: Number of directors — minimum 3 for public, 2 for private, 1 for OPC. Independent directors required for listed public companies and prescribed categories. Age limit: maximum 70 years; re-appointment after 70 requires special resolution.
  • Section 152: Appointment of directors requires DIGITAL SIGNATURE and DIN. First directors appointed in incorporation; subsequent directors elected at AGM. Natural succession applies — retirement by rotation for non-independent directors.
  • Section 164: Disqualifications include unsound mind, undischarged insolvent, conviction for offense involving moral turpitude within past five years, failure to pay calls for six months, and divergence from disqualification under other laws.
  • Section 177: Audit Committee — constituted for listed companies and prescribed classes. Comprises majority of independent directors with financial expertise. Functions include oversight of financial reporting, internal controls, and auditor appointment.
  • Section 178: Nomination and Remuneration Committee recommends appointment and remuneration of managerial personnel.
  • Section 188: Related Party Transactions require approval of board or shareholders as applicable. Material related party transactions require special resolution. Disclosures must be made in board reports.
  • Section 189: Disclosure of interest by directors in contracts or arrangements — must disclose nature and extent at first board meeting or upon becoming interested.
  • Section 196: Managerial remuneration — maximum 11% of net profits for managing director/whole-time director (with approval); 5% each where multiple MDs/WTDs; total not exceeding 5% for non-executive directors.
  • Section 203: Key Managerial Personnel — MD, WTD, CEO, CFO, CS mandatory for listed companies and prescribed public companies.
  • Section 204: Secretarial Audit required for listed companies and prescribed public companies. Report in Form MR-3 to be filed within 60 days of AGM.
  • Section 212: Holding and subsidiary company — holding holds >50% voting power or controls composition of board. Subsidiary is controlled by holding. Cross-holdings and circular holdings regulated.
  • Section 215: Definition of associated company.
  • Section 230-232: Compromise, Arrangement, and Amalgamation — NCLT approval required. Scheme to be filed with registrar. Opposed claims settled by NCLT. Valuation by registered valuer.
  • Section 241-242: oppression and mismanagement — petition by 100 members or 1/10th members; relief includes regulation of conduct, purchase of shares, or winding up.
  • Section 245: Class action suits permitted by subscribers or depositors for wrong against company.
  • Section 246: Whistleblower vigil mechanism required for prescribed companies. Protection against victimization.
  • Section 252: National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) has original jurisdiction over company matters. National Company Law Appellate Tribunal (NCLAT) hears appeals.
  • Section 292: Board powers — default powers include selling, leasing, borrowing, investing, and guaranteeing. Special powers need shareholder approval.
  • Section 123: Declaration of dividend — only out of profits or surplus; prior year losses set off; depreciation written off; reserve transfer per Section 123(1)(a). Interim dividend by board; final dividend by shareholders.
  • Section 124: Unpaid dividend transferred to Investor Education and Protection Fund (IEPF) after seven years. Claim within 3 years from date of transfer.
  • Section 128: Books of account maintained at registered office for eight years. Annual financial statements include balance sheet, profit and loss, cash flow, statement of changes in equity, and notes.
  • Section 130: Financial statements must present true and fair view. Auditor's report to state whether compliance with Accounting Standards.
  • Section 134: Board responsibility for preparing financial statements; director's responsibility statement; signatory requirements.
  • Section 139: Appointment of auditors — first auditors within 30 days of incorporation; subsequent auditors at AGM. Term: five years; removal requires NCLT approval after special resolution with Central Government consultation.
  • Section 143: Auditor's rights and duties — right to information; duty to report frauds above threshold; certification of compliance.
  • Section 148: Audit fees fixed by shareholders in general meeting or board within limits fixed by rules.
  • SEBI Act, 1992 — Section 11: SEBI may make regulations for protection of investors, regulation of stock exchanges, and intermediaries. Penalties for violation.
  • SEBI (LODR) Regulations, 2015: Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements — disclosure of financial results, significant events, corporate governance practices, and related party transactions.
  • SEBI (Substantial Acquisition of Shares and Takeovers) Regulations, 2011: Trigger point at 25% — open offer obligation to acquire additional 26%. Exemptions for preferential allotment, inheritance, and certain institutional transfers.
  • Competition Act, 2002 — Section 3: Prohibition of anti-competitive agreements (cartels, price fixing, market sharing). Abuse of dominant position prohibited under Section 4. Combination regulation under Sections 5-6 with notification thresholds.
  • FEMA, 1999 — Section 6: Current account transactions freely permissible; capital account transactions permissible only as prescribed. Section 7: Exporters required to realize proceeds within specified period.
  • Section 8: Borrowing and lending in foreign exchange regulated. Section 9: Repatriation of foreign exchange mandatory for NRI accounts.
  • Section 17: Penalties for contravention — up to three times the amount involved or fixed monetary penalty.
  • IBC, 2016 — Section 7: Operational creditor may initiate CIRP with evidence of default. Section 9: Financial creditor may invoke with minimum default of ₹1 lakh.
  • Section 10: Application to NCLT for initiation of CIRP. Adjudication committee of creditors (CoC) constituted with financial creditors. Resolution professional takes over management.
  • Section 12: Moratorium period — stays all proceedings, suits, and execution. CIRP completion timeline: 180 days extendable to 330 days.
  • Section 29: CoC constituted; voting share based on value of debt. Resolution plan requires 66% approval (now 75% per amendment).
  • Section 30: Resolution plan binding on all stakeholders. Implementation by resolution professional.
  • Section 33: Liquidation proceedings if no resolution plan approved or implementation fails. Liquidator appointed; assets realized; distribution per priority order.
  • Section 53: Priority of distribution in liquidation — (i) insolvency resolution costs, (ii) workmen wages (24 months), (iii) secured creditors (if waived), (iv) employees' wages, (v) government dues, (vi) unsecured creditors, (vii) remaining debts, (viii) preference shareholders, (ix) equity shareholders.
  • Section 60(5): NCLT exercises appellate jurisdiction over NCLAT decisions and original jurisdiction over IBC matters.
  • Companies (Accounts) Rules, 2014 — Form MGT-7 for annual return; AOC-4 for financial statements; MGT-14 for special resolution filing.
  • Companies (Management and Administration) Rules, 2014 — E-voting, secretarial audit procedures, meeting notices.
  • Companies (Corporate Governance) Rules, 2014 — applicable to listed companies only.
  • SEBI (Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2015: Regular disclosure requirements include quarterly results, annual results, board meeting outcomes, shareholding pattern, and risk management.
  • Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India (IBBI): Regulatory body established under IBC to regulate insolvency professionals, agencies, and information utilities.

Memory aids

  • MOA Clauses Mnemonic — 'NAVELS': Name, Address (Registered Office), Vision (Objects), Ability (Liability), Size (Capital), Signature (Association). Remember: A company's NAVELS define its identity.
  • Board Meeting Requirements — QUORUM = 1/3rd of total strength or 2 directors, whichever is higher. Notice = 7 days. Distance travel for board decisions needs separate resolution. Audio-visual + physical participation allowed.
  • Annual General Meeting — Within 6 months of financial year end for public companies. Minimum 15 days' notice. Quorum: 5 members for public (>1000 members), 2 for others. Chairman presides.
  • Independent Director Qualification — Minimum 3 for listed public; age max 70; max two terms of 5 years; cooling off period of 3 years before re-appointment; no pecuniary relationship except sitting fees and ESOPs.
  • Audit Committee — Minimum 3 members, majority independent, at least one financial expert. Meets at least 4 times a year. Reviews financial statements, internal controls, and related party transactions.
  • Related Party Transaction Approval Ladder — Board approval required for all RPTs; material RPTs require special resolution; related party must disclose interest; annual disclosure in board report. Think: 'Board First, then Shareholders for Material deals.'
  • Call on Shares Procedure — Board resolution → Notice (14 days) → Non-payment → Forfeiture notice (7 days) → Forfeiture resolution → Re-issue. Remember: 'Call, Notice, Forfeit, Reissue.'
  • Buyback Limits and Conditions — Maximum 25% of paid-up capital; completed within 1 year; ratio not more than 1:1; no buyback in preceding 1 year; DRR equal to 25% of buyback amount. Memory hook: 'Quarter cap, year span, reserve grab.'
  • CIRP Timeline Anchor — 180 days initial + 90 days extension (one time) + 30 days NCLT time = 330 days total. Hook: '180 starts, 90 extends, 30 completes.'
  • Liquidation Priority Sequence — 'IRC' then 'W' then 'G' then 'U' then 'E': Insolvency Resolution Costs → Workmen/Employees Wages → Government Dues → Unsecured Creditors → Equity/shareholders. The 'IWGUE' rule for order of payment.
  • Takeover Trigger — 25% acquisition triggers open offer for additional 26%. Total takeover = 51%. Memory: 'Quarter triggers, quarter more, half plus one.'
  • Key Managerial Personnel — CEO, CFO, CS, MD, WTD. Only KMPs. Think 'CEO FC' — Chief Executive runs it, CFO handles money, CS ensures compliance, MD/WTD manage operations.
  • Sections for Quick Recall: Section 2(68) = Private Co; Section 3 = Formation; Section 7 = Incorporation; Section 12 = Registration Office; Section 22 = Change of Name; Section 39 = Calls; Section 149 = Directors; Section 177 = Audit Committee; Section 230 = Amalgamation; Section 241 = Oppression/Mismanagement; Section 292 = Board Powers; Section 123 = Dividend; Section 139 = Auditors; Section 7 IBC = Operational Creditor; Section 9 IBC = Financial Creditor; Section 53 IBC = Liquidation Priority.

Exam tips

  • Section references are critical — always cite specific sections when writing descriptive answers. ICMAI expects precise legal citations.
  • For numerical/legal limit questions, memorize percentages, timelines, and thresholds precisely. Even small deviations attract marking deductions.
  • Differentiate between 'may' and 'shall' language in provisions — 'shall' indicates mandatory requirement; 'may' indicates discretionary power.
  • For case-based questions, identify the legal issue first, then cite the applicable section, then apply facts to conclude. Structure: Issue → Law → Application → Conclusion.
  • When comparing Companies Act with SEBI/FEMA/IBC, create parallel tables in revision to avoid confusion between similar-sounding provisions.
  • Focus on recent amendments (as of 2024-25) especially to IBC, Companies Act (foreign exchange, ESG disclosure), and SEBI LODR regulations.
  • For descriptive answers, use headings and sub-headings matching question structure. Bullet-point format preferred for clarity.
  • Practice drawing flowcharts for processes: incorporation, buyback, amalgamation, CIRP, liquidation. Visual representation earns marks.
  • In objective questions, eliminate options that contradict explicit statutory provisions. Negative marking elimination strategy works well.
  • Remember that ICMAI questions often test 'except' type — find the one incorrect statement among correct ones. Read carefully.
  • For IBC-related questions, understand the distinction between operational creditors (goods/services suppliers) and financial creditors (lenders). Different provisions apply.
  • Cross-link topics: e.g., when studying buyback, link it to Section 68, Section 69, Section 70, Section 71 together, not in isolation.
  • Revision approach: First pass — all provisions; Second pass — sections with numbers; Third pass — exceptions and limitations. Three passes build retention.

Common traps

  • Confusing MOA and AOA — MOA defines outer limits (ultra vires doctrine); AOA contains internal rules. Acts beyond MOA are void; violation of AOA may be ratified by shareholders. Trap: Students often reverse these concepts.
  • Public vs Private Company Confusion — Minimum members: public = 7, private = 2, OPC = 1. Maximum: private = 200, public = unlimited. Trap: Many confuse the numeric thresholds and transfer restrictions.
  • Special Resolution vs Ordinary Resolution — Special requires 75%; ordinary requires simple majority (>50%). Matters like alteration of MOA, reduction of capital, and buyback need special resolution. Trap: Students forget the 75% threshold.
  • Ultra Vires Doctrine — Acts beyond MOA are void ab initio — cannot be ratified even by unanimous shareholders. Acts beyond AOA may be ratified. Trap: Applying ultra vires concept to intra-vires but AOA-violating acts.
  • Call forfeiture Procedure — Must serve 7-day notice before forfeiture; forfeiture must be actual act of board, not merely resolution. Capital returned on forfeiture stays with company. Trap: Skipping procedural steps or confusing forfeiture with surrender.
  • Independent Director Re-appointment — Cannot hold office beyond two full terms; 3-year cooling off period. Trap: Assuming re-appointment is automatic or confusing with other director categories.
  • CIRP Duration Calculation — The 330 days includes court/stay periods but excludes periods of suspension. Extensions beyond 180 days require NCLT approval. Trap: Adding extensions incorrectly or misunderstanding the floor.
  • Resolution Plan Approval Threshold — Originally 66% of voting share; amended to 75% for financial creditors. Operational creditors vote separately at 66%/75% as applicable. Trap: Using old thresholds or applying uniform percentage across creditor classes.
  • Secured vs Unsecured Creditor in Liquidation — Secured creditors can either realize security independently or surrender and prove as unsecured. Trap: Assuming secured creditors automatically rank ahead without considering election option.
  • IEPF Transfer Timeline — Unclaimed dividends transferred after 7 years from due date of payment, not from declaration date. Claim period is 3 years from transfer date. Trap: Confusing the starting points.
  • Buyback Eligibility — Company must not have defaulted in repayment of deposits, interest, debentures, or repayment of statutory dues. Trap: Overlooking the default disqualification condition.
  • NCLT vs NCLAT Jurisdiction — NCLT is trial court; NCLAT is appellate. Appeals from NCLAT go to Supreme Court. Trap: Filing appeals in wrong forum or confusing jurisdictional boundaries.
  • FEMA vs Companies Act — FEMA governs foreign exchange transactions; Companies Act governs domestic corporate matters. Transactions involving foreign exchange require FEMA compliance additionally. Trap: Treating them as mutually exclusive when both apply simultaneously.
  • Open Offer Exemptions — Preferential allotment to promoters does not trigger open offer; inheritance and interscript transfers also exempt. Trap: Assuming all acquisitions above 25% trigger open offer without checking exemptions.
  • Related Party Transaction Scope — Includes contracts with director, relative, or entity controlled by director. Exception: wholly-owned subsidiary contracts need not be approved if disclosure made. Trap: Missing the subsidiary exception.
  • Secretarial Audit Scope — Covers compliance with Companies Act, rules, regulations, and articles. Does not cover tax laws or SEBI LODR compliance directly. Trap: Expanding audit scope beyond statutory requirement.
  • Dividend Distribution — Cannot declare dividend if books not closed; cannot declare if losses exceed reserves without board recommendation. Interim dividend can be declared between AGMs. Trap: Forgetting interim/final dividend distinction.
