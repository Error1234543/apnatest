CMA Final — Direct Tax Laws and International Taxation | Quick Revision

CMAEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Assessee: Any person who is liable to pay tax under the Income Tax Act, 1961 — includes a person in respect of whom any proceedings for assessment are underway or who has incurred tax liability.
  • Person: Includes an individual, HUF, company, firm, AOP, BOI, local authority, artificial juridical person, and any other entity treated as a person under the Act.
  • Assessment Year (AY): The twelve-month period beginning 1st April each year during which income earned in the Previous Year is assessed and tax payable.
  • Previous Year (PY): The financial year immediately preceding the Assessment Year in which income is earned and tax is assessed; the basic unit of taxation under the Act.
  • Total Income: Gross total income after deducting all allowable exemptions u/s 10, deductions u/s 80C to 80U, with loss set-off and carry-forward permitted under Chapter VI-A.
  • Residential Status: Determines the scope of total income taxable in India — Resident (R) has worldwide income taxed; Non-Resident (NR) has only India-sourced income taxed.
  • Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR): A resident who fails either the 182-day physical stay condition or the earlier-residence condition during the preceding 4 PYs; limited global income scope.
  • Very Highly Resident (RVR): A resident individual whose total income includes foreign source income exceeding ₹15 lakh and who has not paid tax on such income under section 5(2)(b).
  • Per capita basis taxation: For an individual, tax is levied on the total income per assessable unit (the individual), unlike entities where income is assessed at the entity level.
  • Head of Income: Classification of income into (1) Salaries, (2) House Property, (3) Business/Profession, (4) Capital Gains, (5) Other Sources — each with specific rules for inclusion and computation.

Key concepts

  • Residential Status Determination (Section 6): Individual: A person is 'Resident' in India if he satisfies at least one of the two conditions: (a) Physical stay in India ≥ 182 days in the PY, OR (b) Physical stay ≥ 60 days in the PY and ≥ 365 days in the preceding 4 PYs. For certain absences (Indian citizen/PEC abroad, crew of Indian ship), the 60-day limit is extended to 182 days. Ordinarily Resident (ROR): Must be Resident AND satisfy both additional conditions — stay in India ≥ 365 days in 7 preceding PYs AND ≥ 243 days in 9 preceding PYs. Failure of either additional condition makes one RNOR. Company: Resident if IPC (Indian control and possession) in the PY; otherwise NR. FIRP/AOP/BOI/Firm/HUF: Resident if IPC in the PY. Special provisions apply for NRIs, PIOs, and OCBs under FEMA framework with corresponding CBDT notifications.
  • Scope of Total Income (Section 5): Resident: Income received or deemed received in India, or accrued/ arisen/ deemed accrued/ arisen in India, anywhere in the world. Also includes income from overseas business control/management established in India (where applicable). Non-Resident: Income received in India, income deemed received in India, and income accrued/ arisen/ deemed accrued/ arisen in India. Income accruing outside India to a non-resident is NOT taxable unless it is income from a business controlled/profession set up in India. Key distinction: ROR vs RNOR — RNOR excludes foreign income from office/employment outside India, foreign business not controlled from India, and foreign assets. RVR attracts full tax on foreign income even if residential status suggests otherwise.
  • Income from Salary (Sections 15–17): Salary includes wages, pension, annuity, gratuity, fees, commissions, perquisites, pro tanto advance of salary, annual accretion to U.P.F., and profits in lieu of salary (section 17(3)). Perquisites (section 17(2)): Any benefit/fringe provided free or at concessional rate by employer — includes rent-free accommodation, automobile, Club membership, interest-free/low-interest loans, security/health insurance premiums, stock options (SOP) — valued as per rule in Rule 3. Value of perquisites: (a) Specified perquisites — valued at cost to employer; (b) Others — standard rules (e.g., rent-free accommodation = percentage of basic salary or actual rent paid); (c) Specified securities/loans. Standard deduction u/s 16(ia): ₹50,000 (FY 2024-25). Entertainment allowance deduction (govt employees) u/s 16(ii): lower of ₹5,000 or 1/3rd of basic salary. Professional tax u/s 16(iii): actual amount paid. Exemptions u/s 10: HRA u/s 10(13A) — minimum of actual HRA received, 50%/40% of basic+DA, or actual rent paid minus 10% of basic; leave encashment for govt employees fully exempt, for others per rule 3AA; LTA u/s 10(5) — actual travel cost for home town journey; PF contribution excess by employer exempt u/s 10(12); interest on PPF u/s 10(11); dividend from Indian company exempt u/s 10(34) w.e.f. FY 2020-21; compensation on termination/u/s 10(10) subject to limits;Gratuity u/s 10(10) subject to limits under payment of gratuity rules.
  • House Property Income (Sections 22–27): Conditions: (a) Must be building/land appurtenant thereto; (b) Owned by assessee; (c) Not used for business/profession carried on by assessee (otherwise taxed thereunder). Annual Value (AV) determination: (a) Municipal value (MV) vs. Fair Rental Value (FRV) — take higher of the two; (b) Compare with Expected Rental Value (ERV) = higher of MV and FRV vs. Actual Gross Receipts (AGR) where property is let; (c) Municipal taxes paid during PY are deductible from AV to arrive at Net Annual Value (NAV). NAV = AV less municipal taxes. From NAV, deduct: (a) 30% standard deduction u/s 24(a); (b) Interest on borrowed capital u/s 24(b) — self-occupied: up to ₹2 lakh (pre-construction period interest max 5 annual installments from AY 6 onwards); vacant/unlet: same limit but excess cannot create negative income beyond ₹2L unless property was let; let property: entire interest deductible. Pre-construction period: period starting from loan sanction date to date of acquisition/completion — interest accumulated over this period can be claimed in 5 equal annual installments starting from AY next to acquisition AY.
  • Income from Business/Profession (Sections 28–43D): What constitutes 'business income': Profits and gains of any business/profession carried on by assessee,including perquisites, any sum received under KARMA YOGA scheme, compensation for loss of office, or on cessation of business, and any sum receivable on dematerialisation of shares (sec 28). Deductions u/s 36–43D: Revenue expenditure wholly/exclusively for business is deductible (sec 37). Specific deductions include: contribution to recognised provident fund (sec 36(1)(va)), gratuity (sec 36(1)(v)), superannuation fund (sec 36(1)(vc)), donation to national defence fund/others (sec 36(1)(viii)), scientific research expenditure (sec 35), advertisement (sec 35DB), expenditure on family labour (sec 36(1)(ia)-(id)), bad debts written off and recovered (sec 36(1)(vii)), legal expenses for recovery of income (sec 36(1)(x)), contribution to recognised superannuation fund. Disallowances: tainting of personal/unknown purpose expenditure u/s 37(1) — 'revenue' expenses allowed but not capital. Provisions u/s 40: interest to partner exceeding benchmark rate, remuneration to partner exceeding reasonable amount, payments to relatives above TDS limits, penalties/punishment (not deductible), tax/wealth tax (not deductible). Drawings by partner not deductible. Capital expenditure never deductible — however, depreciation on fixed assets is allowable u/s 32. Block of assets concept applies for depreciation calculation.
  • Capital Gains (Sections 45–55): Chargeability: Transfer (sec 2(47)) of a capital asset situated in India by an assessee. Capital asset means property of any kind held by assessee, excluding stock-in-trade, personal effects, and specified gold/silver/vouchers. Short-term capital asset: held ≤ 36 months (12 months for listed equity shares/SIU). Long-term capital asset: held > 36/12 months. STCG (sec 48 read with 45(1)): Gain = Full value of consideration minus cost of acquisition/improvement minus expenses wholly/exclusively for transfer. Taxed at 20% (with indexation allowed) or 15% (for listed shares/SIU under sec 111A). LTCG (sec 48): Same formula but with indexation benefit. Indexation factor = BLDI of year of transfer / BLDI of year of acquisition/deployment. Special provisions: Sec 54 exemption on residential house reinvestment; Sec 54F exemption on investment of LTCG from any long-term capital asset in residential house; Sec 54EC — investment in specified bonds (NHAI/REC) within 6 months, limit ₹50 lakh per PY; Sec 54D — reconstruction of industrial undertaking. For pre-31-01-2018 acquisitions, old cost can be adopted as per sec 55(2)(ab). Post-July 2024 Budget: LTCG on unlisted shares now taxed at 20% with indexation; STCG on listed shares at 20% replacing 15% u/s 111A.
  • Other Sources (Sections 56–59): Income from other sources is the residuary head — includes any income not chargeable under the other four heads. Includes: (a) Dividends (taxable at slab rates post-FY 2020-21); (b) Interest income from savings bank, bonds, debentures; (c) Rent from plant/machinery/land not let as building; (d) Win从 lottery, crossword, horse race, etc. — taxable at 30% plus cess surcharge u/s 58A; (e) Gift income u/s 56(2)(x) — any sum of money or property without adequate consideration exceeding ₹50,000 in aggregate is taxable. Exceptions: gifts from relatives (defined u/s 56(2)(v)), on marriage, under will/by succession, from local authority, fromFund/Trust registered u/s 12A/12AB, from specified institutions u/s 35CCA, etc. Standard deduction u/s 57: interest income — 33% or ₹5,000 whichever is less; life insurance premium — actual premium paid; family pension — 33% or ₹25,000 whichever is less. Loss under other sources cannot be carried forward except in case of loss from letting of plant and machinery.
  • Set-off and Carry Forward of Losses (Sections 70–80): Intra-head set-off (sec 70): Loss from one source under a head can be set off against profit of another source under the same head in the same PY. Inter-head set-off (sec 71): Loss under one head can be set off against income under another head, except loss under 'Capital Gains' can be set off only against income under that head, and loss under 'Other Sources' (except from letting of plant & machinery) cannot be set off against salary/head property income. Special restrictions: Speculative business loss (sec 73) can be set off only against speculative business income; brought forward speculative loss cannot be set off against any other head. Loss from short-term capital gain u/s 111A can be set off only against income under that head. Carry forward: (a) Loss under capital gains — 8 years; (b) Loss from business/profession — 8 years; (c) Speculative loss — 4 years; (d) Loss from letting of house property — 8 years; (e) Loss from other sources — 8 years. Condition: Return of loss must be filed within due date (sec 139(1)). Unabsorbed depreciation can be carried forward indefinitely and treated as business loss for set-off purposes.
  • Deductions u/s 80C to 80U (Chapter VI-A): Sec 80C: Investments in PPF, EPF, NSC, life insurance premium, tuition fees, 5-year FD (543), ELSS, NPS (employer contribn only), Sukanya Samriddhi, capital gain reinvestment in residential house (sec 54), senior citizen savings scheme. Max deduction: ₹1,50,000. Sec 80CCC: Premium paid for annuity plan — included within 80C limit. Sec 80CCD(1): Self-contribution to NPS — additional deduction up to 10% of gross total income or ₹50,000 (over and above 80C limit). Sec 80CCD(1B): Additional deduction of ₹50,000 over and above 80C for NPS contributions — independent ceiling. Sec 80CCD(2): Employer contribution to NPS — deductible up to 10% of salary. Sec 80D: Health insurance premium — self/family: ₹25,000 (below 60) / ₹50,000 (senior citizen); parents: ₹25,000 (below 60) / ₹50,000 (senior citizen); preventive health check-up included within above limits. Sec 80DD: Maintenance of dependant with disability — ₹1,50,000 (severe) / ₹75,000 (other). Sec 80DDB: Medical treatment for specified diseases — ₹1,50,000 (senior citizen) / ₹40,000 (others) — doctor's prescription required. Sec 80E: Interest on education loan — no ceiling, full interest deductible over 8 years. Sec 80G: Donations to specified funds/charities — 50% or 100% depending on donee, with/without ceiling. Sec 80GG: Rent paid by assesseeg not receiving HRA — lower of actual rent paid minus 10% of total income, ₹5,000/month, or 25% of total income. Sec 80TTA: Interest on savings account (individual/HUF) — up to ₹10,000. Sec 80U: Self-disability — ₹75,000 (severe) / ₹1,25,000 (other) — no medical certificate needed from designated authority.
  • Tax Rates and Surcharge Structure (Finance Act): For FY 2024-25 (AY 2025-26), new vs old regime applies. New regime (sec 115BAC): Lower rates but fewer deductions — individuals below 60 years: up to ₹3L nil; ₹3L–₹7L at 5%; ₹7L–₹10L at 10%; ₹10L–₹12L at 15%; ₹12L–₹15L at 20%; above ₹15L at 30%. Rebate u/s 87A: Nil tax if total income ≤ ₹7L (new regime). Surcharge: 10% on income between ₹50L–₹1Cr; 15% on income between ₹1Cr–₹2Cr; 25% on income between ₹2Cr–₹5Cr; 37% on income above ₹5Cr. Additional surcharge u/s 84B: 25% on dividend income of resident individual/HUF with foreign income exceeding ₹15L where tax not paid on such income under sec 5(2)(b). Cess: 4% on total tax + surcharge. Old regime rates similar but higher slabs and various deductions available. Section 115BAC election is irrevocable for that PY. Tax rebate u/s 87A limit for old regime is ₹12,500 (income up to ₹5L).
  • Advance Tax (Section 207–211): Advance tax is payable when estimated tax liability during the PY exceeds ₹10,000. Due dates: 15th June (15% of total advance tax), 15th September (45% cummulative), 15th December (75% cummulative), 15th March (100% cummulative). For digital transactions (section 206AB), higher TDS/TCS rates may apply. Interest u/s 234A: Interest @ 1% p.m. on overdue tax until actual date of payment. Interest u/s 234B: Interest @ 1% p.m. on shortfall of advance tax below 90% of assessed tax. Interest u/s 234C: Interest for deferment of advance tax in instalments — penalty on delayed installments. Adjustments: If tax deducted at source or tax collected at source, these are adjusted against total tax liability while computing advance tax.
  • TDS Provisions (Sections 192–194ZD): TDS rates vary by nature of payment: Salary u/s 192 — at prescribed rates; Interest on securities u/s 193 — 10%; Dividend u/s 194 — 10% (since FY 2020-21, dividend now taxable in hands of recipient); Deposit with banking co u/s 194A(3) — 10%; Insurance commission u/s 194DA — 5%; Winfrom lottery/crossword/horse race u/s 194B — 30% + cess; Payment to contractor/consultant u/s 194C — 1%-2% (10% if PAN not furnished); Payment to supplier u/s 194Q — 0.1% on purchase above ₹50L; Rent u/s 194I — 10% (2% for plant/machinery); FDC/payment to resident u/s 194BB — 10%; Stock transfer u/s 194T — 10%; Payment by Lottery/Bet u/s 194E — 30%. Section 194A(5): Interest on deposits with cooperative banking companies — nil TDS. Section 194K: Distribution by mutual fund — 10%. Section 194LA: Compensation on acquisition of immovable property — 10%. Section 194N: Cash withdrawal > ₹1Cr — 2% (1% if PAN furnished). Section 194Q: Purchase of goods — seller's TCS if buyer's turnover exceeds ₹10Cr in preceding PY.
  • Tax Audit u/s 44AB: Mandatory audit when: (a) Business — turnover exceeds ₹1Cr (₹10Cr if cash transactions ≤ 5% of total turnover/gross receipts); (b) Profession — professional receipts exceed ₹50L (₹100L if cash receipts ≤ 5%); (c) Presumptive taxation u/s 44ADA — if income declared is less than 60% of book income, audit may be required; (d) Presumptive u/s 44AD — if turnover exceeds ₹30L (₹3Cr for digital); (e) Presumptive u/s 44AE — vehicles exceeding prescribed number. Forms: CA certificate in Form 3CA/3CB with annexures 3CD. Time limit for completion: 30th September of PY immediately following relevant FY. Penalties u/s 271B: 0.5% of total sales/gross receipts or ₹1,50,000 — whichever is less. Records to be maintained per sec 44AA: Books of account, supporting documents, audit reports, etc.
  • Assessment Procedures (Sections 143–147): Default assessment u/s 143(1): Summary processing — intimation sent, discrepancies adjusted, interest charged/refund sanctioned. No hearing given to assessee. Assessment u/s 143(2): Where Assessing Officer (AO) considers it necessary to examine whether income has been understated or excess deduction claimed, notice u/s 143(2) is issued for proper assessment. Scrutiny assessment u/s 147: Where AO has reason to believe income chargeable to tax has escaped assessment, he may assess/ reassess after following due process (notice u/s 148) — time limit: up to 4 years from end of AY for regular escape, up to 6 years for income escaping assessment due to failure to disclose material facts. Retrospective amendment u/s 148A is required. Revision u/s 147 requires satisfaction of 'reason to believe'. Order u/s 144: Best judgement assessment when assessee fails to comply with notice or books are inadequate. Penalty u/s 270A: Under-reporting/ mis-reporting of income — 50%/100%/200% of tax differential depending on nature.
  • International Taxation — DTAA: Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement (DTAA) operates under sec 90/90A. In case of conflict between domestic law and DTAA, provisions of DTAA prevail over domestic law (superior provision). Article-wise analysis essential: Article 4 (Resident), Article 5 (Permanent Establishment — agency PE, construction PE, service PE, dependent agent), Article 8 (Shipping/Air transport — 50/50 rule for passenger/ freight income), Article 12 (Royalties — definition and rate ceilings), Article 13 (Capital gains — gains on immovable property taxable in SRS, gains on shares deriving >50% value from immovable property taxable in SRS), Article 21 (Other income — residuary article). Withholding tax rates under DTAA are generally lower than domestic TDS rates — e.g., royalty under India-USA DTAA is 15% versus 10% domestic. Fee for technical services (FTS) has separate rate ceilings. Branch remuneration, technical fees, management fees need careful treaty analysis.
  • Transfer Pricing (Sections 92–92F): Applicable to 'international transactions' and 'specified domestic transactions'. Arm's length price (ALP) — the price which would be charged between independent persons u/s 92C. Methods for determining ALP: (a) Comparable Uncontrolled Price (CUP); (b) Resale Price Method (RPM); (c) Cost Plus Method (CPM); (d) Profit Split Method (PSM); (e) Discounted Cash Flow (DCF); (f) TNMM (Transactional Net Margin Method). As per Amendment, DCF can only be used for evaluation of intangible usage. TNMM is most commonly used method for Indian assessees. Penalties u/s 271AA/271AAT: 2% of the amount of adjusted income for failure to maintain documents/reports; 100% to 300% of tax sought to be evaded for mis-reporting. Penalty u/s 270A also applicable. Transfer pricing adjustment increases total income, and surcharge @ 2% u/s 92CE is levied on adjusted income. Compliance: Form 3CEB (now largely merged with income tax return filing in schedule DP), Form 3CD, documentation requirements u/s 92D read with Rule 10D.
  • Anti-Avoidance Rules — GAAR: General Anti-Avoidance Rules (GAAR) introduced w.e.f. 1st April 2017 (AY 2017-18 onwards) to counter abusive tax avoidance arrangements. Defined u/s 99–101 and Rule 108–116. Key test: (a) Arrangement exists; (b) Purpose or effect is to obtain tax benefit; (c) Transaction lacks commercial substance OR is aberrant/malafide. Procedural safeguards: (a) Approval of Approving Panel (AP) for initiation of inquiry; (b) Show cause notice to assessee; (c) Right to be heard before passing order. GAAR does not apply to certain transactions including inter-group restructuring, listed company acquisitions above 75% shareholding, and transactions approved by AP. Principal purpose test (PPT) now part of India's treaty network per BEPS Action 6. India has opted for 'PPT + Principal Benefit Test' hybrid approach.
  • Presumptive Taxation Schemes: Sec 44AD: For eligible assessment years and assessees engaged in eligible business — presumptive income at 6% of turnover (digital transactions 4%). Applicable to residents (individual/HUF/ partnership firms, excluding LLP) with turnover ≤ ₹2Cr (₹3Cr for digital). Sec 44ADA: For eligible practitioners (doctors, engineers, architects, legal professionals, accountants, technical consultants, interior decorators, company secretaries, cost accountants, surveyors, financial consultants, insurance professionals) — presumptive income at 60% of gross receipts up to ₹75L, 50% above ₹75L. Sec 44AE: For persons carrying on business of plying/hiring/leasing goods caravans — presumptive income at ₹1,00,000 per month per vehicle (₹1,20,000 for hired vehicles). Sec 44BB: Non-residents in business of exploration of mineral oils — 5% of aggregate sum paid/ payable. Sec 44BBB: Foreign technical collaboration in specified projects — 5% of aggregate sum received/payable.
  • Penalties and Prosecution Provisions: Sec 270A: Under-reporting penalty @ 50% of tax on under-reported income; mis-reporting @ 100%; deliberate mis-reporting @ 200%. Under-reported income = assessed income minus determined income (positive). Sec 271: Concealment of income — penalty up to 200% of tax sought to be evaded (substituted by 270A). Sec 271AAD: Evidence of bogus entries in books — 100% of amount involved. Sec 271AAC: Search/seizure proceedings — 10% to 200% of undisclosed income. Sec 272A: Failure to furnish return/audit report — penalty up to ₹5,000 per default. Sec 274B: Failure to deduct/short-deduct TDS — 100% of tax evaded. Sec 276CC: Fraudulent refund claim — rigorous imprisonment 3–7 years + fine. Sec 276CD: Willful attempt to evade tax — imprisonment 1–4 years + fine. Compromise settlement u/s 245H before ITAT/authority is not available post introduction of new provisions.
  • Key Recent Amendments (FY 2024-25 Focus): LTCG on unlisted securities taxed at 20% with indexation from 23 July 2024 (retrospective from FY 2024-25). STCG on listed equity shares now taxed at 20% replacing 15% u/s 111A from 23 July 2024. Securities Transaction Tax (STT) paid is no longer admissible as deduction u/s 37(1). Increased limit of TDS u/s 194Q — threshold raised to ₹10Cr from ₹5Cr. Section 115BAC elections remain irrevocable once made. New regimedeemed to apply unless opted out. TDS u/s 194S on transfer of virtual digital assets — 1% (or higher if PAN not furnished). Surcharge cap u/s 84AA — surcharge not to exceed 25% for income above ₹2Cr (reduced from 37%). Interest on delayed TDS u/s 201(1A) enhanced to 1.5% p.m. (from 1%). Dividend distribution tax abolished — dividends now taxable in hands of shareholders. Angel investment u/s 80-IA(4) — exemption for startups on capital gains reinvestment. Faceless assessment expanded further with greater reliance on automated algorithms.

Important facts

  • Residential status is determined on a PY-by-PY basis, not permanently.
  • Section 90/90A gives overriding effect to DTAA over domestic provisions.
  • For a company, 'control and management' wholly in India = Resident; if wholly outside India = Non-Resident.
  • Perquisite valuation is based on rules in Rule 3 of IT Rules — different perquisites have different valuation mechanisms.
  • HRA exemption formula: Minimum of (actual HRA received), (50%/40% of basic+DA), (actual rent paid minus 10% of basic).
  • Pre-construction period interest u/s 24(b) is deductible in 5 equal annual instalments starting from AY next to the year of acquisition.
  • Capital gain indexing uses BLE (Base Year = 2001) indices for acquisition/improvement and transfer years respectively.
  • Section 80C limit is ₹1,50,000 — investments in PPF, LIC, NSC, 5-year FD, ELSS, tuition fees, etc. are eligible.
  • Section 80D health insurance limit: ₹25,000 (self/parents below 60); ₹50,000 (self/parents senior citizens).
  • TDS u/s 194C rates: 1% (contractor — individual/HUF), 2% (others); for non-residents 10%-20% depending on treaty.
  • Transfer pricing applies to 'international transactions' between AE — ALP must be determined using specified methods.
  • GAAR can be invoked only after approval of the Approving Panel (AP) constituted u/s 99(3).
  • Presumptive taxation u/s 44AD: 6% of turnover (4% for digital) for eligible businesses — applicable till turnover of ₹2Cr.
  • Interest u/s 234A: 1% per month or part thereof on delayed filing of return where tax liability exists.
  • Interest u/s 234B: 1% per month on shortfall of advance tax below 90% of assessed tax — from 1st April of PY.
  • Section 270A penalty for under-reporting: 50% of tax on difference; for mis-reporting: 100%; for deliberate mis-reporting: 200%.
  • Under-reporting of income u/s 20(24): assessed income minus determined income; if result is positive, penalty applies.
  • Surcharge rates: 10% (₹50L-₹1Cr), 15% (₹1Cr-₹2Cr), 25% (₹2Cr-₹5Cr), 37% (above ₹5Cr). Cap at 25% u/s 84AA from FY 2024-25.
  • Additional surcharge u/s 84B: 25% on dividend income of RVR where foreign income tax not paid.
  • Cess: 4% on total tax + surcharge — applied uniformly across all tax types.
  • Section 115BAC new regime is default unless assessee opts for old regime; election is irrevocable.
  • LTCG on unlisted shares/SIU now taxed at 20% with indexation from FY 2024-25 (post 23 July 2024 Finance Bill).

Memory aids

  • SALARY acronym: S — Standard deduction ₹50K, A — Allowances (HRA/LTA/leave encashment), L — Perquisites (Rule 3), A — Allowable deductions u/s 16, R — Exemptions u/s 10.
  • HOUSE acronym for HP income: H — Head ownership, O — Ownership conditions met, U — Unitary annual value, S — Standard 30% deduction, E — Expenditures deductible u/s 24.
  • BUDGET for Business income: B — Business/profession carried on, U — Unlimited deductions (revenue only), D — Disallowances u/s 40/40A, G — Goodwill/goods — capital never deductible, E — Expenditure wholly/exclusively for business, T — Taxes not deductible u/s 40(a)(ia).
  • GAINS mnemonic: G — Gain on Transfer, A — Arm's length price for capital assets, I — Indexation for LTCG, N — Net consideration minus Cost, E — Exemption u/s 54/54F/54EC.
  • CHAPTER mnemonic for Chapter VI-A deductions: C — 80C limit ₹1.5L, H — 80HUF (HUF deductions), A — 80A (aggregate), P — 80P (co-operative societies), T — 80T deductions, E — 80E education loan, R — 80R remittances.
  • DTAA structure: Treaties follow OECD/UN model — Articles 1-5 (scope/residence/PE), 6-9 (income classification), 10-21 (specific incomes), 22-29 (non-discrimination/resolution).
  • Transfer pricing methods mnemonic: CRITICAL — CUP, RPM, CPM, IPM/PSM, DCF, TNMM, Local comparables.
  • Penalty thresholds: 270A (50/100/200%), 271AA (2% of adjusted income), 271AAT (same as penalty on appeal).

Exam tips

  • For CMA Final taxation paper, focus on numerical problems involving computation of total income under different heads — especially Salary, HP, and Business income.
  • Practice at least 5-6 problems on residential status determination with mixed scenarios (NRI returning to India, Indian citizen abroad, etc.).
  • Master DTAA article-wise analysis — exam frequently asks for taxation of specific income (royalty, FTS, director's fee, capital gains) with reference to specific treaties.
  • Transfer pricing problems require understanding of ALP determination and documentation; practice questions involving adjustment of IPO/ALP.
  • GAAR questions often test knowledge of procedural safeguards — know the role of Approving Panel and timelines precisely.
  • For recent amendments (post-July 2024), prepare changes related to LTCG on unlisted shares, STCG rate change, surcharge cap, TDS on VDA, and new default regime implications.
  • Penalty questions under sec 270A require computing under-reported income — understand the formula: (assessed income − determined income) and applicable rates.
  • Always write section references in answers — examiners award marks for citing correct provisions even if numerical answer is wrong.
  • For international taxation, distinguish between domestic TDS rates and DTAA rates — and state which prevails.
  • Practice previous year's questions on computation of total income covering all five heads — at least 3-4 comprehensive problems per head.

Common traps

  • Trap: Assuming non-resident Indian (NRI) automatically means non-resident for income tax purposes. A person can be NRI under FEMA but still be a 'Resident' u/s 6 of IT Act.
  • Trap: Forfeited security deposit received on leaving employment is taxable u/s 17(3)(iii) as 'profit in lieu of salary', not as capital receipt.
  • Trap: Indexation benefit applies only to long-term capital gains — not to STCG. Many students confuse this.
  • Trap: Pre-construction period interest u/s 24(b) is claimed in 5 equal annual instalments — NOT in a single year. Commencement of construction does not affect this period.
  • Trap: Deduction u/s 80C is available only for payments made during the relevant PY — not merely committed. Actual payment must be made.
  • Trap: TDS u/s 194C applies to contractors AND sub-contractors — separate provisions exist, and rate depends on payee category (individual/HUF vs others).
  • Trap: GAAR is not applicable to every aggressive tax planning — only to 'impermissible avoidance arrangements' as defined u/s 99.
  • Trap: Section 44AD presumptive scheme cannot be invoked by a partnership firm having a partner who is a body corporate or HUF.
  • Trap: Dividend u/s 115A–115E were taxable in hands of shareholder only if dividend distribution tax (DDT) was not paid — but DDT is now abolished and dividends are fully taxable u/s 56(2)(x) in hands of recipient.
  • Trap: LTCG exemption u/s 54 requires investment in residential house within 2 years before or 1 year after transfer — not within any other period.
  • Trap: TDS u/s 194J (professional/technical fees) rate is 10% for residents — but for non-residents the treaty rate may be lower. Always check DTAA first.
  • Trap: Interest u/s 234B is charged from 1st April of PY even if return is filed later — it is not connected with late filing of return.
