1. Residential Status of Assessees and Its Impact on Tax Liability

Residential status is the foundational determinant of an assessee's tax liability in India. It governs the scope of total income chargeable to tax under Section 5 of the Income-tax Act, 1961.

Categories of Residential Status:
- Resident and Ordinarily Resident (ROR)
- Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR)
- Non-Resident (NR)

Conditions for 'Resident' under Section 6 (FY 2024-25 / AY 2025-26):
For Individuals/HUF: Basic condition — stay in India for ≥ 182 days in the preceding FY OR ≥ 60 days in the preceding FY + ≥ 365 days in 4 preceding years combined. The 60-day limit is extended to 182 days for Indian citizens/PIOs leaving India for employment or visiting India during FY when no income tax is payable outside India.
For Companies: Ordinarily resident if incorporated in India. For foreign companies, ordinarily resident if Indian control and management throughout the preceding PY is in India; otherwise non-resident.
For AOPs/BOIs: Ordinarily resident if control and management is situated in India throughout the PY; otherwise check individual member residency test.

Ordinary Residence Conditions (Section 6(6)): An assessee is 'ordinarily resident' only if:
(a) He has been resident in India in at least 2 out of 10 previous years immediately preceding the relevant PY; AND
(b) He has stayed in India for ≥ 730 days during the 7 preceding previous years.
Failure to satisfy either condition makes him 'Not Ordinarily Resident'.

Scope of Total Income — Section 5:
- Resident and Ordinarily Resident: Global income — all income received/deemed received in India or accrued/ arisen/ deemed to accrue in India or outside India.
- Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident: India-sourced income + income received/deemed received in India + income accrued/arising outside India from a business controlled or profession set up in India.
- Non-Resident: Only income received/deemed received in India or accrued/arising/deemed to accrue/arise in India.

Key Case Law: In CIT v. Morgan Stanley (2007) 292 ITR 32 (SC), the Supreme Court held that FDI portfolio investment gains are taxable under the head 'Capital Gains' and not 'Business Income', emphasizing the distinction between strategic and portfolio investment.

Practical Implication for CMAs: When advising MNC clients on structuring operations, residential status determines whether foreign branch profits, overseas royalties, and export income attract Indian tax. RNOR status provides partial protection against global taxation, making it a strategic consideration for expatriate assignments.

2. Concept of 'Income' — Deemed Receipt, Accrual and Charges to Tax

The term 'income' under the Act encompasses receipt, accrual, arising, and deemed accrual. Understanding these concepts is critical for timing of taxation.

Receipt vs. Accrual:
- Receipt requires actual physical receipt or legal right to receive (money or money's worth). Cash system vs. mercantile system of accounting applies to determine recognition.
- Accrual means the right to receive income becomes legally enforceable, even if not yet received. Under mercantile system, income is taxed on accrual basis regardless of actual receipt.

Deemed Receipt and Deemed Accrual (Sections 4, 5, 145):
- Section 4: All income deemed to accrue/arise in India includes income which accrues through any business control/profession set up in India, income from any asset situated in India or intangible right used in India.
- Deemed income provisions (e.g., perquisites, gifts, sum received from ESOP, property transfer without adequate consideration) expand the scope beyond actual cash flows.

Charges to Tax (Section 4): Tax is charged on total income of every person at the rate prescribed by the Finance Act. Every person falling within the definition of 'person' under Section 2(31) — including individuals, HUF, AOP, BOI, artificial judicial person, companies, firms, local authorities — is liable.

Previous Year concept: Income is charged to tax in the year following the year in which it is earned (PY). Assessment Year (AY) is the period from 1 April to 31 March next to the PY.

Method of Accounting (Section 145): Income must be computed in accordance with the method regularly employed by the assessee — cash or mercantile. Change is permitted only with prior approval of the Assessing Officer. Consistency is mandatory; if the method produces inflated income, the AO may reject it under Section 145(2).

Important Judicial Pronouncement: In CIT v. Calcutta Press (1995) 214 ITR 622 (SC), the Supreme Court held that income is earned when the right to receive it accrues — not when it is actually received. This reinforces the accrual principle over the mere receipt test.

CMA Relevance: For cost-accounting professionals dealing with transfer pricing and intercompany transactions, understanding when revenue is recognized for tax purposes versus accounting purposes is essential to manage deferred tax implications and working capital planning.

3. Heads of Income — Salaries, House Property, Profits and Gains of Business/Profession

Under Section 14, all income is classified under five heads. The first three heads are detailed below with CMA-relevant emphasis.

HEAD 1: SALARIES (Section 15-17B)
Taxable components include wages, pension, gratuity, remuneration, fees, commissions, perquisites, profits in lieu of salary, and advance of salary. Key deductions:
- Standard deduction: ₹50,000 (Section 16(ia)) — applicable to all salaried individuals.
- Professional tax: Actual payment, maximum ₹2,500 per month (Section 16(ii)).
- Family pension: ⅓ of family pension or ₹15,000, whichever is less (Section 57(iii)).
- Commuted pension: Full exemption for government servants; for others, depends on whether provident fund is recognized (Section 10(10a)).

Perquisites (Section 17(2)): Valuation rules differ by type:
- Rent-free accommodation: Govt — actual cost; Employer-provided — 10% of salary or actual rent paid/fair rent minus concession.
- Specified securities/ESOP: Value = FMV on exercise date minus amount paid.
- Transport: 3,000/month standard deduction if employer provides transport facility; otherwise actual value.

Perquisites NOT taxable: Medical reimbursement up to ₹15,000 (Section 17(2)(vi)), mobile phone provided by employer, leave travel concession (LTC) under specified conditions.

HEAD 2: HOUSE PROPERTY (Section 22-28)
Annual Value (AV) determination follows a hierarchy: Expected AV (fair rent × extent of standard area) vs. Actual Rent Received — higher of the two, subject to municipal ceiling. Deductions from AV:
- 30% standard deduction (Section 24(a)) — flat one-third, no proof required.
- Interest on borrowed capital (Section 24(b)): Up to ₹2,00,000 for self-occupied; no upper limit for let-out property (but pre-construction period interest limited to ₹2,00,000 in 5 equal annual instalments).
- Municipal taxes paid (Section 23(1)): Only deducted if paid by owner.
Note: For SVP (self-occupied property), if AV < 0 (due to high interest/municipal taxes), AV is taken as zero; negative AV cannot be set off against other heads.

HEAD 3: PROFITS AND GAINS OF BUSINESS OR PROFESSION (PGP) — Section 28-44DA
Incomes chargeable under PGP include profit from business, speculation business, professional earnings, fees, and income from commission/employment.

Deductions under Chapter VI-A (Sections 30-43B) include:
- Section 30: Rent, rates, taxes, repairs, insurance of premises used for business.
- Section 31: Depreciation, insurance of plant/machinery.
- Section 32: Normal and written-down value (WDV) depreciation at prescribed rates.
- Section 32A: Investment allowance for new plant/machinery in specified industries (50% of investment).
- Section 35: Scientific research expenditure (175% deduction for approved labs), advertisement/marketing (33⅓% for certain media), sponsorship (100%).
- Section 35D: Preliminary/expansion/capital repair expenditure amortization over 5 years.
- Section 35DD: Expenditure on staff training in specified sectors (textiles, construction, etc.).
- Section 35E: Development fees for infrastructure projects.
- Section 35CC: Education/technical training expenditure (100% deduction).
- Section 35AD: 100% deduction for specified businesses (hotels, cold storage, warehousing).
- Section 35ABA: Deduction for agricultural research (150% for notified institutions).
- Section 35AC: Deduction for infrastructure project expenditure (100% in PY of completion).
- Section 35AD: Digital media/content creation — 100% deduction.
- Section 35CCC: Rural development infrastructure (100% deduction).
- Section 35AD: E-commerce marketplace infrastructure (100% deduction).
- Section 35AD: Specified business infrastructure (cinema halls, hotels above star rating).
- Section 43B: Deductions for payments of tax, duty, toll, fee, contribution to PF/ESI, bonus to workers, commission to agents — deductible ONLY when actually paid (not accrued). Payment must be made before due date of filing return.
- Section 43BA: Interest on overdue payments to MSMEs — disallowed unless paid with interest.

Presumptive Taxation (Sections 44ADA, 44AE, 44AAC, 44AF):
- Section 44ADA: Specified professionals (medical, legal, engineering, architectural, accountancy, technical consultancy, interior design, certification) — 50% of gross receipts (≤ ₹75 lakh) deemed profit.
- Section 44AE: Goods transport owners — prescribed rate per vehicle per month.
- Section 44AAF: Not commonly invoked currently; relates to export of computer software.
- Section 44AF: Commission/brokerage businesses — 7.5% of turnover.

Loss Carry Forward Conditions (Section 24 & 72-73): Business loss can be carried forward for 8 assessment years only if return is filed on time (Section 139(1) deadline). Speculation loss carry forward requires return filing. Loss from speculative business can be set off only against speculative income. Short-term capital loss can be set off against any capital gain. Long-term capital loss can be set off only against LTCG. Unabsorbed depreciation can be carried forward indefinitely and is treated as depreciation of the current year — set off against any income.

4. Capital Gains — Computation and Exemptions

Capital gains arise on transfer of capital assets. The computation framework under Sections 45-55A is extensive.

Definition of Capital Asset (Section 2(14)): Any property of any kind held by an assessee, excluding:
- Stock-in-trade
- Personal effects (jewellery, vehicles, furniture, etc.) — but diamond jewellery, art paintings, sculptures, stamps, coins above ₹10 lakh are capital assets
- Agricultural land in rural India (beyond specified limits)
- SGLA bonds, notified bonds, gold notification bonds held by individuals/HUF

Short-term vs Long-term Capital Assets:
- Listed equity shares/units of equity-oriented funds: Holding period > 12 months → LTC; ≤ 12 months → STC.
- Immovable property (land, building, goodwill, tramway systems): Holding period > 24 months → LTC; ≤ 24 months → STC.
- All other capital assets: Holding period > 36 months → LTC; ≤ 36 months → STC.

Indexation (Section 48): For LTCG on non-equity assets, cost of acquisition and improvement are indexed using Cost Inflation Index (CII). This reduces taxable gain by accounting for inflation.

Computation Steps (Section 48-55):
1. Full value of consideration (FVC) — Section 48
2. Less: Cost of acquisition (indexed for LTC) — Section 48
3. Less: Expenditure wholly/exclusively on transfer — Section 48
4. = Capital Gain (STCG/LTCG)
5. Less: Exemptions under Section 54/54EC/54F/54G/54GB/54D — Sections 54-55
6. = Taxable capital gain

Special Provisions:
- Section 50: Depreciable assets — FVC less WDV = short-term capital gain (no indexation benefit).
- Section 50B: Transfer of block of assets — entire block treated as one asset; full consideration less WDV = gain/loss.
- Section 55: Cost of acquisition — inheritance (FMV on date of transfer by predecessor); gift ( FMV on date of gift); mutual fund switch (original cost).

Exemptions (Key Sections):
- Section 54: Residential house — reinvestment in another residential house within 2 years before/1 year after transfer, or construct within 3 years. Exemption = proportionate gain reinvested. Section 54F covers exemption for transfer of any long-term capital asset other than house property — similar conditions apply but requires investment of full sale consideration.
- Section 54EC: Investment in specified bonds (NHAI/REC) — up to ₹50 lakh per PY.
- Section 54D: Rehabilitation of displaced persons.
- Section 54G: Shift from urban to industrial area — machinery and land.
- Section 54GB: Equity shares of eligible start-ups — conditional exemption for LTCG on house property.
- Section 54DB: Medical treatment abroad — investment in govt securities within 1 year.
- Section 54F(5A): Investment in house property (rented) within 3 years.

STCG on Equity (Section 111A): 15% tax on gains from listed equity shares/units if STT paid. Additional surcharge + cess applicable.

LTCG on Equity (Section 112A): 10% tax on gains exceeding ₹1 lakh (without indexation benefit). Gains up to ₹1 lakh exempt.

Case Law Highlight: In CIT v. Panampilly Menon (1990) 183 ITR 417 (SC), the Supreme Court held that cancellation of share application money is a capital receipt and not revenue receipt, highlighting the distinction between capital and revenue transactions.

5. Set Off, Carry Forward and Set Off of Losses

The mechanics of set off and carry forward of losses under Chapters V and VI of the Act are crucial for tax planning and compliance.

Current Year Set Off (Sections 70-74):
- Section 70: Inter-head set off — loss from one head can be set off against income from another head (except where specific provisions restrict).
- Section 71: Intra-head set off — loss under one head can be set off against income under the same head.
- Section 72: Business loss (non-speculation) — set off against any head except Salary. Brought forward only if return filed on time (Section 139(1)). Available for 8 assessment years.
- Section 72A: Loss from specified industrial undertaking (SEZ, backward area, etc.) — set off only against business income of same eligible business.
- Section 73: Speculation loss — set off only against speculation business income. Carry forward for 4 years.
- Section 74: Capital loss — STCL set off against STCG; LTCL set off against LTCG. STCL carry forward 7 years; LTCL carry forward indefinitely (subject to return filing condition).
- Section 74A: Loss from horse races — set off only against income from horse races. Carry forward 4 years.

Key Restrictions:
- Unabsorbed depreciation: Treated as depreciation of current year — can be set off against any income including salary. No time limit for carry forward.
- Loss from speculative business: Cannot be set off against salary income. Must be carried forward if not set off in current year.
- Set off of foreign income: Income from foreign sources cannot be set off against Indian income.

Order of Set Off (Current Year):
1. Intra-head set off
2. Inter-head set off
3. Set off of brought forward losses
4. Set off of unabsorbed depreciation

Carry Forward Requirements:
- Return must be filed on or before the due date under Section 139(1) for most losses (business loss, speculation loss, capital loss under Section 74(2)).
- Unabsorbed depreciation does not require timely return filing for carry forward.
- For carry forward of unabsorbed depreciation/set off of loss, return filing is mandatory under Section 139(1).

Practical Illustration: If an assessee has business loss of ₹5 lakh, STCG of ₹3 lakh, and LTCG of ₹8 lakh:
- Business loss can be set off against STCG and LTCG (₹3L + ₹2L = full offset)
- Remaining business loss of ₹2L carried forward for 8 years
- STCG of ₹0 (fully set off) but STCG brought forward set off first against remaining STCG
- Note: Set off order matters significantly for optimal tax planning

6. Deductions from Gross Total Income — Chapter VI-A (Sections 80C to 80U)

Chapter VI-A provides deductions from GTI to arrive at Total Income. These are among the most frequently tested provisions.

Section 80C (₹1,50,000 limit): EPF, PPF, NSC, life insurance premium, tuition fees (2 children), ELSS, NSSF, senior citizen savings scheme, post office monthly income scheme, repayment of home loan principal, SCSS, KVP, unit-linked insurance plans (ULIP) — subject to conditions.

Section 80CCC (₹1,50,000 aggregate with 80C): Premium paid for annuity/pension plan.

Section 80CCD(1) — NPS: Employee contribution up to 10% of salary (20% for unorganized sector). Additional deduction under 80CCD(1B) — extra ₹50,000 beyond 80C limit.

Section 80CCD(2) — Employer NPS contribution: Up to 10% of salary (basic + DA). Not included in 80C limit.

Section 80D (Health Insurance): Self/family: Individual ≤ 60 years — ₹25,000; Senior citizen — ₹50,000. Parents: same limits. Additional ₹5,000 for preventive health check-up (included in overall limit).

Section 80DD (Disabled Dependent): ₹75,000 (40% disability) to ₹2,00,000 (severe disability) depending on degree of disability.

Section 80DDB (Medical Treatment): ₹40,000 (individual ≤ 60 years); ₹1,00,000 (senior citizen) for specified diseases on prescription.

Section 80E (Education Loan): Interest component only — no ceiling, available for 8 years or until fully paid, whichever is earlier. Applies to higher education in India or abroad.

Section 80G (Charitable Donations): 50% or 100% deduction depending on donor — 10% of gross total income ceiling applies for 50% donations.

Section 80GG (Rent Paid): Up to ₹50,000 per annum or 25% of adjusted total income, whichever is less. Conditional on not receiving HRA and not owning residential property.

Section 80TTA (Savings Account Interest): Up to ₹10,000 for individuals and HUF (other than senior citizens). For senior citizens, Section 80TTB applies — up to ₹50,000 on interest income from deposits.

Section 80TTB (Senior Citizens — Interest Income): Up to ₹50,000 for senior citizens from interest on deposits with banks, post office, cooperative banks.

Section 80U (Self Disability): ₹75,000 (40-60% disability) to ₹1,25,000 (severe disability ≥ 80%) for individuals.

Section 80TTB and 80TTA Distinction: 80TTB is for senior citizens; 80TTA is for individuals/HUF below 60 years. Both cover savings account/d deposit interest.

Section 80LA — Special Economic Zone (SEZ): 100% deduction for first 5 years, 30% for next 5 years, and 30% of ploughed-back profit for next 5 years (proportionate to export growth) — subject to conditions.

Section 80IA — Infrastructure/Industrial Projects: 100% for first 5 years, 30% for next 10 years (for SEZ units), with conditions related to export obligation and new infrastructure.

Section 80IB — Specific Industries: 100% for 5 years from commencement of business (hotels, hospitals, power generation, etc.), then 30% thereafter.

Section 80-IHD — Newly Incorporated Manufacturing/Production Companies: 100% deduction for first 5 years, 30% for next 5 years on exported profits.

Section 80-IED — Start-ups: 100% deduction on profits for 3 consecutive years out of 10 years from incorporation, subject to conditions.

Important Restriction: Deductions under Sections 80C to 80U are allowed from GTI. If GTI is negative (loss), these deductions are not available. Section 80CCD(1B) deduction is additional to the 80C limit of ₹1,50,000, making maximum deduction possible ₹2,00,000 under 80C/80CCC/80CCD(1)/80CCD(1B).

7. Advance Pricing Agreements (APA) and Transfer Pricing

Transfer pricing (TP) and Advance Pricing Agreements (APA) are central to CMA Final taxation paper, especially in the international taxation context.

Transfer Pricing Framework (Sections 92-92F):
- Section 92C: ARM (Arm's Length Price) — price which would be charged between independent persons. Methods include CUP, Resale Price, Cost Plus, Profit Split, and DMTA (Dominant Margin Test Analysis).
- Section 92CC: Documentation requirements — Form 3CEB (TP report) must be furnished by a chartered accountant.
- Section 92CB: Prescribed methods — CUP, Resale Price, Cost Plus, Profit Split, DMTA, Any other prescribed method.

Advance Pricing Agreement (APA) — Sections 92C-C and 92CA:
An APA is an agreement between the assessee and the Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs (CBDT) determining the ARM in advance for specified international transactions over a specified period (typically 3-5 years).

Types of APA:
- Unilateral APA: Between taxpayer and Indian tax authority only (no coverage in other treaty jurisdiction).
- Bilateral APA: Under Mutual Agreement Procedure (MAP) — involves tax authorities of both countries (India and counterpart treaty country).
- Multilateral APA: Involves three or more jurisdictions.

Benefits of APA: Certainty in TP pricing, avoidance of litigation, reduced compliance burden, and protection against worst-case TP adjustments.

Eligibility: Any person engaged in international transactions or specified domestic transactions (SDTs) can apply. Transactions must be ongoing.

Procedure (Rule 10AA-10AE): Application in Form 34IC to International Taxation and Advisory (ITA) cell. Screened for eligibility. Then substantive proceedings commence. Hearing opportunities given. APA determined based on submitted data and analysis.

Special Domestic Transactions (SDTs) — Section 92BA: Certain transactions between domestic associates are treated as international transactions for TP purposes. Threshold: transactions exceeding ₹1 crore (₹10 crore for specified domestic transactions from FY 2015-16 onwards).

Penalties (Section 270A): Underreporting penalty — 50% of tax shortfall; misreporting penalty — 200% of tax shortfall. TP adjustment constitutes underreporting if ARM not adopted correctly.

Safe Harbour Rules (Rule 10B): CBIC provides safe harbour thresholds: ±5% for goods, ±5% for services, ±10% for intellectual property. Within threshold, TP adjustment not made.

Case Law: In DIT v. Vodafone Holdings (2012) 342 ITR 343 (SC), the Supreme Court held that Vodafone's share purchase was an offshore transaction and not taxable in India, emphasizing the substance-over-form principle and the limitations of indirect transfers before the 2012 amendment.

8. Double Taxation Avoidance Agreements (DTAA) and Relevant Case Law

DTAA (also called Tax Treaty) provisions prevent dual taxation of the same income in two countries. India has DTAA with over 90 countries.

Key Principles of DTAA:
- Residency Tie-breaker: If a person is resident in both countries, tie-breaker rules determine sole residency — usually based on permanent home, centre of vital interests, habitual abode, nationality.
- Permanent Establishment (PE) — Article 5: A fixed place of business through which business of an enterprise is wholly or partly carried on. Includes construction PE (≥ 6 or 12 months depending on treaty), agency PE, and service PE.
- Attribution of Profits — Article 7: Only profits attributable to PE in India are taxable in India. Force of attraction principle may apply depending on treaty.
- Limitation on Benefits (LOB) Clause: Prevents treaty shopping — only residents who are also 'qualified persons' (substantial business presence, public trading, etc.) can claim treaty benefits.

Important Articles:
- Article 8: Shipping and Air Transport — profits taxable only in home state.
- Article 10: Dividends — withholding tax limited to 5% (10% for portfolio investors).
- Article 11: Interest — typically 10% or 15% withholding.
- Article 12: Royalties/Fees for Technical Services — typically 10-15% withholding.
- Article 13: Capital Gains — gains from transfer of immovable property taxable in source country; other gains generally taxable only in residence country.
- Article 23: Elimination of Double Taxation — credit method or exemption method.

Specific Indian DTAA Provisions:
- Withholding Tax Rates: Vary by treaty country. Common rates: dividends 5-15%, interest 10-15%, royalties 10-15%, FTS 10-20%.
- Permanent Establishment Tests: Construction PE threshold varies — 6 months (common), 12 months (some treaties), or not provided.

Tax Residency Certificate (TRC): Required to claim DTAA benefits under Section 90. Must be obtained from the country of residence.

Key Case Laws:
- CIT v. Vivendi Universal (2007) 296 ITR 434 (SC): Held that dividends received by a French company from Indian subsidiary are taxable in France under DTAA; India cannot tax them.
- Azadi Bachao Andolan (2003) 263 ITR 708 (SC): SC upheld the validity of WTTO (Withholding Tax Treaty Option) and held that tax havens can be used legitimately.
- Vodafone Holdings (2012) 342 ITR 343 (SC): Offshore transactions not taxable in India.
- Union of India v. Azadi Bachao Andolan (2003): DTAA benefits cannot be denied merely because受益人 resides in a tax haven.

Section 90(2) — Provision More Beneficial to Assessee: If DTAA provides lower rate or broader exemption than domestic law, DTAA prevails (provided section 90 conditions met).

9. Advance Rulings, Special Audit, and Investigating Authorities

Advance rulings provide certainty on tax matters before transactions are executed. The mechanism operates through the Authority for Advance Rulings (AAR) now reconstituted as the Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs (CBIC) Advanced Ruling Authority.

Advance Rulings (Sections 245L-245RR):
- Applicable to residents and non-residents engaged in international transactions.
- Can seek ruling on: (a) taxability of proposed transaction; (b) computation of GTI; (c) eligibility for DTAA benefits; (d) validity of APA application; (e) any other matter referred by CBIC.
- Application in Form 245Q within 6 months of proposed transaction or 12 months from end of relevant PY.
- Ruling binding on applicant and CBDT/Assessing Officer.
- Appeal to ITAT within 60 days.

Special Audit (Section 142(2A)):
- AO can appoint a CA for audit where his satisfaction is that accounts are not correct/complete or principles of accounting have not been followed consistently.
- Auditor reports in Form 3CD (extended to cover TP aspects).
- Time limit: 180 days (extendable).
- Costs borne by assessee unless AO directs government to pay.

Investigation Wing (IW) and Directorate of Enforcement (ED):
- IW investigates cases of suppression, evasion, and serious tax fraud.
- ED operates under PMLA for money laundering cases connected to tax evasion.
- Search and seizure under Section 132; notice under Section 131 (for producing documents).

Penalty Provisions:
- Section 270A: Undervaluation/misreporting penalty — 50%-200% of tax shortfall.
- Section 271(1)c: Concealment of income — 100%-500% of tax evaded.
- Section 271AA: TP documentation penalty — 0.5% of underreported income.
- Section 271BA: Failure to furnish TP report — ₹1,00,000.
- Section 271BA: Failure to furnish Form 3CEB — ₹1,00,000 per default day.
- Section 272A: Failure to furnish returns/documents — ₹500/day.
- Section 276CC: Willful attempt to evade tax — imprisonment up to 7 years + fine.
- Section 276D: Tax evasion — imprisonment up to 3 years + fine.

Disclosure of Foreign Assets (Schedule FA):
- Return of income must disclose foreign assets (bank accounts, securities, immovable property, etc.).
- Non-disclosure attracts penalty under Section 271FA — 10% of undisclosed foreign income + tax on such income.

10. Recent Amendments, Key Provisions, and Tax Planning for Corporates

The CMA Final examination tests knowledge of both foundational principles and recent legislative developments.

Key Recent Developments (FY 2024-25 / AY 2025-26):
- Corporate Tax Rate Stability: New regime corporate tax rate remains at 22% (plus surcharge and cess) for manufacturing companies; old regime at 25% for companies satisfying certain conditions. Opted-out regime at 30% (plus surcharge).
- Deferred Tax Accounting (AS 22 / Ind AS 12): Deferred tax assets/liabilities arise from timing differences between accounting profit and taxable profit. Recognize DTA only if probable future taxable profit exists.
- Minimum Alternate Tax (MAT) — Section 115JC: 18.5% (plus surcharge) on book profit for companies. MAT credit available under Section 115JAA — can be carried forward for 15 years.
- Alternative Minimum Tax (AMT) — Section 115JB: Applies to companies and LLPs at 18.5% on adjusted book profit. AMT credit carried forward for 15 years under Section 115JBAA.
- Presumptive Taxation Expansion: Section 44ADA expanded to more professions. Threshold increase to ₹75 lakh for specified professionals.
- Start-up Incentives: Section 80-IHD and Section 80-IHBD provide 100% deduction on profits for eligible start-ups in specified sectors (electronics hardware, semiconductor, medical devices).
- Angel Investor Tax Benefits: Section 56(2)(viib) relaxed for investments from SEBI-registered angel funds.

Tax Planning Strategies for Corporates:
- Utilize depreciation benefits under Section 32 (WDV method).
- Take advantage of Section 35 deductions for R&D.
- Plan capital structure to optimize interest deduction under Section 36(1)(iii).
- Use Section 44AB for audit thresholds — maintain books if turnover exceeds ₹1 crore (₹10 crore if 95% payments/receipts via banking channels).
- Consider DTAA benefits for overseas transactions to minimize withholding tax.
- Structure intercompany transactions at ARM to avoid TP adjustments.
- Utilize MAT credit entitlement for future tax planning.
- Plan dividend distribution to minimize tax incidence under Section 115-O.
- Leverage Section 80P(2)(a)(iv) for cooperative societies.

Important Judicial Trends:
- CIT v. Dell Software (2022): SC held that mere use of software does not constitute PE.
- CIT v. Google (2021): HC held that Google's advertising revenue from Indian users is not taxable as FTS.
- CIT v. Vivendi (2007): SC decision remains landmark on DTAA interpretation.
- Azadi Bachao Andolan (2003): Upheld legitimacy of treaty shopping.

CMA-Specific Focus Areas:
- Understand both the legal provisions AND their business implications.
- Be able to compute taxes under both regular and alternative regimes.
- Apply transfer pricing principles to practical scenarios involving MNC group companies.
- Advise on tax-efficient structuring of cross-border transactions.

Important facts

  • Residential status determines scope of total income: ROR = global income; RNOR = India income + foreign income from Indian business/control; NR = India-sourced income only.
  • Standard deduction for salaried individuals: ₹50,000 (Section 16(ia)).
  • Maximum deduction under Chapter VI-A: ₹1,50,000 under Section 80C (includes 80CCC); additional ₹50,000 under Section 80CCD(1B) for NPS.
  • STCG on equity (Section 111A): 15% with STT; LTCG on equity (Section 112A): 10% on gains exceeding ₹1 lakh without indexation.
  • Holdings period for LTC vs STC: Listed equity/units = 12 months; Immovable property = 24 months; Other assets = 36 months.
  • Written Down Value (WDV) depreciation is the normal method; straight-line method available for select assets under Section 32(1).
  • Section 43B deductions (tax, duty, PF, interest to MSME) are deductible only on actual payment — not on accrual.
  • Transfer Pricing: ARM = Arm's Length Price. Methods: CUP, Resale Price, Cost Plus, Profit Split, DMTA.
  • APA types: Unilateral, Bilateral (under MAP), Multilateral. Application in Form 34IC.
  • MAT rate: 18.5% on book profit; AMT rate: 18.5% on adjusted book profit. Both credits carried forward for 15 years.
  • Section 90(2): If DTAA is more beneficial than domestic law, DTAA prevails (provided section 90 conditions met).
  • PE threshold for construction contracts: Usually 6 or 12 months depending on treaty; no PE若无固定场所则不构成常设机构。
  • Trust Income: Trusts registered under Section 12A/12AB enjoy exemption under Section 10(23C) on qualifying income.
  • Option for new corporate tax regime: Lower rate (22% for manufacturing) but cannot claim most exemptions/deductions.
  • Due date for filing return of income for companies: 31st October (if audit required) or 31st July (if no audit required) of relevant AY.

Formulas and relationships

Annual Value (Let-out Property) = Higher of Expected AV and Actual Rent Received, less Municipal Taxes paid
Deduction u/s 24(b) for let-out = Interest on borrowed capital (max ₹2,00,000 for SVP; unlimited for let-out)
Net Annual Value = Annual Value less Standard Deduction (30%) less Interest on Borrowed Capital
Indexed Cost of Acquisition = Actual Cost × (CII of year of transfer / CII of year of acquisition)
Capital Gain = Full Value of Consideration less Indexed Cost of Acquisition less Expenditure on Transfer less Exemptions
Mat/Amt Surcharge applicable: 12% if total income > ₹5 crore but ≤ ₹10 crore; 15% if > ₹10 crore (FY 2024-25 rates may vary — verify with latestFinance Act)
Book Profit (for MAT) = Net Profit as per P&L adjusted as per Sections 115JA(1)(a) to (s)
Adjusted Book Profit (for AMT) = Book Profit adjusted as per Sections 115JB(1)(b) to (xv)
Deduction u/s 80HHA = Lower of: (a) 8% of Adjusted GTI or (b) ₹3,00,000 (for individual/HUF without business income)
Speculative Business Loss Carry Forward = 4 years (Section 73)
Non-Speculative Business Loss Carry Forward = 8 years (Section 72)
STCG Tax Rate = 15% (Section 111A) on gains from listed equity if STT paid
LTCG Tax Rate = 10% (Section 112A) on gains exceeding ₹1,00,000 from listed equity (no indexation)
TP Adjustment = ARM determined price less actual price charged in international transaction
DTAA Credit Method = Foreign tax paid credited against Indian tax liability on same income

Common mistakes

  • Confusing residential status with citizenship — foreign citizen can be resident if staying in India ≥ 182 days.
  • Forgetting that RNOR's foreign income from foreign business is taxable in India only if controlled from India.
  • Applying indexation benefit on equity shares — NO indexation for equity LTCG (Section 112A).
  • Mixing up STCG and LTCG holding periods — 12 months for equity, 24 months for property, 36 months for others.
  • Treating unabsorbed depreciation as a separate loss — it is treated as current year depreciation and carries forward indefinitely.
  • Assuming Section 43B deduction is available on accrual — it is strictly on actual payment basis.
  • Ignoring the timely filing requirement for carrying forward business losses — Section 139(1) deadline is mandatory.
  • Overlooking that deduction under Section 80CCD(1B) is OVER AND ABOVE the ₹1,50,000 limit of Section 80C.
  • Treating TP adjustments as penalties rather than income additions — penalty is separate under Section 270A.
  • Forgetting that APA ruling is binding only on the applicant and CBDT, not on other assesses with similar transactions.
  • Not distinguishing between Section 92C (ARM) and Section 92CA (TP determination by TPO) — latter is for disputed cases.
  • Assuming all foreign income is exempt under DTAA — only items specifically covered by treaty articles get relief.
  • Confusing the scope of 'perquisite' taxation — ESOP perquisite value = FMV at exercise minus exercise price.
  • Ignoring that Section 54 exemption requires actual investment in house property within specified timelines — mere intention is insufficient.
  • Forgetting to deduct standard deduction (30%) under Section 24(a) while computing house property income — very common calculation error.
