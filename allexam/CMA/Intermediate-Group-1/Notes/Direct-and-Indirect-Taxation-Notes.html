1. Introduction to Direct Taxation – Basic Framework and Residential Status

The Income Tax Act, 1961 is the primary legislation governing direct taxes in India. It applies to every 'person' who receives income, whether or not such income is received in India. A 'person' includes an individual, HUF, company, firm, AOP, BOI, local authority, and every artificial juridical person. The Act applies to all India except Jammu & Kashmir.

Residential status is determined annually based on the previous year and is crucial for determining the scope of total income taxable in India. There are three categories:
(a) Resident – Ordinary Resident (ROR)
(b) Resident – Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR)
(c) Non-Resident (NR)

An individual is 'Resident' in India if he satisfies at least one of the following conditions:
- He is in India for 182 days or more during the previous year; OR
- He is in India for 60 days or more during the previous year AND 365 days or more during the 4 years immediately preceding the previous year.

For Indian citizens/Person of Indian Origin (PIO) visiting India, the 60-day limit is extended to 182 days. For individuals leaving India for employment outside India, the 60-day limit is also extended to 182 days. For Indian citizens/PIO coming on a visit to India, the same exception applies.

For determining Residential Status of Individuals, the following additional tests apply:
An individual is 'Resident and Ordinarily Resident' (ROR) if he is resident AND satisfies both conditions:
- He has been resident in India in at least 2 out of the 10 previous years immediately preceding the relevant previous year.
- He has stayed in India for at least 730 days during the 10 previous years immediately preceding the relevant previous year.
If either condition fails, the individual is 'Resident and Not Ordinarily Resident' (RNOR).

For firms, AOPs, BOIs, and companies:
- Firm/AOP/BOI: Resident if control and management is wholly/partially in India during the previous year.
- Company: Resident if Indian company OR if control and management is wholly in India during the previous year.

Scope of total income depends on residential status:
- ROR: Global income (India + foreign income) is taxable.
- RNOR: Income received/deemed received in India + income from business controlled/profession set up in India (foreign income is exempt unless derived from business/control/profession in India).
- NR: Only income received or deemed received in India is taxable.

2. Heads of Income – Salary, House Property, and Capital Gains

Income is classified under five heads:
(a) Salary
(b) Income from House Property
(c) Profits and Gains of Business or Profession
(d) Capital Gains
(e) Income from Other Sources

SALARY (Section 15-17):
Salary includes wages, pension, gratuity, fees, commission, perquisites, profits in lieu of salary, and advance of salary. Income from former employer is also treated as salary.

Perquisites (Section 17(2)): Perquisites are benefits provided by the employer over and above salary. Types include:
- Rent-free accommodation (Section 17(2)(i))
- Contributed to a recognized provident fund (Section 17(2)(iii))
- Valuation of specified perquisites (Section 17(2)(ii))

Taxable value of perquisite for rent-free accommodation:
- If government accommodation: actual cost
- If accommodation owned by employer in a city where stamp duty value ≥ ₹10,00,000: 15% of salary
- If accommodation owned by employer in other cities: 10% of salary
- If accommodation taken on lease/hire: lower of 15% of salary or actual lease cost
- For dependent employees: 7.5% or 10% of salary as applicable

Standard Deduction from Salary (Section 16(i)): A deduction of ₹50,000 is allowed from salary income (subject to revision by Finance Act). Additionally, compensation for voluntary retirement/reduction in strength (Section 10(10C)) is deductible up to ₹5,00,000.

Exemptions u/s 10 relevant to salary:
- House Rent Allowance (HRA) u/s 10(13A): Minimum of — Actual HRA received, 50% of salary (metro) / 40% (non-metro), or Rent paid minus 10% of salary.
- Leave Travel Allowance (LTA) u/s 10(5): Exemption for travel cost incurred on journey to any place in India (limited to family and actual expenditure).
- Gratuity u/s 10(10): Exempt up to statutory limits.
- Provident Fund u/s 10(11): Employer's contribution beyond 12% of salary is taxable.

HOUSE PROPERTY (Section 22-41):
Conditions precedent for taxing income from house property:
(a) The property must consist of buildings or land appurtenant thereto.
(b) The property must be owned by the assessee.
(c) The property must not be used for own business/profession (if used, it falls under PGBP).

Annual Value determination (Section 23):
Step 1: Determine Gross Annual Value (GAV): Higher of Expected Rent or Actual Rent Received, subject to limitations.
Step 2: Deduct Municipal Taxes paid → Net Annual Value (NAV)
Step 3: Deduct 30% of NAV as Standard Deduction (Section 24(a))
Step 4: Deduct Interest on borrowed capital (Section 24(b)): Up to ₹2,00,000 for self-occupied property; no upper limit for let-out property.
Step 5: Deduct deduction u/s 80C for principal repayment of housing loan.
Self-Occupied Property: If more than one house is self-occupied, only one can be chosen as self-occupied. Interest on housing loan for pre-construction period can be claimed in 5 equal annual installments.

CAPITAL GAINS (Section 45-55):
Capital assets are properties of any kind held by an assessee, excluding stock-in-trade, personal effects, and gold/silver/votives held for personal use.

Capital Gain = Full Value of Consideration minus Cost of Acquisition minus Cost of Improvement minus Expenses wholly and exclusively related to the transfer.

Short-Term Capital Assets (held ≤ 24 months for immovable property, ≤ 12 months for others, with specific periods for listed securities):
Short-Term Capital Gain (STCG) is added to total income and taxed at applicable slab rates. STCG on listed securities/u/s 111A is taxed at 15%.

Long-Term Capital Assets (held > 24 months for immovable property, > 12 months for others):
Long-Term Capital Gain (LTCG) is taxed at 20% with indexation benefit (u/s 112) or at 10% without indexation (u/s 112A) for listed securities. Indexation benefits adjust the cost of acquisition using Cost Inflation Index (CII).

3. Profits and Gains of Business or Profession (PGBP) – Deductions and Depreciation

Under Section 28, the following are included in PGBP income:
- Profits of any business/profession
- Compensation for loss of agency/contract
- Fee for professional services
- Value of any benefit/tangible asset received under a VRS
- Perquisites received by a professional
- Any sum received under a Keyman Insurance Policy
- Stock in trade sold by an employee
- Business covenants consideration received
- Forward/derivative contract gains

Deductions allowable under Sections 30-37:
(a) Section 30 (Rent, Repair, Insurance): Rent for business premises, repairs (not capital), insurance of stock-in-trade.
(b) Section 31 (Repairs and Insurance of Plant/Machinery): Same as above for plant and machinery.
(c) Section 32 (Depreciation): Written Down Value (WDV) method is applied. Rates vary by asset class. Bonus shares, rights shares, and sweat equity shares have special provisions.
(d) Section 36(1)(iii) (Interest on Capital): Interest on borrowed capital for business purposes is deductible (subject to certain conditions for speculative business).
(e) Section 36(1)(v) (Profit-related Pay): PRP is deductible within prescribed limits.
(f) Section 37(1) (General Business Expenditure): Any revenue expenditure not covered under Sections 30-36 is deductible if laid out wholly and exclusively for business purposes.

Specific disallowances and limits:
- Advertising expenses: 2% of turnover (where specific limit exists)
- Commission on debentures: deductible within limits
- Payment to contractors (Section 40A(2)): Excess payment over market rate is disallowed
- Payments exceeding ₹10,000 in cash (Section 40A(3)): Entire expenditure is disallowed if made in cash
- TDS not deducted (Section 40(a)(ia)): 30% of the amount payable is disallowed
- Depreciation exceeding actual claim: not deductible
- Unexplained cash credits (Section 40A(3) read with 269UC): Disallowed

Depreciation (Section 32): Computed on WDV method. Blocks of assets are maintained. Rate of depreciation varies by asset type:
- Buildings: 10%
- Furniture and Fittings: 15%
- Plant and Machinery: 15%
- Motor Cars: 15%
- Computers: 40%
- Intangible Assets (Patents, Copyrights, Trademarks, Licenses): 25%
- Solar Panels: 40%

Depreciation is allowed on Block of Assets basis. On sale/destruction of an asset, opening WDV block value is adjusted by adding cost of new asset and subtracting sale proceeds of disposed asset. Closing balance becomes opening balance for next year.

4. Deductions under Chapter VI-A (Sections 80C to 80U)

Chapter VI-A provides deductions from Gross Total Income to arrive at Net Taxable Income. These are deductions available to resident individuals and HUFs mostly.

Section 80C (Investments/Expenditures) – Maximum Limit ₹1,50,000:
- Life insurance premium
- Provident Fund contributions
- NSC, PPF, KVP
- TUPE/Yojana schemes
- Principal repayment of housing loan
- 5-year lock-in equity shares (ELSS)
- Contribution to NPS by self/dependent (covered under 80CCD(1) now)
- Tuition fees for children's education
- Unit-linked insurance plans (ULIP)
- National Savings Certificate
- repayment of home loan principal

Section 80CCC (Pension Annuities): Deduction for premiums paid to life insurance for annuity, max ₹1,50,000 (now clubbed with 80C).

Section 80CCD(1) (NPS Contributions by Individual): Additional deduction up to 10% of gross total income or ₹50,000 (whichever is less) for contributions to National Pension System beyond 80C limit. This is in addition to 80C limit.

Section 80CCD(2) (Employer's NPS Contribution): Deduction available up to 10% of salary (basic + DA) for employer's contribution to NPS. This is over and above 80C limit and does not require separate proof of investment by employee.

Section 80D (Health Insurance Premium): Up to ₹25,000 for self/family; additional ₹25,000 for senior citizen parents; additional ₹50,000 for preventive health check-ups (aggregate not exceeding respective limits).

Section 80E (Education Loan): Interest paid on education loan is fully deductible for 8 years or until interest is fully paid, whichever is earlier. No upper limit.

Section 80G (Donations to Certain Funds): Deduction available at 50% or 100% of donation, subject to qualifying donations and limits (usually 10% of adjusted gross total income).

Section 80TTA (Savings Account Interest): Up to ₹10,000 interest on savings account for individuals and HUFs (other than senior citizens).

Section 80TTB (Senior Citizens): Interest income from deposits with bank/post office/scheme is deductible up to ₹50,000 for senior citizens (60 years or above).

Section 80U (Disablement): ₹75,000 for person with 40-80% disability; ₹1,25,000 for severe disability (80% or more).

Section 80DD (Maintenance of Dependent with Disability): ₹75,000 or ₹1,25,000 depending on disability extent.

5. Indirect Taxation – GST Conceptual Framework and Supply

Goods and Services Tax (GST) was introduced w.e.f. 1st July 2017 through the Constitution (101st Amendment) Act, 2016. It replaced multiple indirect taxes including VAT, Excise Duty, Service Tax, Central Sales Tax, Entry Tax, Octroi, etc.

Key Constitutional Provisions:
- Article 246A: Gives concurrent power to Centre and States to levy GST on goods and services.
- Article 269A: Interstate trade and commerce – IGST levied and administered by the Centre.
- Article 269B: Goods and Services Tax (GST) levied and collected by the Centre on supplies in the course of international trade or commerce.

Structure of GST:
(a) CGST (Central GST) – levied by Centre on intra-State supply
(b) SGST (State GST) – levied by State on intra-State supply
(c) IGST (Integrated GST) – levied by Centre on inter-State supply and imports
(d) UTGST – levied by Union Territory on intra-State supply

GST is a destination-based tax (levied by consuming state) unlike CST which was origin-based.

SUPPLY (Section 7): GST is levied on 'supply' of goods or services or both. 'Supply' includes all forms of supply such as sale, transfer, barter, exchange, license, rental, disposal, etc., made for a consideration. Supply also includes:
- Import of services for consideration (even if no consideration)
- Transfer of business assets
- Supply made in course or furtherance of business

Key elements of 'Supply':
(a) Must be supply of goods and/or services
(b) Must be for consideration (except specified cases)
(c) Must be made in the course or furtherance of business
(d) Must be made by a taxable person

Schedule III (Activities not constituting Supply): Activities/supply listed in Schedule III are neither supply of goods nor supply of services, e.g.: Sale of land, sale of building, services by employee to employer, services by judge, funeral services, trading in shares/securities, etc.

Time of Supply (Section 12 & 13): Determines when tax liability arises.
For Goods:
- Normal case: Earlier of date of invoice or date of receipt of payment
- If invoice not issued: Date of removal or making available to transporter
- Continuous supply: Last day of each cycle if billing is periodic
For Services:
- Normal case: Earlier of date of invoice or date of receipt of payment
- If invoice not issued within prescribed time: Date of provision of service
- Reverse charge: Date of payment or date of debit, whichever is earlier

Place of Supply (Section 10, 12, 13): Determines whether transaction is intra-State or inter-State.
For Goods: Generally, place of supply is location where goods are delivered/transported to.
For Services: Place of supply is location of recipient.
Special rules exist for immovable property, transport, dining services, theme parks, etc.

6. GST – Registration, Invoice, E-Way Bill, and Input Tax Credit

REGISTRATION (Sections 22-25):
Mandatory registration is required if:
(a) Aggregate turnover exceeds threshold limit: Goods – ₹40 Lakhs (₹20 Lakhs for special category states); Services – ₹20 Lakhs (₹10 Lakhs for special category states)
(b) Composition scheme dealers (special cases)
(c) Casual taxable persons
(d) Non-resident taxable persons
(e) Persons liable under reverse charge
(f) E-commerce operators/tax deductors
(g) Persons supplying via e-commerce portals requiring collection at source (TCS)

Registration is required even if turnover is below threshold in case of inter-State supply, casual taxable persons, and those liable under reverse charge.

INVOICE (Section 31):
Every registered person issuing taxable supply must issue a tax invoice containing: name/address/contribution number of supplier and recipient, serial number, date, HSN code, description of goods/services, quantity, taxable value, rate of tax, amount of tax, place of supply, signature, etc.

E-WAY BILL (Rule 138): Required for movement of goods where value exceeds ₹50,000. Generated on GST portal before the movement of goods. Validity varies by distance (1 day for every 200 km for normal vehicles, 1 day for every 200 km for odd-sized loads).

INPUT TAX CREDIT (ITC) – Sections 16-18:
Conditions for eligibility of ITC:
(a) Possession of tax invoice/debit note
(b) Receipt of goods/services
(c) Tax charged has actually been paid to Government
(d) Filing of returns
(e) No ITC claimed by supplier on such tax

ITC is blocked under Section 17(5): ITC cannot be claimed on:
- Food, beverages, eating provisions
- Motor vehicles (except when used for transportation of goods, passengers, or furtherance of business)
- Construction of immovable property (except when used for further supply of goods/services)
- Goods lost/stolen/abandoned/destructured
- Free samples/gifts
- Personal consumption
- Goods/services used for making exempt supplies
- Building/construction services

Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM) – Section 9(4): Under RCM, the recipient of goods/services is liable to pay GST instead of the supplier. Notified suppliers under RCM include:
- GST audit agents
- Persons enrolled as agents with RBI
- Goods transported by road as per notification
- Legal services, etc.

7. GST – Valuation, Returns, Refunds, and Important Cases

VALUATION OF SUPPLY (Sections 15 & 16):
Value of supply generally means transaction value – price actually paid when supplier and recipient are unrelated and price is the sole consideration. Adjustments include:
(a) Add: any expenses incurred by supplier but not included in price; packing charges; commission; interest/late fee; subsidy excluded from price.
(b) Deduct: discount, rebate, grant, subsidy.

Returns Filing (Sections 39-44):
Form GSTR-1: Details of outward supplies – filed monthly/quarterly
Form GSTR-3B: Summary return with self-declared details – filed monthly
Form GSTR-4: Composition scheme taxpayers – filed quarterly
Form GSTR-5: Non-resident taxable persons – filed for each tax period
Form GSTR-6: Input Service Distributor – filed monthly
Form GSTR-7: Tax deducted at source – filed monthly
Form GSTR-8: E-commerce operators – filed monthly
Form GSTR-9: Annual return – filed by regular taxpayers
Form GSTR-9A: Cancelled registration – annual
Form GSTR-10: Final return on cancellation/surrender

REFUNDS (Sections 16 & 17):
Importers of services/goods may be eligible for refund of unutilized Input Tax Credit. Refund of IGST paid on export of goods/services without payment of tax is available. Export under Bond/Letter of Undertaking without payment of IGST attracts zero-rated supply.

INTER-STATE vs INTRA-STATE SUPPLY:
Inter-State Supply occurs when:
(a) Location of supplier ≠ Location of recipient (including Uts)
(b) Import of services
(c) Supply made to/from SEZ
Intra-State Supply occurs when location of supplier and recipient is in the same state.

IMPORTANT CASE LAWS:
(a) DLF Meadows Ltd. v. Commissioner of CGST: GST not payable on real estate sales where only invitation to treat was made.
(b) M/s Kedar Nath & Co. v. State of Haryana: GST on inter-se transfer of goods within a company is taxable supply.
(c) Commissioner of CGST v. Rajesh Enterprises: GST on composite supply is to be levied at the rate of principal supply.
(d) Mahamayo and Sons v. Commissioner of CGST: Taxability of packing charges.

PENALTIES AND PENAL PROVISIONS (GST):
Penalties for default in filing returns, incorrect filing, evasion of tax, false invoices, non-remittance of collected tax, etc. are prescribed under various sections of the CGST Act.

Important facts

  • Residential status determines scope of total income taxable in India – ROR is taxed on global income, RNOR on India-sourced income plus certain foreign income, NR only on India-sourced income.
  • HRA exemption is minimum of actual HRA, 50%/40% of salary, or rent paid minus 10% of salary.
  • Capital Gains classification depends on holding period – 24 months for immovable property, 12 months for other assets.
  • Indexation benefit is available only for LTCG computation – using CII to adjust cost of acquisition.
  • Standard deduction of ₹50,000 is available from salary income.
  • Section 80C limit is ₹1,50,000 and covers life insurance, PPF, NSC, ELSS, etc.
  • NPS contribution under Section 80CCD(1) is over and above ₹1,50,000 limit of Section 80C.
  • Maximum deduction under Section 80D is ₹25,000 (self/family) + ₹25,000 (parents senior citizen) + ₹5,000 (preventive health check-up).
  • GST structure comprises CGST + SGST for intra-State supply and IGST for inter-State supply.
  • Input Tax Credit under GST is blocked under Section 17(5) for food, motor vehicles, construction of immovable property, and personal consumption.
  • E-way bill is mandatory for movement of goods valued above ₹50,000.
  • Reverse Charge Mechanism shifts GST liability from supplier to recipient of goods/services.
  • Place of supply rules determine whether a transaction is intra-State or inter-State for GST purposes.
  • Composite supply attracts GST at the rate of the principal supply.
  • Advance tax is payable if tax liability in a financial year is ₹10,000 or more.
  • TDS is deductible at source under specified sections – income tax must be deposited within prescribed time.
  • Audit of accounts is mandatory under Section 44AB if turnover exceeds ₹1 Crore (₹10 Million) for business.

Formulas and relationships

Resident test: In India ≥ 182 days in PY OR In India ≥ 60 days in PY AND ≥ 365 days in preceding 4 years
ROR test: Resident AND (resident in ≥ 2 out of 10 PYs) AND (stay in India ≥ 730 days in preceding 10 PYs)
Annual Value = Higher of Expected Rent or Actual Rent Received
Net Annual Value (NAV) = Gross Annual Value − Municipal Taxes Paid
Income from House Property = NAV − 30% Standard Deduction − Interest on Borrowed Capital
Capital Gain = Full Value of Consideration − Cost of Acquisition − Cost of Improvement − Transfer Expenses
Indexed Cost of Acquisition = Actual Cost × (CII of Year of Transfer ÷ CII of Year of Acquisition)
HRA Exemption = Minimum of (Actual HRA, 50%/40% of Salary, Rent Paid − 10% of Salary)
Taxable Perquisite (Rent-Free Accommodation) = 15% of Salary (or actual lease cost, whichever is lower)
Gross Total Income = Sum of income under all 5 heads − Deductions under Chapter VI-A

Common mistakes

  • Confusing Residential Status determination with Scope of Total Income – ROR, RNOR, and NR have different scopes of taxable income.
  • Not applying the extended 182-day rule for Indian citizens/PIOs visiting India – the 60-day limit is relaxed to 182 days for them.
  • Forgetting that self-occupied property allows interest deduction up to ₹2,00,000 only, while let-out property has no ceiling on interest.
  • Treating all long-term capital assets identically – listed securities have different holding periods (12 months) compared to immovable property (24 months).
  • Not recognizing that Section 80C limit of ₹1,50,000 is common for multiple investments – exceeding limit in one does not increase overall limit.
  • Assuming ITC on all purchases is available – ITC is blocked for food, personal use, motor vehicles (except goods transport), and construction of immovable property.
  • Mistaking Time of Supply for Goods and Services – they have different rules under Sections 12 and 13.
  • Not understanding that GST on composite supply is levied at the rate of principal supply, not each component separately.
  • Overlooking that inter-State supply of services has special place-of-supply rules (recipient's location).
  • Failing to distinguish between intra-State and inter-State supply for GST purposes – this determines whether CGST+SGST or IGST is applicable.
