Financial Accounting — Quick Revision | CMA Intermediate Group 1

CMAEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Financial Accounting is the systematic process of recording, classifying, summarising and presenting financial transactions to produce true and fair financial statements for decision-making by internal and external users.
  • Accounting Standards are authoritative statements issued by the Institute of Cost Accountants of India (ICMAI) or ICAI, prescribing uniform treatment and disclosure norms so that financial statements are comparable across entities.
  • Consignment Accounting records the relationship between a Consignor (owner who sends goods) and a Consignee (agent who sells goods); risk remains with the consignor until sale.
  • Branch Accounting recognises that separate branch operations may use either Dependent Branch Method (branches' accounts closed into Head Office) or Independent Branch Method (branches maintain full self-contained books).
  • Hire Purchase Agreement is a contract where goods are delivered to a hirer who pays in instalments; ownership transfers only upon payment of the final instalment.
  • Installment Sale differs from hire purchase in that property passes to the buyer at the time of delivery, and the seller retains only a charge over the goods for unpaid consideration.
  • Insurance Claim under Fire/Stock Policy follows the principle of indemnity: the claim is proportional to underinsurance and limited to actual loss, policy limit, and depreciation if applicable.
  • Departmental Accounts treat each department as a profit centre within the same business; inter-department transfers are recorded at cost or marked-up cost with unrealised profit adjusted in stock valuation.
  • Joint Venture Accounting is undertaken when two or more parties combine resources for a specific project; a separate set of books or a joint bank account is maintained and the venture is dissolved on completion.

Key concepts

  • CMA Intermediate requires working knowledge of broadly 15–20 Accounting Standards applicable to cost/financial reporting. Must-know standards and their essence: AS-1 (Disclosure of Accounting Policies), AS-2 (Valuation of Inventories — lower of cost or NRV, FIFO/Weighted Average), AS-3 (Cash Flow Statement — operating/investing/financing activities, indirect/direct method), AS-4 (Contingencies and Events after Reporting Date — adjusting vs non-adjusting events), AS-5 (Net Profit/Loss and Extraordinary Items), AS-7 (Construction Contracts — percentage-of-completion vs completed-contract), AS-9 (Revenue Recognition — point of sale, milestone, proportionate completion), AS-10 (Accounting for Property Plant and Equipment — cost model vs revaluation model, depreciation methods), AS-11 (Foreign Exchange Transactions — closing rate method, monetary vs non-monetary items), AS-13 (Accounting for Investments — cost vs equity method), AS-15 (Employee Benefits — defined contribution vs defined benefit, actuarial gains/losses), AS-17 (Segment Reporting), AS-18 (Related Party Disclosures), AS-20 (Goodwill and Negative Goodwill on consolidation), AS-21 (Consolidated Financial Statements), AS-23 (Equity Accounting in Consolidated FS), AS-24 (Discontinuing Operations), AS-26 (Intangible Assets), AS-29 (Provisions, Contingent Liabilities and Contingent Assets). Memorise which standard covers which topic; the exam frequently tests cross-reference identification.
  • A complete set of financial statements for a manufacturing/trading entity includes: (a) Statement of Profit and Loss (revenue from operations, other income, finance costs, taxonomy-wise expenditure heads, tax, share of profit/loss of associates/joint ventures, discontinued operations, net profit/loss). (b) Statement of Changes in Equity (share capital, reserves including revaluation reserve, retained earnings, OCI components). (c) Balance Sheet (non-current assets: PPE, intangibles, deferred tax assets, long-term investments; current assets: inventories, trade receivables, cash & cash equivalents, short-term loans; equity and liabilities: share capital, share premium, non-current liabilities, current liabilities). (d) Cash Flow Statement (operating — indirect method starting from P&L profit before tax adjusted for non-cash items and working capital changes; investing — purchase/sale of fixed assets and investments; financing — issue/repayment of debt and equity). Key classification rules: borrowings repayable within 12 months → current liability; trade receivables held for collection → current asset; dividend declared but not yet paid → current liability; security deposit received for >12 months → non-current liability.
  • Consignor sends goods at cost plus loading; Consignee incurs non-recurring expenses (directly borne) and recurring expenses (received on account). Treatment on consignor's side: (1) Goods sent on consignment A/c debit, Consignment A/c credit (at invoice/cost price plus loading if any). (2) Consignee expenses A/c debit, Consignment A/c credit. (3) Bills drawn by consignor accepted by consignee: Bills Receivable A/c debit, Consignment A/c credit. (4) On sale: Bank/Debtor A/c debit, Consignment A/c credit. (5) Closing stock valuation: debit Closing Stock A/c, credit Consignment A/c — valued at cost plus proportionate non-recurring expenses and loading (on invoice value basis if loaded). (6) Gross profit = proceeds − (cost of goods sold + recurring expenses + bad debts + abnormal loss). Abnormal loss due to destruction of goods: value at stock-in-transit or estimated selling price less proportionate profit; insured portion claimed from Insurance Co., rest borne by Consignment A/c. Never forget: recurring expenses are NOT added to closing stock value, only non-recurring expenses are.
  • Two methods: (i) Dependent Branch — branch does not maintain full double-entry books; head office maintains Branch A/c and Debtors A/c for it. Goods sent to branch are invoiced above cost (loading retained by HO). Stock is valued at invoice price; inter-branch transfers also carry loading. On reconciliation, unrealised profit in closing stock of branch is eliminated by crediting Branch A/c and debiting Branch Stock A/c adjustment. (ii) Independent Branch — branch maintains its own books; HO maintains Branch Ledger. Comparison of Trial Balance of branch with Branch Ledger at HO reveals discrepancies that are rectified through a suspense account. Key adjustments at year end: (a) Goods in transit from HO to branch not yet recorded by branch → add to branch purchases, deduct from HO dispatches. (b) Cash in hand/ bank at branch not yet credited in HO books → adjust. (c) Bad debts written off by branch but not admitted by HO → reverse if disputed. (d) Loading on stock at branch → calculate and eliminate: unrealised profit = loading % × stock at invoice price.
  • Under Hire Purchase: instalments are partly principal and partly interest. If default occurs before transfer of ownership, the vendor may repossess goods. If goods are repossessed partially: retain an instalment (right of retainer) equal to estimated loss on repossession, calculated as: Total unpaid instalments − (Value of goods retained by buyer + Estimated realisable value of repossessed goods). Journal entries on repossession: (a) Hire Purchase A/c debit with retainer amount, (b) Repossession A/c debit with estimated realisable value, (c) Goods given on HP A/c credit with original cost. If repossessed goods are sold later, difference goes to P&L. Under Installment Sale: since property has already passed, seller sues for recovery; if goods are recovered through court, they are valued at market price and adjusted against decree amount — no 'repossession account' in technical sense, merely a realization adjustment. Distinction that exams love: HP = ownership with seller until full payment; Installment sale = ownership with buyer from delivery date, seller's remedy is legal recovery.
  • Claim formula under fire policy for stock destroyed: Claim = (Opening Stock + Purchases + Direct Expenses − Closing Stock not affected) × (Loss / turnover base) subject to average clause if underinsured. Steps: (1) Prepare Stock Account / Trading Account to find gross profit ratio. (2) Deduct known/clear stock from total stock to get destroyed stock. (3) Apply Average Clause: Claim = Actual Loss × (Sum Insured / Agreed Value or Full Value). (4) Deduct salvage value of debris. (5) Deduct any stock saved and used in business. (6) For goods in transit, decide whether covered — if no specific mention, assume not covered unless proved otherwise. Key trap: gross profit ratio is normally taken on turnover (sales), not on cost; some policies use gross profit on sales, others on cost — check the question. Also remember: depreciation of building/equipment is separately claimed under building/equipment policy, not mixed with stock claim.
  • When goods are transferred from one department to another, transfer price is usually cost plus a markup. The receiving department credits the transferring department's account. At year end, unrealised profit in closing stock of the receiving department must be eliminated because from the organisation's view, no profit has been earned externally. Adjustment: Transfer Dept DRL A/c debit, Profit & Loss A/c credit (amount of unrealised profit). Unrealised profit = markup % × closing stock of receiving dept at transfer price. Divisional P&L shows individual department performance including internal margin, but consolidated segment result excludes it. Other allocations: common expenses like rent, salaries, depreciation should be apportioned on equitable bases (floor space, number of employees, plant value, etc.). Bad debts of one department are borne solely by that department unless instructed otherwise.
  • In a Joint Venture, co-venturers contribute assets/cash for a specific purpose. Two approaches: (i) Separate set of books — each venturer records his own contributions and drawings; a Joint Venture A/c is maintained by each party, and the balancing figure represents the amount payable/receivable between them. (ii) No separate books — each venturer records his own transactions in his personal books and prepares a Joint Bank Account or Settlement A/c at the end. Entries by Venturer A: (a) Contribution of assets: Joint Venture A/c debit, Asset A/c credit. (b) Cash contribution: Joint Venture A/c debit, Bank A/c credit. (c) Expenses paid: Joint Venture A/c debit, Bank/Cash A/c credit. (d) Share of revenue: Bank A/c debit, Joint Venture A/c credit. On settlement, balance in Joint Venture A/c is the net amount payable between venturers. Common mistake: treating joint venture as a partnership firm — it is not; there is no perpetuity, no goodwill, and profit-sharing is based on agreement for that particular venture only.

Important facts

  • AS-2 requires inventory to be valued at the lower of cost and net realisable value (NRV); NRV = estimated selling price less estimated costs of completion and costs necessary to make the sale.
  • AS-3 (revised) mandates Cash Flow Statement for all entities; operating activities include primary revenue-generating activities plus other operating items; investing activities involve acquisition/disposal of long-term assets and investments; financing activities involve changes in equity and borrowings.
  • AS-10 permits two models for PPE: Cost Model (cost less accumulated depreciation and impairment) and Revaluation Model (fair value at revaluation date less subsequent depreciation and impairment). Revaluation surplus goes to Revaluation Reserve under equity.
  • AS-11 requires foreign currency monetary items to be translated at the closing rate at balance sheet date; non-monetary items carried at historical cost remain at the transaction rate; exchange differences on monetary items are recognised in P&L.
  • AS-29: Provision is recognised when (a) present obligation exists as a result of past event, (b) outflow is probable, and (c) reliable estimate can be made. Contingent liability is disclosed but not provided for; Contingent asset is neither provided nor disclosed unless realization is virtually certain.
  • Under AS-9, revenue from sale of goods is recognised when significant risks and rewards of ownership are transferred to the buyer, irrespective of when legal title or possession passes.
  • In consignment, the consignee's commission is usually: (a) ordinary commission — a percentage of goods sold, (b) dedominium commission — percentage of unsold stock (in addition to ordinary), (c) bonus commission — additional percentage on sales exceeding a target. All three are charged on the consignor's books.
  • Loading in branch stock = (Invoice Price − Cost Price)/Cost Price × 100%. To find unrealised profit in closing stock at invoice value: Profit = Loading %/(100+Loading %) × Closing Stock at Invoice Price.
  • Under the Average Clause in fire insurance, if sum insured is less than actual value, the claim is reduced proportionately: Claim = Actual Loss × (Sum Insured / Actual Value).
  • In departmental accounting, if closing stock of one department is transferred to another during the year, it is treated as a purchase by the receiving department and a sale by the sending department; any profit therein is unrealised until the goods leave the enterprise.
  • AS-7 (Construction Contracts): revenue is recognised using the percentage-of-completion method based on contractual work performed; foreseeable losses are recognised immediately in full.

Memory aids

  • AS numbering mnemonic: 1-Policies, 2-Inventory, 3-Cash Flow, 4-Contingencies/Events, 5-NP/EOI, 7-Construction, 9-Revenue, 10-PPE, 11-FX, 13-Investments, 15- Employee Benefits, 17-Segments, 18-Related Parties, 20/21/23-Consolidation trilogy, 24-Discontinued, 26-Intangibles, 29-Provisions.
  • Consignment A/c always mirrors Trading & P&L A/c: debit side shows all costs (goods sent, expenses, abnl loss, commission) and credit side shows all receipts (sales, closing stock, bills receivable, insurance claim for abnormal loss); the balancing figure is Gross Profit or Loss.
  • Hire Purchase retainer formula quick form: Retainer = Unpaid Instalments − (Value retained by buyer + Realisation value of recovered goods); never let retainer be negative — if positive recovered value exceeds detentions, the vendor still cannot claim more than total unpaid instalments.
  • Branch loading elimination shortcut: if goods are invoiced at cost plus 25%, then profit element in invoice price = 1/5th; if invoiced at cost plus 33⅓%, profit = 1/4th; if invoiced at 120% of cost, profit = 20% of cost = 1/6th of invoice value.
  • Insurance claim under average clause memory aid: 'Insured over value, claim gets smaller — multiply loss by insured divided by true value.'
  • Departmental unrealised profit rule: 'Profit trapped in closing stock belongs to the transferring department but is unrealised until external sale; eliminate it before showing segment profit.'

Exam tips

  • CMA Intermediate Financial Accounting questions frequently combine two standards in one problem — e.g., AS-2 (inventory valuation) with AS-10 (depreciation) or AS-11 (FX) with AS-29 (provisions). Practice integrated numericals rather than isolated standard problems.
  • When a question asks for 'true and fair view' or 'compliance with Accounting Standards', explicitly name the standard(s) you are applying in your working notes; examiners award steps for standard reference even if the final figure has a minor error.
  • In consignment and branch problems, always draw a small T-account sketch for Consignment A/c or Branch A/c before journalising — this prevents mixing of recurring/non-recurring expenses and ensures correct closing stock treatment.
  • For hire purchase and repossession, write down the retainer formula explicitly in your answer; even if your numerical is wrong, the formula and setup carry marks.
  • In insurance claim questions, state the gross profit ratio calculation clearly and note whether it is on sales or cost — this single step often decides whether your claim figure is correct.
  • When preparing Cash Flow Statement under AS-3, always start from 'Profit before tax' and then adjust for depreciation, interest, gain/loss on sale, changes in working capital — following this sequence avoids missing any line item.
  • For departmental accounts, always show the elimination entry for unrealised profit in working notes; omitting it will cost full marks on the profit appropriation section.
  • Memory tip for standards: practice writing at least one line of key requirement for each of AS-2, AS-3, AS-9, AS-10, AS-11, AS-29 — these six appear most frequently in descriptive answers.

Common traps

  • Confusing hire purchase with installment sale: in HP, ownership stays with seller until full payment and repossession is a contractual right; in installment sale, ownership passes at delivery and recovery is through civil suit.
  • Treating all consignee expenses as recurring: only recurring expenses (commission, advertising, warehouse rent) reduce gross profit; non-recurring expenses (octroi, freight, warehousing charges paid upfront) are capitalised into stock value.
  • Ignoring unrealised profit in branch/department stock: exam questions often give closing stock at invoice/transfer price and expect you to back-calculate the profit element; forgetting this inflates profit and stock values.
  • Applying average clause to the full policy value instead of the actual loss: the average clause reduces the claim, it does not increase it; a common error is to multiply loss by (True Value/Sum Insured) instead of (Sum Insured/True Value).
  • Mixing up AS-11 monetary vs non-monetary treatment: monetary items (receivables, payables, bank balances) use closing rate; non-monetary items (PPE, inventory) use historical rate unless revalued.
  • Forgetting that abnormal loss in consignment is shared between Consignment A/c and Insurance Claim A/c: only the uninsured portion hits the consignor's P&L.
  • Valuing consignment closing stock at selling price instead of cost plus non-recurring expenses: closing stock is ALWAYS valued at cost (plus proportionate non-recurring expenses and loading), never at market/selling price.
  • Not adjusting inter-department transfers at unrealised profit in segment reporting: both the revenue and the profit element in inter-department stock must be eliminated; only the net cost transfer survives.
  • Confusing AS-4 adjusting vs non-adjusting events: adjusting events provide evidence of conditions existing at balance sheet date (e.g., bankruptcy of a customer confirming receivable was impaired); non-adjusting events indicate conditions arising after balance sheet date (e.g., fire destroying godown).
