CMA Inter Group 1 — Direct and Indirect Taxation: Quick Revision

CMAEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Income Tax: A direct tax levied on the total income of an assessee under the Income-tax Act, 1961, payable based on slab rates or applicable rates after allowable deductions.
  • Residential Status: Classification of an individual/company/firm/HUF as Resident/Non-Resident under Sections 6–7 of the Act, determining the extent of income taxable in India.
  • Assessment Year (AY): The year commencing on 1st April immediately following the Previous Year (PY), during which income earned in the PY is assessed and tax is charged.
  • Previous Year (PY): The financial year immediately preceding the Assessment Year, in which the income is earned.
  • Head of Income: One of the five categories under Section 14 — Salary, House Property, Profits & Gains of Business or Profession (PGBP), Capital Gains, and Other Sources — into which gross total income is classified.
  • Gross Total Income (GTI): Aggregate of income computed under all five heads before deducting Chapter VI-A exemptions/deductions.
  • Total Income: Gross Total Income minus deductions under Chapter VI-A (Sections 80C to 80U), upon which tax is calculated.
  • Input Tax Credit (ITC): Credit of GST paid on inputs/input services, available to a registered person for discharging output liability, governed by Sections 16–18 of CGST Act.
  • Composite Supply: Two or more supplies naturally bundled in ordinary course of business, one of which constitutes the principal supply, taxed at the rate of that principal supply (Section 8, CGST Act).
  • Mixed Supply: Two or more supplies offered together for a single price, without natural bundling, taxed at the rate having the highest incidence among the components (Section 8, CGST Act).
  • Advance Ruling: Authoritative determination by the Advance Ruling Authority (AAR) on applications made by applicants regarding classification, applicability of notification, eligibility for ITC, or valuation — binding on the applicant and the concerned department.
  • Tax Avoidance: Arrangement within the legal framework but defeating the object of the law; distinguished from tax evasion (illegal) and tax planning (permissible). GAAR may apply to arr angements with main purpose of tax avoidance.
  • Presumptive Taxation u/s 44AD: Eligible assessees (individuals, HUF, partnership firms, not companies LLPs) with turnover ≤ ₹2 crore can declare presumptive income at 6% (digital receipts) or 8% (other receipts) of turnover, instead of maintaining books.

Important facts

  • Previous Year = Financial Year; Assessment Year starts 1st April following PY.
  • Basic exemption limit for individuals: ₹2,50,000 (old regime); new regime default from FY 2023–24 with rebate u/s 87A up to ₹7,00,000 income (new regime exemption limit).
  • New residential regime slab rates FY 2023–24: up to ₹3L nil; ₹3–7L 5% (with 87A rebate); ₹7–10L 10%; ₹10–12L 15%; ₹12–15L 20%; above ₹15L 30%.
  • Section 80C limit is ₹1,50,000 per annum.
  • Section 80CCD(1B) additional NPS deduction: ₹50,000 over and above 80C.
  • Section 80D health insurance: below 60 — ₹15,000; senior citizen — ₹25,000; preventive check-up ₹5,000 included.
  • Section 54EC bond investment ceiling: ₹50 lakh per FY.
  • Indexation benefit available only for LTCG on transfer of long-term capital assets; not available for STCG on listed securities (Sec 111A).
  • GST composition dealer cannot claim ITC; output tax collected is not shown separately; file GSTR-4 quarterly.
  • CGST + SGST = same rate (intra-State); IGST = rate (inter-State).
  • TDS on salary u/s 192: employer computes tax on total income for the year and deducts proportionately each month.
  • TDS u/s 194Q threshold: purchase consideration exceeds ₹50 lakh in FY; buyer's turnover > ₹10 crore in preceding FY.
  • TDS u/s 194J threshold: ₹30,000 (single payment) / ₹1,00,000 (aggregate).
  • Section 43B specify dues: must be paid by due date of filing return (sect 139(1)) to be allowed as deduction.
  • First-year rule for depreciation (first asset in block): half-year convention applies (50% of normal depreciation).
  • GAV for self-occupied property is nil unless let out partially — then GAV of let-out part is computed separately.
  • Gift of share capital not perquisite to employee u/s 17(2)(xviii).
  • Perquisite u/s 17(2)(ii): rent-free accommodation — 15% of annual rate (metro) / 10% (non-metro) for employer-provided; or actual rent paid by employer where not provided.

Memory aids

  • Five heads of income mnemonic: S-H-C-P-O → Salary, House property, Capital gains, PGBP, Other sources.
  • Residential status flow: Basic conditions (182 days or 60+365) → Special conditions → Additional conditions → ROR/RNOR/NR.
  • Indexation formula mnemonic: 'CII of Transfer year ÷ CII of Acquisition year' — multiply cost by this fraction.
  • TDS section quick recall: 192=Salary, 194C=Contractor, 194J=Fees/professional, 194Q=Purchase of goods, 195=NRI payments, 194A=Interest, 194BB=Lottery.
  • GST ITC blocked (Sec 17(5)) rhyme: 'Motor, Food, Beauty, Gym, Magazine, Works, Personal, Gift, Loss' — first letters help recall major blocks.
  • Chapter VI-A limits: 80C = 1.5L (same as old basic exemption), 80D health = 15K/25K, 80GG rent = 5K or 25% income or net rent — pick lowest.
  • Mnemonic for capital gains exemptions: M-E-C → 54 (house), 54EC (bonds), 54F (any asset → house).

Exam tips

  • CMA Inter taxation questions commonly test: computation of income u/s various heads, TDS amount and due dates, residential status determination, ITC availability under GST, GST rate classification, and valuation rules.
  • In numericals, always show steps clearly: head → GAV/gross salary → deductions → NAV/net income → GTI → total income → tax → rebate/cap on rebate → surcharge → cess → final tax.
  • For GST ITC questions, systematically check: (1) eligible person, (2) invoice in hand, (3) goods/services received, (4) tax paid to govt, (5) return filed, (6) not in blocked list, (7) not used for exempt supply.
  • Remember the distinction between deemed perquisites (Sec 17(2)) and excluded perquisites — exam often tests exceptions like free tickets for air hostesses, medical facilities at workplace.
  • For compound/assignment-type questions, read carefully whether old or new tax regime applies — from FY 2023-24 new regime is default for individuals; if question mentions 'option not exercised', assume old regime.
  • In TDS questions, always check: (a) section applicable, (b) threshold crossed, (c) PAN furnished/not, (d) due date of deposit, (e) penalty u/s 201A if late, (f) interest u/s 201(1A).
  • GST questions frequently test classification: composite vs mixed supply — remember composite = naturally bundled (principal supply decides); mixed = independent items bundled for convenience.
  • Carry forward of losses: business loss 8 years, speculative loss 4 years, capital loss 8 years, unabsorbed depreciation indefinite — memorise these periods precisely.
  • Be careful with rate changes in GST notifications — if exam provides rates in question, use those; otherwise refer to latest schedule applicable at time of study.

Common traps

  • Trap: Confusing Residential Status rules for individuals vs companies vs firms. Company test = control & management in India; firm/test entity = POEM in India. Do not mix up.
  • Trap: Treating gift of shares u/s 56(2)(x) as fully taxable. Actually, gift of shares without consideration from relative is NOT taxable; from non-relative exceeding ₹50,000 is taxable.
  • Trap: Applying indexation to listed equity shares held ≤ 12 months — STCG u/s 111A has NO indexation benefit. Indexation applies ONLY to LTCG u/s 112/112A (for certain assets) or 112 (non-equity).
  • Trap: Assuming TDS u/s 194Q applies on all purchases. It applies ONLY if buyer's aggregate turnover exceeded ₹10 crore in preceding FY AND purchase consideration in current FY exceeds ₹50 lakh.
  • Trap: Forgetting that pre-construction period interest on house loan is deductible in 5 equal annual installments starting from FY of completion, not in single year.
  • Trap: Treating depreciation as revenue expense. Depreciation u/s 32 is allowed as deduction but capital expenditure is not; also depreciation on building (other than factory) is NOT deductible u/s 32(1)(ii) — only u/s 32(1)(iiia) for specific cases.
  • Trap: Thinking MAT credit can be set off against regular tax. MAT credit u/s 115JEE can be set off only against regular tax liability in future years, subject to conditions.
  • Trap: Assuming composition dealer can claim ITC. Composition dealers are specifically barred from claiming ITC u/s 10(2).
  • Trap: Confusing e-invoicing threshold (₹500 crore for FY 2022-23, reduced to ₹100 crore from FY 2023-24, further reduced subsequently) — always cite latest notified figure if known; for exam purposes use figure given in study material.
  • Trap: Computing TDS on salary without considering investments declared by employee and deductions u/s 80C–80U — salary TDS must be on estimated total income.
