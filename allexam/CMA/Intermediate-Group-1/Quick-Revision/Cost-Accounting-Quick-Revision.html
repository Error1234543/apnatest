Cost Accounting — Quick Revision (CMA Inter Group 1)

CMAEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Cost Accounting: The systematic recording, classification, and analysis of costs to assist management in planning, controlling, and decision-making.
  • Cost Unit: A unit of product, service, or time in relation to which costs are ascertained (e.g., per kg, per unit, per hour, per patient-day).
  • Cost Centre: A location, person, or item of equipment for which costs are collected and controlled; it may be nominal or dimensional.
  • Absorption Costing: A costing method under which all costs (fixed + variable) are charged to products using normal capacity.
  • Marginal Costing: A method where only variable costs are charged to products; fixed costs are treated as period costs and written off against contribution.
  • Standard Costing: A system where predetermined costs are established and then compared with actual costs to compute variances for control purposes.
  • Activity-Based Costing (ABC): A method of overhead absorption where costs are allocated based on cost drivers linked to activities rather than traditional volume-based bases.
  • Batch Costing: A form of job costing where costs are collected for a batch of identical products; cost per unit = total batch cost ÷ number of units in batch.
  • Process Costing: A costing method used where production is continuous and homogeneous; costs are averaged over units produced in each process.
  • Service/Operating Costing: A method used by service organizations (transport, hospitals, hotels) where cost per unit of service capacity is determined.

Key concepts

  • Classification of Costs — Direct vs Indirect: Direct costs (material, labour, expenses) can be fully traced to a cost unit. Indirect costs (overheads) cannot be traced directly and require apportionment. Classification depends on materiality and ease of traceability. Material may be direct or indirect depending on whether it is specifically identifiable with a single cost unit.
  • Prime Cost, Works Cost, Cost of Production, Total Cost: Prime Cost = Direct Material + Direct Labour + Direct Expenses.
    Works Cost (Factory Cost) = Prime Cost + Factory Overheads – Opening WIP + Closing WIP.
    Cost of Production = Works Cost + Office & Administration Overheads.
    Total Cost (Cost of Sales) = Cost of Production + Selling & Distribution Overheads.
    For service industries: Operating Cost = Total Cost adjusted for revenue-like items.
    Note: These steps differ from CMA Final (Strategic Cost Management) which uses Marginal/CAP costing at higher depth.
  • Overhead Absorption: Overheads are absorbed into cost using an Absorption Rate = Estimated Overheads ÷ Estimated Base.
    Common bases:
    • Factory Overheads → Machine Hours / Direct Labour Hours
    • Office Overheads → % on Works Cost or Direct Labour Cost
    • Selling & Distribution → % on Cost of Goods Sold or Sales Value
    • Units Produced → when output is uniform
    Under-absorption occurs when actual overheads > absorbed overheads; Over-absorption is the reverse. Treatment in final accounts: close through Costing P&L.
  • Material Costing: Key techniques:
    • EOQ (Economic Order Quantity) = √(2 × D × S / H), where D = annual demand, S = ordering cost per order, H = holding/carrying cost per unit per year.
    • Re-order Level = Maximum Usage × Maximum Re-order Period.
    • Min Stock Level = Re-order Level – (Normal Usage × Normal Re-order Period).
    • Max Stock Level = Re-order Level + EOQ – (Min Usage × Min Re-order Period).
    • Danger/Lower Re-order Level is rarely computed but conceptually = Min × Min period.
  • Labour Costing: Time Rate System: wages paid based on time worked regardless of output.
    Piece Rate System: wages paid based on number of units produced.
    Halsey Plan: Wage = Time Rate × Hours + 50% of (Time Saved × Rate per hour).
    Rowan Plan: Wage = Time Rate × Hours + (Time Saved / Time Allowed) × (Time Rate × Time Saved) — bonus is proportional, unlike Halsey's flat 50%.
    Idle Time: Normal idle time is included in overhead rate; Abnormal idle time is transferred to Costing P&L Account.
    Overtime Premium: General overtime often absorbed into overhead; special overtime directly charged to the job/job order.
  • Cost Sheet Preparation: Format (ICMAI prescribed):
    1. Prime Cost
    2. Add: Factory Overheads, less abnormal items
    3. Add/Subtract WIP (factory)
    4. Works/Factory Cost
    5. Add: Office/Admin Overheads
    6. Cost of Production
    7. Add/Subtract WIP (finished goods)
    8. Cost of Goods Manufactured/Sold
    9. Add: Selling & Distribution Overheads
    10. Cost of Sales / Total Cost
    11. Profit
    12. Selling Price
    Always adjust for abnormal gains/losses, under/over-absorbed overheads, and non-costing items (e.g., interest, dividends).
  • Batch Costing: Used when identical units are produced in batches. Cost per unit = Total Batch Cost ÷ No. of Units.
    If there is defective units, cost per good unit increases.
    If scrap value is recovered from the batch, deduct it from total batch cost before dividing.
    Losses due to abnormal causes go to Costing P&L, not to unit cost.
  • Process Costing: Process: a distinct stage of production. Each process is treated as a separate job.
    Key features: equivalent units (for WIP), normal loss (scrap value reduces cost per unit), abnormal loss/gain treatment.
    Equivalent Units = Units completed + (WIP × % completion).
    Normal loss: no cost assigned to lost units; their scrap value reduces the cost of output from the process.
    Abnormal loss: valued at full cost per unit and transferred to Costing P&L.
    Abnormal gain: valued at cost per unit and credited to process account; transferred to Costing P&L.
  • Joint Products & By-Products: Joint products: two or more products of equal importance arising from the same process up to the split-off point. Costs up to split-off are apportioned using: (i) Sales Value at Split-off, (ii) Net Realisable Value (NRV) method, (iii) Physical units method, (iv) Weighted average method.
    By-products: secondary products of relatively minor value. Commonly treated by: (i) deducting NRV from total cost (making by-product 'free'), (ii) apportioning a small share of joint cost, (iii) treating as other income.
  • Service/Operating Costing: Used by transport, hospitals, hotels, canteens, power plants.
    Cost units: per km (transport), per bed-day (hospital), per guest-night (hotel), per kWh (power).
    Statement of Operating Cost is prepared with columns for each cost unit.
    Rate per unit = Total Cost ÷ Number of Units.
  • Marginal Costing: Contribution = Sales – Variable Cost.
    P/V Ratio (Profit Volume Ratio) = (Contribution / Sales) × 100 = (Change in Contribution / Change in Sales) × 100.
    Break-even Point (units) = Fixed Cost / Contribution per unit.
    Break-even Point (value) = Fixed Cost / P/V Ratio.
    Margin of Safety = Actual Sales – Break-even Sales.
    Margin of Safety (units) = MOS / Contribution per unit.
    No. of units for target profit = (Fixed Cost + Target Profit) / Contribution per unit.
    Graphically represented by Break-even Chart and Profit-Volume Graph.
  • Budgetary Control: Budget: a quantitative expression of a plan for a defined period.
    Budgetary Control: the establishment of budgets, responsibility for conformity, and comparison of actual with budgeted results.
    Types: Fixed Budget (does not change with activity level), Flexible Budget (adjusted for different levels of activity).
    Flexible Budget Formula: Total Cost = Fixed Cost + (Variable Cost per unit × Activity Level).
    Advantages: planning, coordination, control, performance evaluation, delegation.
  • Standard Costing & Variance Analysis: Material Variances:
    • Material Price Variance = (SP – AP) × Actual Quantity
    • Material Usage Variance = (SQ – AQ) × SP
    • Material Cost Variance = MPCV = MPV + MUV
    • Material Mix Variance = (Actual Mix – Standard Mix) × SP
    • Material Yield Variance = (Actual Yield – Standard Yield from Actual Input) × SP
    Labour Variances:
    • Labour Rate Variance = (SR – AR) × Actual Hours
    • Labour Efficiency Variance = (SH – AH) × SR
    • Labour Cost Variance = LCV = LRv + LEv
    • Labour Mix Variance & Yield Variance similar to material.
    Sales Variance:
    • Sales Value Variance = (SP – AP) × AQ = SSV + Suv
    • Sales Quantity Variance = (SQ – AQ) × SP
    • Sales Mix Variance = (Actual Mix – Standard Mix) × SP
    Overhead Variances:
    • OH Cost Variance = absorbed – actual
    • Sub-variances: Spend, Efficiency, Capacity, Volume (fixed & variable components).
  • Activity-Based Costing (ABC): Four steps:
    1. Identify activities and assign overheads to activity cost pools.
    2. Determine cost drivers for each activity.
    3. Calculate activity rate = Cost pool ÷ Total driver units.
    4. Assign overheads to products based on driver consumption.
    ABC provides more accurate product costing when overheads are high and diverse compared to traditional single-rate absorption.
  • Reconciliation of Cost and Financial Accounts: Cost accounts and financial accounts may be maintained separately. Differences arise due to: items in financial accounts but not in cost accounts (e.g., interest, donation, extraordinary losses); items in cost accounts but not in financial accounts (e.g., by-product valuation, over/under-absorbed overheads, stock revaluation); different valuation methods (FIFO vs weighted average); different depreciation methods; under/over-absorption of overheads.
    Reconciliation Statement starts with either Cost Profit or Financial Profit and adjusts for differences to arrive at the other.

Important facts

  • EOQ formula: √(2DS/H). Holding cost (H) can be given as ₹ per unit per annum or as a % of unit cost.
  • Re-order Level = Max Usage × Max Lead Time. This is the most commonly asked computation.
  • In Process Costing, normal loss is not assigned any cost; its scrap value is credited to the process account and reduces the cost per unit of good output.
  • Abnormal loss always goes to Costing P&L at full cost per unit. Abnormal gain is valued at the same rate and transferred to Costing P&L on credit side.
  • Halsey Plan gives 50% of time saved as bonus; Rowan Plan gives bonus in proportion to time saved/time allowed.
  • P/V Ratio = Contribution/Sales × 100. It remains constant per unit but changes in total with sales volume.
  • Break-even Point in value = Fixed Cost ÷ P/V Ratio. This is frequently tested numerically.
  • Flexible Budget allows variation in variable costs but fixed costs remain unchanged across different activity levels.
  • In Joint Product Costing, the NRV (Net Realisable Value) method is preferred when further processing is required after split-off point.
  • By-product accounting: most common treatment is to deduct its NRV from total cost, making the by-product effectively free to the main product.
  • Marginal Cost = Variable Cost only. Fixed Cost is treated as a period cost, not inventoriable.
  • Cost Audit under Companies Act 2013 (Section 148) applies to companies specified in rule 2 of Companies (Cost Records and Audit) Rules — frequently tested as factual MCQ.
  • Equivalent Units concept is central to Process Costing when WIP is partially complete.
  • Under-absorbed overhead increases cost of sales; Over-absorbed overhead decreases cost of sales.
  • ABC is particularly useful when overheads constitute a large proportion and products consume activities in differing proportions.

Memory aids

  • EOQ = 'Two D S over H, root it' — √(2DS/H)
  • RO Level = Max × Max (usage × lead time) — 'Maxima rule'
  • Halsey gives half; Rowan gives ratio — Halsey is flat 50%, Rowan varies with efficiency
  • Contribution = Sales – Variable; P/V = Contribution ÷ Sales — 'CON-VARIABLE'
  • BEP = FC ÷ Cu or FC ÷ PVR — BEP has two faces
  • Flex Budget = Fixed stays, Variable flexes — 'Fixed is rigid, Variable is flexible'
  • Normal Loss = Free (scrap value only); Abnormal = Goes to P&L
  • ABC Steps: Activities → Drivers → Rates → Assign — 'A-D-R-A'
  • P/V Ratio formula: PV = (Change in CM ÷ Change in Sales) — slope interpretation
  • Reconciliation: start with known profit, add differences to reach the other

Exam tips

  • CMA Inter numerical problems typically carry 5–10 marks each. Practice EOQ, BEP, Cost Sheet, Process Costing, and Variance Analysis computationally.
  • MCQ section tests definitions, formula application, and conceptual distinctions — revise all formulas and be comfortable with rapid calculation.
  • In Process Costing questions, always compute cost per equivalent unit before valuing closing WIP or abnormal loss/gain.
  • In variance analysis, always write the formula before substituting values; even if computation is wrong, correct formula gives step marks.
  • For P/V Ratio and BEP questions, note that an increase in selling price improves BEP marginally but increasing sales volume has a direct impact.
  • Reconciliation of Cost and Financial Accounts is a short-answer favourite — remember both one-sided and two-sided approaches.
  • ABC is increasingly important in ICMAI exams — practice assigning overheads through multiple cost pools.
  • In Joint-Byproduct questions, read carefully whether by-product cost is to be deducted from total or shown as income; the treatment differs.
  • For Short Notes, aim for 80–120 words covering definition, key features, and one example or application.
  • Revision shortcut: memorise all variance formulas with abbreviations (MPCV, MPV, MUV, MMV, MYV, LCV, LRV, LEV) for quick recall during the exam.

Common traps

  • Confusing Holding Cost (H) as a percentage of unit cost vs absolute amount — convert % to ₹ before using EOQ formula.
  • Inflexible Budget vs Flexible Budget — Flexible Budget adjusts variable costs for actual output; Fixed Budget does not.
  • Using Selling Price instead of Contribution in BEP formula — BEP requires contribution per unit or P/V ratio, not selling price alone.
  • Double-counting abnormal loss — it must NOT be included in process output valuation; it goes to Costing P&L separately.
  • Forgetting to adjust for opening/closing WIP in Cost Sheet — both factory WIP and finished goods WIP must be considered.
  • Mixing up Material Mix and Material Yield variances — Mix compares different input proportions; Yield compares actual vs standard output from given input.
  • Treating fixed overheads as variable in marginal costing — fixed costs do not vary with output in the short run.
  • Valuing abnormal gain at scrap value — abnormal gain is valued at full normal cost per unit, not scrap value.
  • Using total cost instead of variable cost while computing P/V Ratio — P/V uses contribution, not gross profit or net profit.
  • Forgetting that by-product NRV reduces the cost of the main product rather than being added as income in some treatments.
  • Applying single overhead rate in ABC questions when multiple cost pools and drivers are given — ABC requires multiple rates.
  • Not annualising ordering cost or holding cost — ensure D, S, and H are all on the same time basis (usually per annum).
