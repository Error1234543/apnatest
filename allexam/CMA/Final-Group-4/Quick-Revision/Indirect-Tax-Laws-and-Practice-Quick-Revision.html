CMA Final Group 4 — Indirect Tax Laws and Practice: Quick Revision

CMAEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Supply (Section 7): Includes all forms of supply of goods or services or both, such as sale, transfer, barter, exchange, license, rental, lease, disposal made or agreed to be made for a consideration by a person in the course or furtherance of business; includes import of services for a consideration even if not in the course of business; specified activities listed in Schedule I even without consideration.
  • Business (Section 2(19)): Includes any trade, commerce, manufacture, consultancy, profession, or any undertaking whatsoever carried on in relation to production or supply of goods or services, whether or not it is carried on with a motive to make profit or gain, and includes activities specified by the Government in the public interest.
  • Person (Section 2(84)): Includes an individual, HUF, company, firm, LLP, association of bodies, body of individuals, local authority, artificial juridical person, or any other entity established under any law having a separate legal existence.
  • Taxable Person (Section 2(114)): A person who is registered under Section 25 and whose registration is active, and includes an assessee, deductor, withholding agent, recipient of backward charges, unregistered person liable to pay tax under reverse charge, temporary importer, and non-resident taxable person.
  • Place of Supply (Section 2(7)): Where the supply is located, relevant for determining whether intra-State or inter-State supply and accordingly levy of CGST+SGST or IGST respectively; distinguished from location of supplier.
  • Point of Consumption (concept): The economic principle underlying GST where tax is intended to be levied in the State where the goods or services are finally consumed; realized through Place of Supply rules for exports/imports and different supplies.
  • Input Tax Credit (ITC) (Section 16): The right to credit of input tax or input services tax paid on inputs used or intended to be used in the course or furtherance of business, set off against output tax liability.
  • Input (Section 2(59)): All goods other than capital goods used or intended to be used by the supplier in the course or furtherance of business, including goods supplied by job worker back to the principal.
  • Capital Goods (Section 2(18)): Goods whose value is depreciated over a period of more than one year in accordance with accounting standards and which are used or intended to be used in the course or furtherance of business.
  • Advance Ruling (Section 28): An authoritative pronouncement on questions relating to classification, eligibility for ITC, determination of time and place of supply, determination of value, and whether a particular supply is taxable or exempt, made by the Authority for Advance Rulings.

Key concepts

  • Constitutional Basis of GST: 101st Constitutional Amendment introduced Articles 246A, 269A, 279A. Parliament may legislate on IGST (Art 269A). GST levied simultaneously by Centre and States on a single transaction of supply (dual GST model). Every State has its own GST law but coordinated through GST Council under Art 279A.
  • Taxability of Supply — Threshold Test (Section 7 read with 9): Step 1: Is it a supply as defined in Section 7? Step 2: Is it made for consideration? Step 3: Is it in the course or furtherance of business? Step 4: Is it by a taxable person? If yes to all, and no specific exclusion applies, the supply is taxable.
  • Schedule I — Supplies Made Without Consideration: Permanent transfer/disposal of business assets where ITC has been claimed on such assets; supply of goods or services between related persons or distinct persons (covered under Section 25(4)) if made in the course of business. These are taxable even without consideration.
  • Schedule II — Treatment of Certain Activities as Supply: Lease of land and buildings is supply of service; construction of complex, building, civil structure where consideration is received after issuance of completion certificate is supply of service; goods incorporated in immovable property is supply of goods; works contract is supply of service; transfer of development rights is supply of service. Distinction between supply of goods and supply of services is critical for classification.
  • Time of Supply — Goods (Section 12): General rule is earlier of date of issue of invoice or last date by which invoice should have been issued, and date of receipt of payment or date of receipt of goods, whichever is earlier. For continuous supply of goods between registered persons, time of supply is last day of each period if invoice not issued within time, or date of invoice if issued, and date of receipt of payment. Special rules apply for goods supplied on approval (on approval acceptance or 5 days from removal).
  • Time of Supply — Services (Section 13): General rule is earlier of date of issue of invoice (within 30 days from date of supply) or date of receipt of payment, and date of receipt of payment, or 60 days from date of invoice for registered persons; extended to 45 days for banks/financial institutions NBFCs. For continuous supply between registered persons, tax invoice must be issued within prescribed time; for unregistered persons within 30 days from date of supply.
  • Place of Supply Rules — Basic Framework (Section 10): If place of supply is in India, it is domestic supply. For goods, general rule is where goods are at time of movement or termination of movement (Section 10(1)). For services, general rule is location of recipient (Section 10(2)). Specific rules override general rules for supply of immovable property, hotel accommodation, transport, restaurant services, personal training, performance art, admission to events, supply to online information database providers, and government receipts.
  • Value of Supply — General Rule (Section 15): Value of supply is the transaction value, i.e., price actually paid or payable where supplier and recipient are unrelated and price is sole consideration. Includes any incidental expenses like packaging, commission, packing materials charged by supplier. Excludes inland freight, GST charged separately, discount before or at time of supply (if duly recorded). For related-party transactions, Section 16 and Valuation Rules apply — arm's length price must be applied.
  • Input Tax Credit — Eligibility Conditions (Section 16): Tax invoice or tax deposited in ledger must be held; goods/services received; supplier has actually paid tax to Government; recipient has furnished return u/s 39; ITC claimed is proportionate to goods/services used for business. Blocked credits u/s 17(5) are not available. ITC on capital goods reduces over 60 monthly installments (for those installed before April 2017 transition); for later installations, normal rules apply.
  • Input Tax Credit — Blocked Credits (Section 17(5)): Motor vehicles up to 13 seats unless used for transportation of goods/passengers, further supply, or training; food, beverages, outdoor catering, beauty treatment, health services, cosmetic/plastic surgery, life insurance, health insurance; gym, fitness, entertainment, sports memberships; medical, dental, hospital services, health check-up; travel benefits to employees; works contract services for construction of immovable property; goods/services received for making tax-exempt supplies; goods/services used for personal consumption; goods lost/stolen/destroyed/written off; goods/services received free from manufacturer.
  • Reverse Charge Mechanism (Section 9(3) and 9(4)): Where specified suppliers receive services from unregistered persons, or notified categories of services, liability to pay tax is on the recipient instead of supplier. Self-supply under RCM where notified. Recipient must pay tax under RCM within 30 days of receiving goods/services plus interest. RCM also applies to CAS, e-commerce operators collecting TCS u/s 52, and specific notified goods.
  • Composition Scheme (Section 10): Available to registered persons with aggregate turnover up to the prescribed limit (revised to Rs 75 lakhs generally, Rs 50 lakhs in special category States). Rates: 1% CGST+1% SGST for manufacturers (others), 5% for restaurants (non-AC), 6% for traders. Cannot claim ITC; cannot make inter-State supplies; cannot supply through e-commerce operator requiring TCS collection; annual return mandatory. Option to opt out after one financial year.
  • Registration — Threshold and Mandatory (Section 24 & 25): Mandatory registration irrespective of threshold: inter-State supply, casual taxable person, online e-commerce operator, person supplying through e-commerce operator requiring TCS, input service distributor, non-resident taxable person, person paying tax under RCM, person required to deduct TDS/TCS, temporary importer, and other notified categories. Aggregate turnover includes all taxable, exempt, export, and inter-State supplies.
  • Invoice Rules (Section 31): Tax invoice mandatory for taxable supply. Must contain GSTIN of supplier and recipient, name/address, serial number, date of supply, HSN, description, quantity/unit, value of supply, taxable value, rate of tax, amount of tax charged, total invoice value, signature. Supplementary/regular invoice for corrections. E-invoicing applicable above prescribed turnover threshold with IRN and QR code.
  • GST Returns — Key Return Types: GSTR-1 (outward supplies details, file monthly or quarterly for composition taxpayer); GSTR-2A/2B (auto-drafted ITC statement based on GSTR-1 filed by suppliers); GSTR-3B (monthly summary return with payment challan, due by 20th of next month); GSTR-4 (composition taxpayer annual return); GSTR-9 (annual return for regular taxpayers); GSTR-9C (reconciliation statement for taxpayers with turnover above threshold); GSTR-5 (non-resident taxable person); GSTR-6 (input service distributor); GSTR-7 (TDS by deductor); GSTR-8 (TCS by e-commerce operator); GSTR-11 (unique ID holders).
  • Anti-Avoidance Provisions — GAAR framework and general anti-abuse rule under GST: Section 166 empowers the government to frame general anti-abuse rules. Principle of substance over form. Transfer pricing adjustments under Section 15 read with valuation rules. Misuse of input tax credit through cascading or fake invoicing attracts penalty and possible prosecution.
  • IGST — Mechanism (Section 18): Levied on inter-State supply of goods or services, imported services, and supplies involving zero-rated supply. Rate equals sum of CGST rate + SGST rate but collected entirely by Centre. Integrated credit mechanism where IGST paid can be set off against IGST first, then balance against CGST/SGST in prescribed order.
  • Compensation Cess (Section 18A): Additional tax levied under Section 18A on demerit goods and certain luxury items like autos, tobacco products, aerated drinks. Collected separately and credited to compensate States for revenue loss during initial transition period under GST Compensation to States Act, 2018.
  • E-Way Bill (Rule 138): Mandatory movement of goods worth Rs 50,000 or more in a single invoice. Electronic way bill generated on GST portal. Requirements for Part A (invoice details, sender/recipient GSTIN, place of delivery, HSN, value) and Part B (vehicle number, transport document number). Consignments of different value from same consigner combined require separate e-way bills. Validity depends on distance.
  • Zero-Rated Supply (Section 16): Export of goods or services and supplies to SEZ are zero-rated. Exporter can supply under bond/LUT without payment of IGST and claim refund of input tax credit. Alternatively, supply can be made on payment of IGST and claim refund of IGST paid. Unutilized ITC on zero-rated supply can be refunded.
  • Appeals and Adjudication (Sections 107-122): First Appellate Authority, GST Appellate Tribunal, revision u/s 108. Jurisdiction of Commissioner u/s 107 appeal limits. Tribunal decision appealable to High Court only on substantial question of law. Settlement Commission exists u/s 128A for compromise of pending proceedings.
  • Penalties and Prosecution (Sections 122-132): General penalty u/s 122 for specified contraventions including false declaration, suppression of turnover, evasion of tax, illegal ITC claim. Penalty ranges from Rs 10,000 to tax evaded amount or 100% of tax evaded depending on provision. Show cause notice mandatory. Compounding u/s 129 for detention of goods in transit and u/s 128A for settlement. Prosecution u/s 132 for offenses with imprisonment up to 5 years for serious fraud.
  • Job Work (Section 2(57) and related provisions): Job worker must register separately. Principal can send inputs/capital goods to job worker without payment of tax under Section 143. Job worker can recover inputs from principal after processing within prescribed time (1 year for inputs, 3 years for capital goods extendable). Credit flow and invoice requirements are specific.
  • Transitional Provisions: Provisions under Section 140 for carry forward of pre-GST credits (CENVAT credit, VAT input tax, CST credit) into input tax credit ledger subject to conditions. Specific documents and timelines apply. Not all pre-GST credits are eligible; only those that would have been admissible under GST if they were paid after commencement.

Important facts

  • CMA Final Indirect Tax syllabus primarily covers GST: constitutional framework, core provisions, procedural aspects, valuation, ITC, registration, invoicing, returns, e-way bill, reverse charge, composition scheme, IGST, compensation cess, anti-avoidance, adjudication, penalties.
  • GST Council under Article 279A meets regularly; recommendations bind Central and State Governments for GST rate changes, threshold revisions, and procedural rules.
  • Section 7 is the anchor provision defining supply; read together with Schedules I, II, III to determine what is taxable, treated as supply of goods/services, or not a supply.
  • Dual GST means both Central and State governments levy GST simultaneously on same transaction; CGST collected by Centre, SGST by State, IGST by Centre but shared with State.
  • Threshold for GST registration: Rs 40 lakhs for goods (general), Rs 20 lakhs for services (general), Rs 20 lakhs for special category States, revised to Rs 75 lakhs for composition scheme.
  • Time of supply for services: general rule is earlier of invoice date (within 30/45 days) and receipt of payment, or receipt of payment itself; latest possible is 60 days from date of supply for registered recipient.
  • Value of supply under Section 15: transaction value is base; additions include packing, commission, incidental expenses borne by supplier; deductions include post-supply discount with concurrent invoice entry and central/state duty borne by supplier.
  • Blocked ITC u/s 17(5) is high-yield — motor vehicles, insurance, health/beauty, catering, immovable property construction, exempt supplies, personal use, damaged goods.
  • Composition taxpayer annual turnover ceiling: Rs 75 lakhs (Rs 50 lakhs in special category States); rates 1%/5%/6% depending on activity; no ITC, no inter-State supply, no TCS exemption.
  • E-way bill threshold: Rs 50,000 per invoice consignment value; part A/B requirements; validity rules by distance; e-way bill not needed for notified exempted goods or specific modes.
  • GSTR-3B due by 20th of following month; late fee Rs 50 per day for CGST and SGST (max Rs 5,000); nil return also to be filed; failure to file attracts consequences on ITC.
  • Appeal u/s 107: first appeal to Appellate Authority, 3 months from order date; revision u/s 108 by Commissioner; Tribunal appeal within 45 days; further appeal to High Court only on substantial question of law.
  • Settlement Commission u/s 128A: jurisdiction over pending proceedings; can settle cases before adjudication or appeal; composition reduces penalty.
  • Export without payment of IGST under bond/LUT; refund of unutilized ITC available; foreign currency or ruppee refund claims differ.
  • Job work: time limit 1 year for inputs returned, 3 years for capital goods returned; extension by Commissioner possible with reason recorded.
  • Cenvat/transition credit u/s 140: eligible credits include those pertaining to final product and inputs carried forward as per old law; certain credits not eligible like those on exempt goods or personal use.
  • SEZ supply: treated as export if underbond/LUT; zero-rated supply rules apply; goods dispatched to SEZ without payment of IGST on fulfilling conditions.
  • Notice periods: issuing show cause notice u/s 73 (no fraud) within 3 years from due date of annual return; u/s 74 (fraud/suppression) within 5 years extendable to 10 years in special cases.
  • HSN classification and its impact on tax rate determination; rate change impact on open book contracts and invoices issued before notification.
  • Difference between e-commerce operator collecting TCS u/s 52 and agent collecting commission; TCS credit can be utilized only for matching with GSTR-2B/2A.
  • Interest u/s 50 on delayed payment of tax; rate revised periodically; applies to self-assessed tax and demand raised orders.
  • Order u/s 60 provisional attachment of bank account in case of significant tax evasion threat; conditions and safeguards.
  • Section 73 vs Section 74: burden of proof differs; u/s 73 on department to prove negligence; u/s 74 requires suppression/fraud with higher penalty exposure.

Memory aids

  • Supply Test Mnemonic: "SCBRI" — Consideration, Business, Registered person, Activity in schedule, Input/output match. For each supply ask: is there consideration? Is it business? Is supplier registered? Does it fall under Schedule I/II?
  • Place of Supply Quick Rule: For goods, follow the goods; for services, follow the recipient — unless it's real estate, hospitality, or event-based where location overrides.
  • Time of Supply Service Formula: earliest of (1) Invoice date (30/45 days from supply date) or (2) Receipt of payment — but never later than 60 days from supply date. Remember: invoice triggers obligation but receipt caps delay.
  • ITC Eligibility Checklist Mnemonic: "IRCTC" — Invoice received, Recipient returned GSTR-3B, Claim proportionate to business, Time-barred ITC excluded, Conditions met (no blocked credits, no personal use). Check these five before claiming.
  • Blocked ITC Categories Mnemonic: "MB CHIEF" — Motor vehicles, Beauty/cosmetic/health, Catering/outdoor, Hospitality/gym/entertainment, Insurance, Food/beverages, Construction of immovable property. When you see these in options, ITC is blocked.
  • Composition Scheme Limit Mnemonic: "75-50-SC" — 75 lakhs general, 50 lakhs special category States, SC for composition. No ITC, no inter-State, rates 1-5-6.
  • E-way Bill Mnemonic: "50K AB" — 50,000 rupee threshold, need Part A and Part B. Distance determines validity; shorter distances get shorter validity.
  • Appeals Timeline Mnemonic: "3-4-5" — 3 months for first appeal, 4 weeks to convert to written submission (practical note), 5 years maximum for fraud cases under Section 74. Appeals go First Authority → Tribunal → High Court (only on SQOL).
  • Reverse Charge Mnemonic: "UCRC" — Unregistered supplier, Composite/Notified services, Recipient pays, Conditions apply. Recipient's liability is statutory, not contractual.
  • Transition Credit Mnemonic: "PRE-GST" — Prior period credits need to map to GST input tax ledger. Only those eligible under both old and new law qualify. Old credit on exempt goods does not become ITC.

Exam tips

  • For CMA Final exams, focus heavily on Section references — questions often test specific subsections like 17(5), 12(3), 13(3), 15(2). Memorize section numbers for blocked credits, time of supply, value of supply, and interest provisions.
  • In the descriptive paper, always start answers with the relevant section citation before explaining the rule. Examiners look for statutory awareness, not just textbook explanation.
  • Practice writing answers on: computation of value of supply with discounts and incidental expenses; calculation of input tax credit with blocked credit application; determination of place and time of supply for mixed/continuous supplies; treatment of goods sent to job worker with time extensions.
  • For MCQ-style practice, watch for option patterns where two provisions sound similar but differ on a keyword — 'shall' vs 'may', 'registered' vs 'unregistered', 'in course of business' vs 'personal use'.
  • Prioritize areas: valuation rules (Section 15 + Valuation Rules), ITC eligibility and blocked credits (Section 16-17), reverse charge mechanism (Section 9(3)/(4)), e-way bill compliance, GST appeals procedure, and penalties/prosecution thresholds.
  • When calculating interest u/s 50, remember it applies on delayed payment of self-assessed tax and also on demanded tax from the due date till actual payment; compound vs simple interest rules vary by context.
  • For annual return and reconciliation statement (GSTR-9/GSTR-9C), know which taxpayers are mandated and what discrepancies must be explained — high-yield for case studies.
  • Remember the shift from old CENVAT/Service Tax/VAT systems to GST transitional provisions — questions may test knowledge of Section 140 credits and conditions for availing them.
  • In exam, if asked about a scenario involving both goods and services, first classify using Schedule II, then determine time/place of supply separately for each component if they are part of composite supply, or as single supply if indivisible.
  • For case-based questions involving e-commerce operators, trace the liability chain: e-commerce operator collects TCS, supplier files return showing outward supplies, recipient claims ITC matching TCS credit — any mismatch creates compliance risk.

Common traps

  • Trap: Assuming all services between related parties are taxable under Schedule I. Correction: Only services between distinct persons or branch accounts are automatically taxable under Schedule I; related party services require consideration to be supply, unless specifically covered.
  • Trap: Treating all motor vehicle ITC as blocked. Correction: ITC on motor vehicles up to 13 seats is blocked ONLY if not used for transportation of goods/passengers, further supply, or driver/trainer training. Vehicles used in the business of transportation are eligible.
  • Trap: Confusing Time of Supply for goods with Time of Supply for services. Goods use date of receipt or invoice; services add the 30/45/60-day framework depending on registration status of recipient.
  • Trap: Believing composition taxpayer can claim ITC on inputs received from unregistered supplier. Correction: Composition taxpayer CANNOT claim ITC at all, regardless of supplier registration status.
  • Trap: Mixing up e-way bill requirement with e-invoicing. E-way bill is movement document; e-invoice is invoice generated on IRP. Both may apply but serve different purposes and have different thresholds.
  • Trap: Assuming refund of unutilized ITC is available for all zero-rated supplies equally. Correction: Export under bond/LUT allows refund; export on payment of IGST allows refund of IGST but rules differ on working capital tied-up refund and interest calculation.
  • Trap: Thinking GSTR-3B can be revised freely. Correction: GSTR-3B is not revisable; corrections can be made in subsequent returns. Errors in 3B require reconciliation in next period's filing.
  • Trap: Overlooking that place of supply for immovable property is where the property is physically located, regardless of recipient location. This overrides general service place of supply rule.
  • Trap: Assuming all notices u/s 74 require proof of fraud. Correction: u/s 74 applies to suppression of facts, furnishing false information, or any equivalent misrepresentation; the department must establish intent but threshold differs from u/s 73.
  • Trap: Confusing GST compensation cess with IGST. Cess is additional tax on specific demerit/luxury goods collected separately; not part of general GST rate structure. IGST is the integrated tax on inter-State supply.
  • Trap: Believing job work time limits are absolute. Correction: Commissioner can extend time limit for returning inputs/capital goods from job worker under recorded reasons; limits are not immutable.
  • Trap: Assuming TCS by e-commerce operator is same as TDS. TCS (Section 52) is collected by platform on net amount of taxable supplies; TDS (Section 51) is deducted by specified authorities on payments. Credit mechanism differs.
