Section 1: Introduction to GST and Constitutional Framework

The Goods and Services Tax (GST) was introduced in India through the Constitution (One Hundred and First Amendment) Act, 2016, with effect from 1st July 2017. It represents a landmark shift from a multi-layered indirect tax structure to a unified national market. GST is a destination-based tax where the input tax credit is available across the entire value chain, and the tax ultimately bears the end consumer.

The constitutional framework provides for a dual GST model — Central GST (CGST) collected by the Centre and State GST (SGST) collected by States. For inter-state supply, Integrated GST (IGST) is levied and collected by the Centre, with the share of State component passed on to the consuming State. Union Territory GST (UTGST) applies to intra-UT supplies.

The key legislations are:
- CGST Act, 2017
- SGST Acts (enacted by each State/UT)
- IGST Act, 2017
- UTGST Act, 2007 (for UTs without legislature)
- GST (Compensation to States) Act, 2017
- CBIC (Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs) administers CGST/IGST/Customs
- Each State's commercial tax department administers its SGST/UTGST

Under Article 246A, both Parliament and State Legislatures have exclusive powers to make laws with respect to GST. Article 269A deals with levy and collection of IGST in course of inter-State trade or commerce. Article 269B empowers Parliament to levy GST on supplies in the course of inter-State trade or commerce where such supplies originate and terminate in a single State (essentially covering e-commerce). Article 279A establishes the Goods and Services Tax Council, which recommends rates, exemptions, threshold limits, and model laws to the Centre and States.

Section 2: Meaning and Scope of 'Supply'

Under Section 7 of the CGST Act, 'supply' is defined broadly and includes all the following when done for a consideration and in the course or furtherance of business:
(a) Ware of goods or services or both
(b) Import of services or goods, as provided under section 5 of the IGST Act
(c) Activities specified in Schedule I (e.g., permanent transfer/disposal of business assets where ITC claimed)
(d) Between related persons or distinct/special persons (Schedule II)
(e) Any treatable transfer of goods where consideration is not wholly between the supplier and the recipient (Schedule II)
(f) Any supply of goods or services as agent for another person including collecting money
(g) Any other supply as notified by the Government.

Schedule I — Supplies Even If Made Without Consideration:
- Permanent transfer or disposal of capital assets where input tax credit has been claimed
- Between related persons or distinct/special persons for furtherance of business (unless supplied as free sample)
- Supply of goods from principal to registered branch outside the state, and between branches within the same state
- Supply of goods or services by a registered person to an unregistered person who is required to register
- Provision by a banking company or financial institution or NBFC of services relating to loans, advance, or any other similar transactions

Schedule II — Acts/Treatments Deemed Supply:
- Transfer of development rights
- Agreeing to the obligation to refrain from an act, to do something, or not to do something
- Letting out of buildings, plots, land, and rented accommodation
- Work contracts and construction of immovable property
- Supply of goods where the ownership transfers but possession remains with supplier (e.g., Hire-purchase)
- Temporary transfer or hire and hire-purchase
- Treatment or process applied to goods belonging to another person (job work)
- Composite supply vs Mixed supply distinction (see below)

Composite Supply means a supply made by a taxable person consisting of two or more supplies naturally bundled and supplied in conjunction with each other in the ordinary course of business, one of which is a principal supply. Mixed Supply means two or more supplies combined together not naturally bundled, offered in substitution for each other, where one or more of such supplies may be made in conjunction with each other, in the ordinary course of business and there is no dependency on any particular supply being made first.

Section 3: When Supply Is Not a Supply (Exclusions Under Section 7)

Section 7(3) and (4) carve out the following from the definition of supply:
- Alcoholic liquor for human consumption — taxable under Entry 84 of List I of the Seventh Schedule of the Constitution; however, GST applies to services related to alcohol.
- Electricity and petroleum crude, high speed diesel, natural gas, and aromatic fuels — notified for GST at a future date (currently not in GST but under separate excise/vat regime).
- Act of the Government or local authority in discharging statutory functions — including acts of judges, public servants, etc.
- Services by election commission in conduct of elections.
- Services by the Reserve Bank of India.
- Services by the Supreme Court, High Courts, and District Courts.
- Import of services by a government department or established persons to carry on any work of science, research, etc. — covered separately under exemption notifications.

The GST Council has periodically expanded exclusions/inclusions through notifications. Importantly, any supply made without consideration is not a supply unless specifically included under Schedule I. The GST regime does not apply to any transaction that does not qualify as 'supply' under Section 7.

Section 4: Place of Supply Rules

The 'Place of Supply' (POS) is critical for determining whether a transaction is intra-State or inter-State, which in turn determines the applicable tax (CGST+SGST vs IGST).

For Goods:
- Supply involving transportation: POS is the destination of the goods — Section 10(1)(a).
- Where the goods are transported by the recipient on his own account (self-collection): POS is the location where the goods are delivered to the recipient — Section 10(2)(a).
- Where transportation is not involved: POS is the location of the goods at the time of delivery — Section 10(1)(b).
- Installment basis supply: POS is the location where each installment is delivered.
- Goods shipped from India to a foreign country: POS is the location outside India.

For Services:
- Where the recipient is registered: POS is the location of the recipient — Section 12(1).
- Where the recipient is unregistered: POS is the location of the supplier — Section 12(2).
- Specific exceptions exist for hotel accommodation, passenger transport, events at predetermined venues, and amusement/fairground services — see Sections 12(3)-(12) for detailed rules.
- For real estate services: POS is the location of the property.

Special rules apply for e-commerce operators, OIDAR services (online information database access and retrieval), and services supplied through intermediaries.

Section 5: Time of Supply — Goods and Services

Time of Supply determines when the tax liability arises and is critical for input tax credit availability.

For Goods (Section 12):
- General rule: The earlier of the date of issue of invoice OR the last date on which invoice must be issued — whichever is earlier — AND the date of receipt of payment OR the date on which payment is due, whichever is later.
- If invoice is not issued: date on which goods are made available to the recipient.
- For continuous supply with evolving obligations: monthly or as soon as the supplier obtains knowledge of a change in obligation.
- If payment date exceeds invoice date: time of supply is the date invoice is issued.

For Services (Section 13):
- General rule: Earlier of date of issue of invoice OR last date for issuing invoice — AND date of receipt of payment — whichever is later.
- If payment is received before invoice date: time of supply is the date of receipt of payment.
- If invoice is issued beyond the prescribed period (30 days from date of supply for B2B, 15 days for B2C): default to date of provision of service.

Special Provisions (Sections 12(8) and 13(8)):
- If the supplier knows the exact tax amount cannot be realized, the time of supply shifts to the date of provision of goods/services regardless of invoice/payment date.
- For contingent payments (insurance claims, etc.), time of supply is when the supplier receives intimation of the award.

Adjustment of Time of Supply (Section 12(7) / 13(7)):
- If the tax paid is less than that payable after revision (e.g., due to later discount), the difference shall be paid along with interest.
- If excess tax is paid, the recipient is entitled to a refund with interest.

Section 6: Valuation of Taxable Supply

Valuation determines the base on which GST is calculated. The primary method is governed by Section 15 of the CGST Act, read with the CGST Valuation Rules, 2017.

Section 15 — Transaction Value:
The price actually paid or payable when the supplier and recipient are not related and the price is the sole consideration.
Inclusions in value (Sec 15(2)):
- Any expenses incurred by the supplier in providing the service/goods, except those charged separately by the supplier
- Packing charges, freight, insurance
- Interest, late fee, penalty for deferred payment
- Subsidies, if directly linked to price (except govt subsidies)
- Commission, brokerage, handling charges

Exclusions from value (Sec 15(3)):
- Discount shown on invoice (pre-supply discount)
- GST taxes shown separately on invoice
- Interstate/Intrastate tax components in case of composite supply
- Any subsidy given by the government

Related Party Transactions (Rule 28 of Valuation Rules):
Where the supplier and recipient are related persons and the price is not the sole consideration, the value is determined by the Valuation Rules. The default method is: transaction value between supplier and person other than the recipient, plus any incidental expenses. Alternatively, open market value or value of goods/service of like kind and quality, or ratio method, or residual method can be used sequentially.

Open Market Value (OMV):
The price of goods/services of like kind and quality freely sold at arm's length in the trade/industry, including all taxes, duties, and discounts.

Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM):
Under Section 9(4), where the supplier is unregistered and the recipient is registered, the recipient is liable to pay GST on a reverse charge basis. Notifications have amended this provision; currently, RCM applies to specific categories such as goods transportation services, legal services, and specified services under Section 9(3).

Section 7: Registration Under GST

Registration is mandatory under Section 22 of the CGST Act for:
- Every person engaged in the supply of taxable goods or services whose aggregate turnover exceeds the threshold limit
- Every casual taxable person
- Every non-resident taxable person
- Every person making an inter-State supply (irrespective of threshold)
- E-commerce operators
- Persons liable to pay tax under RCM
- Persons supplying through e-commerce operators who are required to collect TCS
- Persons required to deduct TDS under Section 51

Threshold Limits:
- Goods: ₹40 lakhs (₹20 lakhs for special category states); Goods transported between states attract no threshold exemption.
- Services: ₹20 lakhs (₹10 lakhs for special category states).
- Composite supply: The higher of goods threshold or services threshold applies.

Composition Scheme (Section 10): Small taxpayers with aggregate turnover up to ₹1.5 crore (₹75 lakhs for special category states) can opt for composition scheme paying GST at a fixed rate on their turnover. Composition dealers cannot make inter-State outward supplies, cannot supply through e-commerce operators, and cannot claim ITC.

Voluntary Registration: A person not liable to register under Section 22 may voluntarily register if they wish to claim ITC or make inter-State supplies.

Cancellation of Registration (Section 29):
- On application by the registered person
- Suo motu by the proper officer for specified grounds including failure to furnish returns for over 12 consecutive months, abnormal operations, or violation of GST provisions.

Special Category States include Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, Himachal Pradesh, Jammu & Kashmir, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland, Odisha, Sikkim, Tripura, Uttarakhand, and Union Territories of Chandigarh, Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu, and Lakshadweep.

Section 8: Input Tax Credit — Eligibility and Blocking of Credits

Input Tax Credit (ITC) is the cornerstone of GST, allowing a registered person to credit the tax paid on inputs against output tax liability. Section 16 of the CGST Act lays down the conditions for availing ITC:

Eligibility Conditions (Section 16):
- The person is registered under the Act
- He is in possession of a tax invoice or debit note issued by a supplier registered under the Act
- He has received the goods or services (or both)
- The tax has been actually paid to the government under Section 39
- The supplier has furnished the invoice details and paid the tax in the relevant return
- No breakdown of tax amount in the invoice is claimed as ITC by another person

Blocked Credits (Section 17(5)):
ITC is NOT available on the following:
- Motor vehicles (5-seater, 9-seater, 13-seater): used for personal purposes, unless the business is transporting of passengers, operating garages, motor vehicle training schools, or selling such vehicles
- Food, beverages, eating house services, and catering services (unless re-supply of taxable goods/services, or food/catering services are an obligatory component of outward supply, or are part of employment facility, or are hospitality services provided under Section 16(2)(b))
- Goods/services used for personal consumption
- Construction of immovable property (other than plant and machinery)
on own account (except when it is further supplied as taxable supply, i.e., construction of complex/building intended for sale to a person other than a registered person)
- Membership of a club, health centre, fitness centre, sports club (not for re-supply)
- Travel benefits to employees (leave/conveyance)
- Goods lost, stolen, wasted, destroyed, or written off
- Works contract services used for construction of immovable property
- Construction of premises (office building, factory, etc.) — not for re-supply
- Goods/services received free of cost
- Tax paid under composition scheme
- Agricultural produce

Reversal of ITC (Section 17(5)): In case of block credit, the ITC claimed must be reversed from the output liability or paid to the government.

Proportionate Reversal (Section 17(2)): If ITC is attributable partly to business use and partly to non-business use, only the business-use proportion is available.

Section 9: ITC Reversal — Rule 37 and Section 16(2A)

Rule 37 of the CGST Rules, 2017 (ITC Reversal for Capital Goods):
When a registered person claims ITC on capital goods/plant/machinery and within five years (10 years for aircraft, ships, vessels) from the date of invoice, makes a supply of such goods without claiming ITC (i.e., where no ITC is available or is blocked), then the proportionate ITC availed must be reversed.

Formula for ITC Reversal under Rule 37:
ITC to be reversed = (P × C/G) × original ITC
Where P = number of months from date of original invoice to date of supply of capital goods, C = 60 months (or 120 months for ships, aircraft, vessels), G = 60 months (or 120 months).

Section 16(2A): If the recipient fails to pay the supplier within 180 days from the date of invoice, the proportionate ITC availed must be reversed. However, when payment is subsequently made to the supplier, the reversed ITC can be availied again.

ITC on Receipt of Goods/Services (Section 16(2)(b)):
- For goods: ITC can be claimed only when the goods are received by the recipient.
- For services: ITC can be claimed only when the services are received.

ITC on Advance Payment (Rule 36A): Input Service Distributor (ISD) can distribute credit on advance payments made by the head office to suppliers.

Matching Provision: ITC is restricted to the extent of discrepancies in invoice details between the supplier's GSTR-2B (auto-drafted) and GSTR-3B returned by the recipient.

Section 10: Invoice Rules, E-Invoicing, and E-Way Bills

Invoice Rules (Section 31 and CGST Invoice Rules, 2017):
Every registered person making a taxable supply shall issue a tax invoice:
- In case of supply of goods: before or at the time of removal of goods for delivery to the purchaser
- In case of supply of services: before or at the time of provision of services
- In case of supply where goods are supplied without transfer of ownership: at the time of supply
- In case of continuous supply: periodic invoice or bill of entry, as applicable

Electronic Invoice (E-Invoicing):
Under Section 31(3)(b), read with Rule 4 of the Invoice Rules, a notified class of taxpayers must generate e-invoices through an empanelled Invoice Registration Portal (IRP). The e-invoice carries a Unique Invoice Reference Number (URN) and QR code. E-invoicing is mandatory for businesses with turnover above ₹5 crore (threshold revised periodically).

E-Way Bill (Section 166 and Rule 138):
An e-way bill is required for the movement of goods:
- Inter-state: when the value of consignment exceeds ₹50,000
- Intra-state: when the value of consignment exceeds ₹50,000 (state-specific notifications may vary)

E-way bill must be generated before the commencement of transportation of goods. It can be generated by:
- Registered person (supplier or recipient)
- Unregistered person (on behalf of a registered person)
- Transportation operator (if no registered person generates it)
- Courrier as an agent of the consignor/consignee

Validity of E-Way Bill:
- For distance up to 100 km: 1 day
- For every additional 100 km or part thereof: an additional day
- For special category states (J&K, NE states, etc.): 2 days per 200 km

Part-B update (transport details) must be completed within 16 hours of generation (for B2B) or 24 hours (for other cases). Transporters carrying goods under e-way bill must carry the e-way bill number in physical or electronic form.

Common challan and part-B for e-way bill is maintained on the GST portal. Transport vehicles must carry goods accompanied by the e-way bill or equivalent bill of transport.

Section 11: Returns, Tax Payment, and Compliance

Returns Filing (Sections 39 and 40):
A registered person must file the following returns:
- GSTR-1: Statement of outward supplies (B2B, B2C large, B2C small, exports, credit/debit notes) — filed monthly/quarterly
- GSTR-3B: Summary return with tax payment — filed monthly (or quarterly for composition dealers)
- GSTR-4: Annual return for composition dealers
- GSTR-5: Return for non-resident taxable persons — filed at the end of the validity period
- GSTR-5A: Return for OIDAR (foreign) suppliers — filed monthly/quarterly
- GSTR-6: Input Service Distributor return — filed monthly
- GSTR-7: Return for persons deducting TDS — filed monthly
- GSTR-8: Return for e-commerce operators deducting TCS — filed monthly
- GSTR-9: Annual return — filed annually
- GSTR-9A: Annual return for regular taxpayers (replaced by GSTR-9)
- GSTR-9B: Annual return for composition dealers (replaced by GSTR-9)
- GSTR-10: Final return (on cancellation of registration)
- GSTR-11: Return for UIN holders (importers of goods and services for central/state govt departments)
- CMP-08: Statement and tax payment for composition dealers (quarterly)
- CMP-09: Annual return for composition dealers

Payment of Tax (Section 39):
Tax liability must be discharged through the cash ledger by filing the return along with the payment. Late fee for delayed filing of returns: ₹50/day (₹20/day for nil returns) under CGST — capped at 10% of the turnover. Interest for delayed payment: 18% p.a. under Section 50.

GSTR-2B is an auto-drafted statement of eligible ITC based on supplier filings, available by the 20th of the subsequent month. ITC can be claimed only to the extent matching with GSTR-2B.

Section 12: Anti-Avoidance Provisions and Audit

Anti-Avoidance (Section 168):
Section 168 empowers the appropriate officer to issue a notice to a registered person for re-examination of any supply, if it appears that the supply was made to avoid or circumvent the provisions of the GST Act. This is the GST equivalent of GAAR (General Anti-Avoidance Rules).

The officer may re-determine the value of the supply or the time of supply, and demand any short-charged tax along with interest and penalties. The provisions apply where:
- There is a lack of arm's length dealings between the supplier and recipient
- The main purpose is to derive a tax benefit
- The transaction involves artificial or contrived structures

Audit (Section 44):
Every registered person whose aggregate turnover during a financial year exceeds ₹2 crore must get his accounts audited by a Chartered Accountant or Cost Accountant and furnish a reconciliation statement (Form GSTR-9C).

Special Audit (Section 46):
The Proper Officer, or any officer authorized by him, may direct a special audit in Form GST AUD-01 if, during the course of scrutiny, investigation, or probe, he is of the opinion that the value has not been correctly declared or credits have not been properly availed. The audit is conducted by a CA/CMA nominated by the proper officer.

Assessment (Sections 60–64):
- Self-Assessment Return (SAR) — Section 60: Every registered person furnishes a return showing tax paid, ITC availed, and tax payable.
- Summary Assessment — Section 61: Made without prior notice in case of urgency.
- Temporary Assessment — Section 62: Where records are incomplete.
- Best Judgment Assessment — Section 62: Where the taxpayer fails to furnish documents or information demanded, or opts for composition scheme wrongly.
- Demand and Recovery — Section 73/74: Where tax is not paid or wrongly availed due to fraud, willful misstatement, suppression of facts, etc.

Section 13: Advance Rulings and Appellate Mechanism

Advance Rulings (Sections 288–294 and GST Authorities and Procedures Rules):
The Authority for Advance Rulings (AAR) is constituted to provide clarity on GST matters. A registered person or an applicant may apply to the AAR for a ruling on the following questions:
- Classification of any goods or services
- Determination of the value of taxable supply under Section 15
- Determination of time of supply under Section 12 and 13
- Eligibility for ITC under Section 16, including blocked credits
- Liability to pay tax on any goods or services under Section 9
- Whether the recipient is liable to pay tax under Section 9(4) (RCM)
- Validity of any notification issued under the GST Act
- Any other question as may be prescribed

The AAR consists of a Judicial Member and an Expert Member. The Central AAR (CAAR) and State AAR (SAAR) operate for Central/IGST and State/UT GST respectively. An application for advance ruling must be made in Form AAR-01 within 9 months from the date of supply. The ruling is binding only on the applicant and the concerned authorities for that particular case.

Appeals (Sections 111–125):
- First Appeal (Section 112): To the Appellate Authority within 3 months from the date of the order (extendable by 1 month with sufficient cause).
- Second Appeal (Section 113): To the Appellate Tribunal within 3 months.
- Revision (Section 107): The Commissioner may call for and examine the record of any proceeding where the order appears to contain a material error.

Review Application (Section 108):
A registered person may prefer an application for review of an order passed by an adjudicating authority within 30 days.

Reference to High Court (Section 117): In case of any difficulty or doubt regarding interpretation, reference can be made to the High Court within 6 months.

GST Tribunal: Established under Section 129 for hearing appeals from orders of the Appellate Authority.

Section 14: Special Provisions — Job Work, Composition Scheme, SEZ/EOU

Job Work (Sections 143–147 and Rule 73):
A job worker is a person who undertakes any process on goods belonging to another registered person. Key features:
- Goods can be sent to a job worker without payment of tax
- Maximum period for returning job-worked goods to the principal: 1 year from the date of sending (extended to 2 years by notification)
- If goods are not returned within the stipulated period, they shall be deemed to have been supplied by the principal to the job worker at the time of sending them for job work
- Job worker can also receive inputs directly from the principal and issue bills of supply
- Input services can also be sent to a job worker

Composition Scheme (Section 10):
- Turnover limit: ₹1.5 crore (₹75 lakh for special category states)
- Tax rate: 1% (CGST 0.5% + SGST 0.5%) on inward supplies; 6% on outward supplies (goods)
- Not available for inter-State supplies
- Cannot claim ITC
- Can supply only through e-commerce operators if TCS is collected by the operator
- Must file annual return (GSTR-4) and quarterly tax payment (CMP-08)
- Cannot issue tax invoices (issues bill of supply)

SEZ and EOU (Special Economic Zones):
- SEZ units are treated as places outside the customs territory of India; supplies to SEZ units are zero-rated under Section 16 of IGST Act
- SEZ developers and SEZ units can claim ITC and claim refunds of unutilized ITC under IGST Act
- Supplies made by SEZ units to domestic tariff area (DTA) attract IGST as if it were an import into India
- DTA supplies to SEZ qualify for zero-rating (bond/LUT) and refund of accumulated ITC

Section 15: E-Commerce and GST

E-Commerce Operators (Section 52 – TCS):
An e-commerce operator is a person who owns, operates, or manages a digital or electronic facility or platform for online supply of goods or services or both. Under Section 52, the e-commerce operator is required to collect Tax Collected at Source (TCS) at the rate of 1% (CGST 0.5% + SGST 0.5%) or 1% IGST on the net value of taxable supplies made through it, after deducting the amount of GST, CGST, SGST, UTGST, IGST collected by the operator.

TCS must be deposited within 10 days from the end of the month in which collection is made. Form GST CMP-08 is to be filed by the e-commerce operator. TCS credit can be availied by the registered e-commerce seller in Form GSTR-2A.

Section 52 differs from Section 51 (TDS): TDS is deducted at source by a specified deductor; TCS is collected at source by the e-commerce operator.

OIDAR Services (Section 166 and IGST Act Section 3(15)):
Online Information Database Access and Retrieval (OIDAR) services supplied by a person located outside India to a recipient in India (who is not registered) are treated as supplied in the course of inter-State trade or commerce, and the place of supply is the location of the recipient. Such suppliers are required to register in India.

E-Commerce Supply Facilitated by Operator:
Where an e-commerce operator itself is the supplier (not just a facilitator), it must charge GST on its own supply and is liable to pay tax on the full invoice value.

Section 16: Refund of GST and Customs Duty

Refunds under GST (Chapter V of CGST Act, Sections 54–59):
A registered person may claim a refund of:
- Export of goods or services or both on payment of IGST (Sec 16 IGST)
- Export of goods or services or both under LUT/Bond without payment of IGST (Sec 16 IGST)
- Inward supply of services where RCM is applicable but no ITC is availiable (Sec 16(3) CGST)
- Accumulated ITC due to inverted duty structure (lower rate on input than on output) — Sec 54(3)(a)
- Unutilized ITC at the time of cancellation of registration — Sec 54(3)(c)
- On supplies made to a SEZ unit/developer — Sec 16(1) IGST
- On deeming supply under Sec 4 of CGST Act
- On supply of goods returned by the recipient within six months — Sec 54(5)

Refund of Customs Duty:
- Drawback: Refund of customs duties paid on imported inputs used in exported goods — governed by Customs Act and drawback notifications
- Refund of anti-dumping duty, countervailing duty, and safeguard duty on exported goods
- Refund on import of goods for export-oriented units or SEZs

Procedure (Sec 54 CGST): Application in Form GST RFD-01 within 2 years from the relevant date. Processing within 60 days. Interest on delayed refund at 6% p.a. from the date of application to the date of refund.

Prohibited Refunds (Sec 54(6)): Refund of unutilized ITC under the composition scheme is not available. Also, refund is not available on inputs/input services used for exempt supplies unless inverted duty structure applies.

Section 17: IGST — Special Provisions

Inter-State Supply (Sec 7 IGST Act):
- Between two different states or a state and a UT
- Between a state and a UT
- Between two UTs
- Import of services (Sec 5 IGST Act)
- Supplies to or from SEZs
- Supplies to or from tropical islands located in territorial waters

Zero-Rated Supply (Sec 16 IGST Act):
Export of goods/services or both is zero-rated, meaning:
- The exporter can supply without payment of IGST under LUT/Bond and claim refund of accumulated ITC
- The exporter can pay IGST and claim refund of IGST paid plus unutilized ITC
Export is defined under Sec 2(18) IGST Act as taking place from India to a place outside India. SEZ supplies are treated as exports.

Import of Services (Sec 5 IGST Act):
Where a service is supplied by a supplier in a non-taxable territory to a recipient in India, IGST is levied under RCM by the recipient.

Place of Supply for IGST (Sec 10 IGST Act):
For goods: the location of the recipient. For services: position of the recipient at the time of providing the service. Special provisions apply for immovable property, transport, events, etc.

Social Welfare Surcharge (SWS): Imposed on basic customs duty at the rate of 10% on all imports (except certain notified items). This is over and above IGST on imports.

Section 18: Customs Duty — Structure and Valuation

Basic Customs Duty (BCD):
Levied under Section 12 of the Customs Act, 1962 on all imports into and exports from India. Rates vary by item classification under the Customs Tariff Act, 1975. BCD rates are specified in notifications under Section 25 of the Customs Act.

Integrated GST on Imports:
As per Section 3(1) of the IGST Act, IGST is levied on all import of goods, including goods imported through SEZs/EOUs. IGST is calculated on the assessable value plus BCD plus SWS plus any other applicable duties.

Social Welfare Surcharge (SWS):
Levied at 10% on the amount of BCD. Applies on almost all imported goods except those notified otherwise.

Additional Duties:
- Countervailing Duty (CVD) — now subsumed into IGST on imports
- Anti-Dumping Duty (ADD) — levied under Section 9A of the Customs Act to counteract the effects of dumping (export at less than normal value)
- Safeguard Duty (SG) — under Section 8B of the Customs Act to protect domestic industry from surge in imports
- Compensation Cess — on specific items like solar cells/modules, extra-ordinary goods

Customs Valuation (Valuation Rules, 2007):
The primary method is the transaction value method (Rule 4). If that cannot be applied, sequential methods are used:
- Transaction value of identical goods (Rule 5)
- Transaction value of similar goods (Rule 6)
- Deductive value method (Rule 7)
- Computed value method (Rule 8)
- Residual method (Rule 9)

Drawback:
Merchandise exports attract drawback of excise duties, customs duties, and GST paid on inputs under the Foreign Trade Policy. The rate is specified in drawback notifications.

Free Ship Station and Foreign Trade Policy:
Exports from Free Ship Stations enjoy special concessions.

Section 19: Penalty, Prosecution, and Settlement

Penalties (Sec 122 CGST Act):
A person is liable to a penalty of:
- ₹10,000 or an amount equal to the tax evaded, whichever is higher — for offences not specifically covered under other provisions
- In case of fraudulent invocation of ITC: penalty equal to 100% of the tax shortfall or Rs. 10,000, whichever is higher
- For contravention not accompanied by fraud: penalty up to 25% of the tax involved

Specific Offences (Sec 132 CGST Act) attracting prosecution:
- Suppressing facts with intent to evade tax
- Taking ITC using fake invoices or bogus documents
- Issuing invoices without actual supply of goods/services
- Manipulating accounts to evade tax
- Hindering the officer from discharging duties
- Destroying, altering, or suppressing evidence
- Obtaining registration by misrepresentation or suppression of facts
Penalties range from ₹10,000 to ₹5 lakh, along with imprisonment of up to 5 years for serious offences.

Compounding of Offences (Sec 129 CGST Act):
For transport of goods in contravention of the GST provisions, the penalty is 100% of the tax payable on such goods or Rs. 10,000, whichever is higher. Such penalty may be compounded.

Settlement Commission (Sec 128 CGST Act):
The Settlement Commission can settle applications from persons who disclose fully and truly the facts of the case and have co-operated in the proceedings. The application must be made before any proceedings are initiated.

Important facts

  • GST became operative in India from 1 July 2017 via Constitution (101st Amendment) Act, 2016.
  • GST Council (Art 279A) recommends rates, exemptions, threshold limits, and model laws.
  • Composite supply: naturally bundled supplies where one is principal supply.
  • Mixed supply: multiple independent supplies offered in substitution for each other.
  • Place of supply for services to registered person = location of recipient (Sec 12(1)).
  • Place of supply for goods with transport = destination of goods (Sec 10(1)(a)).
  • Time of supply for goods/services = earlier of invoice date (or due date for invoice) and payment date.
  • IGST is a single integrated tax — no CGST/SGST component; apportioned between Centre and consuming State.
  • Input Tax Credit is NOT available on motor vehicles up to 13-seater (for personal use).
  • Blocked credits under Sec 17(5) include food, personal use, construction of immovable property, membership of clubs, travel benefits to employees.
  • E-invoicing mandatory for turnover above ₹5 crore (subject to periodic revision).
  • E-way bill threshold: ₹50,000 for both intra-State and inter-State.
  • Composition scheme: turnover up to ₹1.5 crore (₹75 lakh for special category states); rate: 1% inward, 6% outward for goods; no ITC; no inter-State supply.
  • Job work: goods can be sent for up to 1 year (extendable to 2 years); deemed supply if not returned.
  • TCS under Sec 52 @ 1% (CGST 0.5% + SGST 0.5% or IGST 1%) by e-commerce operators.
  • TDS under Sec 51 @ 1% by specified deductors (e.g., government departments).
  • Zero-rated supply (exports/SEZ) — exporter can supply under LUT/Bond without IGST and claim refund of ITC.
  • Refund of unutilized ITC available only in inverted duty structure (lower rate on input than output).
  • Advance Ruling (Sec 288-294) application within 9 months from date of supply.
  • Appeal to Appellate Authority within 3 months; to GST Tribunal within 3 months from order date.
  • Social Welfare Surcharge = 10% on Basic Customs Duty (except exempted items).
  • Special audit (Sec 46) is triggered by officer during scrutiny/investigation.
  • Anti-avoidance (Sec 168) empowers officer to re-examine supplies to prevent tax evasion.
  • Rule 36(4) caps ITC claimed in GSTR-3B to 90% (or 100% in FY 2024-25 onwards as per notification) of ITC reflected in GSTR-2B.
  • Nil-rated supplies are still taxable supplies; exempt supplies are outside GST scope.

Formulas and relationships

Time of Supply (Goods): Earlier of (a) Date of Issue of Invoice / Due Date for Invoice, whichever is earlier; (b) Date of Receipt of Payment / Date Payment Due, whichever is later.
Time of Supply (Services): Earlier of (a) Date of Issue of Invoice / Last Date for Invoice (within 30 days for B2B / 15 days for B2C), whichever is earlier; (b) Date of Receipt of Payment.
ITC Reversal for Capital Goods (Rule 37): ITC to reverse = (Original ITC × Months used ÷ Total useful life in months). Useful life = 60 months (10 years for ships, aircraft, vessels = 120 months).
Net Value for TCS (Sec 52): Net Value = Aggregate value of taxable supplies made through the e-commerce operator MINUS total tax collected MINUS amount refunded by the e-commerce operator.
IGST on Import of Goods = (Assessable Value + BCD + SWS) × IGST Rate.
SWS (Social Welfare Surcharge) = 10% of Basic Customs Duty (where applicable).
Open Market Value (Rule 28): Price of goods/services of like kind and quality freely sold at arm's length including discounts but excluding taxes.
E-way Bill Validity: Up to 100 km = 1 day; additional 100 km or part = additional 1 day.
Late Fee for Delayed Return: CGST = ₹50/day (₹20/day for nil return), maximum = 10% of turnover.
Interest for Delayed Tax Payment (Sec 50): 18% per annum on the amount of tax not paid on or before the due date.
Composition Scheme Tax: CGST 0.5% + SGST 0.5% on inward supplies; CGST 3% + SGST 3% on outward supplies for manufacturing/trading (total 6%).
Inverted Duty Structure Refund: Difference in ITC rate between input (higher) and output (lower) — refundable unutilized ITC allowed under Sec 54(3)(a).

Common mistakes

  • Confusing 'nil-rated' supply (taxable but at 0%) with 'exempt' supply (outside GST) — ITC on exempt supplies is blocked unless inverted duty structure applies.
  • Incorrect application of place of supply rules for services supplied to non-registered recipients — the default rule shifts to supplier's location, but specific services (hotels, transport, events) have special POS.
  • Treating e-commerce TCS (Sec 52) as equivalent to TDS (Sec 51) — TCS is collected from the seller's consideration; TDS is deducted from payments made by specified deductors.
  • Confusing GST time of supply with customs time of import — these are distinct provisions with different consequences.
  • Applying composition scheme without checking inter-State supply restriction — a composition dealer cannot make inter-State supplies, making the scheme unsuitable for businesses with pan-India operations.
  • Claiming ITC on goods used for personal consumption or construction of immovable property — both fall under Sec 17(5) blocked credits.
  • Not reversing ITC under Rule 37 when capital goods are disposed off within 5/10 years without claiming ITC.
  • Ignoring the requirement of ITC matching with GSTR-2B — ITC can be claimed only to the extent it matches with auto-populated GSTR-2B.
  • Forgetting that job work goods must be returned within 1 year (extendable to 2 years); failure results in deemed supply and tax liability.
  • Confusing advance ruling applicability — only questions listed under Sec 288 are answerable by the AAR; other questions must be decided by authorities.
  • Assuming that SEZ supplies are always exempt — they are zero-rated ( taxable but refundable), which is legally distinct from exemption.
  • Missing that negative list (Sec 17(5)) includes specific services like food/catering, personal use of goods, and membership of clubs.
