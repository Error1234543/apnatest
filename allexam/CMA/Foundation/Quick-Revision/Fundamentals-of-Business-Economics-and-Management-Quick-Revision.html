Quick Revision: Fundamentals of Business Economics and Management — CMA Foundation

CMAEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Business Economics: Application of economic theory and methodology to business decision-making and the formulation of optimal business policies. It bridges pure economics and management practice.
  • Demand: The quantity of a commodity that consumers are willing and able to purchase at various prices during a given period of time, ceteris paribus.
  • Law of Demand: There is an inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded of a commodity, ceteris paribus — as price rises, quantity demanded falls, and vice versa.
  • Elasticity of Demand: A measure of the responsiveness of quantity demanded to a change in one of its determinants, most commonly price. Ed = (% change in Qd) / (% change in Price).
  • Law of Supply: There is a direct relationship between price and quantity supplied, ceteris paribus — as price rises, quantity supplied rises, and vice versa.
  • Market Equilibrium: The state where the quantity demanded equals the quantity supplied at a particular price (equilibrium price), with no tendency for change unless disturbed.
  • Production Function: The technical relationship between inputs (factors of production) and outputs. Q = f(L, K, ...).
  • Returns to a Factor (Law of Variable Proportions): As one variable input is increased while others remain fixed, total product initially rises at an increasing rate, then at a decreasing rate, and finally declines.
  • Returns to Scale: Change in output when ALL inputs are changed in the same proportion. Increasing, constant, or decreasing returns to scale.
  • Cost of Production: The expenditure incurred by a firm on various factors of production. Includes TC = TFC + TVC, with associated average and marginal concepts.
  • Perfect Competition: A market structure with many buyers and sellers, homogeneous product, free entry and exit, perfect knowledge, and no transport cost. Firms are price takers.
  • Monopoly: A market with a single seller, unique product, barriers to entry, and the firm being a price maker. Can earn super-normal profit in short and long run.
  • Monopolistic Competition: Many sellers, differentiated products, free entry/exit. Combines features of perfect competition and monopoly. Zero economic profit in long run.
  • Oligopoly: Few large sellers dominate the market. Interdependent decisions. Barriers to entry exist. Kinked demand curve is a notable model.
  • National Income: The total money value of all final goods and services produced by a country during an accounting year. Measured by GDP, GNP, NNP at factor cost.
  • Management: The process of planning, organizing, staffing, directing, and controlling organizational resources to achieve defined objectives efficiently and effectively.
  • Principles of Management (Fayol): Henri Fayol proposed 14 principles including division of work, authority & responsibility, discipline, unity of command, unity of direction, subordination of individual interest, remuneration, centralization, scalar chain, order, equity, stability of tenure, initiative, and esprit de corps.

Important facts

  • Giffen goods and Veblen goods are exceptions to the Law of Demand (demand rises with price).
  • Cross elasticity of demand: positive for substitutes, negative for complements.
  • Income elasticity: positive for normal goods, negative for inferior goods.
  • The demand curve is the marginal benefit curve.
  • The supply curve is the marginal cost curve (above shutdown point).
  • In perfect competition, AR = MR = P in both short and long run.
  • In monopoly, MR < AR = P always (downward sloping AR/MR).
  • Kinked demand curve in oligopoly explains price rigidity.
  • LAC (LRAC) envelope curve touches each SAC at one point only.
  • Minimum efficient scale is where LRAC reaches its minimum.
  • National Income = NNP at factor cost.
  • Real GDP adjusts for price changes; Nominal GDP does not.
  • GNP = GDP + Net Factor Income from Abroad.
  • Depreciation = Consumption of fixed capital.
  • Fayol is known as the Father of Modern Management Theory.
  • Henry Ford introduced the concept of mass production and assembly line.
  • Scientific Management was propounded by Frederick Winslow Taylor.
  • Elton Mayo conducted the Hawthorne Studies emphasizing human relations.
  • SWOT = Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats.
  • Budgetary control is a tool of coordination and control in management.

Memory aids

  • D-U-R-S-T for demand shifters: Price, Related goods, Un income, Substitutes/complements tastes, Trends.
  • H-A-M-M for motivation theories: Maslow, Herzberg, McGregor, McClelland.
  • P-O-S-D-C for management functions: Planning, Organizing, Staffing, Directing, Controlling.
  • "Perfect" has many firms; "Mono" means one; "Mono-polistic" differentiates; "Oligo" means few.
  • MARSHALL'S method of elasticity: M = Mid-point, A = All outlay, R = Rectangular hyperbola, S = Slope, H = Halving, A = Arithmetic, L = Logarithm, L = Leontief.

Exam tips

  • For CMA Foundation, both objective and descriptive questions appear. Practice numerical problems on elasticity, cost calculations, and national income aggregation.
  • Memorize the numerical values and formulas: Ed formula, MC/AC relationships, GDP components, NNPfc calculation.
  • In management section, focus on matching principles with their correct descriptions and identifying the right theorist for each contribution.
  • For market structures, be prepared to draw and label diagrams (AR/MR curves, cost curves, equilibrium positions) — these frequently carry diagram marks.
  • In descriptive answers, always define the concept first, then explain with reasoning, and conclude with a relevant example or application.
  • Pay special attention to exceptions (Giffen goods, Veblen goods) — they are favorite trap questions.
  • National Income: know the chain of conversion from GDPmp → NNPfc step by step; this is a very common numerical question type.
  • Differentiate clearly between microeconomics (individual units) and macroeconomics (aggregate economy).
  • For motivation theories, know the order (Maslow's hierarchy) and the key factors under each theory — expect assertion-reason type questions.

Common traps

  • Confusing change in quantity demanded (movement along curve due to price) with change in demand (shift of curve due to non-price factors).
  • Thinking monopoly can earn super-normal profit in LR — it CAN, unlike perfect competition where it earns normal profit.
  • Forgetting that MC cuts AC at its minimum, not at its own minimum.
  • In national income: confusing NNPmp with NNPfc; remember NNPfc = NNPmp - IT + Subsidies.
  • Assuming AR = MR in monopoly — false; only in perfect competition is AR = MR.
  • Mixing up normal and inferior goods by income elasticity sign: normal = +ve, inferior = -ve.
  • Considering Giffen goods as following law of demand — they are famous EXCEPTIONS.
  • In Taylors' Scientific Management: his 8 principles include science not rule of thumb, harmony not discord, cooperation not individualism, etc. — do not mix with Fayol's 14 principles.
  • Forgetting that in long run all costs are variable; there is no fixed cost.
  • In oligopoly: assuming no interdependence among firms — interdependence is the KEY feature.
  • Treating depreciation as a cash outflow — it is a non-cash expense.
