Quick Revision: Fundamentals of Financial and Cost Accounting — CMA Foundation

CMAEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Accounting: The art of recording, classifying, and summarizing financial transactions and interpreting their results.
  • Financial Accounting: Systematic recording of business transactions to determine profit/loss and financial position.
  • Cost Accounting: Process of ascertaining costs of products/services for cost control and decision-making.
  • Double Entry System: Every transaction has dual effect — debit one account, credit another (Debit comes in, Credit goes out).
  • Account: A record of transactions pertaining to a particular person, thing, or aspect.
  • Journal: Book of original entry where transactions are recorded chronologically with dates.
  • Ledger: Collection of all accounts where journal entries are posted classified by account type.
  • Trial Balance: Statement showing equality of debits and credits to check arithmetical accuracy.
  • Final Accounts: Statements comprising Manufacturing Account (if applicable), Trading Account, Profit & Loss Account, and Balance Sheet.
  • Bank Reconciliation Statement: Statement reconciling bank balance as per cash book with pass book balance.
  • Depreciation: Systematic reduction in the book value of a fixed asset due to usage, time, or obsolescence.
  • Rectification of Errors: Correcting mistakes in recording, posting, or balancing that affect trial balance or financial statements.
  • Cost Unit: Unit of product/service to which costs are ascertained (e.g., per kg, per unit, per kilometer).
  • Prime Cost: Direct material + Direct labor + Direct expenses; also called Works Cost before factory overheads.
  • Work Cost (Works Cost): Prime Cost + Factory/Works Overheads.
  • Cost of Production: Work Cost + Administrative Overheads.
  • Cost of Sales (Total Cost): Cost of Production + Selling & Distribution Overheads.
  • Cost Sheet: Statement showing cost classification and ascertainment in item-wise detail.
  • Material: All items consumed in manufacturing process — direct materials form part of product; indirect materials are overheads.
  • Reorder Level: Maximum level at which inventory should be replenished = Max Usage × Max Lead Time.
  • Economic Order Quantity (EOQ): Optimal order size minimizing total ordering and carrying costs = √(2 × S × O / C), where S = annual demand, O = ordering cost per order, C = carrying cost per unit per annum.
  • EOQ Formula Variants: If carrying cost is given as percentage of average inventory value: EOQ = √(2 × S × O / (C% × unit price)).
  • Labour Cost: Wages paid to workers directly engaged in manufacturing; includes direct and indirect labor classifications.
  • Overhead: Indirect cost that cannot be directly charged to a specific cost unit; classified as factory, administrative, selling, distribution.
  • Job Costing: Cost ascertainment for specific jobs/orders where work is done to customer specifications.
  • Batch Costing: Cost ascertainment for a batch of identical products produced together.
  • Process Costing: Cost ascertainment for continuous production processes where units pass through successive stages.

Key concepts

  • ACCOUNTING PRINCIPLES AND CONCEPTS: Business Entity Concept — owner and business are separate; Money Measurement Concept — only monetary transactions recorded; Going Concern Concept — business will continue indefinitely; Accounting Period Concept — usually 12 months; Dual Aspect Concept — assets = liabilities + capital; Cost Concept — assets recorded at purchase cost; Realisation Concept — revenue recognized on sale; Accrual Concept — revenue/expenses recognized when earned/incurred not when received/paid; Consistency Concept — methods remain uniform across years; Conservatism (Prudence) — anticipate losses, not profits; Materiality Concept — significant items disclosed separately; Gaap (Generally Accepted Accounting Principles) — framework governing accounting practices.
  • TYPES OF ACCOUNTS AND RULES: Personal Account — relates to person/entity; rule: debit receiver, credit giver. Real Account — relates to asset/property; rule: debit what comes in, credit what goes out. Nominal Account — relates to income/expense/gain/loss; rule: debit all expenses/losses, credit all incomes/gains. This is the modern/classical approach still tested in CMA Foundation.
  • DOUBLE ENTRY JOURNAL ENTRIES: Cash received from customer — Debit Cash, Credit Customer. Goods purchased on credit — Debit Purchases, Credit Supplier. Goods sold on credit — Debit Customer, Credit Sales. Rent paid — Debit Rent A/c, Credit Cash. Commission received — Debit Cash, Credit Commission A/c. Purchase of machine for cash — Debit Machine, Credit Cash. Bad debt written off — Debit Bad Debt, Credit Debtor. Depreciation charged — Debit Depreciation, Credit Asset Account.
  • TRIAL BALANCE TYPES: Two-column format — debit and credit columns; Single column with debit/credit balances listed separately; Balanced format showing tallied totals. Differences in trial balance indicate errors — some can be located (e.g., wrong casting), some cannot (e.g., compensating errors, omission of entire transaction, principle error, complete reversal of entries).
  • FINAL ACCOUNTS STRUCTURE: Manufacturing Account shows cost of goods manufactured — Direct Material consumed + Direct Labor + Direct Expenses + Factory Overheads — Closing WIP + Opening WIP. Trading Account shows gross profit — Purchases + Direct Costs – Opening Stock + Closing Stock. Profit & Loss Account shows net profit — Gross Profit – Operating Expenses + Other Incomes. Balance Sheet shows financial position — Liabilities side (Capital, Loans, Reserves, Current Liabilities) and Assets side (Fixed Assets, Investments, Current Assets, Miscellaneous Expenditure). Capital Account treatment: opening balance at start, add profit/less loss during year, add/additional capital, less drawings — closing balance carried forward.
  • BANK RECONCILIATION STATEMENT: Starting from cash book balance, adjust for: checks issued but not presented (subtracted from cash book balance if passed through but not yet cleared); checks deposited but not collected (added if not yet credited by bank); bank charges/debits not in cash book (subtracted); interest credited by bank not in cash book (added); direct deposits by customers not in cash book (added); errors in cash book (corrected). Starting from pass book balance: reverse the above adjustments. Key mnemonic: IBBS — Interest Bank credits (add), Bank Charges (subtract), Bounces/Dishonor (subtract), Standing Instructions not in cash book.
  • DEPRECIATION METHODS: Straight Line Method (Uniform Method) — equal depreciation every year = (Cost – Salvage Value) / Useful Life. Written Down Value Method (Diminishing Balance) — depreciation on reducing balance each year = Rate applied to book value at beginning of year. Units of Production Method — based on output/profit ability. Sum of Years Digits Method — accelerated depreciation technique. Oil Well Method — based on estimated reserves.
  • RECTIFICATION OF ERRORS: Error of Omission — transaction not recorded at all; no entry needed in rectification other than passing the original entry. Error of Commission — wrong posting within correct class; correct by adjusting the affected account. Error of Principle — recording transaction violating accounting principle; rectify by reversing incorrect entry and passing correct one. Compensating Errors — two or more errors cancel each other; usually ignored unless affecting final figures. Recoverable from wrong account — if X was debited instead of Y, debit Y and credit X. Over/Under casting — correct the difference by adding/subtracting.
  • COST CLASSIFICATION: By Nature/Element — Material, Labor, Expenses. By Behavior — Fixed Costs (constant in total within relevant range), Variable Costs (proportional to output), Semi-variable/Mixed Costs (combination). By Function — Manufacturing/Production, Administrative, Selling & Distribution, Research & Development. By Normality — Normal Cost (expected under normal conditions), Abnormal Cost (unusual, not expected). By Traceability — Direct Cost (traceable to cost unit), Indirect Cost (cannot be traced directly). By Time — Historical Cost (actual past cost), Predetermined Cost (estimated in advance), Standard Cost (pre-set benchmark).
  • MATERIAL COST CONTROL: Maximum Level = Reorder Level – (Min Usage × Min Lead Time). Minimum Level = Reorder Level – (Normal Usage × Normal Lead Time). Danger Level = Minimum Level + Reorder Quantity. Average Level = (Minimum Level + Maximum Level) / 2. Reorder Level = Maximum Material Usage × Maximum Lead Time. Consumption Rate for Given Stock Level = Stock Available / Days of Supply Required.
  • LABOR COST CONTROL: Time Rate System — wage = Hours Worked × Rate per Hour. Piece Rate System — wage = Number of Units × Rate per Unit. Halsey Plan — Wage = Time Rate + 50% of Time Saved × Rate per Hour. Rowan Plan — Wage = Time Rate + (Time Saved / Time Allowed) × Time Rate; bonus is proportional to time saved ratio. Both plans incentivize time saving; Rowan gives higher bonus at same efficiency level than Halsey.
  • OVERHEAD DISTRIBUTION: Overhead Rate = Total Overhead / Allocation Base. Common bases: Floor Area (for rent, rates, lighting), Power Meter (for power), Number of Machines (for depreciation of machinery), Weight of Material (for materials handling), Direct Labor Cost % (for supervisory salaries), Number of Employees (for personnel department). Apportionment — shared costs divided among departments using suitable basis.
  • JOB COSTING: Used when work is undertaken to specific customer orders/jobs. Each job gets a Job Card/Labor Sheet. Cost of Job = Prime Cost + Factory Overheads applied. If job is incomplete at period end, work-in-progress valuation needed. Invoice Price = Cost + Profit Margin.
  • BATCH COSTING: Similar to job costing but applies to batches of identical items. Cost per Unit = Total Batch Cost / Number of Units in Batch. Used in pharmaceuticals, garments, food processing industries.
  • PROCESS COSTING: Used in continuous mass production (chemicals, textiles, food). Equivalent Units = Units Completed + (WIP Units × % Completion). Cost per Equivalent Unit = Total Process Cost / Total Equivalent Units. Abnormal Loss/Gain adjustment: Abnormal Loss valued at normal cost per unit and transferred to Abnormal Loss Account; Abnormal Gain valued similarly and transferred to Abnormal Gain Account. Normal Loss has no cost assigned to it (scrap value adjusted).
  • COST SHEET PREPARATION: Direct Material Consumed = Opening Stock + Purchases + Carriage Inward + Free Samples – Closing Stock – Purchase Return – Materials Returned to Supplier. Direct Labor = Wages + Direct Benefits – Direct Deductions. Prime Cost = Direct Material + Direct Labor + Direct Expenses. Works Cost = Prime Cost + Factory/Works Overheads. Cost of Production = Works Cost + Administrative Overheads. Cost of Sales = Cost of Production + Selling & Distribution Overheads. Cost of Sales + Profit = Sales. Adjustments for WIP at beginning and end of period needed for manufacturing cost.
  • COST LEDGER SYSTEM: In cost ledger, nominal accounts show control accounts (e.g., Material Ledger Control, Work-in-Progress Ledger Control, Factory Overhead Control, Admin Overhead Control). Reconciliation between financial and cost ledger maintained via reconciling statement. Difference arises due to items appearing in one system but not other (e.g., dividends, interest, non-operating items in financial accounts; over/under absorption of overheads in cost accounts).

Important facts

  • ACCOUNTING EQUATION: Assets = Liabilities + Capital. This is the foundation of double entry system.
  • GROSS PROFIT RATIO: (Gross Profit / Sales) × 100. Used to verify trading account figures.
  • NET PROFIT RATIO: (Net Profit / Sales) × 100.
  • OPERATING RATIO: (Cost of Revenue from Operations / Net Sales) × 100. Lower ratio indicates better operating efficiency.
  • CURRENT RATIO: Current Assets / Current Liabilities. Standard ratio considered healthy is 2:1.
  • QUICK RATIO (Acid Test): (Current Assets – Inventory) / Current Liabilities. Standard ratio 1:1.
  • INVENTORY TURNOVER RATIO: Cost of Goods Sold / Average Inventory. Shows how many times inventory is converted to sales.
  • DEBTORS TURNOVER RATIO: Credit Sales / Average Debtors. Average collection period = 365 / Debtor Turnover Ratio.
  • CREDITORS TURNOVER RATIO: Credit Purchases / Average Creditors.
  • WORKING CAPITAL TURNOVER RATIO: Net Sales / Average Working Capital.
  • RETURN ON CAPITAL EMPLOYED (ROCE): (PBIT / Capital Employed) × 100. Capital Employed = Total Assets – Current Liabilities = Equity + Non-current Liabilities.
  • DEPRECIATION RATE (WDV): r = 1 – (S/C)^(1/n), where S = salvage value, C = cost, n = useful life.
  • LABOR TURNOVER RATE (Serriemann's): Number of Separations / Average Number of Workers employed × 100. Also: (Separations + Replacements) / 2 × Average Workers × 100.
  • MATERIAL ECONOMIC ORDER QUANTITY: Q* = √(2DS/H) where D = annual demand, S = setup/ordering cost per order, H = holding cost per unit per period.
  • REORDER LEVEL FORMULA: RR = Maximum Usage × Maximum Lead Time. Also: RR = Safety Stock + (Average Usage × Average Lead Time).
  • COST OF DIRECT MATERIAL CONSUMED: Opening Stock of Raw Material + Purchases + Direct Expenses on Purchase – Closing Stock – Purchase Returns – Materials Returned to Supplier.
  • UNDER/OVER ABSORPTION OF OVERHEAD: Actual Overhead Incurred – Overhead Absorbed. Positive = Under-absorbed; Negative = Over-absorbed.
  • ABNORMAL LOSS PER UNIT = (Cost of Normal Process – Scrap Value of Normal Loss) / Normal Output of Process.
  • ABNORMAL GAIN PER UNIT = (Cost of Normal Process – Scrap Value of Normal Loss) / Normal Output of Process.
  • WORKING CAPITAL REQUIREMENT: Based on estimated costs for a specified period — typically covering raw material, labor, overheads, and working capital margin for operating expenses.
  • CASH BOOK TYPES: Single column (cash only), Double column (cash + bank), Triple column (cash + bank + discount). Cash book is both journal and ledger for cash/bank transactions.
  • PROVISION FOR DISTRESSED DEBT: Created when there is uncertainty about recovery; recorded as expense and deducted from debtors.
  • MANUFACTURING ACCOUNT vs TRADING ACCOUNT: Manufacturing account is prepared when company manufactures goods; trading account is for trading concerns buying and selling finished goods. Manufacturing account feeds into trading account.
  • FIXED ASSET VALUATION IN BALANCE SHEET: Shown at original cost less accumulated depreciation. Freehold land is not depreciated.
  • STATUTORY RESERVES: Reserves created by operation of law (e.g., Securities Premium Reserve). GENERAL RESERVES: Created out of profits without specific purpose. SPECIFIC RESERVES: Created for specific purpose (e.g., Debenture Redemption Reserve).
  • CLASSIFICATION OF EXPENSES IN P&L: Administrative expenses, Selling expenses, Distribution expenses, Financing costs, Exceptional items, Extraordinary items — each shown separately as required.
  • NOTES TO ACCOUNTS: Provide additional disclosures not shown in main financial statements — accounting policies, contingent liabilities, subsequent events, related party disclosures, segment information.

Memory aids

  • DEAD CLAW: Debit Expenses, Assets, Drawings, Losses; Credit Liabilities, Income, Assets/Gains, Capital, Withdrawals. Helps recall debit/credit rules.
  • BRIO: Business Recorded In Only transactions that can be Measured in Money terms — reinforces money measurement concept.
  • SCROLL: Statement of Receipts and Payments is for cash basis (non-profit), while Income and Expenditure is for accrual basis (non-profit). Think: 'Scroll through cash receipts, but Income and Expenditure scrolls accruals.'
  • ABC OF ACCOUNTS: Assets = Liabilities + Capital. The fundamental equation that never changes.
  • COST SHEET FLOW: Material → Labor → Expenses → Prime Cost → Factory Overhead → Works Cost → Admin Overhead → Cost of Production → Selling Dist. Overhead → Cost of Sales ± Adj → Total Cost + Profit = Sales.
  • BRS APPROACH: 'Bank Says' — start from bank statement and adjust for items bank knows but cash book doesn't (unpresented checks, direct deposits). 'Cash Book Says' — start from cash book and adjust for items cash book knows but bank doesn't.
  • DEPRECIATION MEMORY: 'Straight Line = Same Line' (equal amount each year). 'Written Down = Waning Down' (decreasing amount each year as base reduces).
  • EOQ MEMORY: 'Economic Order — √2SO/C' — think 'Easy Over Order' = √(2 × Demand × Order Cost / Carrying cost).
  • LEVEL MEMORY: 'Max is highest, Min is lowest, Danger is middle with buffer, Average is the midpoint between max and min.' Reorder sits between Min and Max — it's the trigger point.
  • ERROR RECTIFICATION: 'Wrong Account? Reverse and redirect.' 'Omitted? Add it back.' 'Commission? One-sided correction.'
  • PROCESS COSTING: 'Normal loss is free (zero cost allocation), Abnormal loss is punished (valued at normal rate), Abnormal gain is rewarded (also valued at normal rate).'

Exam tips

  • JOURNAL ENTRIES — PRACTICE DAILY: Write at least 15–20 journal entries daily covering all transaction types. Focus on accuracy of narration and correct account selection. This is foundational for all subsequent topics.
  • FINAL ACCOUNTS — STEPWISE APPROACH: Always prepare in order: Manufacturing A/c → Trading A/c → P&L A/c → Balance Sheet. Use trial balance items systematically. Mark off each item as you use it. Practice with different formats until automatic.
  • BRS — DRAW DIAGRAMS: Always draw the reconciliation diagram mentally: list cash book balance, list pass book balance, identify differences, show additions/subtractions clearly. Write a note explaining each adjustment. This earns full marks even if minor calculation error occurs.
  • DEPRECIATION — KNOW BOTH METHODS COLD: Be able to calculate depreciation for any number of years under both methods. Memorize the WDV formula and practice finding the rate when cost, salvage value, and life are given.
  • ERROR RECTIFICATION — CLASSIFY FIRST: Before rectifying, identify the TYPE of error (omission, commission, principle, compensating). Then apply the correct correction. Always pass a journal entry for rectification with proper narration.
  • COST SHEET — FOLLOW THE STRUCTURE: Do not skip any line. Always verify that prime cost → works cost → cost of production → cost of sales → total cost → sales flow logically. Cross-check with given data. Show all calculations.
  • EOQ AND REORDER LEVEL — UNITS MATTER: Ensure all units are consistent (e.g., annual demand vs monthly usage). Convert appropriately. Practice converting daily/weekly/monthly usage to annual. Always show the formula before substituting values.
  • PROCESS COSTING — EQUIVALENT UNITS FIRST: Always calculate equivalent units for each cost element separately before computing cost per unit. Handle normal loss, abnormal loss, abnormal gain distinctly. Show reconciliation of physical units.
  • RATIO ANALYSIS — COMPARE WITH STANDARD: When calculating ratios, always compare with standard/industry benchmarks and comment on the implication. A calculated ratio without interpretation loses half the marks.
  • TIME MANAGEMENT: For descriptive answers, allocate time proportionally. Journal — 2 min/question. Ledger — 5 min/account. Final accounts — 15 min. Cost sheet — 12 min. BRS — 8 min. Process costing — 15 min.
  • PRACTICE PREVIOUS YEARS QUESTIONS: ICMAI pattern emphasizes application over rote memorization. Solve at least 10 years of questions for each sub-topic. Pay attention to which areas carry more weight — final accounts and cost sheet are consistently high-weightage.
  • WRITE IN BULLET POINTS WHERE POSSIBLE: Especially in process costing and cost sheet problems, present data in tabular form. neatness and presentation carry implicit marks in evaluation.
  • KNOW THE FORMAT COLD: Financial statements have prescribed formats. Practice writing them from memory multiple times. Even if numbers are wrong, correct format earns significant marks.
  • COST ACCOUNTING TERMINOLOGY PRECISION: Use exact terminology — 'factory overheads' not 'production costs', 'administrative overheads' not 'office expenses', 'selling overheads' not 'marketing costs'. Precise terminology distinguishes top scorers.
  • REMEMBER THAT CMA FOUNDATION VALUES PROCESS: Step-wise marking is common. Show every step clearly even if final answer is incorrect. Partial marks are substantial when steps are well-presented.

Common traps

  • CONFUSING MANUFACTURING ACCOUNT WITH TRADING ACCOUNT: Manufacturing account shows cost of production only; trading account shows gross profit by comparing cost of goods sold with sales revenue. Manufacturing account is a predecessor to trading account.
  • FORGETTING TO ADJUST OPENING/CLOSING STOCK: In trading account, opening stock is added to purchases, closing stock is subtracted from purchases. Both must be correctly placed — opening on debit side, closing either on credit side or as deduction from purchasing account.
  • WRONG TREATMENT OF DIRECT EXPENSES: Freight inward, import duties, installation charges on machinery purchased for production are direct expenses and go to manufacturing/trading account, NOT to P&L.
  • MIXING UP DEPRECIATION METHODS: In straight line, depreciation amount is constant but rate changes. In WDV, rate is constant but amount changes. Exam questions often test this distinction explicitly.
  • ERROR IN BRS: Treating unpresented checks as 'not in cash book' — they ARE in cash book (credited when issued) but not in pass book yet. Similarly, directly deposited amounts are in pass book but not cash book. Many students reverse these adjustments incorrectly.
  • RECTIFICATION WITHDRAWAL FROM CAPITAL: Drawings reduce capital, they are not expenses. Drawing account is personal in nature (debited when owner withdraws), not an expense account.
  • COST SHEET UNDER/OVER ABSORPTION: If overheads are under-absorbed, add to cost of sales. If over-absorbed, deduct from cost of sales. Students frequently reverse this relationship.
  • PROCESS COSTING ABNORMAL LOSS: Normal loss is not assigned any cost (its scrap value is deducted from total process cost). Abnormal loss IS assigned full cost per unit and shown separately in abnormal loss account. Do not include normal loss units in equivalent units calculation.
  • WORK IN PROGRESS VALUATION: WIP is valued at a percentage of completion for each cost element (material may be 100%, labor 60%, overheads 50%). Never assume WIP is complete.
  • JOURNAL ENTRY SEQUENCE: Always write narration. Date format matters. Debit column first, then credit column indented. These formatting details carry marks in descriptive papers.
  • CLASSIFYING INDIRECT EXPENSES: Insurance on factory machinery goes to manufacturing account, not P&L. Office rent goes to P&L, not manufacturing. Students confuse factory vs office vs selling expenses placement.
  • CURRENT ASSETS INCLUDE NOT JUST CASH: Debtors, stock, prepaid expenses, bank balance — all current assets. Remember to include all when computing current ratio or working capital.
  • FIXED COSTS PER UNIT VARIES: Fixed cost per unit decreases as output increases (because total fixed cost is spread over more units). This inverse relationship is frequently tested.
  • CASH BOOK AS LEADER: Cash book is the principal book for cash/bank transactions and is also a ledger account. It does not require posting to a separate cash ledger account.
