Corporate Accounting and Auditing — CMA Intermediate Group 2 Quick Revision

CMAEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Share Capital (Companies Act, 2013): The capital raised by a company through the issue of shares. It includes Equity Share Capital and Preference Share Capital. Under Section 2(57) of the Act, share capital means the sum nominal value of shares issued, divided into fixed monetary units called shares.
  • Equity Share: A share that carries voting rights and is entitled to dividends after preference shareholders are paid. The dividend rate is not fixed and varies with profits.
  • Preference Share: A share carrying preferential right to receive fixed dividend and repayment of capital before equity shareholders. Sub-categories: Cumulative/Non-cumulative, Participating/Non-participating, Redeemable/Irredeemable, Convertible/Non-convertible.
  • Forfeiture of Shares: The cancellation of shares by the company when a shareholder fails to pay calls or any amount due on shares despite due notice. Forfeited shares become the property of the company.
  • Reissue of Forfeited Shares: When forfeited shares are reissued to new shareholders at a discount or premium. Any profit on reissue is transferred to Capital Reserve; any loss goes to Profit & Loss Account or forfeited share account.
  • Redemption of Shares: The process by which a company buys back its own shares from shareholders, reducing the share capital. Governed by Sections 55-60 of Companies Act, 2013.
  • Debenture: A written acknowledgment of debt by a company, carrying a certificate of indebtedness and usually creating a charge on assets. It is issued under the company's seal and carries a right to interest at a fixed rate.
  • Minority Interest: The portion of equity in a subsidiary not attributable, directly or indirectly, to the parent company. Under Ind AS 110 and Section 236, it is shown as a separate line item in the consolidated financial statements.
  • Amalgamation: The undertaking of the business of one or more companies by another company, as defined under Section 2(1B) of the Income Tax Act, 1961. Types: Pooling of Assets Method and Purchase Method.
  • Merger: The legal combination of two entities into one. In merger, the transferor company ceases to exist; only its assets and liabilities are taken over by the transferee company.
  • Internal Reconstruction: A scheme where an existing company reorganizes its capital structure without winding up, typically by writing off accumulated losses and capitalized losses against share premium or fresh share issuance.
  • External Reconstruction: A scheme where an old company is wound up and a new company is formed to take over its business, usually to shed losses or change capital structure.
  • Consolidated Financial Statements: Financial statements that present the results of a parent and its subsidiaries as a single economic entity, eliminating inter-company transactions. Governed by Ind AS 110 (Consolidated Financial Statements).
  • Audit: An independent examination of financial information of an entity, whether public or private, irrespective of its size or legal form, with a view to expressing an opinion thereon. Defined under Section 2(1)(a) of Companies Act, 2013 and SA 200.
  • Auditor: A person who is qualified to act as an auditor. Under Section 2(1)(bb), it includes a firm, and refers to the person or persons appointed for the purpose of audit. A person cannot be appointed as auditor unless they hold a certificate of practice from ICMAI.
  • Statutory Audit: An audit required by statute (law). For companies, the first statutory audit is conducted within 6 months from the date of commencing business. It is mandatory for every company under Section 139 of Companies Act, 2013.
  • Branch Audit: An audit conducted at a branch office. A branch audit may be conducted by the central auditor or by a local auditor. The report must include the central auditor's comments on local audit findings.
  • Co-extensive Liability: In case of fraud, both the company and its auditor are liable jointly and severally for the damages. Under Section 448 and 449 of Companies Act, 2013, penalties apply for concealment of facts and furnishing false information.
  • Goodwill Valuation Methods: Key methods include: (1) Average Profit Method with super profit = actual profit minus normal profit; (2) Capitalisation of Super Profit; (3) Capitalisation of Average Profit; (4) Annuity Method; (5) Multi-period Purchase of Goodwill (MPG) Method.
  • Share Valuation Methods: Common methods: Net Asset Method (intrinsic value), Yield Value Method (based on earnings), Market Price Method, Replacement Cost Method, and Break-up Value Method.

Key concepts

  • Issue of Shares at Premium and Discount: Under Section 52 of Companies Act, 2013, premium on issue of shares must be credited to Securities Premium Account, which can only be used for: writing off preliminary expenses, writing off share issue expenses, premium on redemption of shares/debentures, issuing fully paid bonus shares, and buying back shares. Discount on issue of shares is allowed only under Sections 53-54 subject to strict conditions including SEBI approval and a time limit of 2 years. The discount cannot exceed 10% of the face value.
  • Accounting Treatment of Forfeited Shares: Steps: (1) Create forfeiture account by debiting Share Account (called up capital) and crediting Forfeiture Account with the amount not paid; (2) Transfer balance of Forfeited Share account to Capital Reserve on reissue if sold at a profit; (3) If reissued at a discount, debit Forfeited Share Account with discount amount; (4) Any loss on reissue is transferred to Profit & Loss Account. Journal entries: Forfeiture entry → Debit Share A/c, Credit Forfeiture A/c; On reissue → Debit Bank, Debit Discount if any, Credit Share A/c, Credit Forfeiture A/c.
  • Redemption of Preference Shares: Governed by Sections 55-60. Mandatory requirements: (i) Full payment must be made before or at redemption; (ii) Redemption can only be out of free reserves or proceeds of fresh issue; (iii) Share Premium Account cannot be used for redemption of preference shares (only for premium on redemption); (iv) A Share Redemption Fund must be created out of profits equal to the amount redeemed; (v) Premium on redemption must be provided before redemption from Securities Premium or Share Premium Account; (vi) All terms must comply with the articles; (vii) 18% of redemption amount must be transferred to Investor Education and Protection Fund (IEPF).
  • Accounting for Debentures — Issue and Redemption: At issue: If at par — Debit Bank, Credit Debenture Account. At premium — Debit Bank, Debit Loss on Issue of Debentures, Credit Debenture Account, Credit Premium on Redemption A/c. At discount — Debit Bank, Debit Loss on Issue of Debentures, Credit Debenture Account. Sinking Fund Method: Annual investment in debenture trust securities + interest accrued. Redemption Method: Create Debenture Redemption Reserve (DRR) equal to at least 25% of debentures before redemption (Section 71 of Companies Act, 2013). DRR can be utilized only on redemption of debentures.
  • Accounting Treatment of Debenture Conversion: When debentures are converted into shares: Debit Debenture Account and Debit/Loss on Issue of Debentures (if any), Credit Share Capital Account and Credit Securities Premium Account (if at premium). The conversion is recorded at the book value of the debentures. Any unamortized loss on issue of debentures should be written off to Profit & Loss Account.
  • Final Accounts of Companies — Format Compliance: Companies prepare final accounts as per Schedule III of Companies Act, 2013. Key differences from partnership: Fixed assets shown at cost less depreciation; Investments shown separately; Reserves and surplus shown separately; Borrowings (long-term and short-term); Trade payables and other current liabilities; Shareholder's funds; Revenue from operations, other income, employee costs, finance costs, tax expense, depreciation.
  • Preparation of Consolidated Balance Sheet: Under Ind AS 110: Step 1 — Identify the parent-subsidiary relationship (control >50% voting rights or de facto control); Step 2 — Determine the reporting date of both entities; Step 3 — Identify the fair value adjustments of identifiable assets/liabilities at acquisition date; Step 4 — Calculate goodwill (Consideration + NCI - Fair value of net identifiable assets); Step 5 — Eliminate inter-company transactions, balances, and unrealized profit; Step 6 — Prepare consolidated statements showing 100% of assets and liabilities of both companies, adjusted for fair value and goodwill, with NCI shown separately. Key eliminations: Parent's investment in subsidiary vs. subsidiary's equity; Inter-company loans; Inter-company trading; Unrealized profit in closing stock; Dividend declared by subsidiary to parent.
  • Minority Interest (NCI) Calculation: NCI % × Fair value of net assets of subsidiary at reporting date, including NCI share of post-acquisition retained earnings and fair value adjustments. Under Ind AS 110, NCI can be measured at either (a) proportionate share of net identifiable assets, or (b) fair value. The choice is made on an acquisition-by-acquisition basis.
  • Amalgamation — Purchase Method: Also known as acquisition method under Ind AS 103. Steps: (1) Determine fair value of consideration transferred (shares issued + cash paid + assumption of liabilities + costs); (2) Determine fair value of net identifiable assets acquired (assets - liabilities at fair value); (3) Calculate goodwill = Consideration transferred + NCI - Fair value of net assets; (4) If negative (bargain purchase), credit to P&L; (5) Record assets at fair value, not book value; (6) Adjust existing reserves — revenue reserves like General Reserve not carried forward, but specific reserves like Debenture Redemption Reserve may be transferred.
  • Amalgamation — Pooling of Assets Method: Older method where assets and liabilities are taken over at their book values. Goodwill = Consideration - Net assets (at book value). This method is generally not permitted under Ind AS 103 except in cases of amalgamation in the nature of merger as per Section 2(1B) of IT Act where specific conditions are satisfied. Under older GAAP, purchase consideration is determined based on net assets taken over at book value.
  • Accounting for Reconstruction — Internal: Steps: (1) Prepare a balance sheet; (2) Show revaluation of assets and write off liabilities as agreed in the scheme; (3) Prepare realisation account for written off amounts; (4) Adjust capital accounts of shareholders as per scheme; (5) Prepare new balance sheet after adjustment. A capital reduction account is prepared, and any balance transferred to Capital Reserve.
  • Share Basis Dividend: Dividend declared by reference to the number of shares held rather than a fixed percentage of nominal value. Common in cooperative societies and mutual benefit organizations. The total dividend amount is calculated and distributed on a per-share basis equally among all shareholders regardless of the nominal amount of each share.
  • Audit Planning (SA 300): The auditor must establish an overall audit strategy and develop an audit plan. Key steps: (1) Perform client acceptance/continuance evaluations; (2) Establish understanding of the entity and its environment, including internal control; (3) Assess risks of material misstatement; (4) Determine materiality levels; (5) Assign suitable staff with appropriate competencies; (6) Direct the engagement team regarding the nature, timing, and extent of procedures. Documentation must include the overall audit strategy, audit plan, and any significant changes during the audit.
  • Audit Evidence (SA 500): Audit evidence is all information used by the auditor to arrive at the conclusions on which the audit opinion is based. It includes information contained in the accounting records and other information. Sufficient (quantity) and appropriate (quality — relevance and reliability) evidence is required. Procedures: Inspection, Observation, External Confirmation, Recalculation, Reperformance, Analytical Procedures, Inquiry. Reliability hierarchy: External evidence > Internal evidence; Original documents > Copies; Direct knowledge of auditor > Indirect knowledge.
  • Internal Control and Internal Audit (SA 610): Internal control provides reasonable assurance regarding objectives of operations, reporting, and compliance. SA 610 deals with using the work of an internal auditor. The external auditor should evaluate: (1) Objectivity and competence of internal auditors; (2) Systematic and disciplined approach; (3) Application of systematic methodology. Areas where external auditor may use internal audit work: substantive procedures and tests of controls where internal audit function is objective and competent.
  • Statutory Audit — Key Provisions (Companies Act, 2013): Section 139 — Appointment of auditors (first audit within 6 months of incorporation, subsequent audits at AGM). Section 140 — Removal, resignation, and rotation (mandatory rotation every 5 years for individual, 10 years for firm). Section 141 — Remuneration. Section 142 — Auditors' duties. Section 143 — Auditors' rights and powers. Section 144 — Prohibition of auditor from rendering services (non-audit services). Section 148 — Penalties. Section 150 — Qualifications and eligibility. Key: Auditor must be a member of ICMAI holding Certificate of Practice. Associate can sign along with CA/CMA.
  • Auditor's Report Types (SA 700, 705, 706, 710): Four types: (1) Unqualified/Clean Opinion — Financial statements present fairly; (2) Qualified Opinion — Material but not pervasive misstatement; (3) Adverse Opinion — Material and pervasive misstatement; (4) Disclaimer of Opinion — Inability to obtain sufficient evidence, material and pervasive. Additional: Emphasis of Matter paragraph (SA 706) — draws attention without modifying opinion; Other Matter paragraph (SA 706). Under Companies Act, section 143(3) specifies matters to be reported.
  • Valuation of Goodwill: Methods: (1) Average Profit Method: Goodwill = Average Profit × Number of Years' Purchase; (2) Super Profit Method: Super Profit = Actual Profit - Normal Profit = Actual Profit - (Capital Employed × Normal Rate of Return); Goodwill = Super Profit × Number of Years' Purchase; (3) Capitalisation of Super Profit: Goodwill = Super Profit / Normal Rate of Return; (4) Capitalisation of Average Profit: Capitalised Value = Average Profit / Normal Rate; Goodwill = Capitalised Value - Net Assets; (5) MPG Method: Used for varying profits; calculate present value factor and sum for the period. Use annuity table factors for MPG.
  • Valuation of Shares: Methods: (1) Net Asset Method: Value = (Net Assets / No. of shares) ± Adjustment for undervalued/overvalued assets and liabilities; (2) Yield Value Method: Equity Share Value = (Normal Dividend per share / Expected Rate of Dividend) × 100; Preferred Share Value = (Fixed Dividend per share / Expected Rate of Dividend) × 100; (3) Market Price Method: Use market price; (4) Super Profit Method: Capitalise super profit and allocate proportionately. For controlling stake, apply premium; for minority, apply discount.
  • Legal Liabilities of Auditors: Civil Liability: For breach of contract, negligence, misfeasance, or breach of duty. Criminal Liability: Under Section 447 — punishment for fraud up to 10 years imprisonment and fine up to 25 times the amount involved. Section 448 — punishment for false statements. Section 449 — punishment for false documents. Professional Misconduct: Under Section 147 — penalties for contravention. Co-extensive liability with company in cases of fraud. Duty to report fraud under Section 143(12) to Central Government within prescribed time.
  • Investment Valuation: Under Schedule III: Current investments at lower of cost and fair value; Long-term investments at cost. For NCFAS, valuation is at fair value through OCI or P&L. Fair value is determined as per Ind AS 113.

Important facts

  • Securities Premium Account can be used for: writing off preliminary expenses, share issue expenses, premium on redemption of shares/debentures, issuing fully paid bonus shares, buying back shares (Section 52, Companies Act, 2013). It CANNOT be used for payment of dividends.
  • Discount on issue of shares is permitted only under strict conditions (Sections 53-54): maximum 10% of face value, approved by SEBI or Tribunal, and only for listed companies. Must be disclosed in balance sheet.
  • Share Redemption Fund is NOT mandatory under Companies Act, 2013 for private companies. For public companies, DRR (Debenture Redemption Reserve) is mandatory at 25% of debentures before redemption (Section 71).
  • Minimum distribution of securities premium is for issuing fully paid bonus shares (Section 52(a)).
  • Preference shares are redeemable only after 5 years from date of issue (Section 55(2)). Redeemable at par or premium depending on articles.
  • If preference shares are redeemed out of profits, the amount equivalent to shares redeemed must be transferred to Capital Redemption Reserve (CRR), which is treated as paid-up capital.
  • Debenture Redemption Reserve (DRR) — minimum 25% of total debentures for public companies (Section 71). Can be created out of profits available for dividend. Only 5% DRR required for private companies and non-profitable companies under certain conditions (as amended).
  • On forfeiture, the amount paid by the defaulting shareholder (except the call unpaid) is forfeited and becomes forfeited share account credit.
  • Minority Interest is shown on the liabilities side of the balance sheet under the head 'Equity' in the consolidated financial statements (Ind AS 110).
  • Under Section 2(1B) of Income Tax Act, for a scheme of amalgamation to qualify as 'merger': (a) All assets/liabilities of transferor become assets/liabilities of transferee; (b) Shareholders holding ≥90% of face value of equity shares in transferor become shareholders of transferee; (c) Consideration is in shares of transferee company only.
  • Amalgamation in the nature of merger — purchase consideration is based on net assets taken over at book value. In the nature of purchase — purchase consideration is based on fair value of net assets.
  • Goodwill is an intangible asset shown under Non-current Assets in the balance sheet. It is amortised over useful life (max 10 years as per old rules; Ind AS requires impairment testing only — no amortisation).
  • SA 560 — Subsequent Events: Auditor must consider events occurring between the date of financial statements and date of audit report. Two types: adjusting events (provide evidence of conditions existing at balance sheet date) and non-adjusting events (indicative of conditions arising after balance sheet date).
  • SA 570 — Going Concern: Auditor must evaluate management's assessment of going concern assumption. If material uncertainty exists, disclose in audit report.
  • SA 580 — Written Representations: Management provides written representations as audit evidence. Date matches audit report date. Covers material matters that are significant to financial statements.
  • Auditor rotation: Individual auditor — every 5 years (cooling period 5 years). Audit firm — every 10 years (cooling period 5 years). This is mandatory for listed companies and large companies (Companies Act, Section 143(2)).
  • Under Section 143(3), the auditor's report must state: (a) Whether they have sought and obtained all information and explanations; (b) Whether proper books required by Section 128 are kept; (c) Whether balance sheet and profit & loss account are in agreement with books; (d) Whether the provisions of the Act are complied with; (e) Whether the financial statements disclose all information; (f) Whether any director is disqualified under Section 164(2).
  • Branch audit report must include the report of the local auditor and the central auditor's comments thereon. Both are signed by the respective auditors (Section 143(8)).
  • Under Section 144, an auditor cannot render any services to the company except as per Section 44A (cost audit) and other services permitted. These include: (a) Financial advisory services; (b) Business advisory services; (c) Performance audit; (d) Internal audit; (e) Actuarial advice; (f) Investment advice; (g) Management advice; (h) Taxation advice and services.
  • Penalty under Section 147: For providing false statement or certificate — imprisonment up to 1 year and fine up to Rs. 25 lakh. For fraud — Section 447 applies with heavier penalty.
  • Cost of audit fees paid by companies must be disclosed in the Board's Report (Section 134(3)(f)).
  • NCI measurement options under Ind AS 110: (a) Fair value method; (b) Proportionate share of net identifiable assets method. Choice is on acquisition-by-acquisition basis.
  • In consolidation, the parent's investment in subsidiary is eliminated against the subsidiary's equity. Any difference is goodwill or bargain purchase gain.

Memory aids

  • SPICY for Securities Premium uses: Share redemption (premium), Preliminary expenses, Issue expenses, Cash bought back shares, Yearly bonus shares (fully paid). Remember: 'SPICY funds can do five things, but never dividend!' This mnemonic helps recall Section 52 uses quickly.
  • FORD — Forfeiture On Reissue: First create Forfeiture A/c, Only then proceed to Reissue, Remember discount first goes to Forfeiture A/c, Deduct loss from profit if needed.
  • RED — Redemption Essentials: Redeem only after 5 years (public prefs), Ensure DRR is created (25%), Distribute as per scheme. This triad ensures all key redemption requirements are remembered.
  • CON — Consolidation: Consideration - Old share capital = New share capital (the basic elimination entry). Always eliminate parent's investment against subsidiary's equity first, then handle goodwill and NCI.
  • AMEND — Amalgamation Methods: Assets at fair value (Purchase Method), Merger qualifies under IT Act (Pooling), NCI shown separately, Eliminate inter-company items, Net assets method for consideration, Differentiate merger vs purchase nature. This checklist prevents missing steps.
  • PEACE for Auditor's Report Modifications: Pervasive = Adverse/Disclaimer, Except for = Qualified, Adverse = material AND pervasive, Clarify via Emphasis of Matter (no modification), Existence of doubt triggers Going Concern discussion.
  • 5-Year Rule for Rotation: 5 years for individual, 10 years for firm, cooling period 5 years. This pattern (5-10-5) follows the same 5-year blocks that are easy to remember.
  • DRR Memory Aid: Debenture Redemption Reserve — Dividend profits only, Redeem after 25%, Required before redemption. Also: DRR is 'Debt Reduction Reserve' mentally — money set aside to reduce debt liability.
  • SA Numbers Quick Reference: SA 200 (Overall Objectives), SA 300 (Planning), SA 500 (Evidence), SA 560 (Subsequent Events), SA 570 (Going Concern), SA 580 (Written Reps), SA 610 (Internal Audit), SA 700 (Audit Report), SA 705 (Modifications), SA 706 (Emphasis/Others). Remember: '200 Plan, 500 Evidence, 700 Report.'
  • Super Profit = Actual - Normal. Normal = Capital Employed × Normal Rate of Return. Goodwill = Super Profit × Years' Purchase. MPG = Multiply Super Profit × Present Value Factor for each year. Simple equation chain.

Exam tips

  • For numerical problems on share forfeiture/reissue, always follow the sequence: forfeiture journal → reissue journal → balance of forfeiture account. Draw a T-account for Forfeited Share Account to track debits and credits clearly.
  • In consolidation problems, always prepare a schedule of net assets of subsidiary including goodwill calculation BEFORE passing elimination entries. This avoids errors in NCI calculation.
  • For amalgamation problems, distinguish clearly between 'in the nature of merger' (purchase consideration = net assets at book value) and 'in the nature of purchase' (purchase consideration = fair value of net assets). This determines the treatment of reserves.
  • In redemption of shares problems, ensure you create the necessary reserves (CRR/DRR) in the correct order. The question often tests your knowledge of which reserve to create and in what amount.
  • For goodwill valuation, practice both the simple average profit method and the super profit method. Be comfortable calculating capital employed (total assets - outside liabilities or shareholders' funds) and normal rate of return.
  • When solving share valuation problems, clearly identify which method is appropriate: net asset method for liquidation/solvency, yield method for listed shares, super profit for acquiring concerns.
  • In audit theory questions, always cite the relevant SA (Standards on Auditing) number and provision of Companies Act, 2013 where applicable. Mentioning specific sections significantly improves answer quality.
  • For branch audit problems, remember that both the central auditor and local auditor sign the report. The central auditor must give their observations on the local auditor's report.
  • In questions on auditor's liabilities, distinguish between civil liability (breach of contract/negligence/misfeasance), criminal liability (fraud/false statements), and professional misconduct. Each attracts different penalties.
  • Practice the exact format for the auditor's report under Section 143(11) and SA 700. ICMAI expects candidates to reproduce the standard report format correctly.
  • When calculating Minority Interest, include the NCI's share of post-acquisition profits and fair value adjustments. Remember that under Ind AS 110, goodwill attributable to NCI is also included.
  • For problems involving inter-company transactions in consolidation, eliminate trading balances, unrealized profit, and interest received/paid between parent and subsidiary. Show the elimination entries clearly.
  • Remember: In the Purchase Method of amalgamation, all assets and liabilities of the transferor company are recorded at fair value. This is a fundamental difference from the Pooling Method.
  • Be thorough with DRR calculations: 25% of debenture value must be transferred to DRR before redemption for public companies. For private companies, the requirement is 5% under certain conditions. Know the amendment details.

Common traps

  • Trapping: Using Securities Premium Account for payment of dividends. Securities Premium is a capital reserve and CANNOT be used for distribution of profits/dividends under any circumstances.
  • Trapping: Treating DRR as a current liability. DRR is part of reserves and surplus, not a liability. It represents appropriated profit, not an obligation.
  • Trapping: Assuming redemption of preference shares can be made from capital. Redemptions must come from free reserves or fresh issue proceeds, NOT from share capital.
  • Trapping: Forgetting that on reissue of forfeited shares at discount, the discount is first adjusted against the Forfeited Share Account balance, and any balance loss goes to P&L.
  • Trapping: In pooling method of amalgamation, writing off accumulated losses through the scheme. Under pooling, accumulated losses are carried forward; they are not wiped off. This is a key distinction from internal reconstruction.
  • Trapping: Confusing minority interest with general reserves. Minority Interest belongs to shareholders outside the parent group and is shown separately under equity, not mixed with consolidated reserves.
  • Trapping: Treating goodwill as a current asset. Goodwill is always a non-current (intangible) asset. Never show it under current assets.
  • Trapping: Forgetting to eliminate unrealized profit in closing stock during consolidation. Only the parent's share of unrealized profit needs to be eliminated against goodwill/reserve, not NCI's share (under traditional method). Under Ind AS 110, both parent and NCI shares of unrealized profit are eliminated proportionately.
  • Trapping: In the MPG method, applying a flat multiplier instead of summing individual present value factors for each year. Each year has a different PV factor based on the discount rate.
  • Trapping: Writing off debenture issuance expenses entirely in the year of issue. These are deferred charges amortised over the tenure of debentures.
  • Trapping: Forgetting that the first audit appointment is made by the Board within 6 months of incorporation, not at the first AGM. Subsequent appointments are at AGMs.
  • Trapping: Considering that the auditor can certify anything beyond the scope of the Companies Act. Auditor's role is limited to expressing an opinion on financial statements, not certifying future viability or managerial efficiency.
  • Trapping: Confusing internal reconstruction with external reconstruction. Internal reconstruction retains the same legal entity; external reconstruction involves forming a new company. This distinction determines accounting treatment of reserves and losses.
  • Trapping: In amalgamation, treating the consideration as purely share-based when it includes cash component. The consideration must be split between share consideration and cash consideration for proper allocation of share capital and reserves.
  • Trapping: Forgetting that the auditor must report whether Section 164(2) disqualification of directors has occurred. This is a mandatory reporting requirement under Section 143(3)(m).
