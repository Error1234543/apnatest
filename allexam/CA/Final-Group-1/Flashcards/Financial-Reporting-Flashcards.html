CA Final - Financial Reporting Flashcards

CAEnglishFlashcards

Card 1

What is Ind AS? Give its legal basis under the Companies Act, 2013.

Ind AS stands for Indian Accounting Standards, which are converged with IFRS issued by the IASB. They are notified under Section 133 of the Companies Act, 2013 read with Rule 3 of the Companies (Indian Accounting Standards) Rules, 2015 (as amended). The central government notifies Ind AS for each class of companies.

Card 2

Which entities are mandatorily required to adopt Ind AS under the Companies (Indian Accounting Standards) Rules, 2015?

List A (Companies): (a) Listed companies; (b) Unlisted companies with paid-up capital of Rs. 500 crore or more; (c) Holding, subsidiary or joint venture companies of listed/unlisted companies referred to above. List B (Banking/Insurance/Finance): NBFCs with net worth ≥ Rs. 500 crore, insurance companies, banking companies, etc., as notified from time to time.

Card 3

What is the objective of Ind AS 1 – Presentation of Financial Statements?

To prescribe the basis for presentation of general purpose financial statements, to ensure comparability with previous periods and with other entities, and to set out overall requirements for structure and content. It requires disclosure of accounting policies, critical accounting estimates, and compliance with Ind AS.

Card 4

What are the five components of a complete set of financial statements as per Ind AS 1?

1. Statement of financial position as at end of period 2. Statement of profit and loss and other comprehensive income for the period 3. Statement of changes in equity for the period 4. Statement of cash flows for the period 5. Notes, comprising significant accounting policies and other explanatory information. Comparative information is also required.

Card 5

What does Ind AS 8 deal with? Explain 'accounting policies' vs 'change in accounting estimate'.

Ind AS 8 deals with Accounting Policies, Changes in Accounting Estimates and Errors. Accounting policies are specific principles, bases, conventions, rules and practices applied in preparation and presentation of financial statements. A change in accounting estimate adjusts the carrying amount of an asset/liability or the periodic consumption of an asset — accounted for prospectively. A change in accounting policy is applied retrospectively unless impracticable.

Card 6

When is retrospective application of a change in accounting policy not practicable as per Ind AS 8?

Retrospective application is not practicable when applying it requires making assumptions about management's intent in a prior period or significant estimates of amounts that can only be determined retrospectively using hindsight. In such cases, the entity applies the new policy prospectively from the earliest date practicable.

Card 7

Explain the classification of items as 'current' vs 'non-current' under Ind AS 1 for assets and liabilities.

Current Asset: (a) Expected to be realised in normal operating cycle, (b) Held for trading, (c) Expected to be realised within 12 months, (d) Cash/cash equivalent unless restricted ≥12 months. All others = Non-current. Current Liability: (a) Expected to be settled in normal operating cycle, (b) Held for trading, (c) Due within 12 months, (d) No unconditional right to defer ≥12 months. All others = Non-current.

Card 8

What does Ind AS 10 deal with? Distinguish between adjusting and non-adjusting events after the reporting period.

Ind AS 10 — Events after the Reporting Period. Adjusting events provide evidence of conditions existing at the end of the reporting period; financial statements must be adjusted. Non-adjusting events are indicative of conditions arising after the reporting period; no adjustment is made but disclosure of nature and estimated financial effect may be required. Dividends declared after the reporting period are non-adjusting.

Card 9

Under Ind AS 10, when is a dividend declared after the reporting period a non-adjusting event? What disclosure is needed?

Dividends declared (including proposed) after the reporting period are non-adjusting events because they represent a condition arising after the reporting date. They are not recognised as a liability at the reporting date. If material, the entity discloses the nature of the dividend and the amount.

Card 10

What is Ind AS 11? Explain 'construction contract', 'fixed-price contract' and 'cost-plus contract'.

Note: Ind AS 11 Construction Contracts has been superseded by Ind AS 115 for contracts entered into on/after 1 April 2018. However, for legacy/understanding: A construction contract builds/constructs a single asset or set of closely interrelated assets. Fixed-price contract: fixed agreed price. Cost-plus contract: cost incurred plus a percentage or fixed fee. Revenue and costs are recognised using the stage-of-completion method.

Card 11

What are the key principles of Ind AS 115 – Revenue from Contracts with Customers?

A 5-step model: Step 1 – Identify the contract with the customer. Step 2 – Identify the performance obligations in the contract. Step 3 – Determine the transaction price. Step 4 – Allocate the transaction price to the performance obligations. Step 5 – Recognise revenue when (or as) the performance obligation is satisfied. Control is key — transfer of control rather than risks/rewards.

Card 12

When does a performance obligation get satisfied over time vs at a point in time under Ind AS 115?

Satisfied OVER TIME if any one of: (a) Customer simultaneously receives/consumes benefits as entity performs; (b) Entity's performance creates/enhances an asset that customer controls as performed; (c) Entity's performance does not create an asset with alternative use and entity has an enforceable right to payment for performance completed to date. Otherwise, satisfied AT A POINT IN TIME.

Card 13

How does Ind AS 115 treat variable consideration? Give examples.

Variable consideration includes discounts, rebates, refunds, credits, incentives, performance bonuses, penalties, and pricing concessions. It is estimated using either (a) expected value method (probability-weighted — best for large portfolio) or (b) most likely amount method (best for single outcome). Constraint: recognise only to the extent it is highly probable that a significant reversal will not occur when uncertainty is resolved.

Card 14

What is treatment of contract costs under Ind AS 115?

Incremental costs of obtaining a contract (e.g., sales commissions): recognised as an asset if expected to be recovered, then amortised consistently with the transfer of goods/services. Costs to fulfil a contract: capitalised if they relate directly to contract, generate/enhance resources, and are expected to be recovered. Both are tested for impairment separately.

Card 15

What does Ind AS 16 deal with? Define 'cost model' and 'revaluation model'.

Ind AS 16 – Property, Plant and Equipment. After recognition, an entity shall choose either: (a) Cost model: cost less accumulated depreciation and impairment losses. (b) Revaluation model: revalued amount = fair value at date of revaluation less subsequent accumulated depreciation and impairment. Revaluations must be sufficiently regular. Whole class must be revalued, not selective assets.

Card 16

How is a change from cost model to revaluation model accounted for under Ind AS 16?

It is treated as a change in accounting policy under Ind AS 8. It is applied retrospectively. The revalued amount becomes the new deemed cost. Any difference between the previous carrying amount and revalued amount is adjusted through OCI (to revaluation surplus) or P&L depending on whether it reverses a previous revaluation deficit on the same asset.

Card 17

What is the impairment test framework under Ind AS 36 – Impairment of Assets?

At each reporting date, assess whether there is any indication of impairment. If yes, estimate recoverable amount (higher of fair value less costs of disposal and value in use). If carrying amount > recoverable amount, recognise impairment loss. Impairment is allocated first to goodwill, then pro-rata among other assets of the CGU. Impairment loss on goodwill is never reversed.

Card 18

What is a 'cash-generating unit' (CGU) under Ind AS 36?

The smallest identifiable group of assets that generates cash inflows that are largely independent of the cash inflows from other assets or groups of assets. Goodwill acquired in a business combination is allocated to each CGU (or group of CGUs) expected to benefit from the synergies of the combination.

Card 19

When can an impairment loss recognised in prior periods be reversed under Ind AS 36?

An impairment loss may be reversed if there is an indication that the impairment no longer exists or has decreased, except for goodwill (which cannot be reversed). Reversal is limited to the carrying amount that would have been determined (net of depreciation/amortisation) had no impairment been recognised. Reversal is recognised in P&L.

Card 20

What is Ind AS 38 on Intangible Assets? Differentiate intangible assets from goodwill.

Ind AS 38 defines an intangible asset as an identifiable non-monetary asset without physical substance. Identifiable = separable or arises from contractual/legal rights. Goodwill is different — it represents future economic benefits from assets that cannot be individually identified and separately recognised. Internally generated goodwill, brands, mastheads, and customer lists are NOT recognised as intangible assets.

Card 21

What are the recognition criteria for intangible assets under Ind AS 38? How is research vs development treated?

Recognition: (a) Probable future economic benefits, (b) Reliable measurement of cost. Research phase expenses are ALWAYS expensed. Development phase: capitalise if entity can demonstrate technical feasibility, intention to complete, ability to use/sell, how it generates economic benefits, availability of resources, and ability to measure expenditure reliably (IAS 38/Ind AS 38 criteria). Otherwise, expense.

Card 22

What is the measurement basis for intangible assets after initial recognition under Ind AS 38?

Two models: (a) Cost model: cost less accumulated amortisation and accumulated impairment losses. (b) Revaluation model: only if there is an active market for the intangible asset (very rare in practice — e.g., landing rights, taxi licenses). Revaluation model follows similar principles as Ind AS 16.

Card 23

Explain Ind AS 2 – Inventories. What costs are included in cost of inventories?

Costs of purchase (purchase price, import duties, transport, handling, less trade discounts), costs of conversion (direct labour + systematic allocation of fixed/variable production overheads), and other costs incurred in bringing inventories to their present location and condition. Excludes: abnormally wasted materials/labour/overheads, storage costs (unless necessary in process), and administrative overheads not related to production. Trading/organisation costs are excluded.

Card 24

How is 'lower of cost and net realisable value' applied under Ind AS 2?

NRV = estimated selling price less estimated costs of completion and costs to sell. Apply NRV comparison: (a) item-by-item basis normally; (b) for similar/homogeneous items, may group them by category; (c) where items are produced/used in batches, may be grouped. Write-down to NRV recognised as expense in P&L. Reversal of write-down permitted if NRV increases, but not above original cost.

Card 25

What cost formulas are permitted under Ind AS 2? When can FIFO/weighted average be used?

Permitted: (a) FIFO (First-In-First-Out) — assumes first items purchased are first sold. (b) Weighted average cost — cost determined by weighting each unit's cost by quantity. Specific identification used for items not interchangeable. LIFO is NOT permitted. Choice depends on nature of inventory; consistent application required.

Card 26

What does Ind AS 17 (still applicable for leases entered before 1 April 2019) say about operating vs finance lease?

Operating lease: lease that does NOT transfer substantially all risks and rewards incidental to ownership. Lease payments recognised as expense on straight-line basis over lease term. Finance lease: transfers substantially all risks and rewards of ownership. Asset and liability recognised at lower of fair value and present value of minimum lease payments. Depreciated by lessee; interest allocation over lease term.

Card 27

What are the key changes introduced by Ind AS 116 – Leases effective from 1 April 2019?

Ind AS 116 eliminates the distinction between operating and finance leases for LESSEES. Lessee recognises a ROU asset and a lease liability for almost all leases. Lessor accounting remains similar (operating/finance lease classification retained). Lease liability measured at PV of lease payments; ROU asset = lease liability + prepayments + initial direct costs + restoration costs. Short-term and low-value exemptions apply.

Card 28

How is 'lease liability' measured initially and subsequently under Ind AS 116?

Initially: PV of unpaid lease payments discounted using (a) rate implicit in lease, or (b) incremental borrowing rate. Payments include fixed payments, variable based on index/rate, exercise price if reasonably certain, residual value guarantees, and purchase option if reasonably certain. Subsequently: increased for interest (effective interest method), reduced for payments made. Remeasured on triggering events (change in lease term, index/rate).

Card 29

What are the exemptions available to a lessee under Ind AS 116?

Two exemptions: (a) Short-term lease: lease term ≤12 months and no purchase option — lessee may elect to recognise lease payments as expense on straight-line basis. (b) Low-value asset lease: underlying asset of low value when new (e.g., tablets, personal computers, office furniture) — lessee may elect to expense on straight-line basis. Election is by class of underlying asset.

Card 30

What is the core principle of Ind AS 109 – Financial Instruments?

Ind AS 109 covers classification and measurement, impairment, and hedge accounting of financial instruments. Financial assets classified based on: (a) business model for managing cash flows, (b) contractual cash flow characteristics (SPPI test). Categories: Amortised cost, FVOCI, FVTPL. Financial liabilities: generally at amortised cost; exceptions include FVTPL and those designated at FVTPL.

Card 31

How are financial assets classified under Ind AS 109 based on business model and SPPI test?

Business model test determines whether cash flows collected are solely principal and interest (SPPI): (1) Hold to collect contractual cash flows → Amortised cost. (2) Hold to collect AND sell → FVOCI (debt). (3) Other business models or fails SPPI → FVTPL. Equity instruments are always FVTPL unless irrevocable election at inception is made to present changes in FV in OCI (no recycling).

Card 32

Explain the expected credit loss (ECL) model under Ind AS 109. What are the three stages?

Stage 1: Performing — ECL = 12-month ECL (losses from default events possible within 12 months). Stage 2: Underperforming (significant increase in credit risk since origination) — Lifetime ECL. Stage 3: Defaulted — Lifetime ECL + interest on gross carrying amount (not net of allowance). Significant increase in credit risk presumed if contractual payments are >30 days past due.

Card 33

When is derecognition of a financial asset required under Ind AS 109?

Derecognise when: (a) Rights to cash flows from the asset expire, or (b) Financial asset is transferred and substantially all risks and rewards of ownership are transferred, or (c) Entity neither transfers nor retains substantially all risks and rewards but has NOT retained control. If control retained, continue to recognise to extent of continuing involvement.

Card 34

What is the treatment of transaction costs for financial assets/liabilities classified at amortised cost vs FVTPL under Ind AS 109?

Transaction costs for assets/liabilities at amortised cost or FVOCI (debt) are CAPITALISED as part of the initial measurement and adjusted to amortised cost using effective interest method. Transaction costs for financial assets/liabilities at FVTPL are EXPENSED in P&L immediately.

Card 35

What does Ind AS 102 – Share-based Payment cover? Distinguish equity-settled vs cash-settled.

Ind AS 102 covers employee and non-employee share-based payments. Equity-settled: entity receives goods/services as consideration for equity instruments of the entity — measured at fair value of equity instruments, recognised in equity (reserve), no remeasurement. Cash-settled: entity incurs a liability to pay cash/other assets based on share price — measured at fair value, remeasured at each reporting date until settlement.

Card 36

How is vesting and non-vesting conditions treated under Ind AS 102 for equity-settled schemes?

Vesting conditions (service/market/non-market performance): include in estimate of number of instruments expected to vest; adjust if conditions not met. Non-vesting conditions: include in fair value determination at grant date; do not adjust for failure to meet. Forfeitures due to non-vesting conditions: already reflected in fair value. Grant date fair value used; no remeasurement thereafter.

Card 37

What is the accounting treatment for cash-settled share-based payments under Ind AS 102?

Recognise a liability at fair value of the obligation. Fair value is remeasured at each reporting date and at settlement date. Changes in fair value are recognised in P&L. The liability continues to be remeasured until settled. If settled within 2 years from grant date and variables remain unchanged, fair value at settlement can approximate grant date fair value for practical purposes.

Card 38

What is Ind AS 20 on Accounting for Government Grants? Distinguish capital vs revenue grants.

Ind AS 20 applies to government grants (both non-monetary at fair value) and government assistance. Capital grant: relates to acquisition of long-lived assets. Revenue grant: relates to expenses/capacity. Two methods permitted: (a) Capital approach — grant deferred and released to P&L over useful life of asset. (b) Income approach — grant recognised in P&L on systematic basis over relevant periods. Tax credits equivalent to grants also covered.

Card 39

When should borrowing costs be capitalised under Ind AS 23?

Borrowing costs directly attributable to acquisition/construction of a qualifying asset (requires substantial period to get ready for use/sale) are CAPITALISED as part of cost of that asset. All other borrowing costs are EXPENSED in P&L in the period incurred. Capitalisation begins when expenditures for asset are being incurred, borrowing costs are being incurred, and activities necessary to prepare the asset are in progress. Suspension during extended interruptions.

Card 40

What does 'qualifying asset' mean under Ind AS 23 – Borrowing Costs?

A qualifying asset is one that necessarily takes a substantial period of time to get ready for its intended use or sale. Examples: manufacturing plants, power plants, investment properties, inventories requiring long production cycles. Assets that are ready for use/sale when acquired, or inventories routinely produced quickly, are NOT qualifying assets.

Card 41

What is Ind AS 37 on Provisions, Contingent Liabilities and Contingent Assets?

Ind AS 37 deals with provisions (liabilities of uncertain timing or amount), contingent liabilities, and contingent assets. A provision is recognised when: (a) Present obligation (legal or constructive) exists, (b) Probable outflow (>50%), (c) Reliable estimate can be made. Contingent liability: possible obligation or present obligation where outflow is improbable or cannot be reliably measured — disclosed but not recognised. Contingent asset: possible asset — disclosed if probable but not recognised until virtually certain.

Card 42

What constitutes a 'constructive obligation' under Ind AS 37?

A constructive obligation arises from an entity's actions where: (a) By an established pattern of past practice, published policies, or a sufficiently specific current statement, the entity has indicated to other parties it will accept certain responsibilities; and (b) As a result, the entity has created a valid expectation on the part of those other parties that it will discharge those responsibilities. Example: frequent restructuring announcements leading employees/customers to expect the entity to honour commitments.

Card 43

How are provisions measured under Ind AS 37? What is expected value vs single obligation?

Provision measured at best estimate of expenditure required to settle the obligation. Single obligation: most likely amount. Large population of items (e.g., warranties): expected value (probability-weighted estimates). Discounting used if time value of money is material — rate is pre-tax rate reflecting current market assessments. Increases in provision due to passage of time (unwinding discount) are recognised as finance cost.

Card 44

What disclosure requirements exist for contingent liabilities and contingent assets under Ind AS 37?

Contingent liability: disclose estimated financial effect, uncertainties, and possibility of reimbursement. Contingent asset: disclose description and estimated financial effect unless remote. If outflow is 'possible' (more than remote but less than probable), it is still a contingent liability requiring disclosure. Contingent assets recognised only when virtually certain (then they become actual assets).

Card 45

What is Ind AS 24 – Related Party Disclosures? Who are 'related parties'?

Ind AS 24 requires disclosure of related party relationships and transactions. Related parties include: (a) Parent, subsidiaries, fellow subsidiaries, associates, joint ventures, and investors in joint ventures. (b) Key management personnel (KMP) and their close family members. (c) Entities controlled, jointly controlled, or significantly influenced by KMP or their close family. (d) Post-employment benefit plans for benefit of employees of the entity or group entities.

Card 46

What are 'key management personnel' under Ind AS 24?

Key management personnel (KMP) are those persons having authority and responsibility for planning, directing and controlling the activities of the entity, directly or indirectly. This includes directors (executive and non-executive) of the board and persons entrusted with similar responsibilities (e.g., CEO, CFO, COO). Close family members of KMP are also related parties.

Card 47

When are disclosures exempt under Ind AS 24 for related party transactions?

Disclosures are NOT required for: (a) State-controlled entities — if disclosure would prejudice the performance of the entity's functions (government provides exemption); (b) Transactions with other KMP of the same reporting entity or group; (c) Pricing differences in transactions between related parties not disclosed if disclosed elsewhere (e.g., interim reports); (d) Subsidiary that is itself a reporting entity — intra-group transactions within the consolidated group are eliminated.

Card 48

What is Ind AS 108 on Operating Segments? How are reportable segments determined?

Operating segment = component of an entity (a) engaged in business activities, (b) whose operating results are regularly reviewed by the chief operating decision maker (CODM), and (c) for which discrete financial information is available. Reportable segments: (a) Quantitative thresholds: revenue ≥10% of total, profit/loss ≥10% of total (higher absolute), assets ≥10% of total; (b) All segments below thresholds aggregated only if similar; (c) Total external revenue of reportable segments must be ≥75% of total entity revenue.

Card 49

What is the CODM under Ind AS 108? Why is identifying the CODM critical?

The Chief Operating Decision Maker (CODM) is the person or group that allocates resources and assesses performance of operating segments. It could be CEO, CFO, executive committee, or even a collective body — identified based on who actually performs those functions, not just title. Segment disclosures are prepared based on the internal reporting used by the CODM, not necessarily GAAP-aligned reports.

Card 50

What is Ind AS 33 – Earnings Per Share? Differentiate basic vs diluted EPS.

Basic EPS = Profit attributable to equity shareholders ÷ Weighted average number of equity shares outstanding during the period. Diluted EPS = Profit attributable to equity shareholders ÷ Adjusted weighted average number of shares (including dilutive potential equity shares). Potential equity shares: convertible bonds, options, warrants, convertible preference shares. For diluted EPS, convertibles are assumed converted at beginning (or issue date); options/warrants use treasury stock method.

Card 51

How are adjustments made for convertible debt in diluted EPS under Ind AS 33?

If dilutive, increase the denominator by the number of shares on conversion. Decrease the numerator by the post-tax interest expense (and any other expenses) that would not have been incurred had the debt been converted. The adjustment reflects the interest saved net of tax. If the instrument is antidilutive, it is ignored in diluted EPS calculation.

Card 52

What is the treasury stock method for calculating dilutive potential equity shares (options/warrants)?

Under treasury stock method: assume options/warrants are exercised at the beginning (or date of grant). Proceeds from exercise = (exercise price × number of shares). Assume proceeds are used to buy back shares at average market price during the period. Incremental shares = shares issued on exercise minus shares repurchased. If exercise price ≥ average market price, the instrument is antidilutive and ignored.

Card 53

What is Ind AS 12 – Income Taxes? Differentiate current tax and deferred tax.

Current tax: tax payable/recoverable on taxable profit for the period, measured using enacted or substantively enacted tax rates. Deferred tax: recognized for temporary differences between carrying amount and tax base of assets/liabilities. Deferred tax asset (DTA): future tax benefit. Deferred tax liability (DTL): future tax liability. Temporary differences arise when carrying amount ≠ tax base. DTA recognised only if probable future taxable profit available against which DTA can be utilised.

Card 54

What are temporary differences? Give examples of taxable and deductible temporary differences.

Taxable temporary difference: gives rise to DTL — Carrying Amount of asset > Tax Base (future recovery will lead to taxable amount). Example: PPE at revalued amount; capitalised borrowing costs. Deductible temporary difference: gives rise to DTA — Carrying Amount of asset < Tax Base or Carrying Amount of liability > Tax Base. Example: provisions, accrued expenses not yet deductible for tax, tax losses carried forward, DTA on unabsorbed depreciation.

Card 55

When is a deferred tax asset recognised under Ind AS 12? What is the constraint?

Deferred tax asset is recognised for all deductible temporary differences, carry-forward of unused tax losses and tax credits to the extent that it is PROBABLE that future taxable profit will be available against which the benefit can be utilised. This requires assessment of future profits (including reversing temporary differences), tax planning opportunities, and past profitable history. For assessing probability of sufficient future taxable profits, the entity considers both timing and amount.

Card 56

How is the tax base of an asset/liability determined under Ind AS 12?

Tax base of an asset = amount that will be deductible for tax purposes against any taxable economic benefits that will flow to the entity when it recovers the carrying amount of the asset. Tax base of a liability = carrying amount less amount deductible for tax purposes in future settlement. For revenue received in advance: tax base = carrying amount less amount not taxable in future.

Card 57

What is the accounting for current year tax, prior year tax adjustments, and deferred tax in the statement of profit and loss?

Current tax expense for the period is recognised in P&L (unless relating to items recognised in OCI or equity). Prior year tax adjustments relate to under/over-provision from previous periods — recognised in P&L in the period of adjustment. Deferred tax is recognised in P&L (except when related to items in OCI or equity, in which case deferred tax is also recognised in OCI/equity). Tax on OCI items is also recognised in OCI.

Card 58

What does Ind AS 104 – Insurance Contracts cover? What is its current status in India?

Ind AS 104 was an interim standard for insurance contracts while the IASB worked on IFRS 17. In India, Ind AS 104 applies to insurance companies that are not yet required to adopt Ind AS 111 or the Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority (IRDA) notified standards. Note: IFRS 17 has been issued by IASB; ICAI may notify an Ind AS equivalent in future. Current treatment: insurance contracts within scope of Ind AS 104 continue to follow the erstwhile Indian GAAP insurance accounting.

Card 59

What is the key principle behind Ind AS 111 – Joint Arrangements?

Joint arrangement = arrangement jointly controlled by two or more parties. Two types: (a) Joint operation: parties have rights to assets and obligations for liabilities — recognise own assets, liabilities, revenue, expenses. (b) Joint venture: parties have rights to net assets — account using equity method (similar to associate). Classification depends on whether the structured entity has its own finance and whether rights/obligations belong to the venturers individually.

Card 60

What distinguishes a joint operation from a joint venture under Ind AS 111?

Joint operation: each venturer has rights to assets and obligations for liabilities relating to the arrangement — venturer recognises its share of assets, liabilities, revenue, and expenses. Joint venture: each venturer has rights to the net assets of the arrangement — venturer applies equity accounting. The classification depends on the structure, legal form, contractual terms, and other facts/circumstances.

Card 61

What is Ind AS 110 – Consolidated Financial Statements? When is control established?

Ind AS 110 defines control as having: (a) Power over the investee (existing rights that give ability to direct relevant activities), (b) Exposure or rights to variable returns from involvement with the investee, (c) Ability to use power to affect returns. Control assessment is continuous. Special purpose entities (SPEs) are consolidated by the entity that controls them even without equity ownership. Loss of control occurs when any one of the three elements ceases.

Card 62

How are non-controlling interests (NCI) measured under Ind AS 110?

NCI at acquisition date can be measured either: (a) At fair value (full goodwill method) — NCI gets full share of goodwill; or (b) At NCI's proportionate share of acquiree's identifiable net assets (partial goodwill method). Choice is made on an acquisition-by-acquisition basis. Subsequently, NCI is measured at its share of net assets plus share of post-acquisition changes in equity.

Card 63

What is the treatment of intra-group transactions and unrealised profits in consolidation under Ind AS 110?

All intra-group assets/liabilities, equity, income, expenses, and cash flows MUST be eliminated in full. Unrealised profits on intra-group transactions are eliminated against the relevant profit line item. For downstream sales (parent to sub), elimination is against parent's profit. For upstream sales (sub to parent), elimination is shared between controlling and NCI interest proportionately.

Card 64

What happens to gain/loss on loss of control of a subsidiary under Ind AS 110?

On loss of control: (a) Derecognise assets/liabilities of subsidiary at carrying amount. (b) Derecognise carrying amount of NCI. (c) Derecognise cumulative translation reserve related to that subsidiary. (d) Recognise fair value of consideration received. (e) Recognise any investment retained at fair value. (f) Reclassify parent's share of OCI to P&L. Gain/loss = consideration + FV of retained interest + FV of NCI − carrying amount of net assets − cumulative OCI. Recognised in P&L.

Card 65

What are the key consolidation adjustments when a parent acquires a subsidiary partway through the year?

Subsidiary's financial statements are used for the entire period from acquisition date to reporting date. Revenue/expenses are consolidated from the date control is obtained. NCI is calculated on post-acquisition profits. Goodwill = consideration + NCI at acquisition − FV of identifiable net assets at acquisition. Any pre-acquisition reserves of subsidiary are eliminated against goodwill/consideration. Retained earnings of subsidiary include only post-acquisition amount.

Card 66

What does Ind AS 109 say about hedging? Describe the three hedging relationships.

Ind AS 109 allows hedge accounting for: (a) Fair value hedge: hedge of exposure to changes in fair value of a recognised asset/liability or unrecognised firm commitment. (b) Cash flow hedge: hedge of exposure to variability in cash flows attributable to a recognised asset/liability or forecast transaction. (c) Hedge of a net investment in a foreign operation. Hedge effectiveness is assessed throughout the hedging relationship. Only highly effective hedges qualify.

Card 67

How are gains/losses on fair value hedges accounted for under Ind AS 109?

Fair value hedge: Gains/losses on the hedging instrument are recognised in P&L. Gains/losses on the hedged item attributable to hedged risk adjust the carrying amount of the hedged item and are also recognised in P&L. If the hedged item is a financial asset at FVOCI, the hedging gain/loss is recognised in P&L and the FVOCI reserve is adjusted accordingly. Discontinuation when hedge ceases to qualify.

Card 68

How are gains/losses on cash flow hedges accounted for under Ind AS 109?

Cash flow hedge: Effective portion of gain/loss on hedging instrument is recognised in OCI and accumulated in cash flow hedge reserve within equity. Ineffective portion is recognised immediately in P&L. Amounts in cash flow hedge reserve are reclassified to P&L in the same period(s) when the hedged expected cash flows affect P&L. If hedge no longer qualifies, cumulative amount in OCI remains until hedged transaction affects P&L (or is recognised in cost of asset/liability).

Card 69

What is Ind AS 105 – Non-current Assets Held for Sale and Discontinued Operations?

Non-current asset (or disposal group) classified as held for sale when: (a) Carrying amount will be recovered principally through sale rather than continuing use, (b) Available for immediate sale in present condition, (c) Sale is highly probable (active program to locate buyer, reasonable expectation that sale will be completed within one year, price is reasonable, sale is unlikely to be withdrawn). Measured at lower of carrying amount and fair value less costs to sell. Not depreciated while classified as held for sale.

Card 70

What constitutes a discontinued operation under Ind AS 105?

A discontinued operation is a component of an entity that either: (a) Represents a separate major line of business or geographical area of operations, (b) Is part of a single co-ordinated plan to dispose of a separate major line of business/geographical area, or (c) Is a subsidiary acquired exclusively with a view to resale. Results of discontinued operations are presented separately in P&L — both continuing and discontinued operations disclosed distinctly.

Card 71

What are the key differences between Ind AS and revised Indian GAAP (Schedule III) regarding financial statement presentation?

Key differences: (1) Ind AS uses complete international format with OCI, while Schedule III prescribes a specific Indian format. (2) Ind AS requires statement of changes in equity. (3) Ind AS 109 vs Schedule III: impairment is expected credit loss vs incurred loss model. (4) Ind AS requires more disclosures (segment, risk management, fair value hierarchy). (5) Ind AS uses revaluation model option for PPE/intangibles; Schedule III generally follows cost model. (6) Ind AS requires recognition of deferred tax assets/liabilities.

Card 72

How is 'other comprehensive income' presented under Ind AS 1 vs Schedule III format?

Under Ind AS 1, 'Other Comprehensive Income' can be presented in either: (a) A single statement (one-statement approach) showing P&L and OCI together; or (b) Two statements (two-statement approach) — a separate statement of P&L and a statement of comprehensive income. Items that may be reclassified to P&L (recycling) and items that will not be reclassified are disclosed separately. Schedule III combines it differently and does not have the same detailed structure.

Card 73

What is the principle of 'materiality' and 'aggregation' in financial reporting under Ind AS?

Materiality: Information is material if omitting, misstating or obscuring it could reasonably influence the economic decisions of users. Entities aggregate similar items; immaterial items are presented separately. Aggregation: Items with similar nature/functions are aggregated; dissimilar items are presented separately. The level of aggregation depends on materiality and the need to provide useful information without excessive detail. Redundant or repetitive disclosures are avoided.

Card 74

What are the essential features of a faithful representation in financial reporting?

Faithful representation requires that financial statements depict substance rather than mere legal form, are neutral (free from bias), and are complete (all information necessary for understanding). Qualitative characteristics: Relevance + Faithful representation constitute fundamental qualitative characteristics. Comparability, verifiability, timeliness, and understandability are enhancing qualitative characteristics.

Card 75

What is the conceptual framework's definition of an asset and liability?

Asset: A present economic resource controlled by the entity as a result of past events. An economic resource is a right that has the potential to produce economic benefits. Liability: A present obligation of the entity to transfer an economic resource as a result of past events. Obligations can be legal or constructive. Future actions by the entity do not create present obligations.

Card 76

When should an entity recognise a provision for onerous contracts under Ind AS 37?

An onerous contract exists when the unavoidable costs of meeting obligations under the contract exceed the economic benefits expected to be received. The entity recognises a provision for the present best estimate of the excess. Before recognising a provision for onerous contracts, the entity assesses whether a present obligation exists. The provision is measured at the lower of cost of fulfilling the contract and compensation/penalties for non-fulfilment.

Card 77

What is the accounting for restructuring provisions under Ind AS 37? What makes a restructuring a 'present obligation'?

A restructuring provision is recognised only when: (a) The entity has a detailed formal plan for the restructuring identifying at least: the business/activity affected, main locations, approximate number of employees, expenditures, and timing; and (b) The entity has raised a valid expectation in those affected that it will carry out the restructuring by either starting implementation or announcing the plan's main features. A general intention to restructure is insufficient.

Card 78

What disclosure requirements does Ind AS 1 have for 'going concern' assumption?

Management shall make an assessment of an entity's ability to continue as a going concern. Financial statements shall be prepared on a going concern basis UNLESS management intends to liquidate or cease trading, or has no realistic alternative. Disclosure required: (a) That financial statements are prepared on a going concern basis, (b) If going concern assumption is not appropriate, disclosure of that fact along with the basis used and the reason. If material uncertainties exist that may cast significant doubt on going concern, those uncertainties must be disclosed.

Card 79

What is the treatment of subsequent events that require adjustment vs disclosure?

Adjusting events (provide evidence of conditions existing at balance sheet date): financial statements are adjusted. Examples: settlement of court cases confirming pre-existing obligation, bankruptcy of customer confirming pre-existing receivable impairment, discovery of fraud/error, sale of inventory below cost confirming pre-existing NRV decline. Non-adjusting events (conditions arose after balance sheet date): no adjustment, but material ones are disclosed. Examples: major business combination, disposals, asset impairment, declaring dividends, major purchase/sale of assets, market crashes, fire/flood.

Card 80

How does Ind AS 115 treat a warranty in a sales contract?

A warranty may be: (a) Assurance-type warranty (standard protection that product works as specified) — covered under Ind AS 37; provision recognised based on past experience. (b) Service-type warranty (provides additional service beyond assurance) — constitutes a separate performance obligation under Ind AS 115; transaction price is allocated to this obligation and revenue recognised over the warranty period. Distinction is critical for correct revenue allocation.

Card 81

What is the 'majority voting rights' vs 'protective rights' distinction in Ind AS 110?

Majority voting rights typically confer control (power to direct relevant activities). However, holding >50% does not automatically mean control — consider whether the rights are substantive and whether the holder has the practical ability to exercise them. Protective rights protect the interest of the holding party but do not give power to direct relevant activities (e.g., approval of budget changes, approval of capital expenditure beyond normal course). Protective rights alone do NOT confer control.

Card 82

What are the disclosure requirements for 'significant judgements and estimates' under Ind AS 1?

Entities must disclose the judgements, apart from those involving estimates, that management made in applying accounting policies that have the most significant effect on amounts recognised in financial statements. For estimates of uncertainty: nature of assumption or other sources of estimation uncertainty, and sensitivity of carrying amounts to changes in those estimates. Examples: useful life of PPE, recoverability of DTA, stage of completion of long-term contracts, provision measurements, fair value of financial instruments.
