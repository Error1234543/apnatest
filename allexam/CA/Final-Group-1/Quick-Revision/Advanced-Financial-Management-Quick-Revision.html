Quick Revision – Advanced Financial Management (CA Final Group 1)

CAEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC): The blended required rate of return of a firm after considering the proportionate weights of equity, debt, preference shares and other sources of capital. It is used as the discount rate for evaluating projects under the corporate viewpoint.
  • Modigliani–Miller (MM) Hypothesis: A set of theoretical propositions on capital-structure irrelevance (under perfect markets) and the effects of leverage, taxes, and flotation costs on firm value. Per-MM without tax, total firm value is independent of capital structure; with corporate tax, value rises with debt due to the interest-tax shield.
  • Adjusted Present Value (APV): An appraisal technique that separates the value of an investment into the base-case all-equity NPV plus the present value of financing side effects such as the tax shield on debt, subsidies, flotation costs and distress costs.
  • Cost of Retained Earnings: The opportunity cost to existing shareholders of forgoing dividends and retaining earnings for reinvestment. It equals the required return on equity calculated via CAPM or the dividend-growth model.
  • Dividend Relevance vs Irrelevance: Lintner–Miller argue dividends do not affect firm value in perfect markets (irrelevance). Miller and Scholes, and observed empirical behaviour, suggest relevance due to clientelle effects, signalling, information asymmetry and tax differentials between dividend and capital-gain income.
  • Buyback vs Dividend: A share buyback reduces outstanding equity and returns cash to shareholders selectively, often preferred when management believes shares are undervalued. A cash dividend distributes profits pro-rata. Tax treatment, signalling, flexibility and control consequences differentiate the two.
  • M&M Dividend Model (with personal taxes): An extension of the MM framework incorporating differences between personal tax rates on dividends and capital gains. When dividend tax exceeds capital-gain tax, firms tend to prefer buybacks; the opposite holds otherwise.
  • Valuation of Business/Share: The process of estimating the fair worth of a business entity or its equity using income, market or asset-based approaches. Income methods include DCF/FCFF/FCFE and residual income; market methods use comparable multiples; asset methods adjust book values to market/replacement values.
  • Hedge Ratio / Effective Forward Rate: The proportion of exposure hedged or the contract rate implied by interest-rate parity, used to compute the synthetic forward rate when hedging cross-border receivables/payables.
  • Swap: A contractual arrangement to exchange cash-flow streams—typically fixed for floating interest payments (interest-rate swap) or principal and interest in two currencies (currency swap)—to manage interest-rate or foreign-exchange risk.

Important facts

  • WACC is an ex-ante marginal cost of capital, not an average of past financing costs.
  • Interest tax shield value = T_c × Debt, under perpetual debt assumption in MM with tax.
  • Under constant-debt policy, WACC method is appropriate; under constant-LTV (target ratio), APV may be clearer.
  • Multiple IRRs arise when cash-flow signs change more than once.
  • In a buyback, earnings-per-share may rise if the repurchased shares were earning less than the marginal cost of capital.
  • Dividend clientele theory predicts that investors self-select into firms whose payout policies match their tax brackets and income needs.
  • A stock split does not change market capitalization; it only increases the number of shares and proportionally lowers price per share.
  • In merger valuation, synergy must be attributed to the combined entity; overlapping functions usually generate cost synergies, while market expansion generates revenue synergies.
  • Forward points = Spot × (IR home − IR foreign) × Days/360 (approximation); exact formula uses compounding.
  • Money-market hedge for a receivable: borrow foreign currency now, convert at spot, invest domestically; repay receivable later.

Memory aids

  • C-CAPS for capital-structure trade-offs: Cost, Taxes, Agency costs, Pecking-order, Signalling.
  • WACC steps mnemonic: Weights → After-tax Kd → Ke via CAPM/Gordon → Sum weighted costs.
  • Dividend models checklist: Walter, Gordon, M&M with/without personal taxes, Residual policy.
  • APV equation mnemonic: ‘Base plus Shield, minus Fees and Distress’ — Base NPV + PV(Tax Shield) − PV(Flotation) − PV(Distress).
  • Hedge selection matrix: If certainty needed, use forwards/futures; if flexibility, use options; if no market, use money-market hedge.
  • NAL decision rule: Positive NAL ⇒ lease is cheaper after tax; Negative NAL ⇒ buy is cheaper.

Exam tips

  • When computing WACC, explicitly state whether weights are book or market and justify your choice.
  • For APV questions, separate operating NPV and financing side effects clearly; show each PV line item.
  • In dividend-policy problems, calculate both payout ratios and per-share amounts; discuss clientelle and signalling implications, not just formula outputs.
  • In merger/NPV questions, distinguish between stand-alone values, synergy amount and fair exchange ratio; justify sharing based on bargaining position.
  • For international-hedging questions, compare cost of forwards, money-market and options; recommend the lowest-cost instrument that meets the risk objective.
  • When discussing capital-structure theory, reference assumptions (perfect markets, no taxes, no distress) before stating conclusions; examiners reward assumption awareness.
  • In lease-vs-buy, prepare a clear schedule of after-tax cash flows for both alternatives and compute NAL to two decimal places.
  • State any extra assumptions you make—for example, perpetual debt, constant tax rate, or full utilization of depreciation shields—so marks are not lost for hidden jumps.
  • Use professional language: refer to ‘free cash flow to firm’, ‘cost of levered equity’, ‘tax shield’, ‘synergy realization risk’, and ‘exposure management’ appropriately.
  • Where asked for recommendations, link quantitative result to qualitative factor—liquidity, covenants, control, signalling and strategic fit—before concluding.

Common traps

  • Using book-value weights in WACC instead of market-value weights for new-project appraisal.
  • Double-counting financing costs by including interest in project cash flows while also adjusting the discount rate.
  • Computing after-tax cost of equity; equity returns are paid from post-tax profit, so no tax adjustment applies.
  • Confusing cost of retained earnings with cost of new equity; flotation costs raise the cost of new equity.
  • Assuming higher leverage always increases value; ignores rising financial-distress and agency costs.
  • Treating sunk costs as relevant; only incremental future cash flows matter.
  • Ignoring working-capital recovery at project end in NPV calculations.
  • Applying the same discount rate to all divisions regardless of risk; divisional WACCs differ.
  • Believing a positive NPV merger must raise EPS; EPS can fall due to share issuance at a premium.
  • Forgetting that buybacks reduce denominator (shares) and may boost EPS even if absolute profit is unchanged.
