Quick Revision – Financial Reporting (CA Final Group 1)

CAEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Financial Reporting: The process of producing financial statements that disclose an entity's financial performance, financial position, and cash flows in accordance with a specified financial reporting framework (Ind AS / IGAAP).
  • Ind AS (Indian Accounting Standard): Accounting standards issued by ICAI and notified under Companies Act 2013, converged with IFRS, applicable to certain classes of companies in India.
  • Consolidated Financial Statements: Financial statements of a group presented as those of a single economic entity, where the parent controls one or more subsidiaries.
  • Subsidiary: An entity controlled by another entity (the parent). Control exists when the investor is exposed to, or has rights to, variable returns from its involvement with the investee and has the ability to affect those returns through its power over the investee.
  • Associate: An entity over which the investor has significant influence but not control or joint control. Significant influence is the power to participate in financial and operating policy decisions but not control those policies.
  • Joint Venture: A joint arrangement whereby the parties that have joint control of the arrangement have rights to the net assets of the arrangement.
  • Fair Value: The price that would be received to sell an asset or paid to transfer a liability in an orderly transaction between market participants at the measurement date.
  • Investment Property: Land or a building (or part thereof) held to earn rentals or for capital appreciation or both (not for use in production/supply of goods/services or administrative purposes, nor for sale in the ordinary course of business).
  • Impairment Loss: The amount by which the carrying amount of an asset exceeds its recoverable amount.
  • Recoverable Amount: The higher of an asset's fair value less costs of disposal and its value in use.
  • Provision: A liability of uncertain timing or amount — a present obligation arising from past events, where it is probable that an outflow of resources embodying economic benefits will be required, and a reliable estimate can be made.
  • Contingent Liability: A possible obligation whose existence will be confirmed only by the occurrence or non-occurrence of uncertain future events; OR a present obligation that does not meet the recognition threshold because an outflow is not probable or cannot be measured reliably.
  • Intangible Asset: An identifiable non-monetary asset without physical substance, including financial rights.
  • Bio-assets: Living animals or plants. Agricultural activity includes biological transformation and harvest of bio-assets.
  • Operating Lease: A lease that does not transfer substantially all the risks and rewards incidental to ownership of an asset.
  • Finance Lease: A lease that transfers substantially all the risks and rewards incidental to ownership of an asset.
  • Value in Use: The present value of future cash flows expected to be derived from an asset or cash-generating unit.

Important facts

  • Ind AS are issued by ICAI and notified under Section 133 of Companies Act 2013.
  • Effective date for Ind AS for listed companies was April 1, 2016 (Phase I); Phase II for unlisted companies followed.
  • Companies (Indian Accounting Standards) Rules specify applicability based on paid-up capital and turnover thresholds.
  • Ind AS 109 replaces old Ind AS 39 for financial instruments (effective April 1, 2018).
  • Ind AS 115 replaces Ind AS 11 (Construction Contracts) and Ind AS 18 (Revenue Recognition) for contracts entered into on or after April 1, 2019.
  • Ind AS 116 replaces Ind AS 17 (Leases) for lessee accounting from April 1, 2019.
  • Ind AS 110 consolidates guidance previously in multiple standards on consolidation.
  • First-time adopters apply Ind AS 101 (First-time Adoption of Ind AS).
  • NCI may be measured either at fair value (full goodwill) or at proportionate share of net assets (partial goodwill) on a transaction-by-transaction basis.
  • Deferred tax assets on unabsorbed depreciation/carried forward losses require virtual certainty.
  • Dividends declared after balance sheet date but before approval of financial statements are NOT recognised as liability; disclosed as contingent liability.
  • Subsidiary: Parent controls >50% voting power OR has power to appoint majority of board OR has de facto control.
  • In consolidation, 100% of subsidiary's assets/liabilities are taken even if holding <100%. NCI gets share of equity and P&L.
  • Retained earnings of subsidiary are eliminated against investment cost in consolidation.
  • Pre-acquisition profit/loss of subsidiary is eliminated against cost of investment.
  • unrealised profit in inventory/stock-in-trade must be eliminated from consolidated financial statements (parent's share only).
  • Goodwill impairment loss is NEVER reversed.
  • Intangible assets with indefinite useful life are not amortised but tested for impairment annually.
  • Revaluation surplus on PPE under Ind AS 16 is transferred to retained earnings on derecognition of the asset (not through P&L).
  • Contingent consideration classified as liability: Changes in fair value recognised in P&L.
  • Operating segment: Based on internal management reporting structure (management approach).
  • Joint venture accounted using equity method in consolidated financial statements of venturer.
  • In joint operation, venturer recognises its share of assets, liabilities, revenue, expenses.
  • Right-of-use asset for leases includes initial direct costs and estimated restoration costs.
  • Short-term and low-value asset leases: Optional exemption from full lease accounting.
  • Impairment testing of goodwill: Allocate to CGU(s) expected to benefit; test annually regardless of indicators.
  • Variable consideration under Ind AS 115: Constrained — only include to extent it is highly probable that significant reversal will not occur.
  • Tax base of asset = amount deductible for tax purposes in future; tax base of liability = carrying amount less deductible amount.

Memory aids

  • EPS mnemonic: Basic = Profit ÷ WAES; Diluted adds potential shares that REDUCE EPS (are dilutive).
  • Consolidation 4 steps: (1) Identify assets/liabilities at FV, (2) Calculate goodwill, (3) Eliminate pre-acq equity, (4) Allocate NCI share.
  • Revenue 5 steps: Identify contract → Identify PO → Determine TP → Allocate TP → Recognise revenue.
  • Lease liability components: Fixed + Index-linked variable + Purchase option (if certain) + Termination penalty (if reflected in term) + Residual guarantee.
  • Impairment reversal rule: Can reverse for all assets EXCEPT goodwill. Goodwill impairment is irreversible.
  • DTA/DTL: DT = D eductible → A sset; T axable → L iability. Mnemonic: D-T-A (Deductible → Tax Asset), T-T-L (Taxable → Tax Liability).
  • Provision vs Contingent: Provision = Present obligation, probable outflow, reliable estimate. Contingent = Possible obligation OR present obligation where probability <50% OR unreliable estimate.
  • Subsidiary/Associate/JV distinction: Control → Subsidiary (consolidate); Significant influence → Associate (equity); Joint control → JV (equity method for net assets).
  • Borrowing cost capitalisation triggers: (1) Expenditure incurred, (2) Borrowing cost incurred, (3) Activities in progress — ALL three must be met.
  • Fair value hierarchy: Level 1 (quoted prices), Level 2 (observable inputs other than Level 1), Level 3 (unobservable inputs).
  • NCI Full vs Partial Goodwill: Full = NCI at FV; Partial = NCI at proportionate share of net assets. Choice per acquisition.

Exam tips

  • In Consolidation questions: Always draw a clear workpaper showing elimination entries. Mark每一步 clearly. Show NCI calculation separately.
  • For Goodwill/Bargain Purchase: Double-check that all fair value adjustments to subsidiary's net assets are included. Don't forget contingent liabilities assumed.
  • EPS questions: Always separate basic and diluted. Check for bonus elements, rights issues, options/warrants. Apply treasury stock method correctly for options.
  • Deferred Tax: Identify ALL temporary differences including revaluation surplus, unamortised goodwill, provisions, and carry-forward losses. Use correct tax rate.
  • Ind AS 115 Revenue Recognition: Follow 5-step model strictly in answers. Clearly identify performance obligations and satisfaction timing (over time vs point in time).
  • Lease Accounting (Ind AS 116): Calculate lease liability accurately. Distinguish between lease and non-lease components. Remember short-term and low-value exemptions.
  • Impairment Testing: For goodwill, always identify the CGU. Test at CGU level, not individual asset level. Remember reversal rules differ for goodwill vs other assets.
  • Foreign Currency Translation: Keep separate track of FCTR for foreign operations. Distinguish between transaction gains/losses (P&L) and translation differences (OCI).
  • Provisions: Ensure you apply the three criteria of Ind AS 37. For onerous contracts, compare expected cost of fulfilment vs penalty — take the lower.
  • Sectoral Accounting: Know banking sector guidelines (NPA classification, provisioning norms), infrastructure accounting (percentage of completion), and insurance contract basics.
  • Presentation: Use proper headings and sub-headings. Show workings clearly. Cite relevant standard numbers (e.g., 'As per Ind AS 103...') for bonus marks.
  • When in doubt, default to the most conservative treatment permitted under the standard and explain your reasoning.
  • Time management: Consolidation questions carry heavy weightage — allocate sufficient time. Practice numericals thoroughly before exam.
  • Stay updated on any new amendments to Ind AS notified by MCA before your exam date.

Common traps

  • Confusing subsidiary (control, consolidate 100%) with associate (significant influence, equity method) and joint venture (joint control, equity method for net assets).
  • Forgetting to eliminate unrealised profit in inventory from consolidated financial statements (only parent's share, not 100%).
  • Treating contingent consideration as part of consideration at acquisition when it is subsequently varied — liability-classified contingent consideration changes go to P&L, not equity.
  • Recognising deferred tax asset on unabsorbed depreciation without ensuring virtual certainty of future taxable profit.
  • Depreciating lease liability (confusing ROU asset depreciation with interest accrual on lease liability).
  • Forgetting to include variable lease payments linked to index/rate in lease liability calculation.
  • Using post-tax discount rate for value in use calculation (must use pre-tax rate unless can be derived from post-tax).
  • Reclassifying FVOCI equity investments to P&L on sale (gains/losses on equity FVOCI investments are NEVER recycled to P&L).
  • Measuring NCI at full fair value and also recognising full goodwill when partial goodwill method is chosen — inconsistent application.
  • Capitalising borrowing costs on land that does not require substantial period of preparation (land is not a qualifying asset unless development occurs on it).
  • Recognising government grant related to assets as income immediately instead of systematic recognition over asset's useful life.
  • Treating all lease payments as operating expense (new Ind AS 116 requires on-balance sheet recognition for lessees).
  • Forgetting to test indefinite-life intangible assets for impairment annually even without indicators.
  • Applying equity method to joint operations (should recognise direct share of assets/liabilities/revenue/expenses).
  • Ignoring the 75% revenue threshold in segment reporting (reportable segments' external revenue must be at least 75% of total entity revenue).
