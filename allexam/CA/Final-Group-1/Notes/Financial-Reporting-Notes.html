1. Introduction to Ind AS and Regulatory Framework

Ind AS (Indian Accounting Standards) are convergent with IFRS, notified by the Ministry of Corporate Affairs under Section 133 of the Companies Act, 2013. ICAI frames Ind AS based on recommendations of the Accounting Standards Board, with MCA issuing corporate circulars (e.g., CDR-11/2015-16) specifying phased implementation across company classes. For CA Final purposes, you must know the applicability matrix: Schedule III companies (public listed), NBFCs, and certain large public companies adopted Ind AS first; private entities and smaller companies remain on Schedule III-format Indian GAAP unless they volunteer to adopt. The regulatory landscape also includes the Companies Act 2013, SEBI LODR for listed entities, and ICAI's Guidance Notes on specific standards where Ind AS leaves room for interpretation.

Key differences between Ind AS and previous Indian GAAP that repeatedly appear in exams:
• Ind AS uses fair value as a primary measurement basis for many items (financial instruments, investment property, biological assets), whereas Indian GAAP historically used historical cost.
• Ind AS eliminates revaluation reserve movements to P&L on disposal (the 'cascading' restriction no longer applies).
• Impairment of assets now follows a cash-generating-unit (CGU) approach rather than asset-by-asset alone.
• Lease accounting requires capitalisation of operating leases (lessee-side), abolishing the operating/finance lease distinction for lessees.
• Separate presentation of OCI items that may be reclassified subsequently to profit or loss vs. those that cannot.
• Share-based payment accounting (Ind AS 109/102 family) is mandatory even for private companies, whereas earlier it was often ignored.

Examiners frequently test whether a candidate can distinguish 'material misstatement' scenarios: failing to recognise a finance lease, not applying expected credit loss (ECL) models, or measuring investment property incorrectly. Always reference the exact standard number when writing answers—ICAI expects precision like 'Ind AS 109 Financial Instruments' rather than vague references.

2. Conceptual Framework for Financial Reporting

The Conceptual Framework (issued by ICAI, aligned with IFRS Foundation's framework) underpins all Ind AS application. For CA Final, master the following elements:

Objective of General Purpose Financial Reporting: Provide decision-useful information about the reporting entity's financial position, performance, and changes in financial position to present/potential investors, lenders, and other creditors.

Fundamental Qualitative Characteristics:
• Relevance — information capable of making a difference in decisions; includes materiality (information is material if omitting/misstating it could influence decisions).
• Faithful Representation — complete, neutral, and free from error; distinguishes between economic substance and legal form.

Enhancing Qualitative Characteristics: Comparability, verifiability, timeliness, understandability.

Elements of Financial Statements: Assets (present economic resource controlled as a result of past events), Liabilities (present obligation arising from past events), Equity (residual interest), Income (increases in assets/decreases in liabilities not from equity contributions), Expenses (decreases in assets/increases in liabilities not to owners).

Recognition Criteria: Probable inflow/outflow of economic benefits AND reliable measurement.

Measurement Bases: Historical cost, current cost, realisable/settlement value, present value, fair value. Fair value definition (Ind AS 113): price received to sell an asset or paid to transfer a liability in an orderly transaction between market participants at measurement date.

Presentation and Disclosure: Ind AS 1 requires classification of assets/liabilities as current or non-current based on expectations, not solely contractual terms; operating profit is distinct from other income/gains; dis-aggregation of information by nature or function is permitted under certain conditions.

Common exam trap: questioning whether an item meets the recognition threshold due to uncertainty in measurement—even if measurement is uncertain, recognition is required if probability and measurability criteria are met; uncertainty affects disclosure, not recognition.

3. Revenue from Contracts with Customers (Ind AS 115)

Ind AS 115 Revenue from Contracts with Customers applies a five-step model and replaces earlier separate standards on revenue (Ind AS 11, Ind AS 110-related guidance). The five steps are:

Step 1 – Identify the contract with the customer: A contract exists when (a) approved and committed, (b) identifiable rights, (c) identifiable payment terms, (d) commercial substance, (e) probable collection. Consider combined contracts, contract modifications (treated as separate if both new goods distinct + price reflects standalone selling price, otherwise cumulative catch-up or prospective), and termination clauses.

Step 2 – Identify performance obligations: Distinct goods/services within the contract. A good/service is distinct if customer can benefit from it on its own or with readily available resources AND it is separately identifiable in the contract. Bundle distinct obligations; a single performance obligation arises when goods/services are highly interdependent or interrelated (e.g., design + manufacture of customised equipment).

Step 3 – Determine the transaction price: Consider variable consideration (estimates via expected value or most likely amount, constrained to avoid significant reversal), time value of money (financing component if >12 months), non-cash consideration (measured at fair value), and consideration payable to customer (netted against transaction price unless payment is for distinct goods).

Step 4 – Allocate transaction price to performance obligations: Use standalone selling prices (observable price or estimated using adjusted market assessment, expected cost plus margin, or residual approach only when price is highly variable or uncertain per the standard's specific criteria). Prorated when bundle discount exists.

Step 5 – Recognise revenue when (or as) performance obligation is satisfied: Point in time vs. over time. Over-time criteria: (a) customer receives and consumes benefits as entity performs, (b) entity creates/enhances an asset the customer controls as created, (c) entity's performance creates an asset with no alternative use AND entity has enforceable right to payment for performance completed to date. For over-time, use input method (cost-to-cost) or output method consistently.

Specific areas frequently tested:
• Bills-and-hold arrangements: revenue recognised only when control passes (typically physical delivery or formal acceptance).
• Right of return: recognise revenue only for expected returns; refund liability for returns; right-of-return asset for returned goods.
• Warranties: service-type warranty = separate performance obligation; assurance-type warranty = provision under Ind AS 37.
• Principal vs. agent: entity is a principal if it controls the good/service before transferring to customer (assesses risk, discretion in pricing, primary obligation).
• Contract costs: incremental costs of obtaining a contract capitalised as asset if expected to be recovered; costs to fulfil contracted goods/services capitalised only if they generate or enhance resources and are expected to recover.

Worked example pattern: Multi-element arrangement with software licence + implementation + support. Identify three POs. Allocate SSP to each. Recognise licence revenue at point of delivery (if control transfers then), implementation over period, and support ratably.

4. Revenue Recognition and Related Tax — Ind AS 18 context now subsumed; transition matters

Note: Ind AS 18 and Ind AS 11 have been superseded by Ind AS 115. Under the old regime, manufacturing entities used 'significant risks and rewards of ownership' test, service entities used percentage-of-completion for long-term contracts, and construction contracts had specific rules. For the CA Final exam, questions may refer to old scenarios during transitional discussions or comparative analysis. However, the examination focus is firmly on Ind AS 115's five-step model described above.

Where old concepts still matter conceptually:
• Transfer of risks and rewards remains embedded in the 'control' concept under Ind AS 115 but has evolved—the entity assesses whether the customer has obtained control, not merely risks/rewards.
• Construction contracts now follow Ind AS 115 over-time recognition rather than a separate Ind AS 11 methodology.

Tax implications of revenue recognition differences: Deferred tax (Ind AS 12) may arise where revenue is recognised for accounting at a different point than for tax purposes (e.g., instalment sales, long-term contracts). Always cross-reference Ind AS 12—exam often combines Ind AS 115 with Ind AS 12 in numerical problems.

5. Leases — Ind AS 116 Lessee and Lessor Accounting

Ind AS 116 Leases distinguishes lessee and lessor treatments fundamentally differently than the previous Ind AS 17.

Lessee Model (single model):
• At commencement, recognise a Right-of-Use (ROU) asset and a lease liability for ALL leases except: (a) short-term leases (≤12 months), (b) leases of low-value assets (individually new value ~INR 50,000 or less—examples: tablets, personal computers, small office furniture). Both exemptions are optional policy choices applied lease-by-lease.
• Lease liability = present value of lease payments not yet paid, discounted at the interest rate implicit in the lease (if readily determinable) or the lessee's incremental borrowing rate.
• Lease payments include: fixed payments (including in-substance fixed), variable lease payments that depend on an index/rate, exercise price of purchase option if reasonably certain to exercise, penalties for terminating lease if term reflects termination option exercise, and amounts expected payable under residual value guarantees.
• Exclude: variable payments not based on index/rate, execution costs, prepayments.
• ROU asset = lease liability initial amount + prepaid lease payments - lease incentives received + initial direct costs + estimated dismantling/restoration costs. Subsequently measured under cost model (cost less accumulated depreciation and impairment) or revaluation model (same as Ind AS 16).
• Depreciate ROU asset over the shorter of lease term and useful life of underlying asset.
• Interest on lease liability using effective interest method—front-loaded expense profile.
• Remeasurement: changes in lease term, assessment of purchase/termination options, or changes in index/rate-based variable payments trigger remeasurement of lease liability with corresponding ROU asset adjustment.

Lessor Model (retains finance/operating lease dichotomy):
• Finance lease: recognise net investment in lease (lease receivable + unguaranteed residual value), derecognise underlying asset, allocate between gross investment and unearned finance income.
• Operating lease: continue recognising underlying asset, recognise lease income on straight-line basis over lease term unless another systematic basis is more representative.

Sale and Leaseback:
• Assess whether transfer of asset qualifies as a sale under Ind AS 115.
• If sale: seller-lessee recognises only the amount relating to the right of use retained; any gain/loss on sale is adjusted. Seller-lessor accounts under applicable lease standard.
• If not a sale: continue recognising the transferred asset and recognise a financial liability for consideration received.

Common exam scenario: Calculate lease liability at commencement; compute depreciation and finance cost for Year 1 and Year 2; show balance sheet extraction. Also: identify what happens when lease is modified mid-term (new lease term, revised payments).

6. Financial Instruments — Ind AS 109 Classification, Measurement, Impairment and Hedge Accounting

This is the most numerically intensive area in Financial Reporting and demands rigorous understanding.

Classification and Measurement:
• Debt instruments: two assessment criteria—(a) Business Model test (hold to collect contractual cash flows? Hold to collect + sell? Other?), (b) SPPI test (Solely Payments of Principal and Interest)—do contractual cash flows represent principal and interest on principal outstanding?
• Three categories: Amortised Cost (hold to collect SPPI), FVOCI (both hold to collect and sell AND SPPI—equity cannot be FVOCI; debt FVOCI gains/losses cycle through P&L on disposal), FVTPL (residual, or designated at FVTPL to eliminate accounting mismatch).
• Equity instruments: always FVTPL unless irrevocable election at inception to present changes in OCI (no recycling on disposal; dividends to P&L).
• Embedded derivatives: bifurcate if host is not an insurance contract and characteristics not closely related; otherwise embedded derivative is separated and measured at FVTPL.

Impairment — Expected Credit Loss (ECL) Model:
• Three-stage approach for purchased/originated credit-impaired vs non-credit-impaired assets:
– Stage 1: 12-month ECL (changes in RMSI over next 12 months)
– Stage 2: Lifetime ECL (significant increase in credit risk since initial recognition)
– Stage 3: Lifetime ECL + interest on gross carrying amount (credit-impaired)
• SICR indicators: payment defaults (30+ days overdue rebuttable presumption), internal/external credit rating downgrade, significant increase in market price volatility, adverse changes in business/financial/economic conditions.
• Gross up/down method for modification accounting: if terms substantially different, derecognise old instrument, recognise new; calculate modification gain/loss.

Hedge Accounting (Ind AS 109 Section 6):
• Requirements: formal designation and documentation at inception; hedged item identifiable and reliably measurable; hedging instrument designated; economic relationship between hedged item and instrument; credit risk does not dominate; hedge ratio matches actual volumes.
• Three types: Fair Value Hedge (hedging exposure to changes in fair value of recognised asset/liability or unrecognised firm commitment), Cash Flow Hedge (hedging exposure to variability in cash flows of recognised asset/liability or forecast transaction), Hedge of Net Investment in Foreign Operation.
• Cash flow hedge: effective portion to OCI, ineffective to P&L; recycling from OCI to P&L when hedged transaction affects P&L.

Numerical emphasis: classification decision trees, amortised cost calculations using effective interest method, journal entries for each stage, impairment provisioning calculations with given PD/LGD/EAD, hedge effectiveness testing.

7. Consolidated Financial Statements — Ind AS 110, 111, 112, 127 and Related Standards

Ind AS 110 Consolidated Financial Statements:
• Parent must prepare consolidated FS unless exempt: (a) wholly-owned subsidiary of another entity and its other owners have been informed and do not object; (b) not publicly traded; (c) has not filed/did not intend to file FS with SEBC or other regulatory body for issuer purpose; (d) ultimate or intermediate parent produces consolidated FS available for public use and complies with Ind AS.
• Control exists when parent has: power over investee, exposure/rights to variable returns, ability to affect returns through power. Assess control continuously.
• Subsidiary: investee controlled by parent. Include from date control obtained, exclude when control lost.
• Non-controlling interest (NCI): measured either at proportionate share of net identifiable assets OR fair value at acquisition date (policy choice per acquisition).
• Business combination: acquisition method. Identify acquirer. Measure consideration transferred (fair value). Identify/acquire identifiable assets and assume liabilities at fair value. Recognise NCI at chosen basis. Goodwill = consideration + NCI + any prior equity interest fair value − net identifiable assets at fair value. Bargain purchase gain to P&L.

Uniform Accounting Policies and Reporting Dates:
• Group policies uniform; if subsidiary uses different policies, adjust to group policies. If reporting dates differ, subsidiary must provide additional FS data as of group reporting date (adjustments not permitted unless impracticable).

Intra-group transactions and balances: Eliminate in full—unrealised profits on upstream/downstream sales must be eliminated against relevant asset and NCI (proportionate for downstream, full elimination then NCI adjustment). Inter-company dividends eliminated.

Changes in ownership interests without loss of control: Equity transaction—adjust parent's reserve and NCI; no gain/loss in P&L. Difference between consideration received/paid and change in NCI goes to parent's equity.

Loss of control: Derecognise assets/liabilities of subsidiary, NCI, and any related reserves/CVI. Recognise any retained interest at fair value. Gain/loss in P&L = disposal consideration + FV of retained interest − carrying amount of net assets − previous OCI recycled to P&L (where applicable).

Joint Arrangements (Ind AS 111): Joint control = contractually agreed sharing of control. Two types: Joint Operation (party has rights to assets, obligations for liabilities—recognise own share of assets/liabilities/income/expenses); Joint Venture (party has rights to net assets—equity method per Ind AS 28).

Disclosure of Interests in Unconsolidated Structured Entities (Ind AS 112): Requirements to disclose nature of risks, exposures, summary financial info.

Separate Financial Statements (Ind AS 117/127): Investments in subsidiaries/joint ventures/associates may be accounted for at cost, Ind AS 109, or equity method. Disclosure requirements apply.

Exam focus: consolidation workings (NCI, goodwill, reserves adjustment, unrealised profit elimination, inter-company loan reconciliation), journal entries for consolidation, loss of control calculations, NCI at fair value vs proportionate method impact on goodwill.

8. Associates and Joint Ventures — Ind AS 28 and Ind AS 111

Ind AS 28 Investments in Associates and Joint Ventures:
• Associate: investor has significant influence (presumed at 20-50% voting power, but substance over form governs—can exist below 20% with board representation, participation in policy-making, material transactions, interchange of managerial personnel, or technological dependency).
• Significant influence lost → account for remaining interest at fair value through P&L (unless it becomes a joint venture, then switch to Ind AS 111).
• Equity method: investment initially at cost; thereafter adjusted for investor's share of post-acquisition profits/losses and OCI of associate; dividend reduces carrying amount.
• Goodwill on acquisition is included in carrying amount (not separately amortised);
• Impairment: carrying amount ≥ recoverable amount (higher of FV less costs of disposal and value in use). Impairment loss not reversed.
• Investor's share of associate's losses may reduce investment to zero; further losses allocated to other components only if investor has incurred legal/constructive obligation or made payments on behalf of associate.
• Unrealised profits on transactions with associate eliminated against investment (proportionate to investor's interest). Downstream vs upstream—elimination is symmetric under equity method.
• Change in ownership (increase/decrease) in associate: re-measure at fair value if significant influence lost; otherwise adjust carrying amount and recognise gain/loss only on disposed portion if de facto disposal occurs (rare).

Ind AS 111 Joint Arrangements:
Reiterated from Section 7 above—in separate FS of venturer, investment in joint venture may be accounted for under Ind AS 109 or Ind AS 28.

Common exam trap: distinguishing between significant influence (associate, equity method) and joint control (joint venture, equity method or proportionate consolidation under Ind AS 111 for joint operations). Also: associate's OCI goes to investor's OCI (not P&L); upon disposal of associate, accumulated OCI is reclassified to P&L only to the extent realized (some items like FVOCI equity instruments under Ind AS 109 may not recycle—

Important facts

  • Ind AS are issued by ICAI and notified by MCA u/s 133 of Companies Act 2013.
  • Consolidated FS exemption conditions under Ind AS 110: four cumulative criteria.
  • Lease liability discount rate: implicit rate first; incremental borrowing rate otherwise.
  • Short-term lease exemption: ≤12 months; low-value asset exemption: individually ~INR 50,000 or less.
  • Revenue from contract with customer: five-step model under Ind AS 115.
  • Debt instrument classification: Business Model test + SPPI test.
  • ECL model: Stage 1 (12-month ECL), Stage 2 (Lifetime ECL), Stage 3 (Lifetime ECL + interest on gross).
  • NCI measurement choice: proportionate share of net assets OR fair value (per acquisition).
  • Goodwill on acquisition = Consideration + NCI + FV of prior equity interest − Fair value of net identifiable assets.
  • Loss of control: gain/loss in P&L includes recycling of previous OCI where applicable.
  • Joint operation vs joint venture: rights to assets/obligations for liabilities vs rights to net assets.
  • Significant influence presumed at 20-50% but substance governs.
  • Associate losses: equity method; losses may exceed investment if obligation exists.
  • Unrealised profits on intra-group transactions must be eliminated in consolidation.
  • Hedge accounting requires formal documentation at inception and ongoing effectiveness assessment.

Formulas and relationships

Goodwill = Consideration transferred + NCI (at choice basis) + FV of previously held equity interest − Fair value of net identifiable assets of subsidiary
NCI at acquisition = NCI% × Fair value of net identifiable assets of subsidiary (proportionate method) OR NCI% × FV of subsidiary (fair value method)
Lease Liability at commencement = Σ PV of lease payments (discounted at implicit rate or incremental borrowing rate)
ROU Asset = Lease liability + Prepaid lease payments − Lease incentives + Initial direct costs + Estimated restoration costs
Amortised Cost of Debt Instrument = Initial recognition amount ± Accumulated amortisation of difference between initial and maturity amount − Repayments − Impairment (ECL)
Effective Interest Rate (EIR) = Rate that exactly discounts estimated future cash payments/receipts through expected life to gross carrying amount of financial asset/liability
Expected Credit Loss (ECL) = PD × LGD × EAD (for stage-wise computation)
Loss on Loss of Control = Disposal consideration + FV of retained interest − Carrying amount of net assets (incl. goodwill) − NCI at disposal date + OCI recycled to P&L
Share of Post-Acquisition Profits in Equity Method = Investor's % × (Associate's total comprehensive income since acquisition date)

Common mistakes

  • Choosing wrong category for debt instruments—failing the SPPI test because contract contains leverage or equity-linked features—leading to incorrect FVTPL vs amortised cost classification.
  • Applying the old 'risks and rewards' transfer test instead of the 'control' test for revenue recognition under Ind AS 115.
  • Eliminating unrealised profits on intra-group sales at the wrong level—for downstream sales, eliminate full profit against parent's equity; for upstream, eliminate proportionately between parent and NCI.
  • Calculating lease liability using stated interest rate instead of implicit rate or incremental borrowing rate.
  • Measuring NCI at fair value one acquisition and proportionate share the next—must apply consistent policy.
  • Forgetting that debt FVOCI gains/losses recycle to P&L on disposal while equity FVOCI (irrevocable election) does not recycle—this is a frequent confusion.
  • Incorrectly applying ECL: using lifetime ECL for Stage 1 or 12-month ECL for Stage 3.
  • Treating sale and leaseback as a simple financing transaction without first assessing whether transfer qualifies as a sale under Ind AS 115.
  • Overlooking that impairment losses on ROU assets follow Ind AS 36 (not Ind AS 36 cross-reference—ROU assets are within scope of Ind AS 36 as well).
  • Assuming joint venture always uses equity method—in joint operations, venturer recognises its share of assets, liabilities, income, and expenses directly.
