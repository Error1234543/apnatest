1. Security Valuation and Analysis: Equity and Preference Shares

Security analysis under AFM involves two complementary stages: fundamental analysis and portfolio management. For equity shares, the core valuation models are the Dividend Discount Model (DDM), Free Cash Flow to Equity (FCFE) model, and Residual Income model.

Dividend Discount Model (Gordon Growth Model):
Vs = D1 / (ke - g), where Vs = intrinsic value, D1 = expected dividend next year, ke = cost of equity, g = constant growth rate. This model assumes dividends grow at a constant rate forever.

Multi-stage DDM:
When growth is not constant, divide into phases: high-growth phase for years 1 to n, then stable growth phase from year n+1 onwards. Calculate present value of dividends during high-growth period, then compute terminal value using Gordon model at the end of high-growth phase, and discount everything to present.

FCFE Model:
FCFE = Net Income - (Capital Expenditures - Depreciation) - Working Capital Requirements + Net Borrowing. Value of equity = sum of discounted FCFE. This is preferred when companies do not pay regular dividends or have negative earnings.

Residual Income Model:
Value of Equity = Book Value per Share + Present Value of Expected Residual Incomes. Residual Income = Net Income - (Equity Charge = Beginning Book Value × Cost of Equity). Useful when dividend or cash flow data is unreliable.

Price/Earnings (P/E) Ratio Approach:
Trailing P/E = Current Price / Last Twelve Months EPS. Forward P/E = Current Price / Estimated Next Year EPS. Implied P/E = Market Price / Current EPS. Justified P/E = Dividend Payout Ratio × (1 + g) / (ke - g).

Preference Shares Valuation:
Pricing = Dividend / ke (perpetuity model). If redeemable, price = PV of dividends + PV of redemption amount using appropriate discount rate.

Key factors affecting share price: macroeconomic factors (inflation, interest rates, GDP growth), industry factors (competition, regulation, technology), and company-specific factors (earnings growth, dividend policy, leverage, management quality).

2. Portfolio Management - Theory and Practice

Portfolio theory begins with Markowitz Modern Portfolio Theory (MPT), which establishes that rational investors maximize expected utility for a given level of risk. Key concepts:

Expected Return of Portfolio:
E(Rp) = Σ wi × E(Ri), where wi is weight of asset i and E(Ri) is its expected return.

Portfolio Variance (two-asset case):
σ²p = w1²σ1² + w2²σ2² + 2w1w2ρ12σ1σ2, where ρ12 is correlation coefficient between returns of assets 1 and 2.

Diversification Benefit: As correlation decreases below +1, portfolio risk decreases without necessarily reducing expected return. Perfect negative correlation (ρ = -1) eliminates risk entirely for appropriate weights.

Efficient Frontier: Set of portfolios offering maximum expected return for a given level of risk. Portfolios on the frontier are 'efficient.'

Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM):
Expected Return = Rf + β × (Rm - Rf), where Rf = risk-free rate, β = systematic risk measure = Cov(Ri, Rm) / Var(Rm), (Rm - Rf) = market risk premium.

β Interpretation: β = 1 means portfolio moves with market; β > 1 means more volatile than market (aggressive); β < 1 means less volatile (defensive). β is computed using regression of security returns against market returns.

Security Market Line (SML): Plots expected return against β. Securities above SML are undervalued (offering higher return for their risk); below SML are overvalued.

Arbitrage Pricing Theory (APT): Multi-factor model by Ross. Expected Return = Rf + β1×RP1 + β2×RP2 + ... + βn×RPn, where RP1, RP2 are risk premiums for different factors (inflation, interest rate changes, oil prices, etc.). More flexible than CAPM as it does not assume market portfolio is efficient.

Jensen's Alpha: α = Actual Return - [Rf + β×(Rm - Rf)]. Positive alpha indicates outperformance; negative indicates underperformance relative to risk taken.

Treynor Ratio: (Rp - Rf) / βp — measures excess return per unit of systematic risk.

Sharpe Ratio: (Rp - Rf) / σp — measures excess return per unit of total risk.

Sortino Ratio: (Rp - Rf) / σdownside — focuses only on downside deviation, penalizing harmful volatility more than upside volatility.

Optimal Portfolio Selection: Tangency Portfolio is the point where the Capital Market Line (CML) touches the efficient frontier. CML: E(Rp) = Rf + [(E(Rm) - Rf) / σm] × σp. All efficient portfolios lie on CML.

3. Mutual Funds and Alternative Investment Products

Mutual Funds:
Open-ended funds allow purchase/redemption at NAV (Net Asset Value) at any time. NAV = (Total Assets - Total Liabilities) / Number of Units Outstanding.
Closed-ended funds issue fixed number of units through IPO; traded on exchange at market price (may be at premium or discount to NAV).
Interval funds combine features of both — open-ended during specified intervals.

Types by Investment Objective:
Equity funds: growth, value, sectoral/thematic, large-cap/mid-cap/small-cap, multi-cap.
Debt funds: liquid, short-duration, long-duration, corporate bond, G-Sec funds.
Hybrid funds: balanced advantage, equity savings, arbitrage funds.
Exchange Traded Funds (ETFs): trade like stocks, track index/basket, lower expense ratio, transparent holdings.

Evaluation of Mutual Funds:
Expense Ratio = Total Expenses / Average NAV. Lower is better.
Turnover Ratio = Lower turnover implies tax efficiency and lower transaction costs.
Standard Deviation of Returns measures risk. Higher Sharpe ratio is preferable.
Alpha measures manager skill. Positive alpha = skilled fund manager.
Beta indicates market sensitivity.
Tracking Error = Standard deviation of difference between fund returns and benchmark returns. Lower is better for index funds.

Alternative Investment Products:
Alternative investments include Hedge Funds, Private Equity, Venture Capital, Real Estate, Commodities, and REITs/InvITs.

REITs (Real Estate Investment Trusts): Must distribute at least 90% of distributable profits as dividends. Listed on exchange, provide liquidity in real estate. Tax-transparent in many jurisdictions.
InvITs (Infrastructure Investment Trusts): Similar structure to REITs but for infrastructure assets. Must distribute minimum 90% of net distributable cash flows.
Hedge Funds: Use leverage, derivatives, short-selling, arbitrage strategies. Not regulated like mutual funds. Charge both management fee (~2%) and performance fee (~20% of profits, 'high-water mark' prevents double charging).
Private Equity: Invest in private companies or take public companies private. Stages: Seed, Startup, Expansion, Mezzanine, Bridge/Replacement capital. Exit via IPO, strategic sale, or buyback.

4. Risk Management - Value at Risk (VaR) and Expected Shortfall

Value at Risk (VaR): Maximum potential loss over a given time horizon at a specified confidence level.

Three approaches to compute VaR:

1. Historical Simulation Method:
Arrange historical returns in ascending order. VaR at 95% confidence = 5th percentile return. No assumption about distribution. Simple but requires long history.

2. Variance-Covariance (Parametric) Method:
VaR = μ - z × σ (for mean-return case)
or VaR = z × σ (assuming zero mean)
where z = Z-score for chosen confidence level (1.645 for 95%, 2.33 for 99%), σ = standard deviation of returns.
Assumes normal distribution of returns. Computationally simple.

3. Monte Carlo Simulation Method:
Generate thousands of random scenarios using statistical distributions. Compute portfolio value under each scenario. VaR = loss at chosen percentile. Most flexible but computationally intensive.

Limitations of VaR:
Not sub-additive (does not satisfy diversification principle universally). Does not measure magnitude of loss beyond VaR threshold. Sensitive to confidence level and time horizon chosen. Assumes historical patterns continue.

Expected Shortfall (ES) / Conditional VaR:
ES = Average of losses exceeding VaR. Addresses VaR's limitation by measuring tail risk. ES at 95% = average of worst 5% outcomes. Now preferred by regulators (Basel III uses ES instead of VaR for market risk capital).

Operational Risk: Risk of loss from inadequate processes, people, systems, or external events. Measured by Basic Indicator Approach (15% of average gross income over 3 years), Standardized Approach (8% of gross income for different business lines), or Advanced Measurement Approaches.

Credit Risk Metrics:
Probability of Default (PD), Loss Given Default (LGD), Exposure at Default (EAD). Credit VaR measures unexpected loss due to credit migrations.

5. Interest Rate Derivatives and Swaps

Interest Rate Swaps:
The most common type is Plain Vanilla Interest Rate Swap — one party pays fixed rate, receives floating rate (or vice versa). No principal exchanged; only net interest payments.

Example: Firm A has variable-rate loan, Firm B has fixed-rate loan. A pays fixed to B; B pays floating to A. Both achieve desired exposure at lower borrowing cost.

Cross Currency Swap: Exchange principal and interest payments in different currencies. Used when entities want funding in foreign currency.

Basis Swap: Exchange floating rates based on different reference benchmarks (e.g., LIBOR vs MIBOR). Used to manage basis risk.

Swap Valuation:
Fixed rate swap value = PV(fixed leg) - PV(floating leg). Floating leg value ≈ notional at reset dates. Fixed rate is set so initial swap value = 0.

Forward Rate Agreement (FRA): OTC contract to exchange interest payment at future date based on reference rate. Settled in cash at beginning of period. FRA rate is locked forward rate.

FRA Settlement = Notional × (Reference Rate - FRA Rate) × Days/360 ÷ (1 + Reference Rate × Days/360)

Interest Rate Futures:
Bond futures, Treasury bill futures, Treasury note futures. Traded on exchanges. Used for hedging interest rate exposure.

Duration-Based Hedging with Futures:
Number of contracts = (Portfolio Duration / Futures Duration) × (Portfolio Value / Futures Contract Value). Adjust for basis risk and hedge effectiveness.

Modified Duration Approach: ΔP ≈ -MD × Δy × P, where MD = modified duration, Δy = change in yield.

Convexity Adjustment: ΔP ≈ -MD × Δy × P + ½ × Convexity × (Δy)² × P. Positive convexity is beneficial; bond holders prefer positive convexity.

Swap Equivalent Rate: For comparing swaps to direct borrowing/lending, compute the break-even swap rate where total cost with swap equals best available alternative.

6. Foreign Exchange Derivatives and Risk Management

Foreign Exchange Exposure Types:
Transaction Exposure: Impact of FX rate changes on outstanding contractual obligations denominated in foreign currency. Arises from receivables/payables in foreign currency.
Translation Exposure: Impact on consolidated financial statements when foreign subsidiaries' financial statements are converted to reporting currency.
Economic Exposure: Long-term impact of unexpected FX movements on competitive position and future cash flows.

Hedging Instruments:
Forward Contracts: OTC agreement to buy/sell currency at predetermined rate on future date. Eliminates uncertainty but locks in rate. Counterparty risk exists.
Spot Contracts: Immediate delivery (T+2 settlement). Used for immediate needs only.
Currency Futures: Exchange-traded standardized forward contracts. Marked-to-market daily. Liquidity and transparency advantages.
Currency Options: Right (not obligation) to buy (call) or sell (put) currency at strike price before/on expiration. Premium paid. Provides insurance with upside participation.
Currency Swaps: Exchange principal and interest in different currencies over period. Used for long-term hedging.
Money Market Hedge: Borrow in foreign currency, convert to domestic, invest. Repay foreign loan from proceeds. Creates synthetic forward.

Option Greeks:
Delta (Δ): Rate of change of option price with respect to underlying price. Call delta ranges 0 to 1; put delta ranges -1 to 0. At-the-money call delta ≈ 0.5.
Gamma (Γ): Rate of change of delta. Measures convexity of option pricing.
Theta (Θ): Time decay of option value. Negative for long options.
Vega (ν): Sensitivity to volatility changes. Higher volatility increases option premium.
Rho (ρ): Sensitivity to interest rate changes.

Put-Call Parity (European options):
C + Ke^(-rt) = P + Se^(-rt)
Where C = call price, P = put price, K = strike, S = spot, r = risk-free rate, t = time.

For hedging receivable in foreign currency: Sell forward OR buy put option. For hedging payable: Buy forward OR buy call option.

Money Market Hedge for Payable: Convert domestic to foreign at spot, invest foreign amount at foreign rate, use investment proceeds to settle payable. Equivalent to buying forward.

7. Derivatives: Futures and Options - Mechanics and Strategies

Futures Contract:
Standardized contract traded on exchange to buy/sell asset at predetermined price on future date. Margin required: Initial margin + Maintenance margin. Marked-to-market daily — gains/losses settled in cash.

Long Futures Profit/Loss: (Settlement Price - Purchase Price) × Contract Size × Number of Contracts
Short Futures Profit/Loss: (Sale Price - Settlement Price) × Contract Size × Number of Contracts

Hedge Ratio with Futures:
Number of contracts = (β × Portfolio Value) / (Futures Price × Contract Multiplier)
Minimum Variance Hedge Ratio: h* = ρ × (σS / σF), where ρ = correlation between spot and futures returns.

Option Types:
Call Option: Right to BUY underlying at strike price. Intrinsic Value = Max(S - K, 0). Time Value = Premium - Intrinsic Value.
Put Option: Right to SELL underlying at strike price. Intrinsic Value = Max(K - S, 0).

Option Valuation (Binomial Model):
Build up/down lattice of possible asset prices. Work backward computing option values at each node. Risk-neutral probability p = (e^(rT) - d) / (u - d), where u = up factor, d = down factor.

Black-Scholes Model (European options on non-dividend paying stock):
C = S×N(d1) - K×e^(-rT)×N(d2)
P = K×e^(-rT)×N(-d2) - S×N(-d1)
where d1 = [ln(S/K) + (r + σ²/2)×T] / (σ√T)
d2 = d1 - σ√T
N() = cumulative standard normal distribution function.

Strategies:
Spread: Buying and selling options/futures with different strikes/expiries to reduce cost/risk.
Butterfly Spread (calls): Buy 1 call at low strike, sell 2 calls at middle strike, buy 1 call at high strike. Profits if underlying stays near middle strike.
Straddle: Buy call + buy put at same strike and expiry. Profits from large move in either direction.
Strangle: Buy out-of-money call + buy out-of-money put. Cheaper than straddle but needs larger move.
Covered Call: Own stock + sell call. Generates income; limits upside.
Protective Put: Own stock + buy put. Insurance against downside.

8. Merger, Acquisition and Restructuring

Merger vs Acquisition vs Takeover:
Merger = combination of two companies into one new entity (A + B = C). Absorption = one company absorbs another (A + B = A).
Acquisition = one company acquires controlling stake in another (becomes subsidiary).
Takeover = forced acquisition against management wishes.

Merger Accounting (Indian Accounting Standards / IFRS 3):
Purchase Method (Acquisition Method): Acquirer identifies assets/liabilities of acquiree at fair value. Goodwill = Consideration Transferred + NCI + FVC - Fair Value of Net Identifiable Assets. Revaluation surplus of acquiree eliminated. Comparatives not restated.

Pooling of Interest Method: Not permitted under IND AS / IFRS 3 except in specific circumstances (rare in India). Historically used when merger treated as combined operation rather than purchase.

Share Exchange Ratio:
Based on earning per share equivalence, dividend per share equivalence, or market price comparison. Adjusted for synergetic benefits. SER = (Market Price of Target / Market Price of Acquirer) × Adjustment Factor.

Valuation in M&A:
DCF approach: Project free cash flows, discount at WACC. Terminal value using perpetuity formula.
NAV approach: Net Asset Value per share adjusted for revaluation.
Comparable Company Analysis: Apply trading multiples (P/E, EV/EBITDA) from peers.
Precedent Transactions: Apply multiples from recent comparable deals.

Synergy Valuation:
Synergy = Combined Value - (Value of A alone + Value of B alone)
Operating synergy: Cost reduction, revenue enhancement.
Financial synergy: Tax benefits, cheaper financing, risk reduction.
Total Synergy = Operating + Financial Synergy.

Acquirer should pay only up to Combined Value - Standalone Value of Target to avoid wealth destruction.

Restructuring Types:
Internal: Business reorganization, product line changes, operational improvements.
External: M&A, divestiture, spin-off, split-off, equity carve-out.
Financial Restructuring: Debt-equity swap, debt refinancing, share buyback.
Corporate Restructuring: Combination of financial and operational changes.

Buyback of Securities (Companies Act, 2013): Can be via tender offer, open market, or proportionate buyback. Maximum buyback cannot exceed 25% of paid-up capital in a financial year. Section 68 provisions apply. Funds from free reserves or proceeds of fresh shares. Debentures may be used for buyback.

9. Internal Reconstruction and Capital Restructuring

Internal Reconstruction: Reduction of share capital and/or reorganization of liabilities WITHOUT creating a new company. Done when company is suffering from capital erosion or accumulated losses but operations remain viable.

Methods:
Writing off issued share capital (reducing face value or number of shares).
Writing off accumulated losses against share premium or forfeited shares account.
Compromising with creditors (accepting less than owed, converting debt to equity).
Surrender of rights by shareholders (reducing dividend rate, deferring claims).
Transfer of reserves to cover losses.

Accounting Treatment (IND AS): Capital reduction requires court/tribunal approval (NCLT). Surplus after satisfying creditors' claims and writing off losses credited to Capital Reduction Account, then transferred to Reserves.

Capital Restructuring: Changing capital structure (debt-equity mix) without necessarily reducing capital.

Types:
Debt-Equity Swap: Creditors convert debt into equity. Reduces interest burden, improves gearing ratio.
Scheme of Arrangement: Court-sanctioned compromise between company and stakeholders.
Buyback of shares: Reduces equity base, increases EPS, improves ROE.
Bonus Issue: Capitalizes reserves, increases share count without cash outflow.
Right Issue: Raises fresh capital from existing shareholders.

Effect on EPS and Shareholder Wealth:
Post-reconstruction EPS = Revised Net Profit / Revised Equity Shares.
EPS Accretion/Dilution test: If post-restructuring EPS > pre-restructuring EPS, EPS accretive (wealth-enhancing). If lower, dilutive.
However, EPS alone is insufficient metric — must consider total shareholder value including ROCE improvement, interest saving, and risk profile change.

10. Dividend Policy and Share Buyback

Dividend Theories:
Irrelevance Theory (Modigliani-Miller): In perfect markets (no taxes, no transaction costs, symmetric information), dividend policy does not affect firm value or cost of equity. Value is determined by earning power and risk of investment policy.
Relevance Theory (Gordon-Lintner): Investors prefer current dividends to uncertain future capital gains ('bird in hand'). Higher dividend payout leads to higher share price. Retained earnings carry higher risk premium than dividend income.
Walter's Model: P = (D + (E-D)×b/k) / k where P = market price, D = dividend per share, E = EPS, b = retention ratio, k = cost of equity. If ROE > ke, retained earnings add value → low payout optimal. If ROE < ke, high payout optimal. At ROE = ke, payout irrelevant.

Dividend Decision Factors:
Legal: Companies Act restrictions (only out of profits/free reserves), maintenance of capital, minimum dividend payment requirements.
Contractual: Loan covenants restricting dividend payments, debenture terms.
Profitability and Cash Flow: Sustainable dividends require consistent cash generation, not just accounting profits.
Growth Opportunities: High-growth firms retain earnings → low payout.
Tax Considerations: Dividend taxation affects investor preference. Investor clientele effects matter.
Shareholder Composition: Institutional vs retail investor preferences differ.
Signaling Effect: Dividend increases signal management confidence; cuts signal distress.
Stability Preference: Investors prefer stable or growing dividends.

Buyback Advantages over Dividends:
Tax efficiency: Capital gains tax often lower than dividend tax.
Flexibility: Not an ongoing obligation.
EPS enhancement: Reduces share count.
Signal of undervaluation: Management believes shares are cheap.
Alternative return to shareholders when no attractive investment opportunities exist.
Prevents earnings dilution from employee stock options.
Disadvantages: Reduces financial flexibility, may be perceived as lack of growth opportunities, regulatory compliance costs, may benefit wealthy shareholders disproportionately.

11. Project Appraisal and Capital Budgeting

Capital Budgeting Techniques:
Payback Period: Time to recover initial investment from cash inflows. Ignores time value of money (simple payback) or includes it (discounted payback). Rule: Accept if < maximum acceptable period. Limitation: ignores cash flows beyond payback period.
NPV (Net Present Value): NPV = Σ [CFt / (1+k)^t] - Initial Investment. Accept if NPV > 0. Best technique — maximizes shareholder wealth. Considers time value of money and all cash flows.
IRR (Internal Rate of Return): Discount rate at which NPV = 0. Accept if IRR > cost of capital. Limitations: Multiple IRRs possible with non-conventional cash flows; reinvestment rate assumption (IRR assumes interim cash flows reinvested at IRR, unrealistic). Mutually exclusive projects: NPV rule prevails over IRR.
PI (Profitability Index): PI = PV of Future Cash Flows / Initial Investment. Accept if PI > 1. Useful for capital rationing.
ARR (Accounting Rate of Return): ARR = Average Accounting Profit / Average Investment. ignores time value of money. Simple but theoretically flawed.

Project Appraisal under Uncertainty:
Sensitivity Analysis: Change one variable at a time to see impact on NPV. Identify 'key drivers' most sensitive to change. Does not measure probability of outcomes.
Scenario Analysis: Simultaneously change multiple variables under defined scenarios (Best Case, Base Case, Worst Case). More realistic than sensitivity analysis.
Simulation (Monte Carlo): Assign probability distributions to variables, run thousands of simulations, generate probability distribution of NPV. Most sophisticated but complex.
Decision Tree Analysis: Maps sequential decisions and probabilistic outcomes. Compute expected NPV at each decision node, work backward (rollback technique). Useful for phased projects.
Real Options: Recognizes managerial flexibility (expand, contract, abandon, defer, switch). Abandonment option has value; deferral option allows waiting for more information.

Real Options valuations: Monte Carlo simulation combined with option pricing models (binomial lattice, Black-Scholes adapted).

Inflation Adjustment in Project Appraisal:
Consistency principle: Nominal cash flows discounted at nominal rate; real cash flows discounted at real rate.
Real rate = (1 + nominal rate) / (1 + inflation rate) - 1
Nominal cash flows = Real cash flows × (1 + inflation rate)^t

12. Working Capital Management and Cash Conversion Cycle

Working Capital Components:
Current Assets: Cash & equivalents, Trade receivables (Debtors), Inventory (raw materials, WIP, finished goods), Short-term investments.
Current Liabilities: Trade payables (Creditors), Bank overdraft, Short-term borrowings, Accrued expenses.

Operating Cycle = Days Inventory Outstanding (DIO) + Days Sales Outstanding (DSO)
Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC) = DIO + DSO - Days Payable Outstanding (DPO)
Lower CCC = more efficient working capital management.

Inventory Management Models:
EOQ (Economic Order Quantity): Q* = √(2×D×S/H), where D = annual demand, S = ordering cost per order, H = holding cost per unit per year. Minimizes total inventory cost (ordering + holding).
Reorder Level = Maximum Usage × Maximum Lead Time.
Safety Stock = (Maximum usage - Average usage) × Lead time.
Just-in-Time (JIT): Minimal inventory, supplies arrive exactly when needed. Reduces holding costs but increases stockout risk.
ABC Analysis: Classify inventory by value — A items (high value, tight control), B items (moderate), C items (low value, simple control).

Receivables Management:
Credit Policy Components: Credit standards (who gets credit), Credit terms (payment period, discounts for early payment), Collection policy (how aggressively to collect).
Collection Float = Mail Float + Processing Float + Clearing Float. Reduce float through lockbox systems, electronic payments, wire transfers.
Factorng vs Overdraft: Factoring converts receivables to cash immediately (with fee). Better when collection expertise needed or balance sheet strengthening required.

Payables Management:
Trade credit cost if discount foregone: Cost = (Discount % / (100 - Discount %)) × (365 / (Full period - Discount period)). If this cost exceeds bank loan rate, take discount.
Optimal Payment Timing: Pay at last possible moment without damaging credit rating. Early payment only if discount offered and cost-effective.

13. International Finance - GDRs, ADRs, FCCBs and External Commercial Borrowings

American Depository Receipts (ADRs):
Certificate representing shares of non-US company, traded on US exchanges. Each ADR represents 1 or more shares. Levels: Level I (OTC, minimal disclosure), Level II (exchange-listed, SEC registration required), Level III (public offering in US, full SEC registration), Depositary Receipts (private placement under Regulation S).
Advantages for issuer: Access to deep US capital markets, enhanced visibility, currency diversification, ability to attract US institutional investors.

Global Depository Receipts (GDRs):
Similar to ADRs but traded on exchanges outside issuing country's home market (commonly London, Luxembourg, Ireland). Governed by ICDR Regulations in India. Issued in US dollars usually.
Advantages: Broader investor base, pricing in dollars reduces FX risk for investor, access to international capital at potentially favorable terms.

Foreign Currency Convertible Bonds (FCCBs):
Bonds denominated in foreign currency, convertible into equity (usually equity shares or ADRs/GDRs) at predefined conversion price. Hybrid instrument combining debt and equity features.
Features: Coupon rate typically lower than domestic bonds. Conversion premium reflects equity upside. Currency risk borne by issuer until conversion.
ICDR Regulations govern FCCB issuance in India. Eligible issuers: listed companies, unlisted companies with net tangible assets of at least ₹500 crore. Maximum open endowment: ₹5,000 crore per year.
External Commercial Borrowings (ECBs):
Borrowings by Indian entities from non-resident lenders in foreign currency. Regulated by RBI under ECB Policy. Minimum average maturity: 3 years (with some exceptions). Floor rate applies (LIBOR/SOFR + spread). Eligible end-uses defined.
Sectoral caps: IT/Software, Pharmaceuticals, Medical Devices, Oil & Gas Exploration, Construction — no sectoral cap. Others have limits based on borrower category (Category I: 75% of annual average outstanding; Category II: 50%).
Use of proceeds: Cannot be used for real estate, stock trading. Must comply with eligibility criteria. Automatic route for most ECBs below threshold amounts.

14. Strategic Financial Management - Comprehensive Framework

Strategic Financial Management integrates all financial decisions — investment, financing, dividend, and risk management — with corporate strategy. It operates at the intersection of finance, strategy, and value creation.

Strategic Financial Management Process:
Strategic Analysis: Understand industry structure (Porter's Five Forces), competitive position, internal capabilities (VRIO framework), macro environment (PESTLE).
Strategic Choice: Evaluate strategic alternatives — growth (market penetration, development, diversification), stability, retrenchment, combination. Use tools like SWOT, BCG Matrix, GE Matrix.
Strategic Implementation: Allocate resources, design organizational structure, establish KPIs, build capabilities, manage change.
Strategic Control: Monitor performance, compare with targets, corrective action.

Cost of Capital as Strategic Tool:
WACC = (E/V)×Re + (D/V)×Rd×(1-t), where E = equity value, D = debt value, V = E+D, t = tax rate.
WACC serves as hurdle rate for investment decisions. Projects with NPV > 0 at WACC create value.
Segment-wise WACC: Different business segments may have different risk profiles requiring differentiated discount rates.

Value-Based Management:
EVA (Economic Value Added) = NOPAT - (Capital Employed × WACC). Positive EVA = value creation. Measures true economic profit after deducting cost of all capital.
MVA (Market Value Added) = Market Value of Firm - Book Value of Invested Capital. Reflects cumulative value created.
Cash Flow Return on Investment (CFROI) = Operating Cash Flow / Gross Investment. Compares to weighted average cost of capital.

Risk-Adjusted Performance Measures for Divisional/Segment Evaluation:
Risk-adjusted ROI, Residual Income at segment level, Economic Profit by segment. Allow for transfer pricing adjustments, inter-segment charges, and allocated overheads.

Important facts

  • CAPM formula: E(Ri) = Rf + βi(Rm - Rf) — beta measures systematic risk only, not total risk
  • Security analysis requires both Fundamental Analysis (macro → industry → company) and Technical Analysis (price trends, volume)
  • Jensen's Alpha = Actual Return - [Rf + β×(Market Return - Rf)]; positive alpha indicates outperformance
  • VaR limitations: not sub-additive, ignores tail risk beyond confidence level — Expected Shortfall addresses this
  • Interest rate swap valuation: fixed leg PV equals floating leg PV at initiation (swap value = 0)
  • Put-Call Parity for European options: C + Ke^(-rt) = P + Se^(-rt)
  • Black-Scholes inputs: Spot price, strike price, time to expiry, risk-free rate, volatility — no dividend assumption for basic model
  • M&A: Purchase method required under IND AS / IFRS 3; goodwill = consideration + NCI + FVC - FV of net assets acquired
  • Synergy = Combined standalone value - Sum of individual values; acquirer should pay ≤ combined value - target standalone value
  • Buyback limit under Companies Act 2013: max 25% of paid-up capital in a financial year; funded from free reserves or fresh issue proceeds
  • Cash Conversion Cycle = DIO + DSO - DPO; lower is more efficient
  • EOQ formula: √(2DS/H) where D = demand, S = ordering cost, H = holding cost per unit
  • WACC = (E/V)×Re + (D/V)×Rd×(1-t); tax shield on debt makes WACC lower than cost of equity
  • EVA = NOPAT - (Capital Employed × WACC); positive EVA = true value creation
  • FCCBs are hybrid instruments convertible into equity; governed by ICDR Regulations, limit ₹5,000 crore/year
  • ADRs come in 3 levels — Level I (OTC), Level II (exchange-listed), Level III (public offering in US)
  • ECB minimum average maturity = 3 years; floor rate = reference rate (LIBOR/SOFR) + spread

VaR (Parametric): VaR = z × σ (or μ - zσ for absolute)
Swap Equivalent Rate = (Loan rate at comparative advantage) - (Rate at which advantage is shared)
Hedge Ratio: n = (β × VP) / (PF × CF)
Modified Duration: MD = Macaulay Duration / (1 + y/k)
Convexity Adjustment: ΔP/P ≈ -MD×Δy + ½×Convexity×(Δy)²
Option Greeks: Delta = ∂V/∂S, Gamma = ∂²V/∂S², Theta = ∂V/∂t, Vega = ∂V/∂σ, Rho = ∂V/∂r
Binomial Option Pricing: p = (e^(rT)-d)/(u-d)
Black-Scholes Call: C = SN(d1) - Ke^(-rT)N(d2); d1 = [ln(S/K)+(r+σ²/2)T]/(σ√T); d2 = d1 - σ√T
Black-Scholes Put: P = Ke^(-rT)N(-d2) - SN(-d1)
Put-Call Parity: C + Ke^(-rt) = P + Se^(-rt)
Goodwill (M&A): Consideration + NCI + Fair Value of Contingent Consideration - Fair Value of Net Identifiable Assets
EPS Accretion/Dilution: Post-restructuring EPS = Revised Net Profit / Revised Shares; compare with pre-restructuring EPS
WACC: WACC = (E/V)Re + (D/V)Rd(1-t)
Real rate: (1+r_nominal)/(1+i) - 1
NPV: Σ[CFt/(1+k)^t] - Initial Investment
PI: PV of Future Cash Flows / Initial Investment
Payback Period: Years before full recovery + Unrecovered cost at start of year / Cash flow during year
Operating Cycle = DIO + DSO; Cash Conversion Cycle = DIO + DSO - DPO
DIO = Average Inventory / COGS × 365; DSO = Average Receivables / Revenue × 365; DPO = Average Payables / COGS × 365
EOQ: √(2DS/H); Reorder Level = Max usage × Max lead time; Safety Stock = (Max usage - Avg usage) × Lead time
Cost of trade credit: (Discount %/(100-Discount %)) × (365/(Full period - Discount period))
EVA: NOPAT - (Capital Employed × WACC); MVA = Market Value - Book Value of Invested Capital

Common mistakes

  • Using CAPM beta for total risk assessment — CAPM beta measures ONLY systematic (non-diversifiable) risk; total risk = β×σm is wrong for unsystematic risk evaluation
  • Confusing Sharpe Ratio (total risk) with Treynor Ratio (systematic risk) — Sharpe uses σp, Treynor uses βp; use correct ratio for the risk measure being evaluated
  • Applying simple payback period for mutually exclusive projects — ignores time value of money and cash flows beyond cutoff; always prefer NPV for project selection
  • Assuming IRR reinvestment at IRR itself — in reality interim cash flows reinvest at cost of capital; when IRR and NPV conflict, NPV rule prevails
  • Forgetting tax shield in WACC calculation — Rd must be multiplied by (1-t) for after-tax cost of debt; debt is cheaper than equity due to tax deductibility
  • Using book values instead of market values in WACC weights — WACC uses market-value weights (E/V and D/V), not book-value weights
  • Valuing options using Black-Scholes for American-style options — BS formula is for European options only; American options require binomial or other numerical methods
  • Ignoring transaction costs and bid-ask spreads in mutual fund evaluation — expense ratio matters but trading costs within fund also erode returns
  • Computing hedge ratio without considering correlation — basis risk (correlation < 1) affects optimal hedge ratio; h* = ρ×σS/σF accounts for this
  • Calculating Expected Shortfall using only VaR output — ES requires averaging all losses beyond VaR threshold; VaR alone gives only a single cutoff point
  • Applying Put-Call Parity to American options — parity holds strictly for European options; early exercise possibility breaks the relationship for Americans
  • Forgetting currency mismatch in ECB usage — ECB proceeds in foreign currency must be used only for permissible end-uses; misutilization attracts RBI penalties
  • Treating goodwill impairment as reversal — under IND AS, goodwill impairment cannot be reversed in subsequent periods
  • Confusing internal reconstruction with external reconstruction — internal keeps same legal entity; external creates new company for scheme implementation
  • Using nominal WACC to discount real cash flows (or vice versa) — always maintain consistency: nominal cash flows with nominal rate, real cash flows with real rate
  • Assuming dividend policy irrelevance in presence of taxes — MM irrelevance assumes no taxes; with dividend tax > capital gains tax, relevance theory applies
