Section 1: Share Capital — Types, Issuance, Allotment and Forfeiture

Share capital represents the portion of a company's capital contributed by shareholders. Under the Companies Act, 2013, share capital is classified into Authorized Capital, Issued Capital, Subscribed Capital, and Paid-up Capital.

Authorized Capital (Nominal Capital): The maximum amount of share capital that a company is authorized to issue as stated in its Memorandum of Association. No stamp duty is payable on unauthorized increases beyond the initial authorization, but additional stamp duty applies when authorized capital is increased.

Issued Capital: That part of authorized capital which is actually offered to the public for subscription.

Subscribed Capital: The portion of issued capital that subscribers have actually taken.

Paid-up Capital: The amount of subscribed capital actually paid or credited as paid by shareholders. When shares are issued at a premium, the premium is transferred to Securities Premium Reserve.

Types of Shares:
- Equity Shares: Carry voting rights and participate in profits after preference shareholders are paid. Dividends are not fixed.
- Preference Shares: Carry preferential rights regarding payment of dividend and repayment of capital. Types include:
* Cumulative vs Non-cumulative: Cumulative preference shareholders can claim unpaid dividends accumulated over previous years. Non-cumulative entitlement lapses if not declared in a particular year.
* Participating vs Non-participating: Participating preference shareholders share in surplus profits after equitable division among equity shareholders.
* Convertible vs Non-convertible: Convertible preference shares can be converted into equity shares as per terms.
* Redeemable vs Irredeemable: Redeemable preference shares are redeemable during the lifetime of the company as per terms.

Provisions for Issue of Preference Shares: Under Section 47 of the Companies Act, 2013, preference shares may be issued as irredeemable or redeemable. Redeemable preference shares can only be redeemed out of (a) fresh issues made for the purposes of redemption, or (b) the proceeds of a special issue, or (c) the profits which would otherwise have been available for dividend, or (d) the proceeds of a fresh issue of shares made for the purpose of redemption. If redeemed out of profits, a Capital Redemption Reserve Account (CPR) must be created equal to the amount redeemed. The CPR can be utilized only for fully paid bonus shares.

Allotment of Shares: Upon application, money received is first recorded in Application and Allotment Account. On allotment, excess application money (if any) is adjusted toward allotment money due. If there is a deficiency, Calls-in-Arrears arise. Shares allotted must be within two months from the date of closure of subscription list.

Section 2: Forfeiture and Re-issue of Shares — Detailed Treatment

Forfeiture occurs when a shareholder fails to pay calls made on shares despite notice. The company forfeits the shares and all shares forfeited become the property of the company.

Procedure for Forfeiture:
1. Board makes a resolution declaring forfeiture after service of notice and non-payment.
2. All calls unpaid, including interest at reasonable rate, become immediately due and payable.
3. The forfeited shares are cancelled.

Journal Entries for Forfeiture:
- Share Allotment/Call Account Dr. (call in arrear)
- Share Premium Account Dr. (premium not yet received)
To Share Capital Account (called-up capital on forfeited shares)
- Share Forfeiture Account Dr. (amount already received on forfeited shares)
To Share Capital Account

Treatment of Securities Premium on Forfeited Shares: Securities Premium already received on forfeited shares remains with the company and forms part of Share Forfeiture Account. It can be used for writing off preliminary expenses, share discount, or issued as fully paid bonus shares.

Re-issue of Forfeited Shares: When forfeited shares are re-issued, the loss on re-issue (if sold below original cost to company) is debited to Share Forfeiture Account. If re-issued at a profit (above cost to company), the profit is added to Share Forfeiture Account. The maximum amount transferable from Share Forfeiture Account to write off loss on re-issue is limited to the amount originally received on those shares plus securities premium received thereon.

Treatment when Re-issued as Fully Paid: If shares forfeited at a call of Rs. 5 per share and re-issued at Rs. 8 per share as fully paid, the receipt on re-issue is Rs. 8. Loss if any is adjusted against Share Forfeiture Account. The original subscriber has no claim over re-issued shares unless company articles provide otherwise.

SAST (Street Advisory Service Team) Note: In CA Intermediate, questions on forfeiture and re-issue often combine with issue of bonus shares from surrendered shares — the surrender amount is credited to Share Forfeiture Account and can be used for various purposes as per company law provisions.

Section 3: Redemption of Debentures — Methods and Accounting

Debentures are instruments that evidence debt owed by a company. They may be secured or unsecured, redeemable or irredeemable, convertible or non-convertible.

Methods of Redemption:

(a) Redemption at Par: Debentures are redeemed at their face value. Two approaches exist:
i. By payment in lump sum: A single lump sum is paid to debenture holders.
ii. By installment: Small amounts are set aside periodically.

(b) Redemption at Premium: If debentures are redeemable at a premium, the premium amount must be provided for before redemption. A Debenture Premium Account (if already created) is written off, and any shortfall is debited to Profit and Loss Account or General Reserve.

(c) Redemption by Purchase in Open Market: The company purchases its own debentures from the stock exchange at market price (which may be above or below par). If purchased above par, the premium is debited to Profit and Loss Account (not to Securities Premium). The difference between purchase price and carrying amount is a capital profit/loss.

(d) Conversion into Equity/Preference Shares: Existing debenture holders convert their debentures into equity or preference shares as per agreed terms.

(e) Redemption out of Profits — Capital Redemption Reserve (CPR):
When redeemable preference shares or debentures are redeemed out of profits (rather than fresh issue proceeds), a CPR must be created equal to the amount redeemed. This is a statutory requirement under Sections 53 and 55 of the Companies Act, 2013. CPR can only be utilized for issuance of fully paid bonus shares. It cannot be used for writing off losses or expenses.

Accounting Treatment:
When debentures are redeemed by payment in lump sum:
- 9% Debentures Account Dr.
- Debenture Premium Account Dr. (if any)
To Debentureholders Account
- Debentureholders Account Dr.
To Bank Account

When redemption is by purchase in open market:
- Purchased Debentures Account Dr.
To Bank Account
- 9% Debentures Account Dr.
To Purchased Debentures Account
- Profit/Loss on Redemption Dr./Cr.
To Capital Profit/Capital Loss Account

Note: Profit on redemption is a capital gain and credited to Capital Profit and Loss Account (not P&L). It can only be used for bonus shares or writing off capital losses.

Section 4: Issue of Shares for Consideration Other Than Cash

A company may issue shares in lieu of cash for acquisition of assets, settlement of liabilities, or payment of commission/underwriting expenses.

Issue of Shares for Acquisition of Assets or Settlement of Liabilities:
The journal entry is:
- Purchases/Sundry Creditors/Liability Account Dr.
To Share Capital Account
To Security Premium Account (if shares issued at premium)

If shares are issued at a discount, the discount is written off against the asset/liability value or debited to Profit and Loss Account as per provisions.

Issue of Shares towards Commission:
Underwriting Commission: Can be paid in cash, shares, or debentures, or any combination. It is treated as an expense.
Brokerage: Can be paid only in cash, not in shares or debentures. brokerage on shares already issued can also be paid by issuing shares.

Issue of Shares towards Deferred Sales Tax Liability:
Deferred tax liability may be settled by issuing shares, recorded at fair value of shares issued.

Issue of partly-paid shares for consideration other than cash: The consideration is the full value of the share (including amount called up but not yet paid). Any unpaid amount becomes a liability of the transferee.

Treatment when shares are issued at a discount: Discount allowed must be disclosed in financial statements. The discount is shown as a deduction from share capital in the balance sheet.

Issue of shares for services: When shares are issued to directors or promoters for services rendered, the value of services should be fairly determined and recorded. This is common in the case of professional advice, technical know-how, etc.

Section 5: Dissolution of Partnership Firm — Complete Procedure and Accounting

Dissolution of a partnership firm means a change in the relationship of partners carrying on business in conformity with the terms of the partnership deed. It differs from a change in constitution (admission/retirement/death) where the business continues.

Modes of Dissolution:
- By agreement: All partners agree to dissolve.
- Compulsory: When all or substantially all of the undertaking is sold to a third party, or the firm becomes unlawful.
- On happening of certain events: As per deed provisions.

Accounting for Realization:
Step 1: Open a Realization Account. Transfer all assets (except cash/bank) and all liabilities (except partners' loans) to Realization Account.
Assets Account Dr. (to Realization)
Liabilities Account Cr. (from Realization)

Step 2: Record realization transactions — sale of assets and payment of liabilities.
Bank Account Dr.
To Realization Account (sale of assets)
Realization Account Dr.
To Bank Account (payment of liabilities)

Step 3: If assets are taken over by partners at agreed values:
Partner's Current/Capital Account Dr.
To Realization Account

Step 4: If liabilities are taken over by partners:
Realization Account Dr.
To Partner's Current/Capital Account

Step 5: Close the Realization Account by transferring profit/loss to partners' capital accounts in their gaining ratio (same as old profit-sharing ratio unless otherwise agreed).

Step 6: Adjust cash/bank account. All receipts and payments through bank. Any deficit in realization is borne by solvent partners in their losing ratio. Insolvent partner's deficiency is adjusted among solvent partners in their gaining ratio.

Important Points:
- Unrealized bills are recorded at nil; if later realized, credited to Realization Account.
- Bills payable accepted during the course of business are treated as liabilities.
- Contingent liabilities: if later claimed, paid, and then recovered from partners — recorded through Realization Account.
- Partners' drawings and capital adjustments pass through Current/Capital Accounts only.

Section 6: Admission, Retirement and Death of a Partner — Goodwill Treatment and Adjustments

Admission of a Partner:
The new partner brings in capital and goodwill. Goodwill is dealt with in two ways:
Method 1: Goodwill remains in the books — New Partner Dr. (Goodwill)
To Old Partners in their sacrificing ratio

Method 2: Goodwill is not shown in books — If old partners want goodwill adjusted without appearing in books:
New Partner's Capital Account Dr.
To Old Partners in sacrificing ratio
Then close old partners' capital accounts and open new partner's capital account with adjusted balances. If new partner does not bring goodwill in cash, it is adjusted through their capital/current account.

Calculation of Gaining Ratio: Gaining Ratio = New Ratio - Old Ratio. When a new partner is admitted, old partners sacrifice a portion of their share in favor of the new partner.

Revaluation of Assets and Liabilities: On admission, assets and liabilities are revalued. Gain or loss on revaluation is transferred to old partners' capital accounts in their old profit-sharing ratio. This is done through a Revaluation Account.

Adjustment of Fixed/Growing Goodwill: If goodwill is valued at a specific figure and needs to be shown in books:
- Rising goodwill: Debit Goodwill Account, credit old partners in old ratio.
- Fixed goodwill: Existing goodwill is retained; new goodwill adjustment goes through partners' capital accounts only.
- Growing goodwill: Goodwill increases each year; the increment is distributed to old partners.

Retirement of a Partner:
- The retiring partner's share of goodwill is adjusted. Remaining partners acquire the retiring partner's share in the gaining ratio.
- Assets and liabilities are revalued. Profit/loss transferred in old ratio.
- Retiring partner's capital account is settled. Amount may be paid in lump sum or installments.
- Interest on drawn-out installment may be charged.
- Remaining partners reconstitute the firm and start afresh.

Death of a Partner:
- Executor(s) of deceased partner's estate get the deceased's share.
- Profit/loss up to date of death is calculated using time basis or sales basis.
- Revaluation Account is prepared for assets/liabilities.
- Goodwill is adjusted. Accumulated reserves are distributed in old ratio.
- Deceased partner's capital account is credited with their share.
- Executor receives: Capital balance + Share of reserves + Share of profit + Share of goodwill + Interest on capital + Any amount due.

Section 7: Change in Profit Sharing Ratio and Guarantee of Profits

Change in Profit Sharing Ratio (Without Admission/Retirement):
When existing partners rearrange their profit-sharing ratio among themselves, the gaining partner compensates the sacrificing partner for goodwill.
Journal Entry:
Gaining Partner's Capital Account Dr.
To Sacrificing Partner's Capital Account
(This is done at the book value of goodwill if goodwill is already in the books, or at the revalued amount if being brought in.)

If goodwill is not in the books but needs to be adjusted:
New Ratio is worked out, sacrificing/gaining ratio determined, and the adjustment made through capital accounts only without creating a Goodwill Account in the books.

Calculation of Gaining/Sacrificing Ratio:
Sacrificing Ratio = Old Ratio - New Ratio
Gaining Ratio = New Ratio - Old Ratio

Treatment of Reserves and Accumulated Profits:
Accumulated reserves (General Reserve, Profit & Loss Account balance) are distributed among old partners in their old profit-sharing ratio. On change in ratio, they are transferred to partners' capital accounts in the new ratio.

Treatment of Fixed Assets:
Fixed assets are revalued. Appreciation/depreciation is adjusted in partners' capital accounts in old ratio.

Guarantee of Profits:
When one partner guarantees another partner a minimum profit, the shortfall is borne by the guaranteeing partner in his own share (unless otherwise agreed).
Example: A guarantees B a minimum profit of Rs. 50,000. If B's share of profit works out to Rs. 30,000, the shortfall of Rs. 20,000 is borne by A. If C also guarantees, the shortfall is shared between A and C in their guaranteed ratio or as agreed.

Journal Entry for Guarantee:
B's Capital Account Dr. (guaranteed amount)
To Profit & Loss Appropriation Account
A's Capital Account Dr. (shortfall borne by A)
To B's Capital Account

Note: In CA Intermediate, guarantee questions often combine with changes in profit-sharing ratios. The shortfall calculation must always consider the revised profit-sharing ratio of the firm first, then apply the guarantee.

Section 8: Valuation of Goodwill and Shares

Methods of Valuation of Goodwill:

1. Average Profit Method:
Goodwill = Average Profit × Number of Years Purchase
Average Profit = Total Profit for given years / Number of years
Adjustments: Add abnormal losses, deduct abnormal profits, adjust for over/under valuation of stock, adjust for interest on capital, etc.
Years purchase: Based on agreement or industry practice.

2. Weighted Average Profit Method:
When profits show a trend, weights are assigned to each year. Higher weight is given to recent years if profits are increasing.
Weighted Average Profit = Sum(Profit × Weight) / Sum of Weights
Goodwill = Weighted Average Profit × Years Purchase

3. Super Profit Method:
Super Profit = Average Profit - Normal Profit
Normal Profit = Capital Employed × Normal Rate of Return (%)
Capital Employed = Total Assets - Outside Liabilities (or Shareholders' Funds + Long-term Borrowings)
Goodwill = Super Profit × Years Purchase

4. Annuity Method:
Goodwill = Super Profit × Present Value Annuity Factor for specified number of years at specified rate.
PVAF = [1 - (1+r)^-n]/r

5. Capitalisation Method:
Goodwill = Average Profit / Normal Rate of Return × 100 - Capital Employed
OR
Goodwill = Capitalised Value of Business - Capital Employed
Capitalised Value = Average Profit × 100/NR%

Valuation of Shares:
Net Asset Method (Intrinsic Value Method):
Value per share = (Net Assets - Preference Capital) / Number of Equity Shares
Net Assets = Total Assets - Outside Liabilities

Earnings Yield Method:
Fair Value per share = (Expected Earnings per Share / Normal Rate of Return) × 100
Market Value per share = Fair Value + (Dividend per Share × 100 / Market Rate of Return)

Dividend Yield Method:
Value per share = (Actual Dividend per Share / Market Rate of Dividend) × 100
Expected Dividend per Share = (Shareholders' Funds / Total Share Capital) × Average Dividend per Share

When shares are held in different companies, consolidate all holdings and compute overall value.

Section 9: Financial Statements of Companies — Schedule III Compliance

As per the Companies Act, 2013 and Schedule III, every company must prepare financial statements comprising:

Balance Sheet (as at the end of the financial year):
Equity and Liabilities:
I. Shareholders' funds: Share capital, Reserves and surplus
II. Share application money pending allotment
III. Non-current liabilities: Long-term borrowings, Deferred tax liabilities, Other long-term liabilities, Long-term provisions
IV. Current liabilities: Short-term borrowings, Trade payables, Other current liabilities, Short-term provisions

Assets:
I. Non-current assets: Fixed assets, Intangible assets, Capital work in progress, Non-current investments, Deferred tax assets, Long-term loans and advances, Other non-current assets
II. Current assets: Current investments, Inventories, Trade receivables, Cash and cash equivalents, Short-term loans and advances, Other current assets

Profit and Loss Account:
Revenue from operations, Other income, Total revenue, Expenses (material, employee benefits, finance costs, depreciation, other expenses), PBT, Tax expense, NPAT

Notes forming an integral part of financial statements disclose: accounting policies, contingent liabilities, capital commitment, related party disclosures, segment reporting (where applicable), shareholding pattern, etc.

Key Requirements:
- Every company must prepare at least one Balance Sheet and one P&L Account per financial year.
- NBFCs, listed companies, and companies above certain thresholds require audit reports with explicit statements.
- Related party disclosures are mandatory under Ind AS and Companies Act.
- Comparative figures for previous year must be shown.
- Significantly, the format has undergone major restructuring — the old classification is obsolete; all ICAI exam questions now use the new Schedule III format.

Section 10: Accounting Standards — Key Standards for CA Intermediate

Accounting Standards (AS) are issued by the Institute of Chartered Accountants of India. As per the new scheme, all entities (except those following Ind AS) must comply with notified AS.

Key Standards for CA Intermediate:

AS 1 — Disclosure of Accounting Policies: Policies that manifestingly affect the financial position and results of operations must be disclosed. Changes in policy are accounted for retroactively; effect disclosed separately.

AS 2 — Valuation of Inventories: Inventories valued at lower of cost and net realizable value. Cost includes all costs of purchase, conversion, and other costs incurred in bringing inventory to present location and condition. Net realizable value is estimated selling price less estimated costs of completion and selling expenses.

AS 3 — Cash Flow Statement: Classified into operating, investing, and financing activities. Indirect method starts from PBT and adjusts for non-cash items and changes in working capital.

AS 4 — Contingencies and Events After Reporting Period: Contingent assets/liabilities disclosed in notes. Events after balance sheet date that provide evidence of conditions existing at balance sheet date require adjustment; those indicating conditions arising after balance sheet date require disclosure only.

AS 5 — Net Profit or Loss for the Period, Prior Period Items and Changes in Accounting Policies: Prior period items are Adjustments of material amounts arising from errors or changes in estimates, recognized in the period they are identified. Extraordinary items are excluded after AS 7 repeal.

AS 7 — Contracts for Construction of Assets: Turnover recognized on percentage completion method when outcome can be reliably estimated. Costs recognized as expenses in the period incurred.

AS 9 — Revenue Recognition: Revenue recognized when significant risks and rewards of ownership have transferred. Interest, royalties, and dividends recognized on time proportion basis.

AS 10 — Accounting for Fixed Assets: Fixed assets carried at historical cost or revalued amount. Depreciation charged systematically over useful life.

AS 11 — The Effects of Changes in Foreign Exchange Rates: Foreign currency transactions recorded at spot rate. Monetary items at balance sheet date translated at closing rate; non-monetary items at historical rate.

AS 15 — Employee Benefits: Defined contribution plans (provision equal to contributions for the period). Defined benefit plans (gratuity) using projected unit credit method; actuarial gains/losses recognized in OTC (Other Comprehensive Income) under Ind AS, but in P&L under AS 15.

AS 17 — Segment Reporting: Primary format is business segments; secondary format is geographical segments. Materiality threshold: 10% of revenue/profit/assets.

AS 18 — Related Party Disclosures: Identifying related parties and disclosing transactions with them including nature of relationship, description of transactions, amounts, and terms.

AS 29 — Provisions, Contingent Liabilities and Contingent Assets: Provisions recognized when present obligation exists, probable outflow, and reliable estimate. Contingent liabilities disclosed but not recognized.

Section 11: Account for Share-Based Payments and NRI Accounts

Account for Share-Based Payments (indicated under AS 30 equivalent guidance):

(i) Share-based Payment Transactions with Employees:
Equity-settled: Measured at fair value of equity instruments granted. Recognized as expense over vesting period with corresponding increase in equity.
Journal: Employee Benefit Expense Dr.
To Share-Based Payment Reserve
On exercise: Share Capital Dr., Security Premium Dr.
To Bank/Share Application Money
To Share-Based Payment Reserve (transfer to General Reserve or retained)

Cash-settled: Measured at fair value of liability. Revised at each reporting date until settlement.
Liability approach: Expense recognized with credit to liability account.

(ii) Share-based Payment Transactions with Non-Employees:
Measured at fair value of goods/services received, or if not reliably measurable, at fair value of equity instruments granted. Recognized immediately.

NRI Accounts:
NRIs holding shares of Indian companies maintain accounts through NRE (Non-Resident External) and NRO (Non-Resident Ordinary) accounts.
- NRE Account: Funds repatriable. Dividends and proceeds from sale of shares can be credited and remitted abroad freely.
- NRO Account: Funds not fully repatriable. Dividends are credited here; remittance is subject to RBI guidelines and tax deduction at source.

Accounting Treatment:
- Dividend declared on NRI shares: Proposed Dividend Account Dr., NRI Shareholder Account Cr.
- Tax deducted at source on dividend: TDS recoverable from NRI.
- Unclaimed dividend after 7 years transferred to Investor Education and Protection Fund (IEPF) as per Companies Act, 2013.
- Forfeiture of NRI shares: Same procedure as domestic shareholders, but proceeds must be handled per FEMA regulations.

Section 12: Proforma Financial Statements, Limited Buy-out Scheme, and Exam Hotspots

Proforma Financial Statements:
When a company prepares financial statements incorporating events that occurred after the balance sheet date but before approval of the statements, proforma adjustments are made. These show the effect of issues of shares, debentures, or other events as if they had occurred at the balance sheet date.
Approach: Start with actual Balance Sheet figures. Pass adjustments for the proposed issue (e.g., debentures issued post-balance sheet date but before approval). Show corresponding changes in capital structure, reserves, and liabilities.

Limited Buy-out Scheme (Rule 10D of Companies (Share Capital and Debentures) Rules, 2014):
If shares or debentures remain unallotted after closing of a public issue due to excess applications, the issuer may offer unallotted securities to subscribers at a buy-back price determined as per the rules. This prevents lock-in of investor money.
Pricing: Buy-back price = Issue Price + Interest (as per prescribed formula). Interest computed at SBI MCLR + 2% per annum for the period between application date and buy-out date.
Application: Must be made within 60 days of closing of issue. Process governed by SEBI regulations and Companies Act provisions.

Hot Topics for CA Intermediate Exams:
1. Forfeiture and re-issue combined with bonus issue from forfeited shares
2. Redemption of debentures by purchase in open market combined with CPR creation
3. Admission of partner with rising/fixed goodwill and revaluation adjustments
4. Dissolution questions combining unknown asset realization and insolvent partner treatment
5. Valuation of goodwill using average profit and super profit methods together
6. Financial statements formatting as per new Schedule III
7. Accounting standards applied in practical problem format
8. Guaranteed profit problems with changing profit-sharing ratios

Important facts

  • Capital Redemption Reserve (CPR) must be created when redeeming preference shares/debentures out of profits. CPR can only be used for issuing fully paid bonus shares.
  • Securities Premium received on forfeited shares can be used for writing off preliminary expenses, share discount, or issuing fully paid bonus shares.
  • On forfeiture, the original share subscriber loses all rights; re-issued shares become the company's property.
  • In dissolution, solvable partners bear insolvent partner's deficiency in their gaining ratio.
  • Goodwill on admission: Gaining ratio = New Ratio - Old Ratio. Sacrificing partners compensated in sacrificing ratio.
  • Goodwill valuation methods: Average profit, weighted average, super profit, annuity, capitalisation methods.
  • Inventories valued at lower of cost and net realizable value per AS 2.
  • Contingent liabilities disclosed but not recognized; provisions recognized when probable outflow and reliable estimate exists (AS 29).
  • Schedule III format requires 'Equity and Liabilities' side followed by 'Assets' side — both in classified (non-current/current) format.
  • NRI dividend unclaimed after 7 years transferred to IEPF. NRE account funds are repatriable; NRO account funds are not fully repatriable.
  • Limited Buy-out Scheme buy-back price = Issue Price + Interest at SBI MCLR + 2% per annum.
  • Prior period items under AS 5: adjustments arising from errors or changes in estimates, recognized in the period identified.
  • Cash flow statement: Operating, Investing, Financing activities. Indirect method adjusts PBT for non-cash items.
  • AS 30-equivalent guidance: equity-settled share-based payments measured at fair value of equity instruments granted; cash-settled measured at fair value of liability.

Formulas and relationships

Goodwill (Average Profit Method) = Average Profit × Number of Years Purchase
Average Profit = Total Profit for Given Years ÷ Number of Years
Super Profit = Average Profit - Normal Profit; Normal Profit = Capital Employed × (Normal Rate of Return ÷ 100)
Goodwill (Super Profit Method) = Super Profit × Number of Years Purchase
Goodwill (Annuity Method) = Super Profit × PVAF(n, r); PVAF = [1-(1+r)^-n]÷r
Capital Employed = Total Assets - Outside Liabilities OR Shareholders' Funds + Long-term Borrowings
Goodwill (Capitalisation Method) = (Average Profit ÷ Normal Rate of Return × 100) - Capital Employed
Gaining Ratio = New Profit Sharing Ratio - Old Profit Sharing Ratio
Sacrificing Ratio = Old Profit Sharing Ratio - New Profit Sharing Ratio
Net Asset Value per Share = (Net Assets - Preference Capital) ÷ Number of Equity Shares
Dividend Yield Value per Share = (Actual Dividend per Share ÷ Market Rate of Dividend) × 100
Buy-back Price under Limited Buy-out = Issue Price + Interest (SBI MCLR + 2% p.a.)
Weighted Average Profit = Σ(Profit × Weight) ÷ ΣWeights
Net Realizable Value = Estimated Selling Price - Estimated Costs of Completion - Estimated Selling Expenses
Capitalised Value of Business = Average Profit × (100 ÷ Normal Rate of Return)

Common mistakes

  • Students often create CPR from Securities Premium directly instead of transferring profits to CPR first. CPR must be created from distributable profits, not from SP reserve.
  • In forfeiture questions, students forget to write off securities premium already received on forfeited shares from the Share Forfeiture Account. Both share capital and premium must be considered.
  • In dissolution of partnership, the insolvent partner's deficiency is sometimes distributed among all partners equally instead of in the gaining ratio of solvent partners.
  • While computing average profit for goodwill valuation, students forget to add back abnormal losses and deduct abnormal profits.
  • Under AS 2, net realizable value is confused with selling price alone. It must be selling price MINUS costs of completion and selling expenses.
  • Students sometimes apply prior period items to opening retained earnings instead of recognizing them in the current period P&L as per AS 5.
  • In retirement questions, the retiring partner's share of goodwill is sometimes distributed in new ratio instead of gaining ratio.
  • Cash flow from operating activities: students often incorrectly include proceeds from sale of fixed assets as operating cash flows — these belong in investing activities.
  • When preparing financial statements per Schedule III, candidates sometimes still follow the old vertical format with arbitrary headings rather than the mandated non-current/current classification.
  • Under AS 29, contingent assets are recognized in accounts — this is incorrect. Contingent assets are merely disclosed, never recognized.
