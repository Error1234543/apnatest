CA Intermediate Group 1 - Taxation (Direct + Indirect): Complete Study Notes

Section 1: Framework of Indian Tax System & Basic Concepts

India's tax system operates through two primary streams:

Direct Taxes — levied under the Income-tax Act, 1961, administered by CBDT. Tax burden cannot be shifted to another person. Includes: (a) Tax on Income of Persons, (b) Wealth Tax (omitted w.e.f. A.Y. 2020-21), (c) Securities Transaction Tax, (d) Higher Education Cess / Shekel Cess.

Indirect Taxes — governed by constitutional provisions and statutory enactments. At CA Inter level, the primary indirect tax is GST (Goods and Services Tax). Other indirect taxes at foundation level included Customs, Excise — now largely subsumed into GST.

Key Terminology:
- Assessee: A person who is liable to pay tax under the Income-tax Act [s.2(7)]. This includes a person in respect of whom any proceeding under the Act has been commenced [s.2(7A)].
- Person [s.2(31)]: (i) Individual, (ii) HUF, (iii) Company, (iv) Firm, (v) AOP/BOI, (vi) Local Authority, (vii) Artificial Juridical Person (AJP).
- Assessment Year (AY): The period beginning 1st April and ending 31st March of the next year, in which income of the previous year is assessed [s.2(9)]. E.g., PY 2024-25 → AY 2025-26.
- Previous Year (PY): The financial year immediately preceding the Assessment Year, in which income is earned [s.3]. For every assessee, it is the previous year itself, unless otherwise provided.
- Current Financial Year (FY): The 12-month period from 1st April to 31st March.

Constitutional Provisions on Taxation:
- Article 265: No tax shall be levied or collected except by authority of law.
- Entry 82, List I: Taxes on income other than agricultural income.
- Entry 97, List I: Residuary legislative power (including any tax not mentioned in other entries).
- Entry 54, List II: Taxes on the sale/purchase of goods (State).
- Entry 97, List III: GST on goods and services (both Centre and States).
- Article 246A: Special provision relating to goods and services tax.

CBDT vs CBIC:
- CBDT (Central Board of Direct Taxes): Administers direct tax laws — Income-tax Act, STA, etc.
- CBIC (Central Board of Indirect Taxes & Customs): Administers GST, Customs, and Central Excise laws.

Section 2: Residential Status & Scope of Total Income

Residential Status of an Assessee [s.6] determines the scope of total income.

For Individuals — determined by stay in India during relevant periods:

| Residential Status | Conditions |
|---|---|
| Resident & Ordinarily Resident (ROR) | (i) Physical stay ≥ 182 days in PY, OR (ii) Physical stay ≥ 60 days in PY + 365 days in 4 years immediately preceding PY + 730 days in 7 years immediately preceding PY |
| Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR) | Resident but fails either of above conditions |
| Non-Resident (NR) | Not a Resident |

Note: The 60-day limit is extended to 182 days for:
- Indian citizens visiting India during PY
- Indian citizens leaving India during PY for employment abroad
- Persons of Indian origin (PIO) visiting India during PY

PIO = Indian citizen with Indian parent/grandparent born in undivided India or specified territories.

For Companies [s.6(3)(d)]: Resident if Incorporated in India OR Place of Effective Management (POEM) in India during PY.
- POEM [s.6(3A)]: The place where key management and policy decisions are substantially made. Guidelines issued vide CBIC circular.

For Firms, AOP, BOI, Bodies [s.6(3)(c)]: Resident if (a) Established/incorporated in India during PY, OR (b) POEM in India during PY.

Scope of Total Income based on Residential Status:

| Residential Status | Scope of Income |
|---|---|
| ROR | Global income (India + Foreign) |
| RNOR | (a) Income received/deemed received in India, (b) Income earned outside India from business controlled in India/profession set up in India, (c) Other foreign income NOT included |
| NR | Income received/deemed received in India + income from sources in India only |

Deemed Income [s.5(2)] — Income deemed to be received in India:
(a) Any amount due/discharged from an account in India
(b) Any amount received outside India by or on behalf of a resident only if it would have been received in India had it been received on his behalf in India
(c) Any income from assets/joint ventures located in India or sources in India

Section 3: Heads of Income — Salary

Salary is the first head under Chapter IV (s.15).

Components chargeable under Salary [s.16]:

(a) Basic Salary — Full salary paid/credited is taxable.

(b) Dearness Allowance (DA) — Taxable in full (only DA forming part of retirement benefits is excluded under s.17(2)).

(c) Commission — Percentage or fixed amount, taxable when received.

(d) Persents/Commission on sales/profit of business — Taxable.

Statutory Deductions from Salary [s.16]:

1. Standard Deduction [s.16(ia)]: ₹50,000 (applicable to both old and new regime). No deduction for pension.

2. Transport Allowance [s.16(2)]: Nil (omitted). Only disability allowance remains for transported employees.

3. Household Servant Allowance [s.16(3)]: ₹1,700/month or ₹20,400/year for blind/low-vision or orthopedically handicapped individual.

Perquisites (Fringe Benefits) [s.17(2)]:

Perquisites are any benefit/talent enjoyed by employee by virtue of employment.

Types of Perquisites & Valuation:

| Perquisite | Valuation Rule |
|---|---|
| Free or concessional transport | Actual cost / fair value less employee contribution |
| Residential premises (owned by employer) | 15% of salary OR actual rent paid by employer — whichever is higher, less employee contribution |
| Residential premises (leased by employer) | Actual rent paid by employer less reimbursement by employee (capped per city: ₹1,000/₹500/₹0) |
| Specified securities/PSUs shares | Lower of: (a) FMV on date of exercise, or (b) Actual payment made |
| Unlisted shares | Method prescribed by Rule 3(1)(vi) (book value method) |
| Club facilities/hospitalization | Nil (unless specified) |
| Car & driver facility | As per Rule 5 of Income Tax Rules |
| Interest-free/concessional loan | SBI rate interest differential (Rule 3(1)(v)) |

Exempt Perquisites:
- Free meals at workplace (canteen)
- Medical reimbursement up to ₹15,000 (now ₹5,000 under new rules)
- Health insurance premium paid by employer
- Specified securities/shares: up to 5% of employees eligible (not available from FY 2020-21)

Allowances [s.16(ii)]:

| Allowance | Exemption Limit |
|---|---|
| House Rent Allowance (HRA) [s.10(13) + Rule 2A] | Least of: (a) Actual HRA received, (b) 50% salary (metro)/40% salary (non-metro), (c) Rent paid minus 10% of salary |
| Children's Student Allowance | ₹100/month per child (max 2 children) |
| Transport Allowance (disabled) | ₹3,200/month (now ₹5,200) |
| Traveling/Migration Allowance | As per rules |
| Conveyance Allowance | Nil (omitted w.e.f. 01.04.2010) |
| Uniform Allowance | Actual expenditure on uniform |
| Daily Allowance (travel) | As per rules depending on place of tour

Section 4: Heads of Income — House Property

Income from House Property [s.22 to s.27] arises from ownership of building/land appurtenant thereto.

Condition for chargeability: The property must be owned by the assessee, not let out solely — self-occupied, lets out, or both.

Gross Annual Value (GAV) Determination [s.23]:

The GAV is determined as follows:

Let-out Property: Higher of Municipal Value (MV) or Fair Rental Value (FRV), subject to Actual Gross Rent Receipts (AGRR).

| Step | Rule |
|---|---|
| 1 | Determine Potential Gross Annual Value (PGAV) = Higher of MV or FRV |
| 2 | If PGAV > AGRR → PGAV is GAV |
| 3 | If PGAV < AGRR → GAV = PGAV (capped at AGRR for self-occupied too) |

Self-Occupied Property [s.23(1)(a)]:
- GAV = Nil
- If more than one self-occupied house: choose one (whichever is more beneficial); others treated as deemed let-out

Deductible Expenses [s.24]:

(a) Standard Deduction [s.24(a)]: 30% of Net Annual Value (NAV)

(b) Interest on Borrowed Capital [s.24(b)]:
- Self-occupied: Up to ₹2,00,000 (for pre-construction period, 5 equal annual installments starting from assessment year following completion)
- Let-out: No upper limit
- Pre-construction period: Maximum 5 years prior to AY in which construction is completed

(c) Municipal Tax [s.24(i)]: Actually paid during the year (not accrued)

Computation of Income from House Property:

```
Gross Annual Value (GAV)
Less: Municipal Taxes Paid
= Net Annual Value (NAV)
Less: Standard Deduction (30% of NAV)
Less: Interest on Borrowed Capital (u/s 24(b))
= Income from House Property
```

Important Cases:
- Unlet/Vacant property: If property could have been let out but remained vacant, and realizable rent > GAV, excess realizable rent is considered while computing GAV.
- Deemed Let-out: When a taxpayer has more than one self-occupied house, the second house is deemed let-out; its GAV = MV.
- Co-operative housing society: Shareholder member paying maintenance charges — these are allowed as deduction u/s 24(b) if they relate to borrowed capital interest component.
- Rent-free accommodation provided by employer: Taxable as perquisite (not under HP).

Section 5: Heads of Income — Profits and Gains of Business or Profession (PGBP)

Income under PGBP [s.28 to s.44DB] is computed after allowing all revenue expenses incurred wholly and exclusively for business.

Inclusions [s.28]:
(a) Profits/gains from any business/profession carried on
(b) Value of any benefit/talent arising from business/profession
(c) Business/capital receipt deemed as revenue receipt (e.g., reading fee, commission)
(d) Fee, commission, perquisite, etc., received in relation to business
(e) any sum received under KVP scheme not withdrawn (deemed income)
(f) Excess of consideration over stamp duty value of property received as gift
(g) Compensation/modification receipts
(h) Sum received on voluntary closure/shift of business — taxable as pgbp

Deductions u/s 30 to 43D:

(a) Section 30 — Rent, Rates, Taxes, Repairs, Renewal of Premises:
- Rent/rates/fresh/taxes on premises used for business are deductible
- Ordinary repairs (not capital) are deductible
- Renewal of lease/deed is deductible

(b) Section 31 — Insurance of Plant, Machinery, Stock:
- Premium on insuring stock-in-trade and plant/machinery is deductible

(c) Section 32 — Depreciation:
Depreciation is calculated on written down value (WDV) method at prescribed rates:

| Asset Type | Rate |
|---|---|
| Building (pucca) | 10% |
| Building (kuccha) | 5% |
| Plant & Machinery | 15%–40% (depends) |
| Furniture & Fittings | 10%–15% |
| Computers | 40% |
| Vehicles | 15% |

Block Concept [s.43(6)]: All assets of same class form a 'block'. WDV of block is computed year-wise.

(d) Section 36(1)(ii) — Interest on Borrowed Capital:
- Interest on capital borrowed for business purpose is deductible
- For partners: interest on partner's loan to firm — up to statutory rate (currently 12% p.a. as per s.40(b))

(e) Section 37(1) — General Deductions:
Any expenditure (not being capital or personal) laid out wholly and exclusively for business/profession is deductible.

(f) Section 38 — Partially Used for Business:
If asset used partly for business and partly for personal purposes, only proportionate depreciation/expenses are allowed.

Specific Deductions:
- Entertainment allowance [s.37(1)]: DBTs only — ₹5,000/month or 5% of salary, whichever is less (for directors/employees of companies)
- Employees' welfare [s.37(1)]: Deductible if utilized for specified purposes
- Professional tax [s.37(1)]: Fully deductible
- Laissez-faire/contribution to PF/ESIC: Fully deductible

Disallowed/Restrictions [s.40 & s.43B]:
- s.40(a): Income tax, wealth tax, ED, corporate dividend tax, super tax — NOT deductible
- s.40(b): Partner's salary/remuneration — subject to limits under s.40(b)(bi/bii)
- s.43B: Certain payments deductible ONLY when actually paid (before end of PY or before due date of return)

Presumptive Taxation [s.44AD, 44ADA, 44AE, 44AC]:
- s.44AD: Presumptive income @ 6% (digital)/8% (non-digital) of turnover (≤ ₹2 cr)
- s.44ADA: Professionals — presumptive @ 50% of gross receipts (≤ ₹75 lakhs)
- s.44AE: Contractors/pound owners — presumptive income per vehicle
- s.44AC: Carrying goods on hire — presumptive @ 5% of gross receipts

Section 6: Capital Gains

Capital Gains [s.45 to s.48] arise on transfer of capital assets. The gain is classified as Short-Term or Long-Term based on the holding period.

Capital Asset [s.2(14)]: Any property of any kind held by assessee, excluding:
(a) Stock-in-trade
(b) Personal effects (wearing apparel, furniture, etc.)
(c) Gold silver, etc., for personal use (exceeding specified limits)
(d) Agricultural land in rural area
(e) Bonds/debentures/golden treasuries (specifically excluded)

Transfer [s.2(47)]: Sale, exchange, relinquishment, extinguishment of rights, compulsory acquisition, conversion of capital asset into stock-in-trade.

Cost of Acquisition [s.48 read with s.48A–48C]:
- For inherited assets: Cost to previous owner (s.49)
- For transferred assets: Consideration received/reasonable value as per stamp valuation
- For self-generated assets: Nil cost
- Indexation applicable to long-term capital assets acquired before 1-4-2001 (option to revise base year from 2001 to 2010 under s.48(2))

Holding Period for Classification:

| Asset Class | LTCL Holding Period | STCL Holding Period |
|---|---|---|
| Listed equity shares/UTI units/EQUITY-oriented MF | > 12 months | ≤ 12 months |
| Immovable property (land/building) | > 24 months | ≤ 24 months |
| Jewellery/artistic paintings/treaure trove | > 24 months | ≤ 24 months |
| Debt-oriented funds/IFCs/unlisted shares | > 24 months | ≤ 24 months |
| Self-generated goodwill/shares | > 24 months | ≤ 24 months |

Special Provisions:
- Sec.112A: LTCG on listed equity/ELSS — 10% over ₹1 lakh (no indexation)
- Sec.111A: STCG on listed shares/units — 15%
- Sec.54: Exemption on house property (LTCG reinvestment)
- Sec.54F: Exemption when selling any asset and buying house property
- Sec.54EC: Exemption on investment in specified bonds (up to ₹50 lakhs in 54EC/NHAI bonds)
- Sec.54D: Rehabilitation/exemption for displaced persons
- Sec.54GB: Exemption on investment in equity shares of eligible start-up

Indexation Benefit [s.48(2)]:
Indexed Cost of Acquisition = Actual Cost × (CII of year of transfer / CII of year of acquisition)

CII values (selected): 2001-02 = 100, 2010-11 = 167, 2023-24 = 331, 2024-25 = 348

Capital Account/Pool Concept [s.49]: For inherited, gifted, or transferred assets, the cost is deemed to be the cost to the previous owner, inflated by indexation.

Section 7: Agricultural Income & clubbing of Income

Agricultural Income [s.2(1A)]: Income from:
(a) Rent/receipt from agricultural land in India
(b) Income from agricultural operations on such land (basic agricultural operations + subsequent operations)
(c) Income from processing of agricultural produce to make it marketable (without changing its essential character)

Exemption [s.10(1)]: Agricultural income is wholly exempt from income tax.

Partial Integration [s.91]: When an assessee has both agricultural and non-agricultural income, the agricultural income is used to determine the tax rate (by adding to total income), but the tax so computed is reduced by tax on agricultural income alone.

Computation under s.91:
1. Compute tax on (Total Income + Agricultural Income)
2. Compute tax on (Total Income excluding Agricultural Income)
3. Tax payable = (Step 1 − Step 2)
4. But if assessee is a domestic company, special rules apply

Clubbing of Income [s.60 to s.65]:

| Section | Provision |
|---|---|
| s.60 | Transfer of income without transfer of assets — income仍归原所有人 |
| s.61 | Revocable transfer of assets — income仍归原所有人 |
| s.62 | Irrevocable transfer — income仍归原所有人 if certain conditions met |
| s.63 | Revocable arrangements — includes re-transfer |
| s.64(1)(i) | Spouse's income from inadequate consideration |

Important facts

  • Indian tax system divided into Direct Taxes (Income Tax Act, 1961) and Indirect Taxes (GST)
  • Residential status (ROR/RNOR/NR) fundamentally determines scope of total income
  • Standard deduction of ₹50,000 available under s.16(ia) to all salaried assessees
  • HRA exemption computation involves least of three conditions
  • Depreciation is on WDV basis at prescribed rates
  • LTCG on listed equity shares: 10% over ₹1 lakh (s.112A, no indexation)
  • Agricultural income is exempt under s.10(1) but partially integrated for rate purposes
  • Clubbing provisions (s.60-65) prevent income splitting to reduce tax liability
  • Presumptive taxation schemes: s.44AD (business), s.44ADA (professionals), s.44AE (transport)
  • GST at Inter level covers: concept of supply, GST structure (CGST/SGST/IGST), Input Tax Credit mechanism, basic rates

Formulas and relationships

HRA Exemption = Min(Actual HRA, 50%/40% of Basic+DA, Rent paid - 10% of Basic+DA)
Income from HP = NAV - 30% std. deduction - Interest on borrowed capital (max ₹2L for self-occ.)
NAV = GAV - Municipal taxes paid
Depreciation = WDV of block × prescribed rate
Capital Gain = Full value of consideration - Indexed cost of acquisition - Transfer expenses
Indexed Cost = Original cost × (CII of transfer year / CII of acquisition year)
Agricultural Income integration: Tax on (Total+Agri) - Tax on (Total ex-Agri)
GST: CGST + SGST = Intra-state supply; IGST = Inter-state supply

Common mistakes

  • Confusing PY with AY — PY is when income is earned, AY is when assessed
  • Using 60-day rule universally for individuals — Indian citizens/PIOs get 182-day concession
  • HRA calculation errors — forgetting rent paid minus 10% of salary condition
  • Claiming depreciation on personal-use assets — must be used for business
  • Applying indexation to short-term capital assets — only LTCG eligible
  • Not checking holding period carefully for each asset class — varies by type
  • Ignoring clubbing provisions — income transferred to spouse/minor still taxed in transferor's hands
  • Mixing up ROR vs RNOR scope — RNOR does NOT get global income taxed
  • For getting that s.43B allows deduction only on actual payment, not accrual basis
  • Treating food coupons/medical benefits below threshold as fully taxable — many are exempt
