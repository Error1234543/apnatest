Formation of a Company Under the Companies Act, 2013

A company is an artificial person created by law with a separate legal entity, perpetual succession, and a common seal. The Companies Act, 2013 governs incorporation, objectives, and compliance.

Types of companies:
• Public Company: Minimum 7 members, minimum 2 directors, cannot invite public to subscribe. Must have 'Ltd' suffix.
• Private Company: Minimum 2 members, minimum 2 directors (max 200), restricts transfer of shares, prohibits public invitation. Must have 'Pvt Ltd' suffix.
• One Person Company (OPC): Single member and single director. Must have 'One Person Private Ltd'. Cannot convert into non-opc if paid-up capital exceeds ₹50 lakh or turnover exceeds ₹2 crore.
• Section 8 Company: Formed for charitable objects such as commerce, art, science, sports, education, research, social welfare, religion, charity, protection of environment, etc. Profit is applied toward promoting objects; prohibition on dividend payment.
• Dormant Company: A company registered with the Registrar as dormant for the purpose of preserving an asset or intellectual property for future use. Must apply within 180 days of incorporation; failure renders it liable to be struck off.
• Holding and Subsidiary: A holding company controls the composition of the board of another company (subsidiary). Control means >50% voting power or right to appoint majority of directors.

Name reservation and incorporation process:
Step 1: Obtain Digital Signature Certificate (DSC) for proposed subscribers/directors.
Step 2: Obtain Director Identification Number (DIN) — every person proposing to be a director must have DIN. Threshold: aggregate consideration > ₹10 lakh or income > ₹1 million in any financial year requires DIN at time of becoming promoter/founder.
Step 3: File RUN (Request for Name) or SPICe+ Part A for name approval with the Registrar.
Step 4: File SPICe+ (Simplified Proforma for Incorporating Company Electronically Plus) — Part A for incorporation, Part B for allotment of shares, EPFO, ESIC, GST, bank account opening, and PAN/TAN application.
Step 5: File INC-21 for commencement of business (if company has share capital and provides for raising money from public — applicable mainly to public companies).

Memorandum of Association (MOA): The charter of the company defining its relationship with the outside world. Contains six clauses:
1. Name Clause — company name as approved.
2. Registered Office Clause — state where registered office is situated.
3. Object Clause — main objects, necessary objects, and other objects. Ultra vires acts beyond object clause are void.
4. Liability Clause — members' liability limited by shares or guarantee.
5. Capital Clause — authorized share capital and division into denominations.
6. Subscription Clause — subscribers' names, addresses, and shares taken.

Articles of Association (AOA): Contains rules and regulations for internal management. Prescribed in Table F (for companies limited by shares) and other tables. AOA must be consistent with MOA and the Act. Alteration of AOA requires special resolution unless Act permits ordinary resolution.

Promoters: Persons who conceive the idea of forming a company and carry out the necessary steps. They stand in fiduciary relationship with the company. A promoter may make secret profits — must disclose and account for them. Remedies: rescind contract, sue for damages, or disgorge profit.

Prospectus, Allotment of Shares, and Issue of Securities

Prospectus means any document inviting deposits or inviting the public to subscribe to securities of a company. Includes red herring prospectus, shelf prospectus, and offer document.

Types of prospectus:
• Initial Prospectus: Issued at the time of incorporation.
• Deferred Prospectus: Issued after the company commences business, filed by a public company before offering shares to public.
• Shelf Prospectus: Issued by companies (NBFCs or public companies rated AAA/AA) for issues over a period of three years. First stage of prospectus contains information about the issues. Second stage called 'offer document' specifies terms of issue.
• Red Herring Prospectus: A prospectus that omits details of price and quantum of securities. Filed with RoC at least 3 days before opening of offer. Final offer document must be filed within 10 days of pricing.

Allotment of securities:
• Minimum subscription: 90% of fixed amount of minimum subscription must be received. Excess collected can be refunded with interest at 6% p.a. if not utilized within 5 weeks.
• Allotment must be made within 30 days from date of closure of subscription list. Default attracts penalty on company and officers.
• Underwriting: Underwriters agree to subscribe to shares if not fully subscribed. Commission payable to underwriters max 5% on allotment + 5% on application money (subject to SEBI guidelines for public issues).

Regulations on allotment of shares:
• No allotment shall be made unless minimum subscription is received.
• No allotment to be made within 30 days from closure of subscription list.
• Allotment must be disclosed in Board meeting within 2 months from first publication of notice.
• Prospectus must state that application money is payable only in cash.
• Application money must not be less than 5% of face value.
• All monies received shall be deposited in a separate bank account.

Private placement: A company may make an offer to such persons as it considers relevant by issuing a letter of offer in prescribed form. Must comply with Sections 42 and 62. Private placement offer must be sent through registered post/e-speed post/e-mail with acknowledgment due.

Further issue of shares: Special resolution required for alteration of capital. Right issue — existing shareholders have pre-emptive rights. Preferential issue — requires special resolution, valuation report by registered valuer, and compliance with Section 81 conditions.ESOP — Employee Stock Option Scheme requires special resolution.

Buy-back of shares: Section 68. A company may buy back its own securities from members on a proportionate basis (not selective buy-back except through tender offer). Maximum buy-back in any financial year cannot exceed 25% of paid-up capital. Buy-back completed within one year from passing of special resolution. No buy-back if default in repayment of debts or discharge of statutory obligations exists.

Share Capital, Debentures, and Transfer of Securities

Types of Share Capital:
• Equity Share Capital: Voting rights attached;Dividend declared out of profits.
• Preference Share Capital: Preferential right to repayment of capital; fixed rate of dividend; usually no voting rights (except on resolution affecting their rights).

Classes of preference shares:
• Cumulative: Arrears of dividend accumulate and must be paid before equity dividend.
• Non-cumulative: Unpaid dividend does not accumulate.
• Participating: Participate in surplus profits after payment of fixed dividend.
• Non-participating: No right to surplus profits.
• Convertible: Convertible into equity shares at predetermined conditions.
• Redeemable: Redeemable during lifetime of company or at predetermined time.

Restrictions on redeemable preference shares:
• Must be fully paid up.
• Can be redeemed only out of profits or fresh issue proceeds.
• Redemption premium if any must be out of Securities Premium Account.
• Redeemable preference shares must be redeemed within 20 years from issue.
• 100% redemption must be funded out of profits before redemption or fresh issue.

Debentures:
• Debenture means any instrument evidencing a debt; includes debenture stock, bonds, and any other securities.
• Types: Secured/Unsecured, Redeemable/Permanent, Convertible/Non-convertible, Registered/Bearer.
• Debenture holders are creditors, not members.
• Company may create charge on assets for debentures. Register of charges must be maintained.

Transfer and Transmission of Shares:
• Transfer: Voluntary act between living persons — requires valid instrument of transfer (Form SH-4), stamped, signed by transferor and transferee, and delivery to company.
• Transmission: Operation of law — on death, insolvency, or lunacy of shareholder. Heir/legal representative applies for transmission with proof of title.
• Company must register transfer/transmission within 1 month.
• Refusal of transfer: Company must send intimation within 15 days with reasons. Member may appeal to NCLT within 3 months.
• Dematerialization: All listed companies and companies with paid-up capital of ₹50 lakh or more must dematerialize securities. Unlisted companies may voluntary dematerialize.

Directors and Key Managerial Personnel

Directors are persons appointed to manage the affairs of the company. They are agents of the company and stand in fiduciary position.

Types of Directors:
• Managing Director (MD): In charge of the general control and management of the company.
• Whole-time Director (WTD): In whole-time employment of the company.
• Independent Director (ID): At least one-third of total directors shall be independent directors.
• Additional Director: Appointed by Board between two AGMs.
• Alternate Director: Appointed to act during absence of original director for ≥3 months.
• Woman Director: Every listed company and specified class of companies must appoint at least one woman director.
• Director appointed by small shareholders: Appointed to represent interests of small shareholders.

Appointment and Qualification:
• First directors may be named in MOA or appointed by subscribers.
• Subsequent directors elected at AGM.
• Director's consent in Form DIR-2 must be filed within 30 days of appointment.
• DIR-3 for DIN application.
• Qualifying shares: Company may require director to hold qualifying shares; default causes vacation of office.

Number and Residential Requirements:
• Private company: Min 2, Max 15 directors (can be increased by special resolution).
• Public company: Min 3, Max 15 directors (can be increased by special resolution).
• OPC: Min 1 director.
• Every company must have at least one director who stays in India for ≥182 days.

Key Managerial Personnel (KMP): CEO, Managing Director, CFO, Company Secretary, Wholesaler Director (if any). Listed companies must also appoint Independent Directors.

Remuneration of Directors:
• MD/WTD/ID: remuneration within limits specified in Schedule V — max 11% of net profits for managing/whole-time directors collectively, 5% for each additional director.
• If no profits or inadequate profits, remuneration as per Schedule V with CSR Committee recommendation.
• Payment to non-executive directors: fees as per Board but not exceeding 1% of net profits (max ₹5 lakhs per meeting).

Director's Responsibility Statement: Board's report must include:
(a) nature of business;
(b) development/improvements in performance and prospects;
(c) conservation of energy;
(d) technology absorption;
(e) human resource developments;
(f) risks anticipated;
(g) efforts towards sustainability;
(h) information on ESG aspects.

Related Party Transactions (RPT): Section 188 requires prior approval of Board for transactions with related parties. Material RPTs require shareholder approval by special resolution. Related parties include relative, HUF, trustee, trustee with substantial beneficial interest, body corporate where relative is a director/key manager, and entities controlled by such persons.

Rotation of Auditors: Statutory auditors must be rotated every 5 years. Audit firm can rotate after completion of 10 years.

Board and General Meetings

Board of Directors Meetings:
• Minimum 4 board meetings per year with gap not exceeding 120 days.
• Notice of 7 clear days required. Shorter notice permitted if agreed by all entitled to attend.
• Quorum: 1/3rd of total strength or 2 directors, whichever is higher. Personal interests do not count.
• Resolutions: Ordinary resolution — simple majority; Special resolution — 3:1 majority; Electronic voting possible.
• Recording: Minutes must be kept in Form MGT-14 within 30 days.
• File form MGT-14 with RoC within 30 days of meeting.

Statutory Meeting and Report (Public Company):
• Called within 6 months from date of certification of commencement of business.
• Statutory report must contain: total shares allotted, cash received, preliminary expenses, contractors' names, etc.

Annual General Meeting (AGM):
• Every company must hold AGM once every calendar year. Gap between two AGMs ≤ 15 months.
• First AGM within 9 months from close of first financial year.
• Notice period: 21 clear days.
• E-voting mandatory for listed companies and specified classes of companies.
• Small shareholders' representatives may attend through video conferencing.

Extraordinary General Meeting (EGM):
• Any general meeting other than AGM.
• Convened by Board or on requisition of members (≥1/10th of voting power or ₹5 lakh paid-up capital).

Class Rights and Debenture holders:
• Class right variation requires consent of holders of 3/4th of issued class shares or sanction by NCLT.

Meetings of bodies other than Board:
• Audit Committee, Nomination and Remuneration Committee, Stakeholders Relationship Committee, CSR Committee — meetings must be held as per Act requirements. Minutes recording applies.

Corporate Governance and Internal Control

Corporate Governance: Framework of rules, practices, and processes by which a company is directed and controlled.

Provisions under Companies Act, 2013:
• Composition of Board: At least one-third independent directors.
• Audit Committee: Mandatory for listed companies and specified class of companies. Composed entirely of independent directors. Minimum 3 members.
• Nomination and Remuneration Committee: At least 3 members with majority independent directors.
• Stakeholders Relationship Committee: Board may constitute this committee.
• CSR Committee: Required if CSR obligations are met. Minimum 3 directors.

Disclosure Requirements:
• Annual report to contain information on corporate governance.
• Board's report must disclose number of Board meetings, attendance of directors.
• Separate section on disclosures regarding listed companies.

Internal Financial Controls:
• Every company required to maintain internal financial controls commensurate with the size, nature, and complexity of its operations.
• Internal audit mandatory for:
- Listed companies
- Public companies with paid-up capital ≥ ₹50 lakh, turnover ≥ ₹20 crore, outstanding loans ≥ ₹100 crore
• Internal audit may be conducted by CA/CMA or through a firm of CA/CMA.

Whistle Blower Mechanism:
• Listed companies and specified class of companies must establish vigil mechanism for directors and employees to report genuine concerns.
• Protections for individuals making such reports.

Code of Conduct:
• Board shall lay down code of conduct for all Board members and senior management personnel.
• Annual compliance report to be included in Board's report.

Related Party Disclosure:
• Details of related party transactions to be disclosed in Board's report and annual report.
• Materiality threshold specified for disclosure.

Winding Up and Liquidation

Modes of Winding Up:
1. Compulsory Winding Up by Tribunal (NCLT):
• Company passes special resolution for voluntary winding up.
• Company unable to pay its debts.
• Default in filing financial statements/annual returns for 5 consecutive years.
• Tribunal is satisfied that company's affairs are being conducted with intent to defraud creditors or for any fraudulent purpose.
• Company is able to pay its debts but tribunal is of opinion that it should be wound up.

2. Voluntary Winding Up:
• Members' Voluntary Winding Up: Declaration of solvency by directors. Company able to pay its debts in full within specified period (not exceeding 3 years).
• Creditors' Voluntary Winding Up: No declaration of solvency. Committee of inspection formed.

3. Winding Up under Supervision of Tribunal:
• Available where company is being wound up voluntarily but Tribunal considers supervision necessary.

Liquidation Process:
• On winding up order, official liquidator appointed.
• Liquidator takes custody of company's assets.
• Call money due from contributories.
• Assets realized and distributed according to priority of payments:
1. Liquidation expenses
2. Secured creditors (to extent of security)
3. Workmen's dues
4. Employees' dues
5. Government dues
6. Unsecured creditors
7. Preference shareholders
8. Equity shareholders

Preferential Payments:
• In case of winding up, certain debts are preferential payments:
- Taxes due to government
- Wages/salaries of workmen/employees
- Provident fund contributions
• Preferential period: 12 months before winding up for workmen dues.

Consequences of Winding Up:
• Company ceases to carry on business except for beneficial winding up.
• Court organs (Board, Manager) cease to function.
• Proceedings against company stay.
• Property devolves on liquidator.

National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) and National Company Law Appellate Tribunal (NCLAT)

NCLT Constitution:
• Central Government constitutes NCLT with bencher at each principal seat.
• Chairman and Members appointed by President on recommendation of Selection Committee.
• Qualifications: Judge of HC or experience of 10 years in law/accountancy/business.

Jurisdiction and Powers:
• Original jurisdiction:
- Appeals against registrar decisions
- applications for compromise/settlement schemes
- oppression and mismanagement cases
- winding up proceedings
- determination of questions arising during winding up
• Appellate jurisdiction: Appeals against orders of Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India (IBBI)

Powers of NCLT:
• Power to regulate its own procedure.
• Dismiss or uphold appeals, confirm or quash orders.
• Award costs.
• Review its own orders.
• Presume official documents to be genuine.
• Compound offences.

NCLAT:
• Established under Section 410.
• Appellate authority for appeals against orders of NCLT and IBBI.
• Benchers at principal seats and such other places as may be specified.
• Chairperson and Members have same qualifications as NCLT.
• Appeal must be filed within 45 days from date of order.

Appeals from NCLT:
• To NCLAT — on question of fact or law.
• To Supreme Court — on substantial question of law only.

Procedure:
•.Application must be made in prescribed form with requisite fee.
•Notice to be issued to concerned parties.
•Hearing as per established procedure.
•Orders to be passed within stipulated timeline.

Limited Liability Partnership (LLP) Act, 2008

Concept:
• LLP is a body corporate and a legal entity separate from its partners.
• Limited liability of partners — liability limited to agreed contribution.
• Perpetual succession — changes in partners do not affect existence.
• Hybrid between partnership and company.

Formation:
• Designated partners (minimum 2) — at least one must be body corporate or individual resident in India.
• LLP agreement between partners governing mutual rights, duties, profit sharing, capital contribution.
• Must file incorporation document in Form INC-1 with RoC.
• Must obtain LLPIN (LLP Identification Number).
• Name reservation through RUN-LLP.

LLP Agreement:
• Must be filed in Form 3 within 30 days of formation.
• May be altered by consent of all partners.
• Alterations to be filed in Form 3 within 30 days.

Features:
• Flexibility in internal governance — governed by LLP agreement, not rigid statutory provisions.
• No requirement of min/max number of partners (unlike companies).
• No requirement of hold AGMs.
• Lower compliance burden compared to companies.

Compliance Requirements:
• Maintain books of accounts at registered office.
• File annual return in Form 11 within 60 days from close of financial year.
• File statement of account and solvency in Form 8 within 30 days from end of 6 months of financial year.
• Get accounts audited if turnover > ₹40 lakh or contribution > ₹25 lakh.
• Maintain auditor's report if turnover > ₹40 lakh or contribution > ₹25 lakh.
• Filing of tax returns.

Designated Partner Responsibility:
• Designated partners are responsible for compliance.
• Vicarious liability for contraventions.
• Must ensure filings are made within timelines.

Conversion:
• A company or body corporate can convert into LLP under certain conditions.
• Mutual conversion between private company and LLP permitted.

Limitation of Liability:
• Partners' liability limited to agreed contribution.
• However, partners personally liable for fraud.
• Piercing corporate veil possible in case of fraud.

Partnership Act, 1932 — Core Provisions

Definition:
• Section 4: Partnership is the relation between persons who have agreed to share the profits of a business carried on by all or any of them acting for all.
• Must arise from contract (agreement).
• Business must be carried on by all or any of them acting for all.

Requirements:
• Agreement between two or more persons.
• Sharing of profits.
• Mutual agency — each partner is agent of firm and other partners.

Registration:
• Registration is not compulsory but highly advisable.
• Unregistered firm cannot sue third parties or other partners.
• Registration filed in Form A with Registrar of Firms.
• Continuity certificate required.

Rights and Duties of Partners:
• Right to take part in management.
• Right to be consulted on matters affecting firm.
• Right to share profits equally (if no agreement).
• Right to interest on capital at 6% p.a. (if agreed).
• Right to reimbursement of expenses incurred for firm.
• Duty to indemnify for loss caused by fraud.
• Duty to render true accounts and full information.
• Duty to act with utmost good faith.
• Duty to indemnify for willful neglect.

Admission and Retirement:
• New partner admitted with consent of all partners.
• Retiring partner remains liable for acts done before retirement until public notice given.
• Absconding partner — firm may dissolve or continue.
• Expulsion — only if power conferred by agreement.

Dissolution:
• By agreement.
• Compulsory — on expiry of term, completion of adventure, illegality.
• By notice — in partnership at will.
• By Tribunal on grounds:
- Insanity of partner
- Permanent incapacity
- Misconduct affecting business
- Transfer of interest
- Constant breaches of agreement

Dissolution of Firm vs Dissociation:
• Dissolution of firm ends all relations between partners and third parties.
• Dissociation terminates relationship only between dissociating partner and firm.

Notice of Dissolution:
• Must be published in official Gazette and newspaper.
• Retiring partner must give notice to avoid continued liability.

SEBI Regulations — Issue, Disclosure, and Takeover Code

SEBI (Securities and Exchange Board of India) established under Section 3 of SEBI Act, 1992.

Primary Functions:
• Protect investors.
• Promote development of securities market.
• Regulate activities of stock brokers and other intermediaries.

SEBI (Issue of Capital and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2018:
• Governs public issues, rights issues, bonus issues, preferential issues.
• Requires disclosure of material facts in prospectus/offers document.
• Price band for book building issues.
• Anchor investor participation.
• Greyness period restrictions.

SEBI (Issue and Listing of Non-Convertible Securities) Regulations:
• Governs issue of NCDs, convertible debentures.
• Credit rating mandatory.
• Trustee appointed for secured issues.

SEBI (Substantial Acquisition of Shares and Takeovers) Regulations, 2011:
• Trigger point: 25% of voting rights requires open offer.
• Open offer size: Based on incremental holding above trigger point.
• Pricing: Highest price paid in last 26 weeks or average of mean weekly closing prices.
• Lock-in period: 12 months for acquirer's holding resulting from open offer.

SEBI (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations, 2015:
• Insider defined as any person who is a member of board or has access to unpublished price sensitive information (UPSI).
• UPSI — information that is not generally available but which, if generally available, would materially affect price.
• Code of fair practices required.
• Trading window closure periods for designated persons.
• Penalty for insider trading.

SEBI (Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2015:
• Mandatory corporate governance requirements for listed entities.
• Disclosure requirements including quarterly results, material events.
• Related party transaction disclosure.
• Risk management policies.
• Annual compliance report by CS.

Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 — Overview

Objective:
• To consolidate laws relating to insolvency resolution for persons and companies.
• Provide for quick resolution of insolvency.
• Maximization of value of assets.
• Promotion of entrepreneurship.
• Availability of credit.

Key Definitions:
• Insolvency: Process initiated when a debtor is unable to pay debts.
• Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP): Process for resolution of corporate debtor.
• Liquidation: Process of winding up of corporate debtor.

Insolvency Resolution Process:
• Creditor may initiate CIRP against corporate debtor.
• Corporate debtor defaults payment of debt.
• CoC (Committee of Creditors) constituted with financial creditors.
• Resolution professional appointed.
• Moratorium imposed — no suits, continuation of proceedings stayed.
• Resolution plan to be submitted within 180 days (extendable by 90 days).
• Approval of CoC by 66% voting share.
• NCLT approves resolution plan.

Liquidation:
• If no resolution plan approved or if plan fails, liquidation initiated.
• Liquidator appointed.
• Assets realized and distributed as per waterfalls mechanism.

Fast Track Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process:
• For specified classes of companies.
• Timeline: 90 days.

Individual Insolvency:
• Application to NCLT.
• Adjudicating authority determines insolvency.
• Fresh start process for individuals with low income.

Voluntary Liquidation:
• By resolution of members.
• Declaration of solvency required.
• Liquidator appointed.

Penalties and Offences:
• Contravention of Code attracts penalties.
• Fraudulent trading punishable.

Sarfarth Act and Other Corporate Laws

Companies (Acceptance of Deposits) Rules, 2014:
• Defines who is a 'depositor'.
• Prohibits acceptance of deposits from public except by way of secured/ unsecured loan from banks, financial institutions, NBFCs.
• Certain categories exempted.
• Penalties for illegal acceptance of deposits.

Companies (Corporate Social Responsibility Policy) Rules, 2014:
• Applicable to companies with ₹1000 crore net worth OR ₹500 crore turnover OR ₹5 crore net profit.
• Must spend 2% of average net profits of preceding 3 financial years on CSR activities.
• CSR Committee of 3 directors (1 independent).
• Unspent amount to be transferred to Fund specified in Schedule VII.

Companies (Managerial Remuneration) Rules, 2014:
• Conditions for managerial remuneration.
• Limits based on nature of company.
• Board's power to pay remuneration within limits.

Company Auditor's Report Order (CARO), 2016:
• Statutory auditor to report on matters specified in CARO.
• Applicable to companies having paid-up share capital of ₹50 lakh or turnover of ₹200 crore.

Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999:
• Consolidates and amends laws relating to foreign exchange.
• RBI regulates foreign exchange transactions.
• Current account transactions freely permitted.
• Capital account transactions regulated.

Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002:
• Prevents money laundering and provides for confiscation of property derived from proceeds of crime.
• Reportable transactions to be reported to FIU-IND.
• Scheduled offences specified.

Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation Act:
• Provides insurance cover to depositors.
• Cover up to ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank.

Important facts

  • A company is an artificial person created by law with perpetual succession and separate legal entity distinct from its members.
  • Minimum members: Public = 7, Private = 2, OPC = 1. Minimum directors: Public = 3, Private = 2, OPC = 1.
  • Section 8 companies are formed for charitable purposes; no dividend is payable.
  • Ultra vires acts — acts beyond the object clause of MOA — are void and cannot be ratified.
  • Memorandum of Association contains 6 clauses: Name, Registered Office, Object, Liability, Capital, Subscription.
  • Articles of Association contain rules for internal management; Table F prescribed for companies limited by shares.
  • Prospectus invites the public to subscribe to securities; includes red herring, shelf, and offer documents.
  • Minimum subscription = 90% of fixed amount; excess is refundable with 6% interest if not utilized within 5 weeks.
  • Allotment must be made within 30 days from closure of subscription; otherwise refund with interest.
  • Redeemable preference shares must be redeemed within 20 years; 100% funding from profits or fresh issue.
  • Buy-back limit = 25% of paid-up capital in a financial year; completed within 1 year of special resolution.
  • Independent directors: At least 1/3rd of total directors for listed companies; qualify per Section 149(6).
  • KMP includes CEO, MD, CFO, Company Secretary, and Whole-time Director.
  • Board meetings: Minimum 4 per year, gap ≤ 120 days; notice of 7 clear days; quorum = 1/3rd or 2 directors.
  • AGM gap ≤ 15 months; first AGM within 9 months from close of first financial year; e-voting mandatory for specified classes.
  • Related Party Transactions: Section 188 requires Board approval; material RPTs need special resolution.
  • Internal audit mandatory for public companies with paid-up capital ≥ ₹50 lakh, turnover ≥ ₹20 crore, or outstanding loans ≥ ₹100 crore.
  • NGT/NCLT: Tribunal can wind up companies; 1/3rd of directors are judicial members; appeals to NCLAT then SC.
  • NCLT jurisdiction includes: oppression/mismanagement, compromise/scheme, winding up, de-registration.
  • LLP: At least 2 designated partners, one must be resident in India; LLP agreement filed in Form 3 within 30 days.
  • Partnership Act: Partnership by agreement to share profits of business carried on by all or any acting for all. Registration optional but advisable.
  • SEBI Takeover Code: Trigger point at 25% voting rights; open offer mandatory; lock-in period 12 months.
  • SEBI Insider Trading: UPSI is information not generally available that would materially affect price; code of fair practices mandatory.
  • IBC 2016: CIRP timeline 180 days (+90 days extension); CoC decides by 66% voting share; moratorium stays all proceedings.
  • CSR: Mandatory for companies with net worth ≥ ₹500 crore OR turnover ≥ ₹1000 crore OR net profit ≥ ₹5 crore; spend 2% of avg net profit.
  • Caro 2016 applies to companies with paid-up capital ≥₹50 lakh or turnover ≥₹200 crore; auditor reports on specified matters.
  • PMLA 2002: Reportable transactions to FIU-IND; scheduled offences specified; fraudulently obtained proceeds can be confiscated.
  • Winding up waterfalls: Liquidation expenses → Secured creditors → Workmen's dues → Employees' dues → Government dues → Unsecured creditors → Preference shareholders → Equity shareholders.

Formulas and relationships

Underwriting Commission (max): 5% on allotment + 5% on application money (subject to SEBI guidelines)
Buy-back limit: Maximum 25% of paid-up equity capital in a financial year
Minimum subscription: 90% of the fixed amount of minimum subscription
Refund interest on excess: 6% per annum if not utilized within 5 weeks
Interest on application money: 6% p.a. if minimum subscription not received
Preferential period for workmen dues: 12 months before winding up
Open offer trigger: 25% of voting rights acquired
Lock-in period for acquirer: 12 months from open offer
CSR spending: 2% of average net profits of preceding 3 financial years
CoC resolution approval: 66% voting share under IBC
Maximum redemption period for preference shares: 20 years from issue date
Interest on redeemable preference shares: As per terms, but maximum 15% p.a. usually
Management fees to external auditor: Not to exceed 5% of net profit before exceptional items for statutory audit
Fee to non-executive directors: Not exceeding 1% of net profits (max ₹5 lakh per meeting)

Common mistakes

  • Confusing ultra vires (beyond MOA — void) with intra vires (within MOA — valid). Ultra vires cannot be ratified even by unanimous shareholders.
  • Thinking preference shareholders have voting rights on all matters. They only vote on resolutions directly affecting their rights (e.g., variation of class rights).
  • Mixing up the minimum/maximum numbers: Private company min 2/Max 200 members, Public company min 7/no upper limit. Board: Private min 2/max 15, Public min 3/max 15.
  • Forgetting that redemption of preference shares must be 100% funded either from profits or fresh issue proceeds — never from share capital alone.
  • Confusing Transfer (voluntary) with Transmission (by operation of law). Transfer requires SH-4; transmission requires probate/letter of administration.
  • Assuming buy-back can be selective. Proportionate buy-back only; selective buy-back not permitted except through tender offer route.
  • Misidentifying who qualifies as Key Managerial Personnel. CFO and CS are KMP; not all employees are.
  • Not applying the moratorium concept correctly in IBC. Moratorium prohibits all actions against the corporate debtor once CIRP is initiated.
  • Forgetting that the liquidator in winding up is appointed by NCLT, not by the company or creditors alone.
  • Confusing LLP and Partnership: LLP has separate legal entity; partnership does not. LLP partners have limited liability; partners in partnership have unlimited liability.
  • Incorrectly calculating the open offer price under SEBI takeover regulations — must take highest price paid in last 26 weeks OR average of weekly closing prices, whichever is higher.
  • Thinking that all companies must appoint independent directors. Only listed companies and specified class of companies under Section 149 require IDs.
  • Miscalculating CSR applicability thresholds. The criteria are alternative (any one condition triggers), not cumulative.
  • Forgetting that rotation of auditors applies to both statutory auditors and firm — 5 years per individual auditor and 10 years per firm.
  • Not realizing that under IBC, the moratorium period gives protection to the corporate debtor from legal proceedings, but does not absolve the debt.
