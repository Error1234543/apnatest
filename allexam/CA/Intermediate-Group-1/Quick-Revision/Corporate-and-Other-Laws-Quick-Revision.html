CA Intermediate — Corporate and Other Laws (Quick Revision)

CAEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • The Companies Act, 2013 is the principal statute governing the incorporation, responsibilities, powers, and conduct of companies in India. It replaced the Companies Act, 1956 and came into force in phases starting April 1, 2014.
  • A company under Section 2(20) of the Companies Act, 2013 means a company incorporated under this Act or any previous company law (including the 1956 Act). It is a voluntary association of persons registered under the Act, possessing a separate legal entity, perpetual succession, and common seal.
  • An 'One Person Company' (OPC) under Section 2(62) means a company which has only one member; an individual or body corporate can form and register one OPC with either a nominee resident in India (age 18+, who is not already a member/nominee of another OPC) or only himself/herself as member.
  • A 'Small Company' under Section 2(85) means a company whose paid-up share capital does not exceed ₹2 crore and turnover does not exceed ₹10 crore; it cannot be a listed company or subsidiary of a larger company.
  • A 'Public Company' under Section 2(71) means a company other than a private company, having minimum paid-up capital as prescribed and free transferability of shares.
  • A 'Private Company' under Section 2(68) means a company having a minimum paid-up capital as prescribed, restricting the right to transfer shares, limiting members to 200 (excluding employee stock holders), restricting any invitation to the public to subscribe for securities, and preventing any invitation to the public to accept deposits.
  • A 'Nidhi Company' under Section 406 read with Section 408 is a non-banking financial institution (NBFC) that cultivates the habit of thrift and savings among members, receives deposits from, and lends to, only its members; it must have at least 300 members.
  • A 'Section 8 Company' under Section 8 is a company formed with objects promoting charity, social welfare, commerce, art, science, sports, education, research, environmental protection, or any other useful purpose, with profits applied toward those objects and prohibition on distribution of dividends.
  • A 'Charge' under Section 2(16) means an interest or lien created on the property or assets of a company or any of its undertakings or both as security and includes a mortgage.
  • 'Financial assistance' under Section 73 means any financial assistance given directly or indirectly by a company for the purpose of or in connection with a purchase or subscription of shares/debentures of the company or its holding company.
  • 'Corporation' as used in ICAI context generally refers to the artificial legal person created under the Companies Act having perpetual succession and a common seal.
  • Under LLP Act, 2008, an LLP is a body corporate and a legal entity separate from its partners, with unlimited liability of partners but limited obligation of partners to the extent of their agreed contribution.
  • The IBC, 2016 consolidates and amends laws relating to reorganization and insolvency resolution of persons (individuals, firms, LLPS, companies) who are in default, to maximize availability of available assets and promote entrepreneurship.
  • SEBI is the Securities and Exchange Board of India, established in 1988 and given statutory powers via the SEBI Act, 1992; it is the regulator for securities markets in India.
  • FEMA, 1999 replaces FERA and aims to facilitate external trade and payments and promote orderly development of foreign exchange market in India.
  • SARFAESI Act, 2002 enables secured creditors (banks/financial institutions) to enforce security interests without court intervention when borrowers are in default.

Key concepts

  • Categorisation of companies: Public, Private, OPC, One-person company, Small, Nidhi, Section 8, Producer Company, Government company (§2(45)), Holding/_subsidiary (§2(46)). Key tests: number of members, transferability, invitation to public, minimum paid-up capital thresholds, and exemption/eligibility conditions.
  • Incorporation process: Promoters file SPICe+ (INC-32) with integrated application; attach DIR-3 (Director Identification), INC-7 (declaration), Memorandum (MGT-13 form) and Articles of Association. ROC issues Certificate of Incorporation bearing CIN. Promoters and first directors are liable for misstatements in incorporation documents.
  • Memorandum of Association (MOA) – fundamental charter; defines objects, liability, capital, and name clauses. Alteration of objects requires special resolution and central government confirmation. Articles of Association (AOA) – internal regulations. AOA must be consistent with MOA and the Act.
  • Prospectus (§2(70)): any document inviting offers to the public or segment of the public for subscription/purchase of securities. Must state material information; false/misleading statements attract civil and criminal liability (§447 fraud, §448 false statement). Red Herring Prospectus may be filed before final pricing.
  • Share Capital: Equity share capital (voting rights) vs. Debenture/Debt (fixed return, creditor status); rights share/compromise share/deferred share categories; difference between issued, subscribed, paid-up capital; minimum subscription and allotment rules.
  • Buy-back (§68): Company may buy back its own securities up to 25% of paid-up capital and free reserves in a financial year; requires board resolution passed at general meeting, disclosure in offer document, and deposit of consideration before completion within 1 year. Prohibited if default exists on debentures/deposits/statutory dues.
  • Distributions/Dividends (§123): Company can declare dividend only out of 'profits of the current year or previous years after providing depreciation'. Must provide 10% to Reserve before declaring dividend unless unabsorbed depreciation/debts exceed surplus. Interim dividend may be declared by Board. Unclaimed dividend transferred to Investor Education and Protection Fund (IEPF) after 7 years.
  • Meetings: AGM within 6 months of FY end, EGM on requisition (§100), Board Meeting quorum ≥ 1/3rd or 2 directors (whichever higher). Notice period: 21 clear days for general meeting, 14 days for Board. Electronic voting required for specified classes of companies.
  • Resolutions: Ordinary (simple majority), Special Resolution (≥75% votes), Resolutions requiring poll (electronic mode mandated), and Written Resolution (for single-member companies). Board resolutions passed at meetings or via circulating resolution.
  • Directors (§149-168): Minimum 2 for public, 1 for private; Maximum 15 unless shareholder approval. Independent Director criteria (§150): composition mandates for listed and certain public companies. Director KYC (§154), DIN requirement, disqualification events (§164). KMP: CEO/MD/WMD, CFO, Company Secretary.
  • Related Party Transactions (§188): All contracts/arrangements between company and related parties (directors, KMPs, relatives, subsidiaries) must be on arm's length basis; require Board/Shareholder approval depending on value and nature.
  • Corporate Social Responsibility (§135): Applicable where net worth ≥₹500 crore OR turnover ≥₹1000 crore OR net profit ≥₹5 crore during immediate preceding financial year; must spend at least 2% of average net profits of last 3 years on CSR activities; unspent amount must be transferred to a fund specified in Schedule VII.
  • Accounting & Audit: Boards must ensure compliance with Schedule III (financial statements format), Schedule VI (notes on accounts), Schedule II (depreciation). Statutory Auditor rotation (§139): audit firm 5 years, partner rotation every 5 years for listed/covered entities. Secretarial Audit (§204) required for certain classes of companies.
  • Inspection & Investigation: ROC inspection powers; Special investigation by Central Govt. (§210); Investigation by Serious Fraud Investigation Office (SFIO) under §211-A; Inspector appointment for wrongful/prejudicial conduct.
  • Minority Protection: Section 241-246 (OPPRESSION AND MISMANAGEMENT) – National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) may grant relief including regulation of conduct, purchase of shares, wind-up. Class action suits permitted under §245 for securities fraud/detrimental conduct.
  • Charges (§39-48): Creation, variation, satisfaction, registration of charge with ROC within 30 days (extendable to 60 days with fee). Charge on properties/assets creates priority over subsequent unregistered charges.
  • Winding Up: Voluntary (members' vs creditors'); Compulsory (by Tribunal on grounds: ultra vires, default in filing accounts, just and equitable). Liquidator appointed; preferential and secured creditors dealt with per priority. Dissolution follows winding up.
  • LLP (Limited Liability Partnership, 2008): Hybrid of company/partnership – separate legal entity, perpetual succession, limited liability. Designated partners (min. 2, at least 1 resident Indian). File annual return (Form 11) and statement of accounts/solvent (Form 8) annually. No requirement for AGM or board meetings strictly.
  • Partnership Act, 1932: Governs relations between partners; no separate legal entity (partners are jointly liable); registration optional but advisable; dissolution events include expiry of term, death/insolvency, unlawful purpose, court order.
  • SEBI Act, 1992: Regulates securities market; prohibits insider trading (§11B read with SEBI PIT Regulations); regulates issue and listing; enforces disclosure norms; penalizes market manipulation, fraudulent trade practices.
  • IBC, 2016 key features: Threshold for application – ₹1 lakh (now revised periodically); NCLT adjudicates corporate insolvency; CIRP (Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process) max 180 days extendable to 330 days; Resolution Professional manages affairs; ⅔ voting value of financial creditors for approval of resolution plan; liquidation follows failed CIRP.
  • FEMA (1999): Distinguishes Capital Account Transactions (need RBI permission) vs Current Account Transactions (free for bonafide purposes); regulates foreign exchange holdings, repatriation, export/import proceeds, overseas investment by residents.
  • SARFAESI (2002): Secured creditors may take possession of secured assets on default notification without court/tribunal intervention, after 60-day notice period; appeal lies to Debt Recovery Tribunal (DRT). Exceptions: residential house, agricultural land, atomic energy, exclusive bank's rights.
  • PMLA (2002): Prevents money laundering; requires reporting entities (banks, financial institutions, intermediaries) to maintain records and file suspicious transaction reports (STRs) / Cash Transaction Reports (CTRs) with FIU-IND; defines 'predicate offences' and attachment/provisional attachment powers.
  • Consumer Protection Act, 2019: Replaced 1986 Act; introduces e-commerce liability, product liability, mediation, class actions, Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA). Consumer Fora at District, State, National levels with pecuniary jurisdiction raised. Penalty for misleading ads, product defects.
  • Prevention of Money Laundering (amendments 2012, 2019, 2023): Enhanced punishment, expanded 'persons' covered, included mutual funds, portfolio managers, intermediaries as reporting entities, introduced 'enhanced due diligence'.
  • Accounting Standards & Ind AS: Relevant standards for CA Inter level include AS 1 (Disclosure of Accounting Policies), AS 2 (Inventory), AS 3 (Cash Flow Statements), AS 10 (Fixed Assets), AS 15 (Employee Benefits), AS 17 (Segment Reporting), AS 26 (Intangible Assets), AS 29 (Provisions, Contingent Liabilities and Contingent Assets), IND AS 109 (Financial Instruments), IND AS 115 (Revenue from Contracts with Customers), IND AS 116 (Leases).
  • Schedule III of Companies Act specifies format for Balance Sheet and Statement of Profit & Loss. Key line items include equity and liabilities side vs asset side classification; notes forming part of financial statements; compliance with AS/IND AS required.

Important facts

  • Companies Act 2013 received Presidential assent on 29th August 2013. Key notifications and amendments made via multiple Ordinances and Acts (Companies (Amendment) Acts of 2015, 2017, 2019, 2020, 2021).
  • Minimum paid-up capital requirement was abolished by Companies (Amendment) Act, 2019 — there is no longer a statutory minimum capital for incorporation.
  • ROC filings deadline: Annual Return (Form MGT-7) and Financial Statements (Form AOC-4) must be filed within 60 days of AGM date; AGM must be held within 6 months of FY end.
  • Director Identification Number (DIN) and Digital Signature Certificate (DSC) are mandatory for electronic filing. DIN application now integrated via SPICe+.
  • Independent Directors: At least 1/3rd of total directors for listed public companies; women director requirement for prescribed classes; term max 2 terms of 5 years each; reappointment permitted only if 1/4th of shareholders pass resolution after specific disclosure.
  • CSR qualifying thresholds and spending: Net worth ≥₹500 cr / Turnover ≥₹1000 cr / Net profit ≥₹5 cr → spend 2% of avg net profit of last 3 yrs. CSR committee of 3+ directors, at least 1 independent director.
  • Section 7 – Name reservation via RUN (now integrated into SPICe+); Section 8 – License for charitable objects; Section 25 (old) → Section 8 now.
  • Penalty for fraud under Section 447: Imprisonment min. 6 months extendable to 10 years + fine min. amount involved extendable to 3 times.
  • Section 448 False statement penalty: Imprisonment up to 3 years + fine up to ₹1 lakh.
  • Section 449 False, misleading statement in document penalty: Imprisonment up to 3 years + fine up to ₹1 lakh.
  • Nidhi Company must comply with Nidhi Rules 2014: net owned funds ≥₹10 cr, 90% of assets in liquid forms, membership norms, borrowing/lending restrictions to members only.
  • Section 43 – Types of shares: Equity shares with different voting rights, Equity shares with differential voting rights (EVR), Compromise/Redeemable preference shares.
  • Red Herring Prospectus (§39): Initial prospectus filed without details of price or quantum of securities; final pricing after book-building or offer process.
  • E-IPO / E-Rights: Offers via electronic mode allowed; compliance with SEBI ICDR Regulations mandatory.
  • Section 73 – Acceptance of deposits: Companies cannot accept deposits from public except through specified channels; non-compliance attracts penalty and possible prosecution.
  • Charge registration – time limits: Creation/Variation/Satisfaction within 30 days from date of creation/variation/satisfaction. Extension up to 60 days on payment of additional fees.
  • Compromise Arrangement (§230-232): Apply to NCLT for scheme sanction; requires ⅔ value of creditors/shareholders present and voting; binding on all classes once sanctioned.
  • Merger/Amalgamation under §232: Transfer of undertaking, property, liabilities; may provide for issue of shares, debentures, cash etc.; must comply with SEBI regulations if listed.
  • Scheme of Arrangement requires ⅔ majority by value of each class of creditors/shareholders and by head-count; Court/Tribunal approval binds all.
  • Section 210 – Investigation of affairs of company: Central Government may order investigation if affairs are being conducted fraudulently or prejudicially to public interest.
  • SFIO (§211-A): Investigates serious/fradulent cases involving listed companies/public interest; Director can direct investigation.
  • Section 245 Class Action Suits: 100 members or 10% of total members (whichever is lower) can file suit against company/promoters/directors for fraudulent, illegal or oppressive acts; for listed companies, any single member can file.
  • Section 241-246 Oppression & Mismanagement: NCLT has wide powers to provide remedies including regulation of conduct, purchase of shares by company or other members, winding up.
  • Section 392 Tribunal powers on oppression/mismanagement applications to wind up company or make such orders as deemed just and equitable.
  • Section 139 Audit: Statutory auditor appointed at first AGM; term 5 years; rotational mandatory for listed and certain other companies; auditor must report if he/she is disqualified.
  • Section 204 Secretarial Audit: Required for listed companies and other prescribed classes; Secretarial Auditor reports on compliance with corporate governance norms; to be attached with Board's Report.
  • Section 128 Books of Accounts: Must be kept at registered office; foreign branch/company must send periodical returns; maintenance in electronic form permitted.
  • Section 129 Financial Statements: Every company must prepare financial statements (Balance Sheet, Profit & Loss, Cash Flow, Statement of Changes in Equity, Notes). For holding company, consolidated financial statements also required.
  • Section 133 Accounting Standards: Central Govt notifies AS; every company must follow AS/IND AS as applicable.
  • Section 143 Auditor's duties: To state whether accounts give true and fair view; to report on compliance with accounting standards; to report on adequacy of internal financial controls; to certify tax deductibility of expenses where applicable.
  • Section 177 Audit Committee: Listed companies and prescribed classes must constitute Audit Committee; min. 3 directors, majority independent, chairperson independent.
  • Section 178 Nomination and Remuneration Committee: Composed of independent directors; decides remuneration policy for directors/KMP.
  • Section 180 Borrowing powers: Board may borrow moneys up to 5x paid-up capital + free reserves unless shareholders authorize higher limit.
  • Section 185 Loans to directors/related parties: Strict prohibition with narrow exceptions; violation is criminal offence.
  • Section 186 Lending/Investments: Company can lend/invest only up to limits prescribed; need special resolution for investment exceeding norms.
  • Section 197 Remuneration of Managing Director, Whole-time Director, Manager: Overall maximum managerial remuneration is 11% of net profits; exceeds limit needs approval.
  • Section 203 Company Secretary: Required for listed companies and other prescribed classes; whole-time CS if turnover/net worth above thresholds.
  • Section 212 Holding-Subsidiary accounts: Holding company must disclose its interest in subsidiary; cross-holding restrictions exist.
  • Section 33 Company Seal: Compulsory for companies limited by shares unless exempted; must have company's name engraved; affixing is authentication of documents.
  • Section 395 Rectification of Register of Members: Tribunal may order rectification if entry wrongly made or omitted.
  • Section 444 Default penalty: Punishment for default in furnishing returns/accounts/document — imprisonment up to 3 years and/or fine.
  • Section 454 Contempt of Tribunal: Tribunal powers to punish for contempt under Contempt of Courts Act.
  • IBC – NCLT Jurisdiction: Appellate authority is NCLAT; further appeal to Supreme Court on question of law only.
  • IBC – 2019 Amendment: Time-limit extension allowed up to 1 year with cost; voluntary liquidation introduced; pre-packaged insolvency for MSMEs; resolution professional eligibility relaxed.
  • IBC – 2021 Amendment: Homebuyers treated as financial creditors; cross-class cramdown permitted; resolution plan implemention timeline enforced; withdrawal of application permitted before approval.
  • FEMA – RBI Approval Routes: Automatic Route (no prior approval) vs Government Route (prior approval required); FDI sectoral caps and conditions apply.
  • FEMA – External Commercial Borrowings (ECBs): Minimum average maturity, prudent eligibility, eligible end-use notified by RBI.
  • SARFAESI – Appeal: Borrower can appeal to DRT within 45 days of measure taken; stay of possession pending appeal must be granted on sufficient cause shown.
  • PMLA – Reporting Entities Obligation: Maintain records for 5 years; report suspicious transactions to FIU-IND within 7 working days.
  • Consumer Protection Act 2019 – CCPA: Central Consumer Protection Authority may recall products, impose penalties up to ₹10 lakh and imprisonment up to 2 years for false advertising/manufacturing defects; class actions allowed.
  • Partnership Act – Registration: Not compulsory but unregistered firm cannot sue third parties or partners; dissolution events include death/insolvency, court order, satisfaction of object, expiry, mutual agreement.
  • LLP – Designated Partners Liability: Unlimited liability for debts arising from willful misconduct/fraud; otherwise limited to agreed contribution.
  • LLP Agreement: Must be filed in Form 3 within 30 days of incorporation; governs mutual rights/duties of partners.
  • Schedule VIII – Objects of Section 8 Company: Cannot distribute dividends; must apply income toward objects; prohibition on remuneration to members except cost reimbursement.
  • Company Name Guidelines: Must not be identical/deceptively similar to existing name; must not contain words restricted without approval (e.g., 'Bank', 'Stock Exchange', 'National'); Hindi names permitted.
  • Registered Office: Company must commence business within 30 days of incorporation; file CON-20 confirming registered office address; failure attracts daily penalty.
  • EPFO/ESIC: Mandatory where employee strength crosses prescribed threshold; contributions due within prescribed timelines.
  • GST: CA Inter syllabus may touch upon indirect taxes but detailed GST study belongs to Indirect Tax Laws paper; basic awareness of GST applicability on goods/services supplied by company is useful.
  • Companies Act 2013 and old Act 1956 cross-references: Many exam questions compare provisions — know key differences like minimum capital (abolished), auditor tenure, independent director norms, CSR mandate.
  • Relevant case laws commonly cited: Salomon v Salomon & Co (separate entity doctrine); Foss v Harbottle (proper plaintiff rule); State of UP v Babu Ram Upadhya (statutory interpretation).
  • NCLT/NCLAT jurisprudence basics: NCLT has original jurisdiction over company matters; NCLAT is appellate tribunal; both follow principles of natural justice and can pass interim orders.

Memory aids

  • Remember 'COMPANY' acronym: C- Certificate of Incorporation, O- Objects clause, M- Memorandum, P- Prospectus, A- Articles, N- Names/Number of members, Y- Years/Perpetual succession.
  • For shareholders' meetings: Use 'GA-ME' mnemonic — General Meeting = AGM/EGM/EVM. For Board: 'BOA-D' → Board = Oath of fiduciary duty.
  • Classify companies with 'P-P-O-S-N-G': Private, Public, One-person, Small, Nidhi, Government — each with unique features and thresholds.
  • Remember penalties: Fraud = 6-10 yrs + 1-3x amount; False statement = up to 3 yrs + ₹1L; Use 'Fraud is harsher' heuristic.
  • CSR threshold: '500-1000-5' (Net Worth 500cr / Turnover 1000cr / Profit 5cr) → Spend 2%. Easy numeric anchor.
  • Buy-back formula memory aid: Max 25% of Paid-up Capital + Free Reserves in a FY; completed within 1 year; consideration deposited before completion.
  • Independent Director rules: 1/3rd for listed co's; max 2 boards + 1 audit committee; term 2×5 yrs; reappointment via special resolution only.
  • Meeting timings: AGM = 6 months from FY-end; Board met minimum 4 times/year (gap ≤ 120 days); EGM on requisition within 21 days.
  • IBC sequence memory: Default → Application to NCLT → Moratorium → RP appointed → CIRP (180+150 days) → Resolution Plan approved/rejected → Liquidation if failed.
  • FEMA vs FERA contrast: FERA was restrictive and punitive (1973); FEMA is facilitative and corrective (1999) — remember the paradigm shift from control to facilitation.
  • SARFAESI timeline: 60-day notice → Possession → Appeal to DRT within 45 days → Stay possible.
  • Charge registration steps: Creation → 30-day filing → Extension up to 60 days → Satisfaction filing.
  • LLP vs Partnership comparison: LLP = separate entity, limited liability, perpetual succession, governed by LLP Act; Partnership = no separate entity, joint/several liability, governed by Partnership Act 1932.
  • SEBI functions mnemonic: 'REGULATE' → Regulate, Evaluate, Guide, Audit, Legal enforcement, Uphold transparency, Guard investors, Educate, Lead market integrity.
  • Key forms quick list: INC-32 (SPICe+), INC-7 (Declaration), MGT-7 (Annual Return), AOC-4 (Financial Statements), Form 3 (LLP Agreement), Form 8 (Statement of Accounts), Form 11 (Annual Return for LLP), CHG-4 (Satisfaction of Charge), CHG-1 (Creation of Charge).
  • Compare CA 2013 vs CA 1956: Key changes — minimum capital removed, independent directors mandated, CSR made mandatory, corporate governance strengthened, NCLT/Tribunals introduced, SFIO created.
  • Oppression/Mismanagement test: 'Just and equitable' ground; look for breach of legitimate expectations, exclusion from management, diversion of assets, non-payment of dividends despite profits.
  • Related party transaction test: Ask 'does this benefit a related party beyond arm's length?' If yes → requires approval under §188; record considerations in Board minutes.
  • Wrongful trading / Fraud distinction: Wrongful trading = carrying on business when director knew/should have known insolvent; Fraud = intentional deception for gain — heavier penalty.
  • Insolvency vs Bankruptcy memory: Insolvency = inability to pay debts as they fall due; Bankruptcy = formal declaration by court leading to liquidation of assets. IBC merges both concepts for corporates.
  • Current vs Capital account transactions: Current = trade payments, dividends, maintenance, travel; Capital = investment, loans, property acquisition. Current = mostly free; Capital = RBI permission needed.
  • Nidhi company special feature: Member-only deposits/lending; cannot accept deposits from public at large; 90% liquid assets norm.
  • Section 8 company special feature: No dividend; profits applied to objects; license from Central Govt; similar to trust but with corporate structure.

Exam tips

  • For CA Inter Corporate and Other Laws, focus on NUMBERED SECTIONS — ICAI explicitly tests section numbers and their content; always quote section numbers when writing answers.
  • Practice writing answers in FORMATTED paragraphs with headings: Issue → Law (cite section) → Application → Conclusion. This matches ICAI answer presentation expectations.
  • Make a table comparing: Private vs Public Company, Company vs LLP, Partnership vs LLP, CA 1956 vs CA 2013, IBC vs SARFAESI — these comparative formats are frequently asked.
  • Memorize key numerical thresholds: 200 members (private), ₹2 cr capital/₹10 cr turnover (small co), 300 members (nidhi), 25% buy-back limit, 2% CSR spend, 7 years IEPF, 60-day charge filing.
  • When writing about NCLT/NCLAT, distinguish between their jurisdictions clearly. Use phrases like 'The application shall be filed before the Principal Bench at...' for precision.
  • For case law questions, know landmark judgments briefly: Salomon v Salomon (separate legal personality), Foss v Harbottle (proper plaintiff), Veena Enterprises v Union of India (insolvency).
  • Always mention the PENALTY provision when discussing an offence — ICAI examiners expect you to state 'punishable with imprisonment up to X years and/or fine up to ₹Y'.
  • Use flowcharts/diagrams for processes like: Incorporation → Prospectus → Allotment → Certification; Winding Up → Voluntary/Compulsory; IBC CIRP stages — these fetch better marks.
  • For numerical problems (e.g., buy-back eligibility, CSR computation, dividend distribution), show step-by-step calculation even if brief.
  • Current Affairs integration: Refer to latest amendments (Companies Amendment Acts 2019, 2020, 2021) and recent ICAI guidance notes when relevant; update your preparation accordingly.
  • Time management in exam: Section A (objective) requires speed — practice MCQs; Section B (descriptive) requires structured answer writing — practice at least 5 full-length answers weekly.
  • Revise the SCHEDULES — Schedule I (forms), Schedule III (financial statement format), Schedule IV (SEZ), Schedule V (independent director), Schedule VI (disclosures), Schedule VIII (Section 8 objects), Schedule XIV (remuneration).
  • Common exam traps to avoid: Don't confuse provisions of Companies Act with SEBI regulations or IBC — maintain clear subject boundaries in answers.
  • Write 'As per Section ___ of the Companies Act, 2013' explicitly — never write generic statements without legal backing.
  • Practice past year ICAI questions — the examiner style values direct application of law rather than theoretical exposition.
  • For topic-test or chapter-test practice, mix theoretical questions with situational/problem-based questions to match actual exam pattern.
  • If asked about 'oppression and mismanagement', structure answer: Definition → Grounds → Remedies → Case law → Conclusion. This ensures comprehensive coverage.
  • Be familiar with the distinction between 'fraud' under Section 447 and 'wrongful trading' — different sections, different penalties.
  • Note recent digital initiatives: SPICe+, e-Forms, MCA21 portal — these are frequently tested in objective sections.

Common traps

  • Confusing Private Company member limit (200) with Employee Stock Holder exclusion — ESOP holders are excluded from the 200 count; remember this exception.
  • Misremembering that Section 8 companies can pay dividends — they CANNOT; all profits must be applied to charitable objects.
  • Thinking Minimum Paid-up Capital still exists — it was ABOLISHED by the 2019 Amendment; do not cite any minimum capital requirement post-2019.
  • Confusing buy-back under Section 68 with share buy-back by listed companies under SEBI buyback regulations — Companies Act rule applies generally; SEBI rules additionally apply to listed companies.
  • Mixing up Special Resolution (75%) vs Ordinary Resolution (simple majority) — remember: alteration of MOA, reduction of capital, buy-back, removal of auditor, change of name require special resolution.
  • Assuming Partnership firm is a separate legal entity — it is NOT; partners are jointly and severally liable. LLP IS a separate entity.
  • Forgetting that LLP designated partners have unlimited liability in case of willful misconduct/fraud — limited liability protection is not absolute.
  • Misapplying Section 185 (loans to directors) to partnerships — Section 185 applies only to companies, not partnership firms.
  • Confusing NCLT original jurisdiction with NCLAT appellate jurisdiction — NCLT hears first instance; NCLAT hears appeals.
  • Assuming all companies must appoint Whole-Time Company Secretary — only listed and prescribed classes; smaller companies may not need WTS CS.
  • Forgetting that auditor rotation applies only to listed companies and certain large public companies — small/private companies are exempt from rotational auditor requirement.
  • Confusing IBC applicability — IBC applies to companies, LLPs, partnership firms, and individuals; but the corporate insolvency resolution process (CIRP) is only for corporate persons.
  • Misunderstanding SARFAESI scope — it applies only to secured creditors (banks/FIs); unsecured creditors cannot invoke it.
  • Assuming consumer forum has unlimited jurisdiction — there are strict pecuniary limits: District (up to ₹50 lakhs), State (₹50L-₹2 Cr), National (above ₹2 Cr).
  • Confusing 'Fraud' definition under Section 447 with common law fraud — s.447 includes concealment of facts, deliberate evasion, breach of duty causing loss, and abetment/conspiracy.
  • Forgetting that Nidhi companies must obtain certification from NPC (Nidhi Regulatory Council) and comply with Nidhi Rules 2014 separately.
  • Misreading 'minority shareholder' rights — any member can move NCLT under §241 but certain thresholds apply for class action suits under §245.
  • Confusing IEPF transfer timing — dividend remains unclaimed for 7 YEARS before transfer to IEPF, not 5 years.
  • Assuming all charges must be registered with ROC — charges created in favour of certain authorities (like statutory corporations) may have different registration rules.
  • Misunderstanding CSR spending — if company doesn't meet 2% spending, it must explain reasons in Board Report; unspent amount goes to specified fund ONLY if 2% threshold met and project not undertaken; transitional provisions exist.
  • Forgetting that financial statements must include Directors' Report, Auditor's Report, Board's Report on CSR — these are integral parts.
  • Misinterpreting SEBI's power over unlisted companies — SEBI's direct regulation primarily covers listed companies and public issues; private placements have lighter regulation.
  • Assuming LLP has minimum designation requirement of 2 — correct, but if only 2 designated partners and one dies/resigns, LLP must appoint new designated partner within 6 months.
  • Forgetting that Companies Act allows e-voting for general meetings for specified classes of companies — this is mandatory, not optional.
