Definitions

  • Partnership Firm: A legal relationship between two or more persons who agree to share the profits of a business carried on by all or any of them acting for all (Partnership Act, 1932). Governed by Sections 4–45.
  • Goodwill: The intangible right that enables a business to earn super profits above the normal rate of return on capital employed. It represents the location advantage, brand reputation, customer loyalty, and other non-physical assets.
  • Super Profit: The excess of actual average profit over the normal profit. Normal Profit = Capital Employed × Normal Rate of Return.
  • Capital Employed: Total assets minus external liabilities (or Shareholders' Funds = Equity Share Capital + Reserves & Surplus − Long-term Borrowings).
  • Buy-back of Shares: A company may buy back its own shares up to 25% of its total paid-up equity capital in a financial year (Section 68, Companies Act, 2013).
  • Surrender of Rights: When a partner surrenders part or全部of their share of goodwill in favor of other partners at the time of admission, retirement, or death, it is called surrender of rights.
  • Consignment: A shipment of goods by the consignor (principal) to the consignee (agent) who sells the goods on behalf of the consignor on commission basis.
  • Joint Venture: A temporary partnership formed for a particular project or undertaking where parties contribute capital and share profits/losses according to an agreed ratio.
  • Branch Accounting: A system of accounting where transactions of a branch are recorded separately from the head office, typically using debit/credit method or stock and debtors system.
  • Hire Purchase Agreement: An arrangement where goods are delivered to the buyer who pays in installments; ownership passes only upon payment of the final installment.
  • Installment System: Similar to hire purchase except that the seller retains no title over the goods after delivery; the buyer becomes the owner immediately but pays in installments.
  • Depreciation: The systematic allocation of the depreciable amount of an asset over its useful life. Not applicable to freehold land.
  • Revaluation Account: An nominal account opened at the time of admission, retirement, or death of a partner to record gains/losses on revaluation of assets and liabilities.
  • Partner's Current Account: Maintained under the floating capital method to adjust for drawings, interest on drawings, share of profit, salary, etc., which vary annually.

Key concepts

  • GOODWILL VALUATION METHODS: (a) Average Profit Method: Goodwill = No. of years' purchase × Average Profit. Variants: Simple average, Weighted average, Super profit method. (b) Capitalisation Method: Goodwill = Capitalised value of business − Net assets. Capitalised value = Average Profit ÷ Normal Rate of Return. Variants: Using average profit, using super profit.
  • CHANGE IN PROFIT SHARING RATIO: When partners change their sharing ratio, the gaining partner compensates the sacrificing partner for goodwill. The value of goodwill is calculated and adjusted through partners' capital/current accounts in the gaining ratio. No journal entry is passed if partners adjust among themselves.
  • ADMISSION OF A PARTNER — KEY JOURNAL ENTRIES: (1) Goodwill adjustment: New Partner's Goodwill A/c Dr. To Old Partners' Capital A/cs (in sacrificing ratio). Gaining Partner's Capital A/c Dr. To Old Goodwill A/c. (2) Revaluation: Assets appreciating credited to Revaluation A/c; liabilities increasing debited; accumulated reserves/losses transferred to old partners' capitals in old ratio. (3) Capital adjustment: New partner's capital = Total capital of firm × New profit-sharing ratio. Adjustment made through capital or current account.
  • REVALUATION ACCOUNT TREATMENT ON ADMISSION: Credits — Increase in value of assets, Unknown liabilities, Workmen Compensation Reserve, General Reserve, Profit & Loss Credit Balance. Debits — Decrease in value of assets, Creation of provision for liabilities, Proposed dividends, Accumulated losses, Bad debts written off. Closing balance transferred to old partners' capital accounts in OLD profit-sharing ratio.
  • RETIREMENT OF A PARTNER — GAINING RATIO: Gaining Ratio = New Ratio − Old Ratio (of continuing partners). If not given, it is assumed to be the same as the new profit-sharing ratio among continuing partners.
  • DEATH OF A PARTNER — CLAIMS OF DECEASED PARTNER: (1) Capital standing to credit — paid along with interest from date of death to date of payment @ agreed rate (usually 12%). (2) Share of profit up to date of death — calculated on proportionate basis (sales/profit method). (3) Goodwill — credited at agreed value. (4) Accumulated reserves/profits — distributed in old ratio. (5) Interest on capital — for the period. (6) Salary/Commission — up to date of death. (7) Loss if any — debited to legal heirs' claim A/c.
  • Dissolution of Firm: Complete stoppage of business. All assets realised, liabilities paid. Realisation A/c prepared. If assets insufficient, Deficiency A/c opened. Each partner entitled to share surplus in sharing ratio. Partner's loan treated at par with outside liabilities unless otherwise agreed.
  • CONSIGNMENT ACCOUNT — KEY POINTS: (a) Consignor prepares Consignment A/c (similar to trading a/c). (b) Consignee prepares Consignee A/c (personal account). (c) Goods sent on approval basis are not consignment. (d) Normal ab/n losses are debited to Consignment A/c and reduce sale proceeds. (e) Abnormal loss (due to fire/theft) is shown at actual cost or market price whichever is lower in Balance Sheet. (f) Commission: Ordinary commission @ agreed % on turnover; Del Credere commission @ additional % for guaranteeing credit sales.
  • JOINT VENTURE ACCOUNTING: Two methods — (a) Separate Books: Each venturer maintains their own books; a Joint Venture A/c and Joint Venturers A/c are opened. (b) No Separate Books: Each venturer records transactions in their own books; a Joint Bank A/c or Joint Purchase/Sales A/c is maintained.
  • BRANCH ACCOUNTING — DEBIT/CREDIT METHOD: Head Office sends goods to branch at cost/invoice price. Branch remits cash to HO. HO maintains Branch A/c as nominal account. Branch maintains HO A/c as personal account. Closing entries transfer gross profit/loss to P&L.
  • BRANCH ACCOUNTING — STOCK AND DEBTORS METHOD: Used when branch is self-contained. HO maintains: (a) Branch Stock A/c, (b) Branch Debtors A/c, (c) Branch A/c. Goods sent to branch at cost plus percentage markup (CDR — Cost to Invoice Ratio = Cost/Invoice Price). Stock reserved at invoice price is adjusted for gross profit.
  • HIR PURCHASE: Seller retains ownership until full payment. On default, seller can repossess goods. Repo price less than outstanding balance — loss to hirer; repo price more than outstanding — gain to hirer. Installment method: Buyer owns goods from start; no repossession right on default — only recovery of arrears.
  • DEPRECIATION METHODS: (a) Straight Line Method: Equal annual charge; book value never reaches zero. (b) Diminishing Balance Method: Charge reduces every year; based on book value. Book value approaches zero but never reaches. Useful for assets with high repair costs over time.
  • PARTNERSHIP ACT PROVISIONS (WHERE NO AGREEMENT EXISTS): (a) Interest on capital: Nil. (b) Interest on drawings: Nil. (c) Partner's salary: Nil. (d) Profit sharing: Equal. (e) Interest on partner's loan: 6% p.a. (f) No limit on number of partners: 50.
  • COMPANY SHARE CAPITAL — BUYBACK CONDITIONS: (a) authorised by articles. (b) special resolution. (c) buyback ≤ 25% of paid-up capital in a year. (d) debt/equity ratio ≤ 2:1 after buyback. (e) all shares fully paid-up. (f) one year gap between two buybacks. (g) funds from free reserves/securities premium/shares bought-back proceeds.
  • ACCOUNTING STANDARDS RELEVANT TO ADVANCED ACCOUNTING: AS 26 (Intangible Assets), AS 10 (Property Plant & Equipment), AS 17 (Segment Reporting), AS 7 (Construction Contracts), AS 9 (Revenue Recognition), AS 29 (Provisions, Contingent Liabilities & Contingent Assets), AS 30/31/32 (Financial Instruments — Recognition & Disclosure).

Important facts

  • Revaluation profit/loss is distributed among OLD partners in OLD profit-sharing ratio on date of change.
  • Goodwill of a firm is NEVER shown in the balance sheet unless it has been purchased (as per Partnership Act provisions and ICAI guidance).
  • In consignment, carriage inwards is borne by consignor; carriage outwards is borne by consignee unless otherwise agreed.
  • Del Credere commission is calculated on credit sales only, not cash sales.
  • In joint venture, if accounts are maintained separately, profit is shared in the agreed ratio, not necessarily equally.
  • Under the debtors method of branch accounting, opening debtors are shown at invoice price and a provision for bad debts is created.
  • Goods sent on approval basis are NOT treated as consignment; they are treated as a sale or return transaction.
  • On retirement, the retiring partner's share of goodwill is adjusted against the continuing partners' capitals in their gaining ratio.
  • In hire purchase, the hirer is treated as the owner for accounting purposes but not for legal purposes until final payment.
  • Section 56(2)(x) of Income Tax Act: Any sum received without consideration exceeding ₹50,000 is taxable as income from other sources in the hands of the recipient.
  • Company may issue shares at a premium but not at a discount (Section 53, Companies Act, 2013 — except CSBP shares under specific conditions).
  • Free reserves are those not created for any specific purpose and are available for distribution as dividends (Section 2(45), Companies Act, 2013).
  • Securities premium account can be used for: (a) writing off preliminary expenses, (b) writing off commission/discount on shares/debentures, (c) providing premium on redemption of debentures/shares, (d) issuance of fully paid bonus shares (Section 52).
  • In the diminishing balance method, depreciation is always charged on the written down value of the asset, not the original cost.
  • A contingent liability is disclosed in notes to accounts but not provided for (AS 29). A provision is recognised when there is a present obligation and reliable estimate is possible.
  • In branch accounting, if goods are sent to branch at cost plus 25% on sales (i.e., CDR = 4/5), then gross profit included in closing stock at invoice price = 1/4 of stock value.
  • Redemption of preference shares can be funded from: (a) fresh issue of shares, (b) free reserves (capital reserve cannot be used), (c) securities premium account.
  • Under AS 26, internally generated goodwill, brand names, publishing titles, and customer lists shall NOT be recognised as intangible assets.
  • For consignment, if normal wastage occurs, the cost of lost goods is spread over the remaining goods by reducing the cost per unit.
  • In a partnership deed, if profit-sharing ratio is not specified, it is presumed to be equal regardless of capital contribution differences.

Memory aids

  • Admission Checklist Mnemonic: G.R.C. — Goodwill, Revaluation, Capital. These three adjustments must always be made in sequence during admission.
  • Retirement/D Death Checklist: C.G.P.S.L. — Capital, Goodwill, Profit/Reserves, Salary/Commission, Loan. Remember each claim of the retiring/deceased partner.
  • Consignment Commission Trick: O.D. — Ordinary on turnover, Del Credere on credit sales. Think: 'Ordinary deals with everything, Del Credere deals with debtors only.'
  • Buyback Conditions Mnemonic: S.A.F.E. — Special Resolution, Articles allowed, Funds from specified sources, Equity ratio 2:1 maintained.
  • Depreciation Choice: 'SLM for stable assets (buildings); WDV for fast-obsolescing assets (machinery, vehicles).' Think of a car losing most value in first few years → WDV is appropriate.
  • Branch Methods: 'DC for simple branches (direct records); SD for independent branches (stock & debtors maintain).' D-C, S-D — match the sounds.
  • Joint Venture vs Partnership: JV is temporary, for a specific project, no separate legal entity necessarily; Partnership is ongoing, mutual agency exists.
  • Goodwill Valuation Quick Rule: 'Years' purchase means multiply profits; Capitalisation means divide by rate.' SP ÷ R% = Capitalised Value.
  • Revaluation Account: 'Gain goes to credit (increase in asset, decrease in liability); Loss goes to debit (decrease in asset, increase in liability).'

Exam tips

  • In CA Inter exam, always show workings clearly. Even if the final answer is wrong, step marks are awarded for correct methodology.
  • For Goodwill valuation problems, always mention the method used (Average Profit / Super Profit / Capitalisation) and write the formula before substituting values.
  • In Admission/Retirement/Dissolution problems, prepare a checklist: Goodwill → Revaluation → Partners' Capitals → New Balance Sheet. Follow this sequence to avoid omission.
  • For Consignment problems, always prepare both Consignment A/c and Consignee A/c. Marks are allocated for both even if one has a minor error.
  • In Branch Accounting, remember that under the Debtors Method, you need three accounts: Branch Stock, Branch Debtors, and Branch A/c. All three must tally.
  • When dealing with depreciation changes (e.g., switching from SLM to WDV), treat it as a change in accounting estimate (AS 5) and apply prospectively — do not restate prior years.
  • In Company Accounts for buy-back, ensure you show the Journal entries for: (a) declaration, (b) payment, (c) cancellation of shares, (d) transfer to Capital Redemption Reserve if applicable.
  • For Share Buyback under Section 68, remember that the company must reduce the subscribed capital by the number of shares bought back and cancel them within 7 days.
  • In Joint Venture problems, if separate books are maintained, each venturer's personal account should tally with the Joint Venture A/c balance.
  • Always check whether the question requires preparation of a Revaluation Account or simply adjustment entries. The marking scheme differs significantly.
  • For Retirement/Death problems, calculate the deceased partner's share of profit using either the Sales Method or Time Method — specify which one you use.
  • In dissolutions, always remember to transfer the balance of Profit & Loss Account (before revaluation) to the old partners' capital accounts in their old ratio.
  • When solving hire purchase / installment problems, construct an analysis showing the split between principal and interest for each installment — this earns full marks.
  • Remember that in CA Inter, presentation matters. Use proper headings, sub-headings, and formatting in your answers. Clean presentation can earn extra courtesy marks.
  • For statutory references (Sections of Companies Act, Partnership Act, ICAI standards), mention the section numbers where possible — it demonstrates depth and fetches additional marks.

Common traps

  • Trap: Distributing accumulated reserves (like Workmen Compensation Reserve, General Reserve) in the NEW profit-sharing ratio during admission. CORRECT: They are always distributed in the OLD ratio among old partners.
  • Trap: Calculating good will on super profit but then applying it to the new partner's capital ratio instead of the sacrificing ratio. CORRECT: Goodwill adjustment between old and new partner is ALWAYS in the sacrificing/gaining ratio.
  • Trap: Treating abnormal loss in consignment at cost in the Trading Account. CORRECT: Abnormal loss is shown at cost OR market price whichever is LOWER directly in the Balance Sheet as an asset, not in the P&L.
  • Trap: Charging depreciation on the original cost every year under Diminishing Balance Method. CORRECT: D.B. method charges depreciation on the WRITTEN DOWN VALUE, not original cost.
  • Trap: Adjusting the retiring partner's goodwill share against their capital account directly. CORRECT: It is adjusted against the CONTINUING partners' capital accounts in the gaining ratio.
  • Trap: Forgetting to debit the Realisation A/c for partner's loan when dissolving a firm. CORRECT: Partner's loan is shown on the liabilities side of the balance sheet and must be settled before distributing surplus to partners.
  • Trap: Including Securities Premium in Free Reserves for funding share buyback. CORRECT: Securities Premium has its own restricted under Section 52; it cannot随意 be used for buyback unless specifically permitted.
  • Trap: Treating hire purchase instalments as revenue expenditure. CORRECT: Each instalment contains a principal component (reduces liability) and an interest component (revenue expense). Only the interest portion is P&L charge.
  • Trap: Using Capital Reserve to redeem preference shares. CORRECT: Capital Reserve CANNOT be used for redemption of preference shares. Only Free Reserves, fresh issue proceeds, or securities premium can be used.
  • Trap: Recording Goods Sent on Approval as Consignment A/c. CORRECT: Goods sent on approval are recorded in Sale/Purchase account, not Consignment A/c.
  • Trap: Forgetting that in the Stock and Debtors Method, the Branch A/c acts as a balancing account while Branch Stock and Branch Debtors are real accounts maintained separately.
  • Trap: Calculating gross profit on closing stock at cost price instead of invoice price in branch accounting. CORRECT: Gross profit on closing stock must be calculated at INVOICE PRICE, not cost.
  • Trap: Assuming that depreciation under SLM will reduce the asset's book value to zero. CORRECT: Under SLM, the asset retains a residual/scrap value which is NEVER fully depreciated unless it is nil.
  • Trap: In joint venture, forgetting to close the joint venture account with a profit/loss balancing figure. CORRECT: JV account is always closed by transferring profit/loss to Joint Venturers' A/cs in the agreed ratio.
