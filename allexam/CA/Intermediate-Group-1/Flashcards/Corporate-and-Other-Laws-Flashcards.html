Corporate and Other Laws - CA Intermediate Flashcards

CAEnglishFlashcards

Card 1

What are the main objects of the Companies Act, 2013?

1) To consolidate laws relating to companies and other forms of associations. 2) To provide for incorporation of companies with limited liability. 3) To regulate the activities of incorporated companies. 4) To promote their participation in economic activities. 5) To ensure their accountability through effective governance.

Card 2

Define 'Company' as per Section 2(20) of Companies Act, 2013.

A company means a company incorporated under this Act or under any previous company law. Key points: - It must be a juristic person separate from its members. - It is an artificial person created by law. - It has perpetual succession. - It has a common seal. - It can sue and be sued in its own name.

Card 3

What is the minimum number of persons required to form a Private Company?

Section 2(68): A private company means a company having a minimum paid-up share capital as may be prescribed, and which by its articles: 1) Restricts the right to transfer its shares. 2) Limits the number of its members to 200 (excluding present and past employees). 3) Prohibits any invitation to the public to subscribe for securities. Minimum members: 2 (one more than Public Company's requirement of 7).

Card 4

What is the maximum number of members allowed in a Private Company?

Section 2(68) read with Rule 3 of Companies (Restriction on Number of Members) Rules, 2014: Maximum members: 200 Exceptions (not counted towards limit): - Persons who are in employment of the company. - Persons who were formerly in employment and were members while in employment. - Trustes of a ESOP scheme.

Card 5

Define 'Public Company' as per Section 2(71).

A public company means a company other than a private company, having: - Minimum 7 members (no maximum limit). - No restrictions on transfer of shares. - Right to invite public to subscribe for its securities. Key features: - Can list on stock exchange. - Must appoint at least 3 directors. - Must hold AGM annually. - Must file annual returns with ROC.

Card 6

What is a One Person Company (OPC) under Section 2(62)?

A One Person Company means a private company which has only one member. Conditions: - Natural person who is an Indian citizen and resident (resided in India for 180+ days in preceding calendar year). - Only one natural person can form OPC. - Can't convert into public company if paid-up capital exceeds ₹50 lakh or turnover exceeds ₹2 crore. - Nominee must be appointed at incorporation. Benefits: - Separate legal entity. - Limited liability. - No need for min 7 members or 3 directors.

Card 7

What are the different types of companies under Section 2(22) and related provisions?

1) Company limited by shares - liability limited to amount unpaid on shares held. 2) Company limited by guarantee - members guarantee to contribute amount on winding up. 3) Unlimited company - no limit on liability of members. 4) Public company - no restriction on share transfer/membership. 5) Private company - restrictions on transfer, membership ≤200, no public invitation. 6) One Person Company - single member. 7) Section 8 Company - formed for charitable objects. 8) Holding/Subsidiary Company - relationship between companies.

Card 8

What is a Holding Company vs Subsidiary Company?

Section 2(46) - Holding Company: A company is holding company w.r.t. another when it controls the composition of board or determines main actions of that company. Section 2(87) - Subsidiary Company: A company is subsidiary when: - Holding company holds >50% voting power OR - Controls composition of board OR - Right to appoint/remove majority of directors Exception: Subsidiary of subsidiary not deemed subsidiary of holding company.

Card 9

What are the conditions for registration of a company?

Section 7 conditions: 1) Memorandum of Association (MoA) 2) Articles of Association (AoA) 3) Consent of persons to act as directors (Form INC-11) 4) Details of directors/prompters (Form INC-12) 5) Registered office address declaration (Form INC-22) 6) Statement of compliance by professional 7) Payment of registration fees All documents to be signed by subscriber and witnesses. MoA and AoA need to be stamped.

Card 10

What is Memorandum of Association (MoA)? Describe its clauses.

Section 2(56): MoA is the charter of the company defining its powers and functions. Six Clauses: 1) Name Clause - Company name with appropriate suffix (Ltd/Pvt Ltd) 2) Situation Clause - State of registered office (ROC jurisdiction) 3) Object Clause - Main objects + other objects (objects beyond cannot be ultra vires) 4) Liability Clause - Extent of liability of members 5) Capital Clause - Authorized share capital divided into nominal value 6) Subscription Clause - Declaration to take shares by subscribers Doctrine of Ultra Vires: Acts beyond MoA are void.

Card 11

What is Articles of Association (AoA)? What matters can it contain?

Section 2(5): AoA contains regulations for management of company. Can contain provisions regarding: - Business of company - Share capital and variations - Rights of shareholders/directors - Dividends and reserves - Transfer/transmission of shares - General meetings and voting - Borrowing powers - Accounts and audit - Winding up If no AoA, Table F of Schedule I applies. AoA must be registered with ROC along with MoA.

Card 12

What is Doctrine of Ultra Vires? What are its effects?

Ultra Vires = Beyond the powers. When a company does something beyond the powers conferred by MoA, it is ultra vires the company. Effects: 1) Act is void ab initio - cannot be ratified even by unanimous consent. 2) Directors personally liable for unauthorized acts. 3) No contract can be enforced against company. 4) Company not bound by obligations. 5) Third parties cannot claim damages. Exception: Doctrine of Indoor Management (Turquand's case) protects outsiders dealing in good faith.

Card 13

What is Doctrine of Indoor Management?

Also known as Turquand's Rule (Royal British Bank v Turquand, 1856). Protection available to outsiders dealing with company in good faith. Rule: Outsider is entitled to assume that internal procedures have been properly followed. Example: If aoa requires board resolution for borrowing, outsider can assume resolution passed even if it wasn't. Exceptions: 1) Knowledge of irregularity. 2) Negligence in not checking. 3) Forgery. 4) Ostensible authority exhausted. 5) Suspicion aroused by irregularity.

Card 14

What is the Prospectus? Define as per Section 2(70).

Section 2(70): Prospectus means any notice, circular, advertisement or other document inviting deposits or inviting investment money from the public for subscription/purchase of securities of body corporate. Includes: Red Herring Prospectus, Shelf Prospectus, Prospectus issued after shelf prospectus. Key points: - Must be dated. - Registered with ROC before issue. - Contains material information about company. - Liability for misstatements exists.

Card 15

What are the penalties for untrue statements in prospectus?

Section 35 Liability: 1) Company: Fine. 2) Every person who issued prospectus: Compensation to subscriber + Imprisonment up to 3 years or fine or both. 3) Directors/Experts: If consented to issue with knowledge of untrue statement. 4) Expert: Liable if made untrue statement without reasonable ground to believe. No liability if: - Statement withdrawn/rectified before issue. - Person withdrew consent before issue. - Had reasonable ground to believe statement was true. - Made correct statement without knowing it was untrue.

Card 16

What is Minimum Subscription? What happens if not received?

Section 39: Minimum subscription is minimum amount must be received by company from applicants for allotment of shares. Excludes: - Commission. - Expenses of issue. - Discount allowed. If minimum subscription not received: 1) All money received must be refunded within 15 days. 2) If not refunded within 15 days: Interest @15% p.a. from expiry of 15th day. 3) Directors jointly liable for refund + interest. 4) Action can be taken against directors for damages.

Card 17

What is a Section 8 Company?

Section 8: Company formed for promoting commerce, art, science, sports, education, research, charity, protection of environment or any other useful purpose. Conditions: 1) Intends to apply profits/income towards promoting objects. 2) Prohibits payment of dividend to members. 3) Apply for license from Central Government. 4) May be registered as limited or unlimited company. 5) Do not require 'Limited' or 'Pvt Ltd' in name. Benefits: - License instead of certificate of incorporation. - Relaxed compliance requirements.

Card 18

What is the procedure for converting a company under Section 8?

Section 8 Conversion Procedure: 1) Application to Central Government for license. 2) Government may grant license on condition: - Apply name exemption. - Alter MoA/AoA. - Other conditions as deemed fit. 3) License valid for specified period. 4) On fulfillment of conditions, company registered as Section 8 company. 5) Existing company can convert to Section 8 company. Revocation of license: - If company breaches conditions. - If company carries on business not in consonance with objects. - Upon revocation, company ceases to be Section 8 company.

Card 19

What are the classifications of shares under the Companies Act?

Types of Shares: 1) Equity Shares: Vote rights + dividend rights. 2) Preference Shares: Fixed dividend + priority in repayment. Preference Share Categories: - Cumulative/Non-cumulative. - Participating/Non-participating. - Convertible/Non-convertible. - Redeemable/ irredeemable. Share Premium Account: Amount received above par value. Sweat Equity Shares: Issued to employees/directors for IP/rights provided.

Card 20

What are the rules for Issue of Sweat Equity Shares?

Section 54: Sweat Equity Shares can be issued to: 1) Directors/Officers of company. 2) Employees of company/infringeestructure development. Conditions: 1) Approval by special resolution. 2) Leave of NCLT required. 3) Minimum gap of 1 year from last issue. 4) Maximum 50% of paid-up capital in any year. 5) Shares locked in for minimum 3 years. 6) Price determined by valuer registered with SEBI. Purpose: Reward talent for intellectual value addition.

Card 21

What are the provisions regarding Buyback of Securities under Section 68?

Section 68: Company may buy back its securities. Conditions: 1) Authorization by AoA or special resolution. 2) Buyback ≤ 25% of total paid-up capital in a financial year. 3) Ratio of secured debt to owned equity ≤ 2:1. 4) All securities fully paid-up. 5) Notice to ROC within 7 days of resolution. 6) Buyback completed within 12 months. Prohibited sources: - Second market/trading platform. - Book-building process. Declaration of solvency required from directors.

Card 22

What is the procedure for Buyback under Section 68?

Procedure: 1) Board meeting to approve buyback (if AoA permits). 2) Special resolution in general meeting (if AoA doesn't permit). 3) File declaration of solvency with ROC. 4) Open bank account for buyback consideration. 5) File letter of offer with SEBI/ROC. 6) Make offer open for minimum 15 days, maximum 30 days. 7) Complete buyback within 60 days of closure. 8) Cancel shares within 7 days of payment. 9) File return with ROC within 30 days. Distributed securities cannot be reissued for 5 years.

Card 23

What are the restrictions on Buyback?

Restrictions: 1) Cannot buy back from existing shareholders pro-rata. 2) Cannot buy back from same class of shareholders in two consecutive years. 3) No buyback if default in repayment of deposits/debentures/salary/wages. 4) No buyback if default in filing annual returns/accounts. 5) No buyback if penal proceedings pending. 6) Buyback cannot exceed 25% of paid-up capital in FY. 7) Secured debt Owned equity ratio must be ≤ 2:1. 8) All securities must be fully paid-up.

Card 24

What is a Share Certificate? What are its features?

Section 46: Share certificate is a conclusive evidence of title. Features: 1) Evidence of ownership. 2) Negotiable instrument. 3) Conclusive proof of title. 4) Issued within 2 months of allotment. 5) Must be under common seal/signature of director/secretary. 6) Must mention: company name, member name, shares held, distinctive numbers, paid-up amount. If not issued within time: Company + officers liable for compensation.

Card 25

What are the provisions for Transfer and Transmission of Shares?

TRANSFER (voluntary): By execution of instrument of transfer (Form SH-4). Transmission (involuntary): By operation of law (death, insanity, bankruptcy). Provisions: 1) Company must register transfer within 1 month. 2) Can refuse transfer on grounds specified in AoA. 3) Refusal order within 14 days with reasons. 4) Transferee can appeal to NCLT within 30 days. 5) Transmission: Executor/administrator can apply for transmission. 6) Company to notify transmission within 15 days. Share warrant: Holder deemed member.

Card 26

What are Provisional Allotment and Final Allotment?

PROVISIONAL ALLOTMENT: - Allotment before receiving minimum subscription. - Money received held in bank account. - If minimum subscription received within 30 days: can make final allotment. - If not received within 30 days: refund money + interest @15%. FINAL ALLOTMENT: - After receiving minimum subscription. - Allotment list to be filed with ROC within 30 days. - Certificate to be issued within 2 months. - Money due on allotment call treated as debt. Forfeiture: If share money not paid, company can forfeit shares after notice.

Card 27

What are the provisions regarding Calls on Shares?

Section 48: Company may make calls on shareholders for unpaid amount. Rules: 1) Call not exceeding 1/4th of face value or callable amount. 2) Gap of minimum 1 month between calls. 3) Notice of call to be sent to shareholders. 4) Shareholder liable to pay from date of notice. 5) Company can pay interest on advance payments. If call not paid: - Company can levy interest. - Can forfeit shares after proper notice. - Shareholder remains liable for unpaid amount even after forfeiture.

Card 28

What are the provisions for Forfeiture of Shares?

Forfeiture occurs when shareholder fails to pay call money. Procedure: 1) Board resolution for forfeiture. 2) Notice of 14 days to shareholder. 3) If not paid, shares forfeited. 4) Forfeited shares become property of company. Effects: 1) Forfeited shareholder ceases to be member. 2) Still liable for amounts due till forfeiture. 3) Company can sue for recovery. 4) Forfeited shares can be reissued. 5) Surplus on reissue goes to General Reserve. Reissue: Can be at par or discount.

Card 29

What is Segregation of Voting Rights?

Section 43: Equity shares with differentiated voting rights can be issued. Types: 1) Shares with superior voting rights. 2) Shares with inferior/special voting rights. Conditions: 1) At least 3 years since first issue of equity shares. 2) Paid-up capital ≥ ₹50 crore. 3) Compliance with SEBI (Share Basis Voting Rights) Regulations. 4) Separate class of shareholders in general meeting. 5) Can't have dual class structure without approval. Purpose: Allow founders/promoters to retain control while raising capital.

Card 30

What are the provisions for Reduction of Share Capital?

Section 66: Company may reduce share capital with NCLT confirmation. Modes: 1) Cancellation of lost/unrepresented capital. 2) extinguishing liability on unpaid shares. 3) paying off excess capital. 4) subdividing shares. 5) consolidating and dividing into larger shares. 6) buying back shares. Procedure: 1) Special resolution for reduction. 2) Application to NCLT. 3) NCLT may confirm with or without conditions. 4) Registration with ROC. 5) Publication in official gazette. Protection to creditors: Court may require creditor consent or security.

Card 31

What are the types of Meetings under Companies Act?

Types of Meetings: 1) Statutory Meeting - Only for public companies, within 1-6 months from incorporation. Report to be filed with ROC. 2) Annual General Meeting (AGM): - Public company: Within 6 months from FY end. - Private company: Within 9 months from FY end. - First AGM: Within 18 months from incorporation. - Interval between AGMs ≤ 15 months. 3) Extra Ordinary General Meeting (EGM) - For special business. 4) Class Meeting - For variation of rights of class shareholders. 5) Board Meeting - For directors' decisions.

Card 32

What are the requirements for convening AGM?

Requirements: 1) Notice of 21 clear days. 2) Quorum: 2 members personally present (private) / 5 members or 1/5th of total members (public). 3) Place: Ordinary place or video conferencing. 4) Day: As per AoA or default Tuesday. 5) Time: Between 9 AM to 6 PM. Convening: - By Board on requisition of members. - By members holding 10% voting power. - By NCLT if company fails to convene. Postal ballot may be used for certain resolutions.

Card 33

What is Quorum for General Meeting?

Section 103: Quorum means minimum number of members present to validly conduct meeting. Private Company: - 2 members personally present (if ≤20 members). - 1/5th of total members or 5 members (whichever higher). Public Company: - 5 members personally present. - 1/5th of total members (whichever higher). Counting only voting members. Members present as representatives/authorizes count. Quorum must be present throughout meeting. If quorum not present within 30 minutes, meeting stands adjourned to same day next week.

Card 34

What are the provisions for Board Meetings?

Section 173: 1) Minimum 4 board meetings per year. 2) Gap between meetings ≤ 120 days. 3) Notice of 7 clear days. 4) Quorum: 1/3rd of total strength or 2 directors (whichever higher). 5) Majority of directors who are in India. 6) Video conferencing allowed. 7) Minutes to be kept within 30 days. Not to be held at places outside India unless permitted. Related party transactions require prior approval.

Card 35

What is a Director? What are the types of directors?

Section 2(34): Director includes any person who occupies the position of director, by whatever name called. Types of Directors: 1) Managing Director (MD) - In whole-time management. 2) Whole-Time Director (WTD) - Chief executive officer. 3) Independent Director (ID) - Independent of management. 4) Woman Director - At least 1 in listed companies. 5) Director having share qualification. 6) Nominee Director - Appointed by financial institutions. 7) Alternative Director - For absence of original director. 8) Additional Director - Appointed between AGMs. 9) Casual Director - Filling casual vacancy.

Card 36

What are the qualifications and disqualifications of Directors?

QUALIFICATIONS: 1) DIN (Director Identification Number) mandatory. 2) DSC (Digital Signature Certificate). 3) No disqualification under Section 164. 4) Consents to act as director. DISQUALIFICATIONS (Section 164): 1) Unsound mind declared by court. 2) Undischarged insolvent. 3) Convicted offense involving moral turpitude (6 years). 4) Default in repayment of deposits/interest. 5) Default in payment of shares/calls. 6) Non-filing of annual returns/accounts for 3 consecutive years. 7) Disqualified by court/authority. No disqualification if penalty removed/removed by court.

Card 37

What is DIN? What are its requirements?

DIN = Director Identification Number. Section 154: 1) Mandatory for every director. 2) Applied through SPICe+ form at the time of incorporation/appointment. 3) Valid for lifetime. 4) Changes in name/address need intimation. 5) Can be deactivated if not used for 3 years. 6) One DIN per person. Penalty for contravention: Up to ₹5 lakh. DSC = Digital Signature Certificate used for e-filing with MCA.

Card 38

What are the provisions for Appointment of Independent Directors?

Section 149: Required for: 1) Listed public company. 2) Public company with paid-up capital ≥ ₹100 crore. 3) Public company with turnover ≥ ₹1000 crore. 4) Public company with outstanding loans/bonds/debentures ≥ ₹500 crore. Criteria: 1) Not promoter/member. 2) Not related to directors/management. 3) Not having financial/other significant relationship. 4) Independent judgment. 5) Integrity and expertise. Term: Max 2 terms of 5 years each. Can't be reappointed beyond 10 years.

Card 39

What are the powers and duties of Directors?

POWERS (Section 179): 1) Manage business of company. 2) Power to borrow money. 3) Power to invest funds. 4) Power to grant loan/security. 5) Power to call for financial statements. 6) Power to appoint officers. 7) Power to make calls on shares. 8) Power to forfeit shares. 9) Power to pay commission. 10) Power to sanction certain contracts. DUTIES (Section 166): 1) Act according to MoA/AoA. 2) Act in good faith for benefit of company. 3) Exercise diligence and care. 4) Avoid conflict of interest. 5) Not achieve undue gain. 6) Act within powers. 7) Independent judgment.

Card 40

What is Related Party Transaction? What are the provisions?

Section 188: Related party includes: 1) Director/relative of director. 2) Key managerial personnel/relative. 3) Body corporate where director is member/director. 4) Private company in which director is member. 5) Firm/HUF in which director is member. 6) Entity on whose board director sits. Transactions require: 1) Prior approval of Board (for material transactions). 2) Approval by shareholders (for certain transactions). 3) Disclosure in board report. 4) Audit committee recommendation. Exceptions: Transactions on arm's length basis, routine transactions.

Card 41

What are the provisions for Remuneration of Directors?

Section 197 read with Schedule V: Total managerial remuneration ≤ 11% of net profits. Maximum to single director: 5% of net profits. Components: 1) Salary/wages. 2) Perquisites. 3) Profit sharing. 4) Commission. Approval required: - From Board for ≤ 5%. - From shareholders by special resolution for > 5%. - From NCLT if no approval obtained. Exceptions: Banking companies, NBFCs, wholly-owned subsidiary.

Card 42

What is Key Managerial Personnel (KMP)?

Section 2(51): Key Managerial Personnel means: 1) Managing Director / Chief Executive Officer. 2) Company Secretary. 3) Chief Financial Officer. 4) Any other officer specified by Central Government (not below GM level). Note: Excludes auditor and comfort person. Applicable to: - Listed companies. - Public companies with paid-up capital ≥ ₹10 crore. - Public companies with turnover ≥ ₹100 crore. KMP must give intimation of appointment/termination within 30 days.

Card 43

What are the provisions for Directors' Report?

Section 134: Directors' report must contain: 1) Fair review of operations. 2) Important changes in capital/movable property. 3) Shareholding pattern of directors/promoters. 4) Particulars of loans/guarantees/investments. 5) Conservation of energy. 6) Technology absorption. 7) Foreign exchange earnings/outgo. 8) Risk factors. 9) Employee information. 10) Related party transactions. 11) Matters regarding quality control. 12) Details of variation in financial statements. 13) particulars of unutilized securities premium. Signed by MD/director authorized. Attached to balance sheet.

Card 44

What are the provisions for Company Secretary?

Section 203: Every listed company and public company with: - Paid-up capital ≥ ₹50 crore, OR - Turnover ≥ ₹500 crore Must appoint whole-time company secretary. Appointments to be reported to ROC within 30 days. Failure to appoint: Penalty on company + officers. Qualifications as per CS Act. Company Secretary to perform duties as may be prescribed.

Card 45

What are the provisions for Auditor appointment and removal?

APPOINTMENT (Section 139): - First auditor: Board within 30 days of incorporation. - Subsequent auditors: AGM within 30 days from FY end. - Term: 5 years, can be reappointed. - Resident auditor: At least 1 individual/partner resident in India. REMOVAL: - Before expiry: Special resolution + Central Govt approval. - Cause shown required. - Auditee has right to be heard. - Notification to ROC within 15 days. VACANCY: - Board to fill within 3 months. - If not filled: existing auditor continues till replacement.

Card 46

What are the qualifications and disqualifications of Auditor?

QUALIFICATIONS: - Member of ICAI. - In practice with certificate of practice. - Firm where partner is member can also be auditor. DISQUALIFICATIONS (Section 141): 1) Not a officer/employee of company. 2) Not relative of director/KMP/officer. 3) Holds shares (except nominal ₹100). 4) Indebted to company > ₹50000. 5) Has business connection with company > ₹2 lakh. 6) Person/book-keeper previously auditor. 7) Person convicted of offense involving moral turpitude. Additional: Auditor can't audit more than 20 companies.

Card 47

What are the powers and duties of Auditor?

POWERS: 1) Access to books/accounts. 2) Require information from officers. 3) Attend general meetings. 4) Receive notice/communication. 5) Be heard at meetings. DUTIES: 1) Investigate accounts. 2) Verify assets/liabilities. 3) Check compliance with accounting standards. 4) Report on financial statements. 5) Report fraud if discovered. 6) Report on internal financial controls. Auditor's report must state: - True and fair view. - Proper books maintained. - Accounts agree with books. - Compliance with accounting standards.

Card 48

What is Rotational Audit? What are the provisions?

Section 139(2): Mandatory rotation of auditors and audit firms. Provisions: 1) Individual auditor: Max 1 term of 5 years. Can't be reappointed immediately. 2) Audit firm: Max 2 terms of 5 years each (total 10 years). Can't be reappointed immediately. 3) Transition period: 1 year after which new auditor appointed. Exceptions: - OPC/private company (not required). - Listed companies/public companies (required). - Partnership firm can continue if partner retires/dies. Purpose: Ensure fresh perspective, prevent long association bias.

Card 49

What are the provisions for Prevention of Fraud?

Section 143(12): Auditor to report fraud. If auditor discovers fraud during audit: 1) Report to Central Government within 60 days. 2) Copy to audit committee. 3) Specify nature of fraud, approximate amount involved. Whistle Blower Policy (Section 177): - Listed companies + public companies with paid-up capital ≥ ₹10 crore must have vigil mechanism. - Employees can report genuine concerns. - Direct reporting to audit committee. Fraud reporting by directors: If director has reason to believe fraud committed, must report to Central Government.

Card 50

What is Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR)?

Section 135: Every company with: - Net worth ≥ ₹500 crore, OR - Turnover ≥ ₹1000 crore, OR - Net profit ≥ ₹5 crore Must spend 2% of average net profits of preceding 3 FYs on CSR. CSR Committee: 3+ directors, at least 1 independent director. Unspent amount: Must be transferred to unspent CSR account within 30 days of FY end. Fund utilization: 3 FYs for ongoing projects, otherwise general revenue fund. Activities: Eradicating hunger, health, education, environment, etc.

Card 51

What are the provisions for Corporate Governance?

SEBI (LODR) Regulations 2015 requires: 1) Board of Directors: At least 50% independent directors for listed entities. 2) Audit Committee: 3+ members, majority independent, chairman independent. 3) Nomination & Remuneration Committee: 3+ members, majority independent. 4) Stakeholders Relationship Committee. 5) Risk Management Committee. 6) Sustainability Reporting. 7) Disclosure of related party transactions. 8) Code of Conduct for directors. 9) Board Evaluation. 10) Internal Financial Controls. NRMC: Determine remuneration policy, identify qualified directors.

Card 52

What are the provisions of LLP Act, 2008?

LLP = Alternative corporate vehicle combining partnership flexibility with company's limited liability. Key features: 1) Separate legal entity. 2) Perpetual succession. 3) Limited liability of partners. 4) No requirement for minimum capital. 5) Flexible internal governance through agreement. 6) Designated partners responsible for compliance. Designated Partner: - Minimum 2 in LLP. - At least 1 must be resident in India. - Responsible for statutory compliance. LLP Agreement: Can be oral/written, governs mutual rights/duties.

Card 53

What is SARFAESI Act, 2002?

Securitisation and Reconstruction of Financial Assets and Enforcement of Security Interest Act, 2002. Enables banks/FIs to enforce security interest without court intervention. Powers: 1) Take possession of security asset. 2) Take over management of borrower's business. 3) Sell security asset. 4) Appoint manager. 5) Accept security for loan. 6) Transfer non-performing asset to asset reconstruction company. Conditions: - Loan must be classified as NPA. - 60 days notice to borrower required. - Borrower can raise objection within 30 days. - Appeal to DRT within 30 days.

Card 54

What is the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016?

Consolidates laws relating to insolvency resolution for individuals, partnership firms, and companies. Key components: 1) NCLT - Adjudicating authority for corporate insolvency. 2) IBBI - Regulator. 3) IRPs - Insolvency Resolution Professionals. 4) ARCs - Asset Reconstruction Companies. Process: 1) Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP) initiated by financial creditor/operational creditor/business owner. 2) moratorium on debts/actions. 3) IRP takes over management. 4) Committee of Creditors (CoC) decides resolution plan. 5) If resolution approved: Implementation. 6) If not: Liquidation. Time limit: 180 days extendable by 90 days.

Card 55

What is Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP)?

Initiation: 1) Application to NCLT by financial creditor/operational creditor. 2) NCLT admits/rejects within 14-60 days. 3) moratorium declared - no suits, execution, security enforcement. IRP Role: 1) Take over management of corporate debtor. 2) Constitutes CoC. 3) Invite resolution plans. 4) Evaluate plans. CoC: 1) Comprises financial creditors. 2) 66% voting majority for approval. 3) Decide resolution plan implementation. Resolution Plan: 1) Approved by CoC. 2) Implemented by IRP. 3) Binding on all stakeholders. Liquidation: If no resolution plan approved or implemented.

Card 56

What is Liquidation Process under IBC?

Triggered when: 1) No resolution plan approved by CoC. 2) Resolution plan approved but not implemented. 3) Willful default by corporate debtor. 4) Voluntary liquidation by own resolution. Process: 1) NCLT orders liquidation. 2) IRP becomes liquidator. 3) Publish notice inviting claims. 4) Verify claims. 5) Realize assets. 6) Distribute proceeds in order of priority: a) Insolvency resolution costs. b) Workmen dues (24 months). c) Secured creditors (without realisation). d) Employees wages (12 months). e) Government dues. f) Unsecured creditors. g) Remaining debts. h) Preferred shareholders. i) Equity shareholders. Time limit: 1 year extendable by 6 months.

Card 57

What is the Securities and Exchange Board of India Act, 1992?

Established SEBI as regulatory body for securities market. Powers: 1) Register and regulate stock exchanges/brokers. 2) Regulate takeovers and acquisitions. 3) Prohibit insider trading. 4) Regulate mutual funds/NBFCs investing in securities. 5) Call for information from issuing companies. 6) Conduct inspections/inquiries. 7) Delegate powers to stock exchanges. Functions: - Protect investors. - Promote stock market development. - Regulate securities transactions. - Prevent fraudulent practices. - Register and regulate intermediaries. SEBI can impose penalties up to ₹25 crore or 3 times profit from offense.

Card 58

What are the provisions for Takeover Regulations?

SEBI (Substantial Acquisition of Shares and Takeovers) Regulations, 2011. Trigger: Acquisition of 25% or more voting rights triggers open offer. Open Offer Requirement: 1) Acquirer must make open offer to public shareholders. 2) Minimum 26% shares to be offered. 3) Price = Highest price paid by acquirer in last 26 weeks or average of last 6 months (whichever higher). 4) Payment within 30 days of acceptance. 5) Escrow arrangement for 25% of consideration. Exemptions: - Acquisition through bonus rights/preferential allotment. - Transfer between persons under same control. - Inheritance/succession. - Qualified institutional buyers. Delisting: Can be done through reverse book building.

Card 59

What is Insider Trading? What are the provisions under SEBI Act?

Insider Trading = Trading in securities by insider on the basis of unpublished price sensitive information (UPSI). INSIDER includes: 1) Connected person. 2) Director/officer of company. 3) Employee of company. 4) Any person who has access to UPSI. UPSI means information which: 1) Is not generally available. 2) Relevant to affairs of company. 3) If generally available would materially affect price. Prohibitions: 1) Cannot trade on basis of UPSI. 2) Cannot communicate UPSI to others. 3) Cannot counsel/procure others to trade. Penalties: Up to ₹25 crore or 3 times profit, whichever higher.

Card 60

What are the provisions of FEMA, 1999?

Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999 - replaces FERA 1973. Objective: Facilitate external trade/payments and promote orderly development of foreign exchange market. Key concepts: 1) Foreign Exchange - includes foreign currency, deposits, credits, bills, securities. 2) Person Resident in India - stayed in India for 182+ days during preceding FY. 3) Current Account Transaction - without approval (trade, payment, etc.). 4) Capital Account Transaction - requires approval. Regulated activities: 1) Acquisition/transfer of immovable property outside India. 2) Export/import of currency. 3) Borrowing/lending in foreign currency. 4) Giving guarantee in foreign currency. Penalties: Civil and criminal liabilities.

Card 61

What are Current Account and Capital Account Transactions?

CURRENT ACCOUNT TRANSACTION (Section 2(j)): 1) Trade-related current account transactions. 2) Payment due to family maintenance abroad. 3) Expenses on travel/education/medical. 4) Interest on loans in ordinary course. 5) Dividends on shares. 6) Maintenance of accounts abroad. 7) Any other transaction not involving capital. CAPITAL ACCOUNT TRANSACTION (Section 2(k)): 1) Investment in securities/shares. 2) Lending/borrowing between residents/non-residents. 3) Providing guarantee/security. 4) Transfer of immovable property abroad. 5) Export/import of currency. 6) Any other transaction not involving current account. Most current account transactions free; capital account restricted.

Card 62

What is Competition Act, 2002?

Enacted to provide for establishment of Competition Commission of India (CCI). Objectives: 1) Prevent practices having adverse effect on competition. 2) Promote and sustain competition. 3) Protect consumer interests. 4) Ensure freedom of trade. Key provisions: 1) Anti-competitive agreements (Section 3). 2) Abuse of dominant position (Section 4). 3) Combination regulations (Section 5-6). CCI Powers: 1) Investigate anti-competitive practices. 2) Order cessation of anti-competitive agreements. 3) Impose penalties (up to 10% of average turnover). 4) Modify agreements. 5) Order separation of enterprises.

Card 63

What are Anti-Competitive Agreements under Section 3?

Horizontal Agreements (between competitors): 1) Price fixing. 2) Limiting production/supply. 3) Market allocation. 4) Bid rigging. Presumed to have appreciable adverse effect on competition (AAC). Vertical Agreements (between different levels): 1) Tie-in arrangement. 2) Exclusive supply/distribution agreement. 3) Refusal to deal. 4) Exclusive supply agreement. Evaluate on rule of reason basis. Penalties: Up to 3 times profit or ₹100 crore, whichever higher.

Card 64

What is Abuse of Dominant Position?

Section 4: Dominant position means position of strength enabling enterprise to operate independently of competitive forces. Abuse includes: 1) Imposing unfair price (directly/indirectly). 2) Limiting production/markets/technical development. 3) Denying market access. 4) Making contracts conditional. 5) Using dominant position in one market to enter another. 6) Discriminating between different purchasers/ sellers. CCI can inquire and order: 1) Cease and desist. 2) Modify agreements. 3) Impose penalty up to 10% of average turnover. Exceptions: IP rights, natural monopoly, essential services.

Card 65

What are the provisions for Merger and Acquisition under Companies Act?

Section 230-232: Compromise, Arrangement and Amalgamation. Amalgamation types: 1) Merger - two companies combine into new company. 2) Absorption - one company absorbed by another. 3) Formation of new company to take over business of transferor company. Procedure: 1) Application to NCLT for convening meeting. 2) NCLT fixes date for meeting. 3) Creditors/shareholders approve by 3/4th majority. 4) NCLT sanctions scheme. 5) File order with ROC. Tax implications: Tax neutral if conditions met.

Card 66

What are the provisions for Winding Up of Company?

Two modes: 1) Compulsory winding up by NCLT. 2) Voluntary winding up by members/creditors. Grounds for compulsory: - Company passes special resolution for voluntary winding up. - Default in filing financial statements/annual returns for 5 years. - company unable to pay debts. - NCLT just and equitable grounds. Effects of winding up: 1) Company ceases to carry on business except for beneficial winding up. 2) All transfers/transmissions of shares void after commencement. 3) NCLT has exclusive jurisdiction over proceedings. Order of distribution: See liquidation priority under IBC.

Card 67

What is the Factoring Regulation Act, 2011?

Provides for regulation of factoring business and recognition of factor. Factors means financial institution licensed by RBI to carry on factoring business. Definitions: 1) Debtor - person who owes debt to factor. 2) Creditor - person who has assigned debt to factor. 3) Debt - monetary claim arising from sale of goods/provision of services. Provisions: 1) Assignment of receivables by notice to debtor. 2) Factor can sue debtor in his own name. 3) Protection for factor against set-off/counter-claim by debtor. 4) Recognition of factor by RBI. Purpose: Develop factoring market to help MSMEs get quick liquidity.

Card 68

What are the provisions regarding Prohibition of Preferential Employment?

Section 47: No person can be excluded from employment/office on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex or place of birth. However, provision made for women/children for particular employment. Section 48: No preference/disadvantage on grounds of membership in any legislative body. These provisions ensure equality of opportunity and prevent discrimination in corporate governance and employment.

Card 69

What is the procedure for Variation of Class Rights?

Section 48: Variation of rights attached to class of shares. Conditions: 1) Holders of 3/4th of issued shares of that class in writing, OR 2) Special resolution passed at separate general meeting of holders of that class. Procedure: 1) Company convenes separate meeting of class shareholders. 2) Special resolution requires 3/4th majority. 3) Resolution can be varigated by NCLT within 21 days. 4) 10% of objecting shareholders can apply to NCLT. Exemption: If AoA permits variation without approval. Rights include: voting rights, dividend rights, return of capital, conversion rights.

Card 70

What are the provisions regarding Share Transfer via Demat?

Companies (Depositories and Securities Registration) Act, 1996 + SEBI regulations. Dematerialization: Physical shares converted to electronic form held with depository (NSDL/CDSL). Benefits: 1) Instant transfer without stamp duty. 2) No loss/theft risk. 3) Free from manual errors. 4) Speedy settlement. Transfer Process: 1) Deliver shares through DP (Depositary Participant). 2) Receiver accepts in their DP account. 3) Settlement through clearing corporation. 4) Intimation to company for updating register. Listing: Listed companies must dematerialize shares by specified date.

Card 71

What are the provisions for E-Voting under Companies Act?

Section 108: Company can provide facility for voting by electronic means. Eligibility: 1) Listed companies - mandatory from FY 2015-16. 2) Unlisted public companies with paid-up capital ≥ ₹10 crore - mandatory from FY 2019-20. 3) Private companies - optional. Procedure: 1) Company provides unique ID to each member. 2) Voting through electronic platform. 3) Return of poll results to NCLT. 4) Scrutinizer appointed to supervise. Proxies can't vote electronically. E-voting period minimum 3 days.

Card 72

What is the role of NCLT in company matters?

National Company Law Tribunal established under Section 408. Powers: 1) Compromise/arrangement/amalgamation (Sections 230-234). 2) Opposition to reduction of capital. 3) Removal of members from register. 4) Convening of meetings. 5) Investigation of company affairs. 6) Winding up of companies. 7) Corporate insolvency proceedings. 8) Appeals against SEBI orders. Benches: Principal Bench at New Delhi, regional benches. Appeal: SAT (Sectoral Appellate Tribunal), then Supreme Court.

Card 73

What are the provisions for Investigation of Company Affairs?

Section 210-222: Investigation powers. Inspector Appointment: 1) Central Government can appoint inspector to investigate affairs of company. 2) Grounds: Ownership, prejudice to members, fraud, mismanagement. 3) Inspector report to Central Government. Investigation by NCLT: 1) Application by members (100 members or 1/10th of total). 2) NCLT may investigate if affairs conducted prejudicially. Investigation of Related Companies: - If companies have common directors, can investigate together. Cost of investigation: May be ordered to be paid by company/inspector.

Card 74

What are the key provisions of the Companies (Management and Administration) Rules, 2014?

Key provisions: 1) Form INC-22: Registered office verification within 30 days of incorporation. 2) Form MGT-7: Annual return filing. 3) Form AOC-4: Financial statements filing. 4) Form NOC: No objection certificate for change of name. 5) Form PAS-3: Return of allotment. 6) Form MGT-14: Filing of resolutions/orders with ROC. 7) e-KYC: Dynamic Power of Attorney for voting. 8) Corporate Identity Number (CIN): Unique identification for every company. 9) DUIN: DIN for nominees/intimate persons. Digital signature mandatory for e-filing.
