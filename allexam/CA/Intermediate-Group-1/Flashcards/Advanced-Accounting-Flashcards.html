CAEnglishFlashcards

Card 1

What are the core joint venture transactions that must be accounted for, and what principle governs their settlement?

Core transactions: purchase of goods/assets, direct expenses (freight, insurance, godown rent, advertising), sale of goods/assets, and final settlement between venturers. Principle: each venturer’s share of profit/loss is settled through the respective account after recording all entries in the joint books and preparing a Joint Venture Account (nominal) or Joint Bank Account.

Card 2

How do you decide whether to maintain separate books or only record in one partner’s books for a Joint Venture?

Use separate books when multiple partners contribute capital/assets and want full transparency; otherwise, record only in one partner’s books. When recorded in one partner’s books, a ‘Joint Venture Account’ (personal account type) is maintained for each venturer to settle claims after the venture concludes.

Card 3

What journal entries are passed by the party maintaining sole records for a Joint Venture?

(1) On purchase of goods/assets: Joint Venture A/c Dr. to Supplier A/c (2) On direct expenses paid: Joint Venture A/c Dr. to Cash/Bank (3) On sale of goods/assets: Cash/Bank A/c Dr. to Joint Venture A/c (4) On capital contribution received from other venturers: Cash/Bank A/c Dr. to Joint Venture A/c (5) Final settlement: Joint Venture A/c Dr./Cr. to respective venturer’s Joint Venture A/c (profit/loss share).

Card 4

If goods are sent on consignment at cost plus 20%, and the consignor’s books show the invoice value as Rs. 1,00,000, what is the actual cost price and gross profit margin?

Actual cost = Invoice value ÷ 1.20 = Rs. 83,333 (approx). Gross profit margin on cost = 20%. If marked up on sales value (not cost), then selling price = Rs. 1,00,000, cost = 1,00,000 × 5/6 = Rs. 83,333, gross profit = Rs. 16,667.

Card 5

Define‘Commission on surplus sales‘and state when it is applicable in consignment accounting.

Commission on surplus sales is an additional percentage over the agreed commission rate, payable when the selling price exceeds the reserve price fixed by the consignor. It incentivizes the consignee to sell above the minimum price and is calculated on the excess amount (selling price − reserve price).

Card 6

An agent is allowed 10% commission on sales plus 5% on surplus sales exceeding the reserve price. Goods are sold for Rs. 2,00,000 against a reserve of Rs. 1,80,000. Calculate total commission.

Normal commission = 10% of 2,00,000 = Rs. 20,000. Surplus = 2,00,000 − 1,80,000 = Rs. 20,000. Surplus commission = 5% of 20,000 = Rs. 1,000. Total commission = Rs. 21,000.

Card 7

Explain the treatment of abnormal loss in consignment accounting and its effect on valuation of closing stock.

Abnormal loss is valued at cost minus any insurance claim or salvage value recovered. It is credited to Consignment Account and debited to Abnormal Loss Account. For valuation of closing stock, non-recurring expenses (like abnormal loss adjustment) are excluded while computing normal profit proportionately; abnormal loss does not affect profit transfer to P&L A/c but impacts consignment profit calculation.

Card 8

Distinguish between normal loss and abnormal loss in a consignment transaction with illustrative figures.

Normal loss: inevitable shrinkage; no monetary value assigned; increased cost per unit of remaining stock. Example: 100 units cost Rs. 10 each, normal loss 5%; total cost Rs. 1,000 becomes spread over 95 units. Abnormal loss: unexpected (fire/theft); valued at cost less realizable value; transferred to Abnormal Loss A/c; shown separately in financials.

Card 9

A branch maintains its books independently using the debtors system. Closing Debtors = Rs. 45,000 (at invoice price), Opening Debtors = Rs. 30,000, Stock at invoice price = Rs. 20,000, Stock in transit (at invoice) = Rs. 10,000, Goods sent to branch (at invoice) = Rs. 2,00,000. Markup is 25% on selling price. Calculate the cost of goods sent and unrealized profit in stock.

Goods sent at cost = 2,00,000 × 4/5 = Rs. 1,60,000. Unrealized profit in closing stock = 20,000 × 1/5 = Rs. 4,000. Unrealized profit in stock in transit = 10,000 × 1/5 = Rs. 2,000. Total unrealized profit to be adjusted in head office P&L = Rs. 6,000.

Card 10

Compare the‘Self-Contained System‘and‘Dependent System‘of branch accounting with respect to balance sheet treatment.

Self-contained: Branch keeps full double-entry books including cash, debtors, creditors. Head Office adjusts through ‘Branch Adjustment A/c‘ only for differences. Dependent: Branch maintains limited records (stock, debtors); Head Office maintains full control. In dependent system,‘Goods in Transit‘and‘Branch Stock Account‘are key adjustments;‘Branch adjustment‘clears inter-branch differences.

Card 11

In the dependent system, if Head Office sends goods to branch at cost + 20%, and the branch reports unsold stock worth Rs. 50,000 at invoice price, how much profit is unrealized and needs reversal?

Unrealized profit = 50,000 × 20/120 = Rs. 8,333 (approx). This profit is included in branch stock but not yet realized, so it must be deducted from branch profit before consolidation into head office P&L.

Card 12

Explain why‘Profit not yet realised‘must be eliminated in the consolidation of branch accounts under Indian Accounting Standards.

IAS 27/Ind AS 110 requires elimination of inter-company profits until goods are sold to third parties. Unrealized profit inflates both branch stock and head office profit. Consolidation demands only realized profits be shown, ensuring faithful representation of group performance and asset values.

Card 13

A hire purchase seller delivers goods with cash price Rs. 1,20,000, interest @10% p.a. on reducing balance. Installments are Rs. 40,000 at end of each year. Find the interest component in the second installment.

Year 1 opening balance = Rs. 1,20,000. Interest = 10% × 1,20,000 = Rs. 12,000. Principal repaid = 40,000 − 12,000 = Rs. 28,000. Closing balance = 1,20,000 − 28,000 = Rs. 92,000. Year 2 interest = 10% × 92,000 = Rs. 9,200. So interest in second installment = Rs. 9,200.

Card 14

When a buyer defaults on the third instalment under a hire purchase agreement and goods are repossessed partially, explain how profit/loss on repossession is computed.

Profit/loss = Value of goods recovered − (Agreed price − Instalments received − Interest accrued). If recovering part of goods, proportionate installments received and gross profit ratio applied to find unrealized profit relating to repossessed portion. Loss on repossession arises if recovery value < net installment debt; gain if >.

Card 15

Differentiate between‘Hire Purchase‘and‘Instalment Sale‘with regard to risk and reward transfer and ownership passage.

Hire Purchase: ownership passes only on payment of final installment; hirer has option to return goods before completion. Instalment Sale: ownership passes immediately upon delivery; buyer is true owner though payments are spread; seller has only a security charge over goods.

Card 16

A royalty bearing unit pays minimum rent of Rs. 1,00,000 per year. Actual royalty output is 80,000 tons at Rs. 2 per ton = Rs. 1,60,000. Short working is Rs. 60,000. If short working can be recouped once output exceeds previous year’s minimum threshold, how would recoupment entry be recorded?

Entry: Royalty Account Dr. 60,000 to Recoupment of Short Working A/c Cr. 60,000 (in year of recoupment). Simultaneously, Profit & Loss A/c Dr. 60,000 to Recoupment of Short Working A/c Cr. 60,000 to close the reserve. Balance in Recoupment Reserve reduces accordingly.

Card 17

Derive the formula for‘Copyright Royalty‘when royalty is payable on‘production basis‘and explain why‘patent royalty‘might differ in calculation.

Copyright royalty = Units produced × Rate per unit. Patent royalty often includes‘minimum rent‘clauses due to uncertain market demand and technology lifespan. Thus patent royalty may have floor protection; copyright typically does not, being tied directly to output volumes.

Card 18

Theatre profit distribution involves: Producer 40%, Distributor 30%, Exhibitor 30%. If gross collection is Rs. 10,00,000 and deductibles (tax, entertainment duty, advertisements) total Rs. 2,00,000, calculate each party’s share of net profit.

Net distributable profit = 10,00,000 − 2,00,000 = Rs. 8,00,000. Producer = 40% × 8,00,000 = Rs. 3,20,000. Distributor = 30% × 8,00,000 = Rs. 2,40,000. Exhibitor = 30% × 8,00,000 = Rs. 2,40,000.

Card 19

Explain the treatment of‘Interest on advance royalties‘received from lessees in royalty accounts.

Interest on advance royalties is income and credited to‘Interest Received A/c‘or‘Other Income‘in Profit & Loss. It is not part of royalty revenue. Advance royalty itself is a liability until earned through production/sales; interest accrues separately on the advance amount.

Card 20

What is the key difference between‘Royalty Account‘prepared for tax purposes and that prepared for financial reporting under Ind AS?

For tax purposes, royalty may be restricted to‘paid or payable‘within the year; for financial reporting under Ind AS, royalty expense is recognized on accrual basis matching with output. This creates temporary differences leading to deferred tax assets/liabilities.

Card 21

In a consignment, goods worth Rs. 50,000 at cost are sent. Commission is 10% on sales excluding GST. If GST at 12% is charged and recovered separately, compute total bill value and commission base.

Commission base = Rs. 50,000 (cost value). But invoice value at cost + 20% = Rs. 60,000. GST = 12% of 60,000 = Rs. 7,200. Total bill value = Rs. 67,200. Commission = 10% of 60,000 = Rs. 6,000 (GST excluded).

Card 22

A Joint Venture between X and Y results in: X contributes goods Rs. 50,000, Y contributes cash Rs. 30,000. Sale proceeds Rs. 1,00,000. Expenses paid by X Rs. 10,000. Profit shared equally. Show X’s and Y’s joint venture accounts.

X’s Joint Venture A/c: Dr. to Goods 50,000, to Bank (expenses) 10,000 → Cr. 1,00,000 = Profit 40,000 → Share to X 20,000, balance to Y. Y’s JV A/c: Dr. 1,00,000 → Cr. to Cash 30,000, to X (profit share) 20,000, balance 50,000 payable to Y.

Card 23

Why is‘Stock Reservation Account‘maintained in branch accounting under the debtors system, and what happens when actual stock differs from reserved stock?

Stock Reservation Account tracks goods sent to branch at cost vs. reserve maintained by HO for pricing policy. Differences arise due to transit delays, destruction, or adjustments. When branch stock exceeds reserve, HO credits Branch Adjustment A/c; when less, debits. It ensures accurate profit computation.

Card 24

A lessee pays minimum rent of Rs. 50,000/year. Actual output is 10,000 tons at Rs. 4/ton = Rs. 40,000. Short working is Rs. 10,000. In the next year, output is 18,000 tons at Rs. 4 = Rs. 72,000, minimum rent Rs. 50,000. Compute royalty payable and recoupment.

Year 1 short working = Rs. 10,000 carried forward. Year 2 surplus over minimum = 72,000 − 50,000 = Rs. 22,000. Out of this, Rs. 10,000 is used to recoup previous shortfall. Royalty payable in Year 2 = Rs. 72,000, but effective burden reduced by recoupment. In accounting, debit Royalty A/c 72,000, credit Cash 62,000, credit Recoupment Reserve 10,000.

Card 25

Explain how‘Gross Profit Ratio‘is determined for calculating unrealized profit in closing stock under hire purchase method.

Gross profit ratio = (Total hire purchase price − Cash price) ÷ Total hire purchase price. Example: HP price Rs. 1,20,000, cash price Rs. 1,00,000. GP ratio = 20,000/1,20,000 = 1/6 ≈ 16.67%. Unrealized profit in stock = Stock at HP price × GP ratio.

Card 26

A theatre produces a film. Producer invests Rs. 20 lakhs, distributor guarantees collection of Rs. 50 lakhs, exhibitor commits Rs. 30 lakhs. Profit-sharing 40:30:30. If total collections net of taxes are Rs. 40 lakhs, show distribution among stakeholders.

Net distributable = Rs. 40 lakhs. Producer = 40% × 40 = Rs. 16 lakhs. Distributor = 30% × 40 = Rs. 12 lakhs. Exhibitor = 30% × 40 = Rs. 12 lakhs. Each receives according to contract irrespective of initial investment unless otherwise agreed.

Card 27

Under ICAI’s guidance, why is‘Consignment Stock Reserve‘not created when goods are sent at cost rather than at cost plus profit?

Stock Reserve is created only when goods are transferred at a markup (cost plus profit) so that unrealized profit embedded in closing stock can be reserved. If sent at cost, there is no embedded profit; hence no reserve is needed—branch/debtors reflect exact cost values.

Card 28

In hire purchase accounting, if the purchaser defaults and the goods are repossessed, but the seller resells them later at a higher price, how is the profit on resale treated?

Profit on resale of repossessed goods is treated as‘Gain on repossession‘and credited to Profit & Loss A/c. It is not part of ordinary operating profit. The initial loss on repossession (if any) is also separately disclosed. Net effect appears in‘Other Income‘section.

Card 29

What entries are passed in the books of the lessor when‘Short Working‘is recouped in subsequent years under a royalty contract?

At recoupment: (a) Royalty A/c Dr. to Bank/Cash A/c (actual payment). (b) Recoupment of Short Working A/c Dr. to Royalty A/c (adjustment). (c) Profit & Loss A/c Dr. to Recoupment of Short Working A/c (transfer to P&L showing expense incurred). Only net cash outflow is shown in cash flow.

Card 30

A branch uses the‘Debtors System‘with opening stock Rs. 20,000, opening debtors Rs. 30,000. During year: purchases Rs. 1,00,000, sales Rs. 1,50,000, closing debtors Rs. 40,000, closing stock Rs. 25,000. Calculate COGS and gross profit assuming no adjust­ments.

COGS = Opening stock + Purchases − Closing stock = 20,000 + 1,00,000 − 25,000 = Rs. 95,000. Gross profit = Sales − COGS = 1,50,000 − 95,000 = Rs. 55,000. Debtor figures help track receivables but are not directly used in COGS calculation here.

Card 31

Distinguish between‘Normal Profit‘and‘Abnormal Profit‘in consignment accounts and how each is transferred to P&L.

Normal profit is anticipated margin computed using a standard ratio based on historical data; transferred to P&L proportional to goods sold. Abnormal profit arises from special circumstances (windfall sale); fully credited to P&L in the year it occurs because it is non-recurring and not part of routine operations.

Card 32

Explain the journal entry pattern in ‘Joint Bank Account‘method of accounting for joint ventures.

All receipts and payments are routed through Joint Bank A/c. Entries: (1) Venturers contribute capital: Cash/Bank A/c Dr. to Joint Bank A/c. (2) Payments made: Joint Venture A/c Dr. to Joint Bank A/c. (3) Receipts realized: Joint Bank A/c Dr. to Joint Venture A/c. (4) Final settlement: Joint Bank A/c Dr./Cr. to Venturer’s A/c depending on surplus/deficit.
