CA Intermediate Group 1 — Taxation (Direct + Indirect): Flashcard Revision Set

CAEnglishFlashcards

Card 1

What is the definition of 'income' under Section 2(24) of the Income-tax Act, 1961? List any five specific inclusions.

Section 2(24) defines 'income' inclusively. Five specific inclusions are: • Profit and gains of business or profession (clause (v)) • Capital receipts not chargeable to tax under section 45 (e.g., certain capital gains) — clause (i) • Value of any benefit or perquisite, whether convertible into money or not, arising from trade, profession, employment — clause (xv) • Any sum received from an unregistered firm — clause (xiv) • Superannuation fund contributions by employer above limits — clause (xx) The definition is wide and includes every species of profit that flows from employment, investment, or property.

Card 2

Explain the concept of 'Previous Year' under the Income-tax Act. What is its significance in taxation?

Previous Year (Section 3) means the financial year immediately preceding the assessment year. It is the year in which income is earned and taxed. Significance: • Income is charged to tax under section 4 only if it accrs/arises/receives during the previous year. • Surveys, searches, reassessment under section 148 apply with reference to previous year rules. • Deductions u/s 80C-80U are computed w.r.t. the previous year. • A new assesse may have an 'initial previous year' — a period of less than 12 months with specified rules.

Card 3

Define 'Aadhaar' and 'AAY' for the purposes of residential status determination under the Income-tax Act.

Section 6 reads these terms as: • Aadhaar: digital identification system (Section 3 of Aadhaar Act, 2016). • AAY: Assessment Year (the year commencing on 1st April next to the Previous Year). However, for residential status under Section 6, critical statuses include: • Resident: Physical stay ≥ 182 days in India during the PY, OR ≥ 60 days in current PY + ≥ 365 days in 4 preceding PYs (with relaxations for Indian citizens/PIOs leaving India). • Non-resident: Failure to meet either test.

Card 4

What is the scope of total income of a Non-Resident (NR) under Section 5(2)? Give three examples.

Section 5(2) limits NR's total income to: 1) Income received or deemed received in India. 2) Income accrued or arising in India. 3) Income accrued or arisen outside India but from a business controlled/profession set up in India (deemed accrual u/s 9(1)(ii)). Examples: • Interest on NRE account (accrued in India). • Salary for services rendered in India (accrued in India). • Foreign dividend received in India bank account (received in India).

Card 5

Under what conditions is salary income taxable in India for a Non-Resident employee working abroad? Refer to the 'Call Centre' type case law principle.

Salary is taxable in India when: 1) It accrues/arises in India — services rendered in India attract Indian tax. 2) If a Non-Resident performs duties partly in India and partly outside, salary attributable to duties performed in India is taxable. Principle from case law (Vodafone/Bharti type): the 'source' test looks at where the services are actually rendered, not merely where payment originates. If the employee is abroad for the entire period and all duties are performed outside India, the salary may be non-taxable in India unless received in India or attributed to an Indian business connection.

Card 6

Explain the rules for taxability of perquisites u/s 17(2). Differentiate between specified and non-specified perquisites for valuation.

Perquisites u/s 17(2) include: • Rent-free accommodation (clause (i)) • Specified services (club, gym, vacation travel) (clause (ii)) • Value of any other benefit or privilege (clause (iii)) Valuation differences: • Specified perquisites (e.g., provided stock options, specified services) — value determined by prescribed rules in Rule 3. • Non-specified perquisites — generally valued at actual cost to employer or market value. Rule 3 provides detailed formulae for rent-free house, owned vs leased accommodation, and perquisite tax rates.

Card 7

How is rent-free accommodation valued for a government employee vs. a private sector employee? Mention key percentage-based rules.

Valuation of rent-free accommodation u/s 17(2)(i): Government employee: • Value = 10% of salary (basic + DA) or actual rent paid by employer, whichever is less. Private sector: • Owned accommodation: 10% of salary (basic + DA) per annum. • Leased accommodation: actual rent paid by employer less standard deduction of 15% of rent. • Tier I city (Mumbai, Delhi, Chennai, Kolkata): higher brackets. City classification affects percentage rates prescribed u/s 17(2)(i) read with Rule 3.

Card 8

What is the treatment of contribution to Recognized Provident Fund (R.P.F.) under Section 16(ii) and Section 17(5)(v)?

Section 16(ii) allows deduction from Salary income: • Contribution to R.P.F. by employer — deductible up to limits prescribed u/s 17(5)(v). Contribution u/s 17(5)(v) is treated as perquisite NOT taxable if within prescribed limit; if exceeding limit, the excess is taxable as perquisite. The 'perquisite' characterization means it falls under section 17(5) specifically, overriding general perquisite valuation.

Card 9

Define 'House Property' income u/s 22. Explain the conditions for 'let out' vs 'self-occupied' property and their respective computation.

Section 22 charges income from House Property where the property is owned by the assessee, not let under a lease of 12 months or more, and used for business/profession or cannot be occupied by owner. Let Out Property: • Annual Value = Higher of Actual Rent Received (ARR) or Expected Annual Value (EAV) • Deductions: 30% standard deduction, interest on borrowed capital Self-Occupied Property: • Annual Value = nil • Deduction: Interest on borrowed capital up to ₹2,00,000 (₹30,00,000 if conditions u/s 24(b)(chi) satisfied) • Note: U/s 23, one self-occupied property can be chosen by taxpayer; second property treated as let-out.

Card 10

What are the deductions allowable on interest on borrowed capital for house property u/s 24(b)? Differentiate self-occupied and let-out positions.

Section 24(b) allows deduction for interest on borrowed capital: Self-Occupied Property: • Maximum deduction: ₹2,00,000 per year • Conditions for ₹30,00,000: acquisition/construction financed from loan taken after 1-Apr-1999, construction completed within 5 years from end of PY in which loan was taken. Let-Out Property: • No upper limit — full interest allowed • Pre-construction interest deductible in 5 equal instalments starting from P.Y. following completion of construction • Interest must be for purpose of acquisition/construction/repair/reconstruction.

Card 11

Explain the concept of 'Business Connection' (BC) under Section 9(1)(i) and how it deems income to accrue in India.

Business Connection (Section 9(1)(i)) is a factual relationship where a non-resident carries on business in India through: • A branch, office, factory, or place of business in India • An agent in India authorized to conclude contracts or habitually playing principal role leading to contract succession Effect: Income accruing/arising 'through or from' BC is deemed to accrue in India and is taxable even if received outside India. Test for BC: permanent commercial link, regularity, substance over form. The Supreme Court in CIT v. Vestergaard emphasized a continuing activity connected with business.

Card 12

When does the 'Depository Receipt' regime apply under Section 112A? How does it relate to capital gains tax on foreign listed shares?

Section 112A provides concessional flat rate of tax (10% + surcharge + cess) without indexation benefit for long-term capital gains (LTCG) arising on transfer of: • Listed equity shares/securities, • Units of UTI/equity oriented mutual funds, • Zero coupon bonds issued by Infrastructure Debt Companies (IDCs) Condition: Securities Transaction Tax (STT) must be payable on such transfer (except in case of issue/fraction allotment scenario). Applicable to residents and non-residents alike for gains arising from transfer.

Card 13

Compute short-term capital gain (STCG) when shares are sold within 12 months of acquisition. What is the applicable tax rate u/s 111A?

Short-term capital gain arises when: • Equity shares listed on recognized stock exchange are held ≤ 12 months, OR • Any other capital asset held ≤ 24 months (36 months for immovable property) Tax rate u/s 111A (for STCG on listed equity shares where STT is payable): • Flat 15% (plus surcharge + cess) Note: Indexation benefit is NOT available for STCG; full gain is taxable at 15%.

Card 14

Explain the set-off and carry-forward of capital losses under Sections 70-74. Can STCG be set off against LTCG and vice versa?

Capital loss set-off and carry-forward (Sections 70-74): Short-Term Capital Loss (STCL): • Set off against STCG or LTCG — both allowed • Carry forward: 8 assessment years from AY following loss year Long-Term Capital Loss (LTCL): • Set off ONLY against LTCG — NOT against STCG • Carry forward: 8 assessment years from AY following loss year Important restriction: Capital loss can only be set off against capital gains — cannot be set off against other heads of income.

Card 15

What is the exemption available on capital gains arising from the purchase of a residential house u/s 54? Specify conditions and limits.

Section 54 exemption on LTCG from transfer of long-term residential house property: Conditions: • Gain arises from transfer of long-term residential house property (residential building + land appurtenant) • Assesse must purchase one residential house within 2 years before or 1 year after transfer, OR construct a residential house within 3 years after transfer • Only one residential house claimed; second property attracts clawback Limit: Exemption = Lower of: • Actual cost of new house – LTCG, OR • Cost of new house Unutilized exemption must be deposited in Capital Gains Account Scheme (CGAS) before filing return; withdrawal permitted for purchase/construction only.

Card 16

Under Section 56(2)(x), when is receipt of property/money without consideration taxable? Differentiate between 'pecuniary consideration' below stamp value and gift from relative.

Section 56(2)(x) taxes certain receipts without adequate consideration: • Property (immovable): difference between stamp duty value and consideration, if consideration < stamp value • Immovable property without consideration: full stamp duty value taxable • Shares/etc.: if FMV exceeds consideration by > ₹50,000, difference taxable Exceptions (NOT taxable): • Gift from relatives (as defined u/s 56(2)(v)) — spouses, siblings, ancestors, descendants, etc. • Gifts on wedding • Gifts received u/s 56(2)(x) via will/holographic will
