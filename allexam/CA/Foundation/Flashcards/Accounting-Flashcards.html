CA Foundation - Accounting: Complete Topic Flashcards

CAEnglishFlashcards

Card 1

What is the Entity Concept in accounting?

The business is treated as a separate economic entity distinct from its owner(s). Only business transactions are recorded; personal transactions of the owner are excluded. This ensures clear separation between personal and business finances.

Card 2

State the Going Concern Assumption.

It is assumed that the business will continue to operate for the foreseeable future and has no intention or necessity to liquidate or cease operations. This assumption justifies the recording of fixed assets at cost and the charging of depreciation over useful lives rather than at liquidation values.

Card 3

Explain the Accrual Concept with an example.

Revenue and expenses are recognized when they are earned or incurred, not when cash is received or paid. Example: Interest due but not yet received is recorded as income in the year it accrues, even if cash is received later.

Card 4

What is the Matching Concept?

Expenses incurred during an accounting period must be matched against the revenues earned in the same period to ascertain true profit or loss. Unexpired or prepaid expenses are carried forward, and outstanding expenses are accrued.

Card 5

Define the Consistency Concept.

Accounting methods and practices should remain consistent from one accounting period to another to enable meaningful comparison. Changes, if any, must be disclosed with reasons and their financial effect.

Card 6

What does the Conservatism (Prudence) Concept imply?

Anticipate no profits but provide for all possible losses. Revenue profits are recognized only when realized; contingent losses are provided for. This prevents overstatement of assets and income.

Card 7

What is the Materiality Concept?

Only facts of significance that can influence the decision-making of users need be disclosed. Immaterial items may be treated simplistically (e.g., expensing low-value assets immediately rather than capitalizing and depreciating them).

Card 8

State the Real Equation of Account.

Assets = Liabilities + Capital. This fundamental accounting equation forms the basis of the double-entry system. Every debit has an equal and corresponding credit.

Card 9

Classify the following accounts and state their normal balances: Capital, Purchases, Rent Received, Outstanding Salary, Machinery.

Capital – Personal (Credit), Purchases – Nominal (Debit), Rent Received – Nominal (Credit), Outstanding Salary – Personal (Debit), Machinery – Real (Debit).

Card 10

What is the Golden Rule of Accounting for Real Accounts?

Debit what comes in, Credit what goes out. Applied to assets like machinery, building, cash, etc.

Card 11

What is the Golden Rule of Accounting for Personal Accounts?

Debit the receiver, Credit the giver. Applied to individuals, firms, companies, and institutions.

Card 12

What is the Golden Rule of Accounting for Nominal Accounts?

Debit all expenses and losses, Credit all incomes and gains. Applied to rent, salary, interest, commission, etc.

Card 13

Distinguish between Double Entry, Triple Entry, and Quadruple Entry systems.

Double entry: every transaction has two effects (debit and credit) – modern system. Triple entry: both sides recorded plus a third entry for verification (old Venetian method). Quadruple entry: each side split into two sub-entries (old Italian method, now obsolete).

Card 14

How is a Journal prepared? Explain the format and key rules.

Columns: Date, Particulars, L.F., Debit (Rs.), Credit (Rs.). Rules: Nominal accounts first (expenses/losses debited, incomes/gains credited); Personal accounts next (receiver debited, giver credited); Real accounts last (asset coming in debited, asset going out credited). narration required.

Card 15

What is the difference between a Cash Book and a Journal?

A Cash Book records only cash and bank transactions and serves as both a journal and ledger for cash/bank. A Journal records all non-cash transactions (credit sales, purchases, adjustments, etc.) and is the book of original entry for such items.

Card 16

Distinguish between Cash Book (Three Column) and Cash Book (Two Column).

Two-column cash book: Cash and Bank columns only. Three-column cash book: adds Discount column (two sub-columns – discount allowed and discount received) alongside cash and bank. Used when frequent discounts are given/received.

Card 17

What is a Contra Entry in a Cash Book? Give an example.

Transactions involving both cash and bank aspects are marked with 'C' on both debit and credit sides. Example: Cash deposited into bank – credited in Cash Book (cash goes out) and entered in Bank column of debit side (bank comes in).

Card 18

What is a Ledger? Why is it called the Book of Final Entry?

A Ledger is a book where journal entries are posted class-wise under appropriate accounts. It is the Book of Final Entry because the Journal (Book of Original Entry) transfers data here for systematic classification and summarization.

Card 19

How is posting from Journal to Ledger performed? State the steps.

Step 1: Identify the account to be debited and post the amount on the debit side with journal page reference. Step 2: Identify the account to be credited and post the amount on the credit side with journal page reference. Step 3: Balance each ledger account at the end of the period by carrying down the difference.

Card 20

What is a Trial Balance? What are its objectives and limitations?

A Trial Balance is a statement of debit and credit balances to test arithmetical accuracy. Objectives: verify journal-to-ledger posting accuracy; assist in preparing final accounts. Limitations: does not detect omission of transactions, compensating errors, wrong postings to wrong accounts with correct amount, or complete non-recording.

Card 21

List the common types of errors and state which ones do NOT affect the Trial Balance.

Errors not affecting TB: (1) Error of Omission – transaction completely omitted. (2) Error of Commission – wrong account but correct amount posted. (3) Error of Principle – wrong class of account (e.g., expense recorded as asset). (4) Compensating Errors – two or more errors cancel each other. (5) Error of Original Entry – wrong amount in both debit and credit.

Card 22

What is Bank Reconciliation Statement (BRS)? Why is it prepared?

BRS is a statement reconciling the cash book bank balance with the bank passbook balance as on a date. Reasons for discrepancy: timing differences (cheques issued but not presented, cheques deposited but not cleared), bank charges/interest not in cash book, direct deposits by customers, errors in cash book or passbook.

Card 23

If the Passbook shows an Overdraft of Rs. 50,000, what adjustment is made to arrive at the Cash Book balance? Explain the logic.

An overdraft in the Passbook means the bank owes the business negative balance. To reconcile with the Cash Book: start from the overdraft and ADD cheques deposited but not collected, SUBTRACT cheques issued but not presented, ADD bank charges/interest debited by bank (not in cash book), SUBTRACT direct credits by bank not in cash book.

Card 24

Define a Bill of Exchange.

A written instrument containing an unconditional order, signed by the maker, directing a certain person to pay a certain sum of money only to, or to the order of, a specified person or bearer. Governed by the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881.

Card 25

Differentiate between a Bill of Exchange and a Promissory Note.

Bill of Exchange: order instrument, two parties (drawer and drawee). Promissory Note: promise instrument, two parties (maker and payee). In BE, drawer orders payment; in PN, maker promises payment. Both are negotiable instruments.

Card 26

What happens when a bill is discounted with the bank before maturity? How is the discount treated?

Discounting means the holder sells the bill to the bank before due date for immediate cash (after deducting bank discount). The bank discount is treated as an expense. If the bill is honoured at maturity, the acceptor pays the bank directly. If dishonoured, the holder is liable to pay the bank (contingent liability until then).

Card 27

Define Depreciation. State two main reasons for its provision.

Depreciation is the permanent, progressive decrease in the book value of a fixed asset due to usage, passage of time, obsolescence, or exhaustion. Reasons: (1) To show true financial position by reflecting actual asset value. (2) To ascertain true profit by matching cost of asset use with revenues earned.

Card 28

Compare the Straight Line Method and Reducing Balance Method of depreciation.

Straight Line Method: depreciation is constant each year on original cost. Suitable when asset's utility is uniform. Reducing Balance Method: depreciation is calculated on written-down value each year, so amount decreases over time. Suitable when asset efficiency declines with age.

Card 29

What is the formula for Straight Line Method depreciation? Illustrate with an example.

Annual Depreciation = (Cost – Residual Value) / Useful Life. Example: Asset cost Rs. 1,00,000, residual value Rs. 10,000, life 9 years → Annual depreciation = (1,00,000 – 10,000)/9 = Rs. 10,000 p.a.

Card 30

Under Reducing Balance Method, if an asset costs Rs. 1,00,000 and depreciation is 10% p.a., what is the WDV after 2 years?

Year 1: Depreciation = 10% of 1,00,000 = Rs. 10,000; WDV = Rs. 90,000. Year 2: Depreciation = 10% of 90,000 = Rs. 9,000; WDV = Rs. 81,000.

Card 31

What is a Provision for Bad Debts? How does it differ from a Bad Debt?

Bad Debt: an specific debt known to be irrecoverable – written off directly. Provision for Bad Debts: an estimate of potentially irrecoverable debts based on past experience – shown as a deduction from Sundry Debtors on the asset side. Bad Debt reduces debtors directly; Provision creates a separate reserve account.

Card 32

If total debtors are Rs. 50,000 and a provision of 5% is to be maintained, what is the journal entry for creating the provision?

Bad Debts A/c Dr. 2,500 To Provision for Bad Debts A/c 2,500 (Being provision for bad debts created at 5% of debtors Rs. 50,000). The provision appears as a deduction from debtors in the Balance Sheet.

Card 33

If a previous provision for bad debts of Rs. 3,000 exists and a new provision of Rs. 2,500 is required, what is the treatment?

Decrease in provision = Rs. 3,000 – Rs. 2,500 = Rs. 500. Journal: Provision for Bad Debts A/c Dr. 500 To Bad Debts A/c 500 (Being excess provision reversed). This credit to Bad Debts increases profit.

Card 34

What is a Provision for Discount on Debtors? When is it created?

It is a provision for early payment discounts expected to be allowed to debtors who settle accounts promptly. Created when a discount is expected to be given on collection of debtors. Shown as a deduction from Sundry Debtors (after bad debt provision) on the asset side of the Balance Sheet.

Card 35

Distinguish between Fixed Capital and Fluctuating Capital for partners.

Fixed Capital: partners' capital accounts remain unchanged; all profits/losses, drawings, interest on capital, etc. are recorded in separate Current Accounts. Fluctuating Capital: all adjustments are recorded in a single Capital Account which keeps changing. Under SA 401, fixed capital is more commonly used in larger partnerships.

Card 36

Calculate the goodwill using the Average Profits Method: Profits for last 3 years were Rs. 50,000, Rs. 60,000, Rs. 70,000. Goodwill is valued at 3 years' purchase of average profit.

Average Profit = (50,000 + 60,000 + 70,000)/3 = Rs. 60,000. Goodwill = 3 × 60,000 = Rs. 1,80,000.

Card 37

What is the Super Profit Method of valuing goodwill? Define super profit.

Super Profit = Actual Average Profit – Normal Profit (Normal Capital Employed × Normal Rate of Return). Goodwill = Super Profit × Number of Years' Purchase. Normal profit is the return expected at the normal rate on capital employed.

Card 38

When a partner is admitted, how is the Gain Ratio calculated?

Gain Ratio = New Share – Old Share (of the sacrificing partner). Alternatively, Gain Ratio = Sacrificing Ratio among old partners (if existing sharing ratio changes). All partners' old shares must be determined first, then new shares allocated to arriving at each partner's gain/sacrifice.

Card 39

What is the revaluation account? What purposes does it serve upon admission of a partner?

Revaluation Account (Profit & Loss Adjustment Account) records the increase/decrease in value of assets and liabilities, unrecorded assets/liabilities, and prior period adjustments as on the date of admission. Its net balance (profit or loss) is distributed among old partners in their old profit-sharing ratio before the new partner's admission.

Card 40

What is the Journal entry for bringing in goodwill of a new partner in kind (without cash transfer)?

If the new partner brings goodwill but it is not to be distributed in cash: Goodwill A/c Dr. To New Partner's Capital A/c. The goodwill remains in the firm but is NOT distributed to old partners.

Card 41

A firm's capital is Rs. 1,00,000. Old partners A and B share profits in 3:2 ratio. C is admitted for 1/5th share. Calculate the sacrificing ratio if A and B sacrifice equally.

C's share = 1/5. Remaining share = 4/5 shared by A and B in 3:2. A's new share = 4/5 × 3/5 = 12/25. B's new share = 4/5 × 2/5 = 8/25. Old shares: A = 3/5 = 15/25, B = 2/5 = 10/25. A's sacrifice = 15/25 – 12/25 = 3/25. B's sacrifice = 10/25 – 8/25 = 2/25. Sacrificing Ratio = 3:2.

Card 42

What happens to accumulated reserves and profits on admission of a partner?

Accumulated reserves (General Reserve, Profit & Loss A/c balance, Reserve Fund, etc.) are distributed among the OLD partners in their old profit-sharing ratio via the Revaluation/Adjustment Account. The new partner does not get a share of these prior reserves unless specifically agreed.

Card 43

What is the treatment of goodwill brought in cash by a new partner, including distribution to old partners?

Step 1: New partner brings goodwill in cash: Bank A/c Dr. To New Partner's Capital A/c. Step 2: Distribute goodwill among old partners in sacrificing ratio: Old Partner's Capital A/c Dr. (each) To New Partner's Capital A/c (new partner's share of goodwill is distributed to old partners in sacrificing ratio). Note: Some versions distribute to old partners only in sacrificing ratio proportionally.

Card 44

On retirement of a partner, how is the gain ratio among continuing partners calculated?

Gain Ratio = New Share – Old Share of continuing partners. The retiring partner's share is redistributed among remaining partners in the new profit-sharing ratio agreed upon. If no new ratio is specified, remaining partners continue in their old ratio.

Card 45

What is the journal entry for transfer of the retiring partner's share of goodwill to continuing partners' capital accounts?

Continuing Partners' Capital A/c(s) Dr. (in gaining ratio) To Retiring Partner's Capital A/c (retiring partner's share of goodwill). This adjusts the capital accounts to reflect the value transferred.

Card 46

What is joint life policy? How is it accounted for in partnership?

A Joint Life Policy is taken by partners on the life of one of them to provide funds for purchasing the deceased partner's share. Premium paid is debited to Joint Life Policy Account (treated as an investment). On death of the insured partner, the policy proceeds (received by nominees) are credited to Joint Life Policy Account and the balance is transferred to the deceased partner's Legal Heirs' Capital A/c.

Card 47

In the death of a partner, how is the profit up to the date of death calculated?

Profit up to date of death is calculated on the basis of the past year's profit or on the basis of time/apportionment. Common methods: (1) Based on last completed year's profit – apportion for the fraction of year. (2) Based on average profits adjusted for the period. The deceased partner's legal heir gets their share of this profit.

Card 48

What is the treatment of revaluation of assets and liabilities on the death of a partner?

Similar to retirement: assets are revalued and liabilities reasessed. The net profit or loss on revaluation is transferred to all partners' (including deceased partner's) capital accounts in old ratio. The deceased partner's share of revaluation profit/loss and accumulated reserves is then credited/debited to their legal heirs' capital account.

Card 49

Explain the concept of Dissolution of a Partnership Firm vs. Change in Constitution.

Dissolution: termination of the entire partnership relationship among ALL partners; firm ceases to exist. Change in constitution (admission, retirement, death): partnership continues with altered arrangement among surviving partners. In dissolution, all assets are sold, liabilities paid, and surplus distributed; in change, the firm continues with revised terms.

Card 50

What is the realisation account? What entries are passed through it on dissolution?

Realisation Account records the sale of assets, payment of liabilities, and distribution of surplus/deficiency on dissolution. Entries: Assets transferred at book value to Realisation (credit side); Liabilities transferred (debit side); Sale proceeds of assets (debit side to Realisation, credit to bank); Payment of liabilities (credit side to Realisation, debit to bank); Expenses of realisation (debit to Realisation).

Card 51

How is the deficiency in realization distributed among partners after dissolution?

After crediting all receipts and debiting all payments in Realisation Account, the balance (deficiency if debit side exceeds credit side) is transferred to Partners' Capital Accounts in their old profit-sharing ratio (unless otherwise agreed). Each partner bears the deficiency in their profit-sharing ratio.

Card 52

What is the treatment of a contingent liability on dissolution if it materializes?

If a contingent liability (not recorded in books) becomes payable on dissolution, it is debited to Realisation Account. If assets are insufficient to cover it, the deficiency is borne by partners in their old profit-sharing ratio. If paid by one partner individually, that partner is compensated by others in the deficiency ratio.

Card 53

Define 'Complete Record System' vs. 'Incomplete Record System' in accounting.

Complete Record System: Full double-entry system maintained with Journals, Ledgers, Cash Book. Financial statements can be prepared directly. Incomplete Record System: Single-entry or incomplete records maintained; no systematic ledger. Additional information and reconstruction of accounts needed to prepare financial statements.

Card 54

How is Closing Stock valued when preparing Final Accounts?

Closing Stock is valued at cost price or market price, whichever is lower (Conservatism Concept). It appears on the asset side of the Balance Sheet and is also credited to the Trading Account. If closing stock is not given in the Trial Balance, it only appears in the Balance Sheet.

Card 55

List the major Adjustments typically made at the time of preparing Final Accounts (Trading and P&L + Balance Sheet).

(1) Outstanding Expenses – debit P&L, show as liability in BS. (2) Prepaid Expenses – credit P&L, show as asset in BS. (3) Accrued Income – debit P&L, show as asset in BS. (4) Deferred Income – credit P&L, show as liability in BS. (5) Depreciation – debit P&L, reduce asset value in BS. (6) Closing Stock – credited to Trading, shown as asset in BS.

Card 56

What is a Receipts and Payments Account? Distinguish it from an Income and Expenditure Account.

Receipts and Payments Account is a nominal account summarizing all cash/bank receipts and payments (both revenue and capital) of a non-profit organization. Income and Expenditure Account is similar to a Profit & Loss Account, recording only revenue items (accrual basis) to ascertain surplus or deficit. R&P includes capital receipts/payments; I&E excludes them.

Card 57

How is the Capital Fund of a non-profit organization calculated when preparing the Balance Sheet?

Capital Fund = Total Assets (excluding cash/bank) + Opening Capital Fund + Surplus from Income & Expenditure Account – Total Liabilities (excluding capital fund). Alternatively: Capital Fund = Excess of Assets over Liabilities at the beginning, adjusted for surplus/deficit of the current year.

Card 58

If subscription received includes Rs. 10,000 for the next year, how is it treated in the Income and Expenditure Account and Balance Sheet?

Subscription received in advance (Rs. 10,000) is a liability. Treatment: Deduct Rs. 10,000 from total subscriptions received in the Income and Expenditure Account. Show Rs. 10,000 as 'Subscription Received in Advance' under Liabilities in the Balance Sheet.

Card 59

How is General Reserve and P&L A/c balance of a firm treated when preparing the Balance Sheet of a company?

General Reserve and Profit & Loss A/c balance are shown under 'Reserves and Surplus' in the Equity section of the Balance Sheet as per Schedule III of the Companies Act, 2013. They form part of shareholders' funds.

Card 60

What is the treatment of preliminary expenses in the Balance Sheet?

Preliminary expenses (legal fees, stamp duty, underwriting commissions, etc.) incurred before incorporation are shown under 'Non-current Assets – Other Non-current Assets' as 'Securities Account' or directly as 'Preliminary Expenses' to be written off over a period. They are amortized over 5-10 years as per company practice.

Card 61

If a partner's capital account shows a debit balance (deficiency), what is the treatment in the Balance Sheet of a partnership?

A debit balance in a partner's Capital Account represents a deficiency (partner owes money to the firm). It is shown on the Assets side of the Balance Sheet under 'Loans and Advances' or specifically as 'Amount due from Partner.' The partner must contribute this amount to the firm.

Card 62

What is the treatment of workmen's participation in profits? How is it shown in the Balance Sheet?

Workmen's participation in profits is an appropriation of profit after the net profit is arrived at. It is shown on the Liabilities side of the Balance Sheet under 'Current Liabilities' as 'Workmen's Participation in Profits Payable' until actually paid.

Card 63

Distinguish between a Bill Receivable and a Bill Payable.

Bill Receivable: an asset for the holder (creditor) who is entitled to receive payment on the due date. Bill Payable: a liability for the drawee/acceptor who is obliged to pay on the due date. Both arise from the same bill of exchange but are viewed from different parties.

Card 64

If a bill receivable is discounted with the bank and later dishonoured, what is the entry for the holder?

On dishonour: Bills Receivable A/c Dr. To Bank A/c (amount of bill). Contingent liability is removed. The holder must pay the bank the full amount and then pursue recovery from the acceptor separately.

Card 65

What is the Journal entry when a partner retires and is paid his capital along with interest on capital at 6% p.a.?

Retiring Partner's Capital A/c Dr. (total including interest) To Bank A/c (amount paid). Interest on capital is calculated from the date of last balance sheet to the date of retirement. Total = Opening Capital + Share of Profit + Share of Goodwill + Interest on Capital – Drawings.

Card 66

What is the treatment of undervaluation of stock in the Revaluation Account on admission?

Undervaluation of stock means the asset was recorded at less than its actual value. On revaluation: Stock A/c Dr. To Revaluation A/c. This increases profit and thus increases the revaluation surplus distributed among old partners. It improves the asset's book value.

Card 67

How is the sacrificing ratio different from the gain ratio?

Sacrificing Ratio: the ratio in which OLD partners give up part of their share in favour of the NEW (admitting) partner. Gain Ratio: the ratio in which CONTINUING partners acquire the share of the RETIRING/DECEASED partner. Both use the same mathematical calculation (New Share – Old Share) but apply to different events.

Card 68

What is Contingent Liability in the context of a partnership? Give two examples.

Contingent Liability is a potential obligation that arises from past events and whose existence will be confirmed only on the occurrence or non-occurrence of uncertain future events. Examples: (1) Acceptance of bills of exchange by one partner on behalf of the firm – liability arises if the bill is dishonoured. (2) Guarantees given by the firm.

Card 69

On admission, if a partner brings additional goodwill in cash, how is the journal entry recorded if the firm policy is to maintain goodwill permanently?

Goodwill A/c Dr. (amount brought in kind/cash) To New Partner's Capital A/c. Also, Old Partner's Capital A/c(s) Dr. (in sacrificing ratio) To New Partner's Capital A/c (if distributing to old partners). For permanent retention: Goodwill A/c Dr. To Old Partners' Capital A/c(s) in old ratio, without writing it off further.

Card 70

What are the three formats of Balance Sheet prescribed under Schedule III of the Companies Act, 2013?

Format I: Horizontal format with items listed under headings (I. EQUITY AND LIABILITIES, II. ASSETS) and sub-headings (Shareholders' funds, Non-current liabilities, Current liabilities; Non-current assets, Current assets). Format II and III are alternative presentations with similar sub-classifications. All companies must follow the vertical format with prescribed line items.

Card 71

What is the treatment of a loss on revaluation of assets in the Revaluation Account on admission?

Loss on revaluation is debited to the Revaluation Account and credited to the respective asset accounts (reducing their book values). The net loss in the Revaluation Account is then transferred to the old partners' Capital Accounts in their old profit-sharing ratio, reducing their capitals accordingly.

Card 72

How is closing stock appearing in the Trial Balance treated differently from closing stock not appearing in the Trial Balance?

Closing stock appearing in TB: Credited to Trading Account AND shown on the asset side of the Balance Sheet. Closing stock NOT appearing in TB: Shown ONLY on the asset side of the Balance Sheet; no entry in Trading Account.

Card 73

If the Profit & Loss Account shows a debit balance of Rs. 50,000 (loss), how is it treated in the Balance Sheet?

A debit balance in P&L Account represents accumulated losses. It is shown on the Assets side of the Balance Sheet under 'Accumulated Losses and Deficits' or deducted from 'Reserves and Surplus' under Shareholders' Funds, reducing the total equity.

Card 74

What is the Journal entry for writing off goodwill on the retirement of a partner?

Continuing Partners' Capital A/c(s) Dr. (in new profit-sharing ratio) To Retiring Partner's Capital A/c (retiring partner's share of goodwill). This is done when goodwill is not shown in the books and the retiring partner is compensated for their share.

Card 75

In dissolution, if a partner takes over an asset at an agreed value, what is the journal entry?

Partner's Capital A/c Dr. (agreed value) To Realisation A/c (agreed value). This records the transfer of the asset to the partner at the agreed value, adjusting their capital account accordingly. The difference between book value and agreed value is absorbed in the Realisation Account.

Card 76

What is the treatment of depreciation on fixed assets in Final Accounts?

Depreciation is debited to the Profit & Loss Account (or Trading Account for manufacturing assets) and credited to the respective Asset Account (directly) or to a Accumulated Depreciation Account. In the Balance Sheet, the asset is shown net of accumulated depreciation under the relevant non-current asset heading.

Card 77

How is interest on drawings treated in partnership accounts?

Interest on drawings is income for the firm. Journal entry: Partner's Capital A/c Dr. To Interest on Drawings A/c. It is credited to the Profit & Loss Appropriation Account and increases the partner's capital account. It is a charge against the partner, not an appropriation of profit.

Card 78

What is the treatment of a guarantee given by one partner to another partner regarding minimum profit?

If Partner A guarantees Partner B a minimum profit of Rs. X, and B's actual share is less than Rs. X, A must make up the shortfall. Journal: A's Capital A/c Dr. To B's Capital A/c (shortfall amount). This adjustment is made in the Profit & Loss Appropriation Account after distributing profits according to the profit-sharing ratio.

Card 79

Explain the concept of 'Common Date' for preparing accounts in admission of a partner.

All accounts (Revaluation, Partner's Capitals, Goodwill, Reserves) are prepared as on the date of admission. This means: (1) Revaluation is done as on admission date. (2) Reserves are distributed in old ratio as on admission date. (3) New partner's capital is brought in as on admission date. (4) Assets and liabilities are adjusted to their values on that specific date.

Card 80

If a bill of exchange is renewed after maturity (drawn by acceptor in favour of holder), what is the accounting treatment?

Old Bill Receivable is cancelled and a new Bill Receivable is created. Journal: New Bill Receivable A/c Dr. To Old Bill Receivable A/c. This extension gives the debtor more time. Any discount/interest charged on renewal is recorded separately.

Card 81

What is the treatment of a provision for bad debts when it appears in the Trial Balance as a credit balance?

A provision for bad debts appearing as a credit balance in the TB is shown on the liabilities side of the Balance Sheet under 'Reserves and Surplus' (as a general reserve-type item). If an additional provision is required, the adjustment is made via the Profit & Loss Appropriation Account; if a decrease, it is credited to P&L.

Card 82

In a non-profit organization, how is subscription received for multiple years treated?

Only the subscription belonging to the current accounting year is credited to the Income and Expenditure Account. Advance subscription (for future years) is shown as a liability. Arrears of subscription (due but not received) are accrued – added to subscription income and shown as an asset. The net subscription for the current year = Received + Accrued – Advanced.

Card 83

What is the effect on the Trial Balance if an asset purchased for cash is wrongly recorded as purchased on credit?

This is an error of original entry/commission. The Trial Balance will still tally because both debit and credit are correct (Purchase A/c Dr. instead of Asset A/c Dr.; Supplier A/c Cr. instead of Cash/Bank Cr.). The TB proves arithmetical accuracy but does not detect this type of misclassification.

Card 84

How is the balance in the Journal Proper used in partnership accounting?

Journal Proper is used for entries that don't fit in special journals (Sales, Purchases, Cash, Bills). In partnership, it records: admission of partner entries, revaluation adjustments, goodwill adjustments, retirement/dissolution entries, and any other adjusting entries not covered by day books.

Card 85

What are the key features of a Partnership Deed that affect accounting?

Key features: (1) Profit-sharing ratio (affects distribution of profits/losses). (2) Interest on capital (rate and whether it is cumulative/non-cumulative). (3) Interest on drawings (rate and frequency). (4) Salary/Commission to partners (fixed or profit-linked). (5) Goodwill treatment. (6) Admission/Retirement terms. These directly determine accounting entries in P&L Appropriation Account.

Card 86

If a partner is entitled to a salary of Rs. 12,000 p.m. plus 10% commission on net profit after charging such salary, how is the commission calculated?

Let Net Profit before salary & commission = P. Salary = 12,000 × 12 = Rs. 1,44,000. Commission = 10% of (P – 1,44,000). This is a case of commission on profit AFTER charging commission, requiring solving the equation. If P = Rs. 2,00,000: Commission = 10% of (2,00,000 – 1,44,000) = Rs. 5,600. Total charge = 1,44,000 + 5,600 = Rs. 1,49,600.

Card 87

What is the 'Correspondence Principle' in the context of Bank Reconciliation?

The Correspondence Principle states that the Cash Book and Bank Pass Book should correspond with each other when properly prepared. Differences arise due to timing lags (cheques in transit) or errors. BRS bridges these differences to ensure correspondence between the two records.

Card 88

In the death of a partner, how is the executor's/heir's capital account prepared?

Heir's Capital A/c: Dr. side – amounts payable TO heir (share of capital, reserves, goodwill, revaluation profit, profit up to date of death, interest on capital). Cr. side – amounts payable BY heir (drawings, loss share). The balance (credit side excess) is the amount payable to the heir; debit side excess means heir owes the firm.

Card 89

What is the treatment of 'Loss on Realisation' in the Balance Sheet of a dissolved firm?

Loss on Realisation is borne by partners in their old profit-sharing ratio. After transferring to Partners' Capital Accounts, if any partner's capital account shows a debit balance (deficiency), it is shown as an asset on the Balance Sheet under 'Loans and Advances' or 'Amount due from Partner'. The firm's remaining assets are distributed to settle liabilities and partners' claims.

Card 90

How is the balance of a partner's Current Account shown when it is in credit vs. debit?

Credit balance in Current Account: shown on the Liabilities side of the Balance Sheet under 'Current Liabilities' or 'Partners' Accounts'. Debit balance: shown on the Assets side under 'Loans and Advances' or 'Current Assets'. The Current Account tracks all adjustments (profit share, interest, salary, drawings) apart from the fixed capital.

Card 91

What is the treatment of a bill drawn by a partner on the firm personally (not in the firm's name) during dissolution?

Such a bill is the personal liability of that partner alone, not the firm's liability. It is not recorded in the firm's books. Upon dissolution, the partner must settle this personally. It does not affect the Realisation Account or other partners' accounts.

Card 92

In the preparation of a Balance Sheet, how are 'Secured Loans' and 'Unsecured Loans' classified under Schedule III?

Under Schedule III, 'Secured Loans' (e.g., loans from banks against security) are shown under 'Non-current Liabilities' as 'Long-term Borrowings'. 'Unsecured Loans' (e.g., deposits not secured by any asset) are also shown under Non-current Liabilities or Current Liabilities depending on their maturity period. Both require disclosure of nature and terms.

Card 93

What is the 'Concept of Entity' in the context of a Company vs. a Partnership Firm?

In a Company, the entity is legally separate from its shareholders – shareholders have limited liability. In a Partnership, the entity and owners are not legally separate – partners have unlimited liability. This affects accounting: company accounts follow Companies Act requirements; partnership accounts follow the Partnership Act with more flexibility.

Card 94

If a partner retires and the remaining partners decide to reappraise assets, what journal entries are passed?

Entry 1 (Appreciation): Revaluation A/c Dr. To Asset A/c (for increase in value). Entry 2 (Depreciation): Asset A/c Dr. To Revaluation A/c (for decrease in value). Entry 3: Revaluation A/c Dr./Cr. To Old Partners' Capital A/c (in old ratio) for net profit/loss on revaluation.

Card 95

What is the difference between 'Provision for Bad Debts' and 'Reserve for Bad Debts'?

Provision for Bad Debts: created on estimated doubtful debts; shown as deduction from Debtors; affects profit. Reserve for Bad Debts: created out of profits as a contingency reserve; shown under Reserves and Surplus on liabilities side; does not reduce debtors. Provision is mandatory for accurate asset valuation; Reserve is discretionary.

Card 96

How is the 'Right to Receive' vs. 'Liability to Pay' tested in journal entries for a bill of exchange?

For the holder: Bill Receivable A/c Dr. To Drawer/Creditor A/c (right to receive). For the drawee/acceptor: Drawee/Debtor A/c Dr. To Bill Payable A/c (liability to pay). On acceptance: No entry needed (already recorded in the books of both parties). On maturity: Bank A/c Dr. To Bill Receivable/Payable A/c respectively.

Card 97

What is the impact on the Trial Balance if a purchase of goods for cash Rs. 10,000 is recorded as Purchase A/c Dr. 5,000 and Cash A/c Cr. 5,000?

Both debit and credit are short by Rs. 5,000. The Trial Balance will still tally because the errors are equal and opposite. However, Purchase A/c and Cash A/c will each show incorrect balances. This is an Error of Original Entry affecting both accounts equally.

Card 98

In a partnership, if the profit-sharing ratio is altered on admission, how is the new ratio determined?

The new profit-sharing ratio is mutually agreed upon by all partners (old + new). It replaces the old ratio from the date of admission. The gaining/sacrificing ratio is computed by comparing the new ratio with the old ratio for each partner. Only this new ratio is used for future profit distribution.

Card 99

What is the treatment of 'Reserve Fund' on the admission of a partner?

Reserve Fund is a accumulated reserve created from past profits. On admission, it is distributed among the OLD partners in their old profit-sharing ratio. Journal: Reserve Fund A/c Dr. To Old Partners' Capital A/c (in old ratio). The new partner does not share in this pre-admission reserve.

Card 100

If a bill receivable is endorsed to a supplier, what is the accounting treatment?

Endorsement of a bill receivable means transferring the right to receive payment to another party (supplier) in settlement of a liability. Journal: Supplier A/c Dr. To Bill Receivable A/c. The bill is now the supplier's asset, and the firm's liability to the supplier is discharged.

Card 101

What are the key differences between a Partnership Firm and a Limited Liability Partnership (LLP)?

Partnership: governed by Partnership Act, 1932; unlimited liability; no separate legal entity from partners. LLP: governed by LLP Act, 2008; limited liability for partners; separate legal entity; perpetual succession; requires registration; more formal compliance. Both allow flexible profit-sharing ratios.

Card 102

How is a 'Capital Redemption Reserve' created and what is its purpose?

Created when a company buys back its own shares or redeems preference shares out of profits. The amount equal to the nominal value of shares redeemed is transferred from distributable profits to Capital Redemption Reserve (CRR). Purpose: to maintain capital base; CRR is treated as paid-up capital and cannot be distributed as dividends.

Card 103

What is the treatment of 'Deferred Tax Liability' in the Balance Sheet?

Deferred Tax Liability arises due to timing differences between accounting profit and taxable profit. It is shown under 'Non-current Liabilities' as 'Deferred Tax Liabilities' in the Balance Sheet under Schedule III. It represents tax payable in future periods when the timing differences reverse.

Card 104

If a firm's books are maintained on incomplete records, how is the opening capital determined?

Opening Capital = Assets (at beginning) – Liabilities (at beginning). If incomplete records exist, the capital is reconstructed using the equation: Capital at end = Capital at beginning + Additional Capital introduced + Net Profit – Drawings. Rearranging: Capital at beginning = Capital at end – Additional Capital – Net Profit + Drawings.

Card 105

What is the 'Prudence Concept' test: should a contingent gain be recorded?

No. Under the Prudence Concept, contingent gains are NOT recognized until they are virtually certain to be realized. Only contingent losses are provided for if probable. Recording contingent gains would violate prudence and overstate assets/income.

Card 106

In dissolution, if assets are taken over by a partner at an agreed value higher than book value, what is the journal entry?

Partner's Capital A/c Dr. (agreed value) To Realisation A/c (agreed value). The Realisation Account will show a profit (credit balance) because the agreed value exceeds the book value transferred to Realisation. This profit is distributed among partners in their old profit-sharing ratio.
