CA Foundation – Business Economics Flashcards

CAEnglishFlashcards

Card 1

Business Economics is the application of economic theory and methodology to analyse business problems. Which type of economics does it primarily rely upon?

Microeconomics (as well as parts of macroeconomics for broader environmental analysis).

Card 2

Economics is classified as positive economics and normative economics on the basis of what criterion?

Positive economics states 'what is' (factual, testable statements), while normative economics states 'what ought to be' (value-based, subjective recommendations).

Card 3

The law of demand establishes an inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded, holding all else constant. What is the technical term for this 'all else constant' assumption?

Ceteris Paribus.

Card 4

If the price of a commodity falls and total expenditure on it increases, the demand for that commodity is said to be:

Elastic (|Ed| > 1). When price falls, quantity demanded rises by a proportionately larger amount, so total outlay increases.

Card 5

Which of the following will cause a RIGHTWARD shift of the demand curve?

An increase in consumer income (for a normal good). Note: a change in the commodity's own price causes movement ALONG the curve, not a shift.

Card 6

What happens to total revenue when price is reduced if demand is unit elastic (Ed = 1)?

Total revenue remains unchanged. The percentage rise in quantity demanded exactly offsets the percentage fall in price.

Card 7

Point elasticity of demand measures elasticity:

At a specific point on the demand curve, calculated as (dQ/dP) × (P/Q) or (ΔQ/ΔP) × (P/Q) for arc approximation.

Card 8

Cross elasticity of demand for substitute goods is:

Positive (+Ve). If the price of tea rises, demand for coffee increases, giving a positive cross elasticity.

Card 9

Price elasticity of supply measures the responsiveness of quantity supplied to a change in:

Own price of the commodity. Formula: Es = (% change in Qs) / (% change in P).

Card 10

In the case of a perfectly vertical supply curve, price elasticity of supply is:

Zero (Perfectly Inelastic). Quantity supplied remains unchanged regardless of price changes.

Card 11

The law of variable proportions applies in which time horizon?

Short Run — where at least one factor is fixed and others are variable.

Card 12

Which of the following costs remains unchanged at all levels of output in the short run?

Fixed Cost (e.g., rent of factory building, salary of permanent staff). Variable costs change with output.

Card 13

When marginal cost is falling, average total cost must also be falling. True or False?

False. ATC continues to fall even after MC starts rising, until MC intersects ATC at its minimum point.

Card 14

Long-run average cost (LRAC) curve is known as the 'envelope curve' because:

It envelops all short-run average cost curves, representing the lowest possible per-unit cost for each output when all factors are variable.

Card 15

Under perfect competition, why is the demand curve facing an individual firm perfectly elastic?

Because the firm is a price taker — its output is too small to influence the market price, and buyers have perfect information about uniform prices.

Card 16

A monopolist maximises profit where:

Marginal Revenue (MR) = Marginal Cost (MC), and the MC curve cuts the MR curve from below. Price is then read off the demand curve.

Card 17

Under monopoly, price is generally ______ marginal cost, unlike perfect competition where price equals marginal cost.

Greater than (P > MC). The monopolist restricts output to raise price above marginal cost, creating deadweight loss.

Card 18

What is the difference between a change in demand and a change in quantity demanded?

Change in demand = shift of the entire curve (caused by non-price factors like income, tastes). Change in quantity demanded = movement along the same curve (caused only by price change).

Card 19

Consumer surplus is defined as:

The difference between the maximum price a consumer is willing to pay and the actual price paid. Graphically, it is the area below the demand curve and above the market price.

Card 20

A Giffen good is characterised by:

An upward-sloping demand curve (violates the law of demand). It is an inferior good where the income effect outweighs the substitution effect when price falls.

Card 21

The expansion path shows:

The locus of least-cost combinations of two variable inputs (e.g., labour and capital) as output expands, connecting the tangency points of isoquants with isocost lines.

Card 22

Which stage of production is considered the 'stage of rational production'?

Stage II — where both MP and AP are declining but MP is still positive (AP has reached its maximum and starts falling). Economists argue that firms operate only in Stage II.

Card 23

Isoquants are assumed to be convex to the origin because of:

Diminishing Marginal Rate of Technical Substitution (MRTS). As more of one input replaces another, increasingly larger amounts of the second input are needed to maintain the same output.

Card 24

Economic cost includes:

Both explicit (accounting) costs AND implicit (opportunity) costs. Accounting cost considers only explicit costs paid in money.

Card 25

A price-discriminating monopolist charges higher prices from consumers who have:

More inelastic (less elastic) demand. The monopolist extracts more consumer surplus from groups less sensitive to price changes.

Card 26

Under perfect price discrimination, what happens to consumer surplus?

It becomes zero. The monopolist charges each consumer exactly their willingness to pay, capturing the entire area under the demand curve above MC.

Card 27

The kinked demand curve model explains:

Rigidity of price in oligopoly. The 'kink' at the current price creates a discontinuity (vertical gap) in the MR curve, meaning cost changes within that gap do not alter the profit-maximising price.

Card 28

Money multiplies into bank deposits through the mechanism of:

Fractional reserve banking — banks keep a fraction of deposits as reserves and lend out the rest, which gets re-deposited and re-lent, creating multiple rounds of credit expansion.

Card 29

Nominal GDP differs from Real GDP because Nominal GDP:

Is measured at current market prices and includes changes in the price level (inflation/deflation), while Real GDP is measured at constant base-year prices, isolating pure output changes.

Card 30

In the Two-Sector Model of circular flow, leakage is represented by savings and injection by:

Investment. Equilibrium requires Savings (leakage) = Investment (injection).

Card 31

The Balance of Payments on Current Account includes all EXCEPT:

Foreign direct investment (FDI) and portfolio investment — these belong to the Capital Account, not the Current Account.

Card 32

The Human Development Index (HDI) is computed using which three indicators?

Life expectancy at birth (health), mean years of schooling / expected years of schooling (education), and GNI per capita (standard of living).

Card 33

Under managed floating exchange rate system:

Exchange rates are primarily determined by market forces of demand and supply, but the central bank occasionally intervenes to prevent excessive volatility.

Card 34

Liberalisation in the context of Indian economic reforms refers to:

The removal or reduction of government controls, licenses, and restrictions on business activity (abolition of licensing/permit raj).

Card 35

The Law of Diminishing Marginal Returns states that as successive units of a variable factor are added to fixed factors, beyond a certain point:

The marginal product of the variable factor begins to decline, even though total product may still be rising.

Card 36

For normal goods, the income elasticity of demand is:

Positive. For inferior goods, it is negative. For luxury goods, it is greater than 1.

Card 37

Which of the following represents a NORMAL good?

A good for which demand increases as consumer income increases (positive income elasticity). E.g., branded clothing, restaurants — as opposed to inferior goods like coarse grain whose demand falls when income rises.

Card 38

Opportunity cost of producing a commodity is best described as:

The value of the next best alternative forgone in order to produce that commodity. It is the fundamental concept behind economic cost.

Card 39

In the short run, if a firm earns zero economic profit, it means:

Total Revenue = Total Cost (including both explicit and implicit costs). The firm is earning normal profit — sufficient to keep it in business but no supernormal profit.

Card 40

Which market structure is characterised by free entry and exit, homogeneous products, and perfect information?

Perfect Competition. Monopoly has barriers to entry; Monopolistic Competition has differentiated products; Oligopoly has few sellers.

Card 41

The shutdown point for a firm occurs when price falls below:

Average Variable Cost (AVC). If P < AVC, the firm cannot even cover its variable costs and should shut down immediately in the short run.

Card 42

Third-degree price discrimination involves:

Charging different prices to different groups of consumers based on their differing price elasticities of demand (e.g., student discounts, senior citizen fares).

Card 43

In the IS-LM framework, the IS curve represents equilibrium in the:

Goods market (where Investment = Saving at every point). The LM curve represents equilibrium in the money market (where Liquidity preference = Money supply).

Card 44

Fiscal deficit is defined as:

Total Expenditure – (Revenue Receipts + Non-debt Capital Receipts). It indicates the government's total borrowing requirement from all sources.

Card 45

Gross Domestic Capital Formation measures:

The total value of gross fixed capital formation plus changes in stocks (inventory) during a given period. It is a component of GDP expenditure method.

Card 46

Recession is technically defined as:

A significant decline in economic activity spread across the economy, lasting more than a few months, normally visible in real GDP, income, employment, and industrial production.

Card 47

When price elasticity of demand equals zero, demand is called:

Perfectly Inelastic. Quantity demanded does not change at all in response to any change in price (vertical demand curve).

Card 48

Gross National Product (GNP) equals GDP plus:

Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA). NFIA = income earned by residents from overseas minus income earned by foreigners from domestic production.

Card 49

Under monopolistic competition, firms achieve productive efficiency in the long run. True or False?

False. Monopolistic competitors produce on the downward-sloping portion of their AC curve (excess capacity), so they do NOT achieve productive efficiency. They achieve only some allocative inefficiency due to P > MC.

Card 50

Which of the following is a feature of Oligopoly?

Interdependence among firms — each firm's decisions on price and output affect rivals, prompting strategic reactions (game-theoretic behaviour).

Card 51

The Phillips Curve shows an inverse relationship between:

Rate of inflation and rate of unemployment. At low unemployment, inflation tends to be higher, and vice versa (short-run trade-off).

Card 52

A Progressive Tax is one where:

The average tax rate increases as the taxpayer's income increases. Higher income earners pay a larger percentage of their income in tax.

Card 53

Under a flexible (floating) exchange rate, an increase in demand for a country's exports will:

Appreciate the country's currency. Greater demand for exports raises demand for the domestic currency, pushing up its value relative to foreign currencies.

Card 54

Which of the following is a function of money as a 'Store of Value'?

Money can be saved and used for future purchases. Unlike perishable commodities, money retains its purchasing power over time (subject to inflation erosion).

Card 55

Value Added Method of calculating GDP avoids the problem of:

Double Counting. Only the value added at each stage of production is counted, not the total value of output at every stage.

Card 56

The Lorenz Curve is used to measure:

Inequality in the distribution of income (or wealth). The further the curve deviates from the line of perfect equality (45° line), the greater the inequality.

Card 57

Capitalism is an economic system characterised by:

Private ownership of means of production, profit motive, freedom of enterprise, consumer sovereignty, and competition. Government plays a limited role.

Card 58

The Law of Increasing Opportunity Cost arises because:

Resources are not equally suited to producing all goods. As production shifts from one good to another, increasingly less efficient resources must be employed, raising the opportunity cost.

Card 59

In a Command (Planned) Economy, resource allocation decisions are made by:

The Government/Central Planning Authority, not by market forces of demand and supply or individual consumers/producers.

Card 60

Which component is NOT part of India's Current Account in the Balance of Payments?

External commercial borrowings. These are recorded under the Financial/Capital Account, not the Current Account.

Card 61

CAGR stands for:

Compound Annual Growth Rate. It measures the mean annual growth rate of an investment or economy over a specified period longer than one year.

Card 62

Which of the following is the primary objective of monetary policy in India?

To maintain price stability (control inflation) while ensuring adequate credit support for growth. The RBI uses various instruments including repo rate, CRR, and SLR.

Card 63

The Marginal Propensity to Consume (MPC) and Marginal Propensity to Save (MPS) always sum to:

1. Any additional unit of income is either consumed (MPC) or saved (MPS); there is no other disposition of income.
