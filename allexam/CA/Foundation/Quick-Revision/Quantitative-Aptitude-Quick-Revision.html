Definitions

  • Arithmetic Ratio: A ratio a:b expresses the quotient of a divided by b. It compares two quantities of the same kind.
  • Proportion: Four quantities a, b, c, d are in proportion if a/b = c/d, written as a:b::c:d. Then ad = bc (product of extremes = product of means).
  • Percentage: A percentage is a ratio expressed per hundred. x% means x/100.
  • Profit: Selling Price − Cost Price when SP > CP. Profit % = (Profit/CP) × 100.
  • Loss: Cost Price − Selling Price when CP > SP. Loss % = (Loss/CP) × 100.
  • Simple Interest (SI): SI = (P × R × T)/100, where P = principal, R = rate p.a., T = time in years.
  • Compound Interest (CI): A = P(1 + R/100)^T; CI = A − P. For n conversion periods per year: A = P(1 + R/(100n))^(nT).
  • Annuity: A series of equal payments made at regular intervals. Future Value of an ordinary annuity: FV = P × [(1+r)^n − 1]/r.
  • Mean: The arithmetic mean of n observations x₁, x₂, ..., xₙ is Σxᵢ/n.
  • Median: The middle value when data are arranged in ascending or descending order. For even n, median = average of two middle values.
  • Mode: The value that occurs most frequently in a data set.
  • Range: Difference between the maximum and minimum values in a data set.
  • Variance (σ²): The average of squared deviations from the mean: σ² = Σ(xᵢ − μ)²/N.
  • Standard Deviation (σ): The positive square root of variance: σ = √(Σ(xᵢ − μ)²/N).
  • Coefficient of Variation (CV): CV = (σ/μ) × 100, used to compare relative variability.
  • Correlation Coefficient (r): r = Σ(xᵢ−x̄)(yᵢ−ȳ) / √[Σ(xᵢ−x̄)² · Σ(yᵢ−ȳ)²]. Measures linear association between two variables; range: −1 ≤ r ≤ +1.
  • Regression Coefficients: bᵧₓ = r·(σᵧ/σₓ) (regression of y on x); bₓᵧ = r·(σₓ/σᵧ) (regression of x on y).
  • Probability: P(E) = n(E)/n(S), where n(E) = favourable outcomes, n(S) = total outcomes. 0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1.
  • Conditional Probability: P(A|B) = P(A∩B)/P(B), provided P(B) ≠ 0.
  • Bayes' Theorem: P(Aᵢ|B) = [P(B|Aᵢ)·P(Aᵢ)] / Σ[P(B|Aⱼ)·P(Aⱼ)].
  • Matrix: A rectangular array of numbers arranged in rows and columns, denoted by capital letters (e.g., A).
  • Determinant: A scalar value computed from a square matrix; used to solve systems of equations and find inverses.
  • Index Number: A statistical measure of change in a variable (price, quantity) over time relative to a base period.
  • Time Series: A sequence of data points indexed in time order, typically decomposed into Trend (T), Seasonal (S), Cyclical (C), and Irregular (I) components.

Key concepts

  • RATIO AND PROPORTION
  • Rule of Componendo and Dividendo: If a/b = c/d, then (a+b)/(a−b) = (c+d)/(c−d).
  • Chain Rule for Ratios: If a:b = m:n and b:c = p:q, then a:c = mp:nq (after making b same in both ratios).
  • VARIATION
  • Direct Variation: x ∝ y ⇒ x = ky for constant k.
  • Inverse Variation: x ∝ 1/y ⇒ xy = k.
  • PERCENTAGE AND CHANGE
  • If a number is increased by x% and then decreased by x%, net change = −(x²/100)%.
  • Successive percentage changes: a% followed by b% = (a + b + ab/100)%.
  • PROFIT, LOSS AND DISCOUNT
  • Discount % = (List Price − Selling Price)/List Price × 100.
  • If dealer allows x% discount and still gains y%, then CP = SP/(1+y/100) and Marked Price = SP/(1−x/100).
  • SIMPLE AND COMPOUND INTEREST
  • In CI, interest for each year is calculated on the accumulated amount, not original principal.
  • For half-yearly compounding, R becomes R/2 and T becomes 2T in the CI formula.
  • Time Work and Pipes
  • If A does a job in x days and B in y days, together they finish in xy/(x+y) days.
  • Pipe filling in x hrs and emptying in y hrs: net fill in xy/(y−x) hrs if y > x.
  • ALGEBRA: MATRICES AND DETERMINANTS
  • Properties of Determinants: Swapping two rows/columns changes sign; multiplying a row by k multiplies det by k; if two rows/columns are identical, det = 0.
  • Cramer's Rule: For ax + by = e and cx + dy = f, x = Dx/D, y = Dy/D, where D is the coefficient determinant.
  • Matrix Multiplication: AB is defined only when columns of A = rows of B. AB ≠ BA generally.
  • Statistics — Classification of Data
  • Qualitative data (attribute) vs quantitative data (discrete/continuous). Frequency distribution organizes data into classes.
  • MEASURES OF CENTRAL TENDENCY
  • For grouped data: Mean = Σ(fᵢxᵢ)/Σfᵢ (where xᵢ = mid-value of class i).
  • Median for grouped data: Median = L + [(N/2 − CF)/f] × h, where L = lower limit of median class, CF = cumulative frequency before median class, f = frequency of median class, h = class width.
  • Mode for grouped data: Mode = L + [f₁−f₀)/(2f₁−f₀−f₂)] × h.
  • MEASURES OF DISPERSION
  • Standard Deviation (grouped): σ = √[Σfᵢ(xᵢ−x̄)²/Σfᵢ].
  • Assumed Mean Method: σ = √[Σfᵢdᵢ²/N − (Σfᵢdᵢ/N)²], where dᵢ = xᵢ − A (A = assumed mean).
  • Step Deviation Method: Same as above but dᵢ = (xᵢ − A)/h, then multiply result by h.
  • CORRELATION AND REGRESSION
  • r = 0 means no linear correlation; r = +1 perfect positive; r = −1 perfect negative.
  • Correlation is independent of origin and scale: changing origin/scale does not alter r.
  • Regression lines always pass through (x̄, ȳ). The angle between them increases as |r| decreases.
  • If r = ±1, the two regression lines coincide.
  • INDEX NUMBERS
  • Laspeyres Index: Σ(p₁q₀)/Σ(p₀q₀) × 100 (uses base-year quantities).
  • Paasche Index: Σ(p₁q₁)/Σ(p₀q₁) × 100 (uses current-year quantities).
  • Fisher's Ideal Index: √(Laspeyres × Paasche).
  • Time Series: Moving averages smooth out irregular fluctuations; exponential smoothing weights recent observations more heavily.
  • PROBABILITY
  • Addition Rule: P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A ∩ B). For mutually exclusive events, P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B).
  • Multiplication Rule: P(A ∩ B) = P(A) × P(B|A). For independent events: P(A ∩ B) = P(A) × P(B).
  • Complement: P(A') = 1 − P(A).
  • Expected Value: E(X) = Σ xᵢ · P(xᵢ).

Important facts

  • CA Foundation Quantitative Aptitude carries approximately 25 marks in Paper 2 (Business Mathematics, Logical Reasoning and Statistics) out of 100.
  • Arithmetic portion typically accounts for the largest weightage among the three sections.
  • Statistics questions in CA Foundation focus mainly on measures of central tendency, dispersion, correlation, regression, and index numbers — not advanced theory.
  • Matrices and determinants questions are direct: properties application, finding inverse, solving equations via Cramer's rule or matrix method.
  • Probability questions test basic rules, conditional probability, and Bayes' theorem at an introductory level.
  • Important formula link: bᵧₓ × bₓᵧ = r² — this is a frequently tested relationship.
  • Fisher's Ideal Index satisfies both the Time Reversal Test and Factor Reversal Test, which is a commonly asked MCQ fact.
  • For CI, if rate is compounded half-yearly: A = P(1 + R/200)^(2T). Quarterly: A = P(1 + R/400)^(4T).
  • In a frequency distribution, sum of all frequencies must equal N (total number of observations).
  • The coefficient of correlation lies between −1 and +1 inclusive; it is dimensionless.
  • In regression Y = a + bx, b is the slope and a is the intercept; the regression line predicts Y from X.
  • Probability of any event is never negative and never exceeds 1.
  • Determinant of a diagonal or triangular matrix equals the product of its diagonal elements.
  • Transpose property: (AB)' = B'A'. Inverse property: (AB)⁻¹ = B⁻¹A⁻¹.
  • Logarithm basics: log(ab) = log a + log b; log(a/b) = log a − log b; log a^n = n log a.

Memory aids

  • CI shortcut: SI and CI differ only after the 2nd year; difference in 2nd year = P(R/100)².
  • Remember 'PED' for profit/loss: P = SP − CP (positive = profit, negative = loss).
  • Chain rule for ratios: make the common term equal by LCM method.
  • Median formula mnemonic: 'Lower limit plus fraction of gap times step' — Median = L + [(N/2−CF)/f]×h.
  • Correlation strength: 0–0.3 weak, 0.3–0.7 moderate, 0.7–1 strong (absolute value).
  • Regression line always passes through means: 'Lines meet at the point of averages.'
  • Fisher's index is ideal because it 'balances both years' — geometric mean of Laspeyres and Paasche.
  • Matrix inverse: A⁻¹ = adj(A)/|A| — remember determinant must be non-zero.
  • Bayes' theorem: 'Reverse the condition' — goes from P(A|B) to P(B|A).
  • Product of regression coefficients = r squared: 'bᵧₓ times bₓᵧ gives r²'.

Exam tips

  • In CA Foundation Math, practice formula-based numericals daily — speed and accuracy matter significantly.
  • Memorize all formulas on one sheet and revise them repeatedly; most questions are direct formula applications.
  • For statistics numericals, maintain a clear worksheet format to avoid sign errors in SD and correlation calculations.
  • In matrices, learn properties first — many questions test conceptual understanding rather than lengthy computation.
  • For probability, draw tree diagrams for conditional probability and Bayes' theorem questions to avoid confusion.
  • Practice MCQs with negative marking awareness — eliminate obviously wrong options before guessing.
  • In time-value-of-money problems, identify whether the question asks for PV, FV, or annuity before applying formulas.
  • For regression equations, always verify that your calculated lines pass through (x̄, ȳ) as a quick check.
  • Index number problems: remember to use the correct quantity/price column based on the index type specified.
  • Read compound interest questions carefully for the compounding frequency — this is the most common trap.

Common traps

  • Confusing profit/loss percentage base: always calculate on COST PRICE, not selling price.
  • In CI problems, forgetting to adjust rate and time for compounding frequency (half-yearly/quarterly).
  • Applying simple interest formula when compound interest is implied (or vice versa).
  • In correlation/regression, assuming r = 0 means no relationship at all — it means no LINEAR relationship.
  • Swapping bᵧₓ and bₓᵧ formulas — remember: regression of Y on X uses σᵧ/σₓ, not the reverse.
  • In Cramer's rule, computing Dx and Dy incorrectly by replacing wrong column.
  • Using (xᵢ − A) directly in SD without dividing by class width when using step deviation method.
  • Treating Laspeyres and Paasche interchangeably — Laspeyres uses base-year quantities; Paasche uses current-year quantities.
  • Assuming P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) always — this holds only for mutually exclusive events.
  • Forgetting that |A| must be non-zero for A⁻¹ to exist.
  • Confusing conditional probability P(A|B) with joint probability P(A∩B).
  • In time and work, treating emptying pipes as adding instead of subtracting from filling rate.
  • In index numbers, not multiplying by 100 to express as an index number.
  • Assuming median always equals mean — true only for symmetrical distributions.
