CA Foundation - Accounting: Complete Quick Revision

CAEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Accounting: The process of identifying, measuring, recording, classifying, summarising, analysing and communicating financial information to users for decision-making purposes.
  • Accounting Equation: Assets = Equity (Owner's Equity) + Liabilities — the fundamental balancing relationship underlying double-entry bookkeeping.
  • Bookkeeping: The systematic recording of business transactions in the books of original entry and subsequent posting to ledgers; it is a subset of accounting concerned primarily with recording.
  • Journal: The book of first entry where transactions are recorded chronologically with debit and credit aspects before posting to the ledger.
  • Ledger: A collection of accounts summarising all transactions pertaining to each asset, liability, equity, income or expense item after journal posting.
  • Trial Balance: A statement listing ledger balances at a date to verify arithmetical accuracy of postings; it is not conclusive proof of correctness.
  • Bank Reconciliation Statement: A statement reconciling the cash book balance with the pass book / bank statement balance by identifying timing differences and errors.
  • Depreciation: The systematic allocation of the depreciable amount of an asset over its useful life, representing consumption of economic benefits.
  • Provisions, Reserves and Surplus: Provisions are amounts set aside to meet known liabilities or diminution in asset value; reserves are appropriations of profit not earmarked for a specific purpose; surplus is the balance in the Profit & Loss Appropriation Account.
  • Partnership: An association of two or more persons to carry on business and share profits, governed by the Indian Partnership Act, 1932, in the absence of a partnership deed.
  • Admission/Retirement/Dissolution: Admission is the introduction of a new partner; retirement is the exit of an existing partner; dissolution is the breaking up of the firm and settlement of all assets and liabilities.

Important facts

  • Sacrificing Ratio = Old Profit-Sharing Ratio − New Profit-Sharing Ratio
  • Gain Ratio on retirement/death = New Ratio − Old Ratio among continuing partners
  • Workings ratio for goodwill = Total Goodwill × Sacrificing Ratio (admission); = Total Goodwill × Gain Ratio (retirement/death)
  • Depreciation SLM annual charge = (Cost − Salvage Value) / Useful Life
  • Depreciation WDV rate = 1 − (Salvage/Cost)^(1/n) × 100%
  • Average Daily Balance for interest on drawings = Opening Drawings + Additions − Withdrawals averaged over the period
  • Bill discounted: Drawer credits Bank with Bill Discounted A/c; upon dishonour, Bill Discounted A/c is debited and Customer's account debited
  • Under incomplete records: Profit = Closing Net Worth − Opening Net Worth + Drawings − Additional Capital
  • Consignment stock reserve: Unrealised profit in unsold stock = Gross Profit on Consignment × Unsold Stock at Invoice Price / Total Stock at Invoice Price
  • Branch profit: Loading on goods sent to branch is eliminated in branch stock adjustment; profit is computed after eliminating inter-company profit
  • Depreciation on addition/deduction during year: Charge from date of addition to date of sale/disposal; prorate for partial periods
  • Bank Reconciliation: Start from Cash Book balance; add unpresented cheques, deduct deposits not yet credited (or reverse), adjust direct debits/credits
  • Rectification of errors: If trial balance disagrees, find difference and place on debit or credit side of Suspense A/c, then rectify
  • AS 10 (Property, Plant and Equipment): Asset cost includes purchase price, import duties, non-refundable taxes, directly attributable costs; borrowing costs capitalised if conditions met (AS 16)
  • AS 2 (Inventories): Valued at cost or net realisable value, whichever is lower; cost includes all costs of purchase, conversion and other costs incurred in bringing inventory to present location and condition
  • AS 9 (Revenue Recognition): Revenue from sale of goods recognised when significant risks and rewards transferred; revenue from services recognised by reference to stage of completion
  • AS 3 (Cash Flow Statement): Indirect method most commonly used for operating activities; cash flow from operations = Net profit before tax ± non-cash items ± changes in working capital
  • AS 11 (Foreign Currency Transactions): Transactions recorded at spot rate on date of transaction; monetary items translated at closing rate; exchange differences recognised in P&L
  • Deferred Tax (AS 22): Temporary differences between accounting and tax bases create deferred tax assets/liabilities

Memory aids

  • Remember journal rules with: DEAR — Debit Expenses, Assets, Drawings; CRAL — Credit Revenue, Liabilities, Capital
  • BLT for bank reconciliation: Begin with Cash Book, Less unpresented cheques, Add deposits not credited
  • Goodwill shortcuts: 'Super Profit means excess over normal; Average Profit times years of purchase'
  • Partnership changes mnemonic: ARD — Admission, Retirement, Dissolution. Admission: Revalue, Goodwill, Reserves. Retirement: Pay gain, Bear loss. Dissolution: Realise, Pay outside first, then partners
  • Cash Flow mnemonic: OIF — Operating, Investing, Financing
  • Depreciation methods: 'SLM = Same Load Monthly'; 'WDV = Winding Down Value'
  • Share forfeiture sequence: Forfeit → Forfeited Shares A/c → Reissue → Capital Reserve/Loss adjustment
  • Consignment special points: Transit loss credited to Consignment A/c at invoice cost; abnormal loss goes to Trading A/c; commission only on normal sales
  • Royalty recovery rule: Recover shortworkings within agreed years; beyond limit write off to P&L

Exam tips

  • Always show workings clearly — even if final answer is wrong, step marks are awarded for correct methodology.
  • In partnership problems, draw a table showing Old Ratio, New Ratio, Sacrificing/Gain Ratio for each partner before computing adjustments.
  • For depreciation questions, prepare a depreciation schedule/table when multiple assets are added/sold during the year.
  • In bank reconciliation, start with the balance given in the question (cash book or pass book) and adjust systematically — do not jump between them.
  • When rectifying errors, first identify the nature of error, then prepare the rectification journal entry with proper narration.
  • For consignment accounts, always compute stock reserve separately and show it as a deduction from stock in final accounts.
  • In share forfeiture/reissue, maintain separate ledgers for Forfeited Shares A/c and track every entry carefully.
  • For cash flow statement, prepare schedules of changes in working capital components separately before arriving at operating cash flow.
  • Read each question thoroughly — determine whether the method is SLM or WDV, fixed or fluctuating capital, etc., before starting computations.
  • In partnership dissolution, follow the statutory order of payment strictly — outside creditors first, then partner loans, then capitals.
  • For AS-based questions, cite the standard number and key principle — e.g., 'As per AS 2, inventories are valued at cost or NRV, whichever is lower.'
  • Practice numerical problems daily — speed and accuracy in partnership, depreciation, and consignment accounts is critical for scoring well.

Common traps

  • Confusing sacrificing ratio with gain ratio — sacrificing ratio applies on admission; gain ratio applies on retirement/death
  • Adjusting goodwill to only new partner's capital — old partners' capitals must also be adjusted to reflect new goodwill value
  • Charging depreciation on full cost in year of purchase when WDV method is used — check whether question requires full year or proportionate
  • Forgetting to eliminate unrealised profit in consignment stock when preparing final accounts — understates profit
  • Treating calls in arrear as revenue expenditure — it is a receivable from the defaulting partner/shareholder
  • Including drawings in profit computation — drawings reduce capital, not expense
  • Assuming all reserves are free — general reserve is free but specific reserve (like CRR) cannot be used for dividend
  • Applying average clause to full policy amount instead of actual loss — claim is proportionate to underinsurance
  • Ignoring interest on capital when computing partners' shares without specific instructions — check if capital is fixed or fluctuating
  • Treating premium on redemption of debentures as revenue loss — it is capitalised and written off over the period
  • Forgetting that cash flow from operations starts with profit before tax (not net profit after tax)
  • Computing branch profit without adjusting for inter-company profit in stock — overstates profit
