Auditing and Ethics - Flashcards

CAEnglishFlashcards

Card 1

What does SA 200 stipulate regarding the objectives of an auditor?

SA 200 states that the objective of an auditor is to obtain reasonable assurance whether financial statements are free from material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error, and to report on them. Reasonable assurance is high but not absolute.

Card 2

What are the fundamental principles of professional ethics as per ICAI Code of Ethics?

1. Integrity: Be straightforward and honest. 2. Objectivity: No compromise of professional judgment. 3. Professional Competence and Due Care: Maintain knowledge at required level. 4. Confidentiality: Respect information confidentiality. 5. Professional Behavior: Comply with laws and avoid discreditation.

Card 3

Define Materiality in auditing context.

Materiality refers to the magnitude of an omission or misstatement of accounting information that, in the light of surrounding circumstances, makes it probable that the judgment of a reasonable person would have been changed or influenced by the omission or misstatement.

Card 4

What is the difference between assurance and non-assurance services?

Assurance services enhance the credibility of information for decision-makers, e.g., audits, reviews. Non-assurance services include agreed-upon procedures, compilations, which do not provide assurance.

Card 5

What is SA 700 concerned with?

SA 700 deals with forming an opinion and reporting on financial statements. It prescribes how the auditor's report should be structured and what to include.

Card 6

Explain the role of Internal Audit.

Internal audit assists management by providing independent, objective assurance and consulting services aimed at adding value and improving an organization's operations. It evaluates risk management, control, governance processes.

Card 7

What are the legal duties of a CA as per Companies Act?

Under Section 143 of Companies Act, 2013, duties include examining books/accounting records, reporting on financial statements, reporting on internal controls, reporting on corporate governance, and reporting on compliance with financial management norms.

Card 8

Describe Qualitative Aspects of Materiality.

Qualitative aspects consider whether misstatements could affect decisions even if immaterial in amount—e.g., misstatements affecting earnings trends, regulatory compliance, contractual obligations, or mask changes in earnings.

Card 9

What is an Emphasis of Matter paragraph?

An Emphasis of Matter paragraph is added in the auditor’s report to draw users’ attention to a matter appropriately presented/disclosed in financial statements that is fundamental to their understanding, without modifying the opinion.

Card 10

When is a Qualified Opinion issued?

A Qualified Opinion is given when there is a material but not pervasive misstatement or limitation on scope. It is expressed as ‘except for’ the effects of the matter.

Card 11

What is the Auditor’s Responsibility regarding Fraud?

Under SA 240, auditor must obtain reasonable assurance that financial statements are free from material misstatement due to fraud, including identifying and assessing risks of material misstatement due to fraud, and responding accordingly.

Card 12

Distinguish between Management Representation and Audit Evidence.

Management representation is a statement by management provided to auditor to support other audit evidence; it does not provide sufficient audit evidence on its own. Audit evidence comprises information used by auditor to arrive at conclusions.

Card 13

What is a Good Audit Report?

A good audit report is clear, concise, informative, and expresses an unmodified opinion when appropriate. It follows format prescribed by standards and communicates key audit matters if applicable.

Card 14

What is an Independent Audit?

Independent audit implies auditors are free from influence of any party that might affect their judgment. It requires both independence in fact and appearance.

Card 15

Define Professional Skepticism.

Professional skepticism is an attitude that includes a questioning mind, being alert to conditions that may indicate possible misstatement due to fraud or error, and critically assessing audit evidence.

Card 16

What is the purpose of Audit Documentation?

Audit documentation provides evidence of auditor’s basis for conclusion, that planning/performance was performed per standards, and auditors were accountable to supervisors.

Card 17

Explain Concept of Going Concern in auditing.

Auditor evaluates whether management’s use of going concern assumption is appropriate. If material uncertainty exists, auditor should disclose or modify opinion.

Card 18

What is a Statutory Audit?

Statutory audit is mandatory under law (e.g., Companies Act). Conducted by statutory auditor (CA) to verify compliance with legal requirements and true/false view of accounts.

Card 19

What is the Auditor’s duty concerning related parties?

As per SA 550, auditor identifies, assesses, and responds to risks of material misstatement associated with related parties. Requires understanding of entity's related party relationships and transactions.

Card 20

What constitutes breach of ethics for a CA?

Breach may involve lack of integrity, failure to maintain objectivity, disclosing confidential information improperly, accepting commissions improperly, incompetence, misleading others, or engaging in business activities that discredit profession.
