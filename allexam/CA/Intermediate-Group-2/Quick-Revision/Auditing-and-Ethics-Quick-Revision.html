Quick Revision — Auditing and Ethics | CA Intermediate Group 2

CAEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Auditing: A systematic examination of financial statements and related records by a competent auditor to express an independent opinion on whether they present a true and fair view in conformity with the applicable financial reporting framework.
  • Internal Control: The process designed, implemented and maintained by those charged with governance, management and other personnel to provide reasonable assurance about achievement of objectives relating to reliability of financial reporting, effectiveness and efficiency of operations, and compliance with laws/regulations.
  • Internal Audit: An independent, objective assurance and consulting activity designed to add value and improve an organization's operations; evaluates effectiveness of risk management, internal control and governance processes.
  • External Audit: Statutory audit conducted by an independent Chartered Accountant appointed under relevant legislation (e.g., Companies Act, 2013) to express an opinion on financial statements.
  • Vouching: Examination of documentary evidence (vouchers, invoices, agreements, etc.) to verify the authenticity and accuracy of transactions recorded in books of account; tests the 'posting from vouches' assertion.
  • Verification: Confirmation of existence, ownership, valuation and disclosure of assets and liabilities as disclosed in the balance sheet; tests balance sheet items as at a given date.
  • Audit Evidence: All information used by the auditor to arrive at the conclusions on which the audit opinion is based; includes accounting records, corroborating information and information obtained from other audit procedures.
  • Materiality: Information is material if its omission or misstatement could influence the economic decisions of users taken on the basis of the financial statements; a threshold combining magnitude and nature.
  • Risk Assessment: The process of identifying and analysing risks of material misstatement in the financial statements, both at the financial statement level and the assertion level, to form the basis for designing further audit procedures.
  • Professional Skepticism: An attitude that includes a questioning mind, being alert to conditions that may indicate possible misstatement due to error or fraud, and a critical assessment of audit evidence.
  • Ethics: Moral principles and standards of conduct that guide professional behaviour; in auditing context, refers to the code of ethics issued by ICAI governing professional duties of CAs.
  • Independence: Freedom from conditions that threaten the ability of a CA to act with integrity, objectivity and professional skepticism; comprises independence of mind and independence in appearance.
  • Quality Control: Policies and procedures designed to provide reasonable assurance that the firm and its personnel perform their work in accordance with professional standards and applicable legal/regulatory requirements.
  • Limited Assurance Engagement: An engagement in which the practitioner concludes whether anything has come to their attention that causes them to believe that the subject matter information is materially misstated; expressed as negative form of assurance.
  • Review Engagement: A limited assurance engagement under SA 910 where the auditor applies analytical procedures and enquiries to obtain negative assurance on financial statements.

Important facts

  • Companies Act, 2013 Section 139: Auditor's term = 5 years from conclusion of first AGM; eligible for re-appointment but subject to rotation.
  • Mandatory auditor rotation: Listed companies + public companies with paid-up capital ≥ ₹50 crore or turnover ≥ ₹100 crore or outstanding loans/deposits/bonds ≥ ₹25 crore — audit firm appointed for more than 2 consecutive terms (10 years) must rotate after cool-off period of 5 years; individual partner after 5 consecutive years with 2-year cool-off.
  • Companies Act, 2013 Section 143(12): Auditor must report whether company has adequate internal control procedures and whether they are adequate and operating effectively.
  • CARO 2020 replaces CARO 2003; applicable to all companies required to conduct statutory audit except Nidhi companies, producer companies and small companies as notified.
  • Audit documentation assembly period: Within 60 days of audit report date (SA 230).
  • Retention period for audit working papers: 7 years from date of auditor's report (SA 230).
  • Minimum number of SAs currently in force: Over 50 SAs notified by ICAI under Companies Act, 2013; aspirants must know at least the landmark ones listed above by number and title.
  • SA 240 on fraud: Auditor is not responsible for preventing fraud but is responsible for obtaining reasonable assurance that financial statements are free from material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error.
  • SA 570 Going Concern: Management is primarily responsible for assessing going concern; auditor evaluates whether material uncertainty exists related to events/conditions that may cast significant doubt.
  • SA 701 Key Audit Matters: Applies to listed entities; KAMs are those matters that, in auditor's professional judgment, were of most significance in the audit.
  • Sec 140 of Companies Act: Resigning auditor must file along with explanatory statement with company and ROC within 30 days; company forwards to ROC within 15 days.
  • Deposits accepted from members/public — verification of compliance with Sections 73-76 is a key CARO requirement.
  • Statutory audit report must be signed by two officers of the audit firm along with seal.
  • Independent auditor's report date cannot be earlier than date on which auditor obtains sufficient appropriate audit evidence.
  • Self-review threat arises when auditor audits own previous work; advocacy threat when auditor promotes client's position; familiarity threat when auditor becomes too sympathetic to client's interests.

  • Types of Audit Memory Aid: 'STITC' — Statutory, Tax, Internal, Stock, Cost (also Internal = I, Statutory = S).
  • Threats to Ethics: 'SSAIFI' — Self-interest, Self-review, Advocacy, Familiarity, Intimidation.
  • CARO 2020 key areas: 'FIXED LAND' — Fixed Assets, Inventory, Loans, Deposits, Employee costs, Dues, Franchise/license, Affiliated transactions, Non-compliance with laws, Domestic & International sales/purchases.
  • SA 230 Documentation: '60-7 Rule' — Assemble within 60 days, retain for 7 years.
  • Auditor Rotation Rule: '5-2-5' — 5 years max for individual partner rotation; 10 years (2 terms of 5) max for audit firm; cool-off 2 years (partner) and 5 years (firm).
  • Going Concern Horizon: '12 Months from Balance Sheet Date' — same logic as in preparation of financial statements.
  • Fraud Triangle (for SA 240 context): 'Pressure, Opportunity, Rationalization' — helps remember why fraud occurs.
  • Vouching vs Verification: 'VP' — Vouching for P&L (Past transactions), Verification for Balance Sheet (Position at a point in time).

Exam tips

  • Always cite the exact SA number and title when the question asks for 'relevant Standard'. For example, write 'SA 315 — Identifying and Assessing the Risks of Material Misstatement' rather than just 'SA 315'.
  • In case-based questions, separate your answer into: (1) Identify the issue, (2) Cite the applicable standard/provision, (3) State what the standard requires, (4) Apply to facts, (5) Conclude with recommendation. This structure fetches maximum marks.
  • For Ethics questions, always mention the five fundamental principles first before discussing threats/safeguards. This shows comprehensive knowledge and secures baseline marks.
  • When writing about CARO 2020, do NOT reproduce all clauses verbatim. Pick the 4-5 most relevant clauses to the facts and explain them concisely with reference to the question.
  • For Auditors' Report questions, learn the standard format cold. Practice writing the header, headings, and concluding paragraph from memory.
  • Use comparison tables wherever possible — e.g., Audit vs Review vs Examination vs Agreed-Upon Procedures — these score well in descriptive answers.
  • Link Companies Act sections with corresponding SAs: e.g., Section 143 duties map to SA 200 objectives; Section 140 relates to removal/resignation; CARO 2020 supplements SA 700/705.
  • Do not confuse tax audit report (Form 3CA/3CB + 3CD) with statutory audit report. These are different reports under different laws.
  • For numerical problems involving audit adjustments/materiality, always show working clearly: state materiality threshold, individual misstatement, aggregate misstatement, and conclude on impact on opinion.
  • Practice writing answers within word limits — examiners appreciate crisp, structured responses over long discursive passages. Bullet-point style with clear sub-headings is preferred.

Common traps

  • Confusing vouching with verification: Vouching tests income/expenditure entries (P&L items) with reference to source documents; verification tests balance sheet items for existence, ownership and valuation. This distinction is frequently tested.
  • Mixing up 'Qualified Opinion' vs 'Disclaimer': Qualified opinion = material but NOT pervasive misstatement/evidence limitation; Disclaimer = material AND pervasive inability to obtain evidence. Adverse = material AND pervasive misstatement.
  • Thinking auditor is responsible for preventing all fraud: Auditor provides reasonable assurance, not absolute assurance; fraud involving collusion or forgery may not be detected. SA 240 makes this clear.
  • Confusing auditor's tenure with company's financial year: Auditor's 5-year term runs from the conclusion of the FIRST AGM, NOT from the date of appointment. Many candidates lose marks here.
  • Ignoring the 60-day documentation assembly rule: Working papers must be assembled within 60 days of report date, not at any convenient time later.
  • Applying CARO 2003 provisions instead of CARO 2020: CARO 2020 has new clauses and modified existing ones; outdated CARO 2003 points are traps in exams.
  • Assuming internal audit is always done by ICAI members: Internal audit can be performed by a CA or by persons qualified under the company's internal audit policy; not necessarily a practicing CA.
  • Confusing review engagement (SA 910) with audit (SA 200): Review provides LIMITED (negative) assurance; audit provides REASONABLE (positive) assurance. Procedures in review are substantially less extensive.
  • Omitting 'Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern' from appropriate cases: When material uncertainty exists but financial statements adequately disclose, opinion is UNMODIFIED but with emphasis-of-matter paragraph — NOT a qualified opinion.
  • Treating 'knowledge' of fraud differently from 'suspected' fraud: Auditor must communicate suspected fraud to management and those charged with governance; if management/response is inadequate, consider implications for report and legal reporting obligations.
  • Forgetting distinction between SA 701 (KAM) and SA 706 (Emphasis of Matter): KAM is for listed entities' audit reports; EOM can be used in any audit when a properly disclosed matter is fundamental to users' understanding.
