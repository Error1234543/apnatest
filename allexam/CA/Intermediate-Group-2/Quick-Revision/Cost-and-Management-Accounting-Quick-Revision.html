CA Intermediate Group 2 — Cost and Management Accounting: Complete Quick Revision

CAEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Material Cost: Total cost of raw materials consumed including purchase price, freight, insurance, import duties, and less trade discount and rebates.
  • Economic Order Quantity (EOQ): The optimal lot size that minimises total inventory costs (ordering cost + holding cost), calculated as √(2DS/H) where D=annual demand, S=order cost per order, H=holding cost per unit per year.
  • Re-order Level: The stock level at which a fresh order must be placed; = Maximum usage × Maximum lead time.
  • Lead Time: The interval between placing an order and receipt of material.
  • Labour Cost: All remuneration payable to employees including wages, salary, incentives, bonus, commission, employer's PF contribution, and fringe benefits.
  • Overheads: All indirect costs of material, labour, and services that cannot be directly charged to a cost unit.
  • Absorption Costing: A costing method where all costs (fixed and variable) are allocated to cost units using one or more absorption rates.
  • Marginal Cost: The variable cost of producing one additional unit; Total Cost = Fixed Cost + Marginal Cost × Output.
  • Contribution: Sales Revenue minus Variable Cost; Contribution = Fixed Cost + Profit.
  • Break-even Point (BEP): The level of output where total revenue equals total cost, yielding zero profit; BEP (units) = Fixed Cost ÷ Contribution per Unit.
  • Margin of Safety: Excess of actual (or budgeted) sales over break-even sales; MOS = Actual Sales − BEP Sales, or MOS% = (MOS ÷ Actual Sales) × 100.
  • Standard Cost: Pre-determined cost of a product or service based on specified standards of efficiency and cost conditions.
  • Variance: The difference between standard cost and actual cost; favourable (F) if actual cost < standard cost, adverse (A) if actual cost > standard cost.
  • Budget: A quantitative expression of expected performance for a future period, subject to approval and policy guidance.
  • Flexible Budget: A budget designed to change in proportion to variations in the level of activity, separating fixed and variable components.
  • Activity-Based Costing (ABC): A costing methodology that assigns overheads to products based on the activities they consume, using multiple cost drivers instead of a single plant-wide rate.
  • Funds Flow Statement: A statement showing changes in financial position (sources and uses of funds) between two balance sheet dates; 'funds' traditionally means working capital.
  • Cash Flow Statement: A statement presenting inflows and outflows of cash and cash equivalents classified into operating, investing, and financing activities over a period.
  • Cost Audit: Systematic examination of cost records to verify accuracy, completeness, and compliance with cost accounting standards and relevant statutory requirements.
  • Under-absorption: When actual overheads exceed absorbed overheads; Over-absorption is the reverse.

Key concepts

  • a) EOQ model assumes: constant demand, constant ordering cost, constant holding cost, no shortages, instantaneous replenishment.
    b) Adjustment for quantity discounts: Compare total cost (purchase cost + ordering cost + holding cost) at EOQ and at each discount-break quantity; choose the point with minimum total cost.
    c) Re-order Level = Max Usage × Max Lead Time; Min Level = Re-order Level − (Normal Usage × Normal Lead Time); Max Level = Re-order Level + EOQ − (Min Usage × Min Lead Time);
    d) Flux Level = Average Level + (Max Level − Average Level)/2 (for buffer adjustment).
    e) HCS (Harmonic Constant Stock) model and JIT are advanced concepts tested in application questions.
  • a) Time Rate vs. Piece Rate: Under time rate, guarantee is hourly/daily wage; under piece rate, payment is per unit produced.
    b) Halsey Plan: Bonus = 50% of time saved × time rate; Taylor Differential Piece Rate: different rates for below-standard and above-standard output.
    c) Rowan Plan: Bonus = (Time Saved / Time Allowed) × Time Taken × Rate per hour; Rowan always gives higher bonus than Halsey for same time saving.
    d) Earning Comparison: At 50% time saving, Halsey and Rowan give equal earnings; beyond 50%, Rowan is more favourable to worker; below 50%, Halsey is better.
    e) Merrick Differential Piece Rate: Three-piece rate system — 80%, 100%, 120% of base rate for below, at, and above standard.
    f) Bedaux System: Uses 'B' units (60 seconds = 1B); guarantees hourly rate plus bonus on B-units produced above standard.
  • a) Overhead Absorption Rate = Estimated Overheads ÷ Estimated Base (direct labour hours, machine hours, units produced, % of direct material cost, % of direct labour cost, or % of works cost).
    b) Multiple Rates: Different departments use different bases (e.g., machine department → machine hours; labour department → labour hours).
    c) Under-absorption occurs when actual overheads > absorbed overheads; add to cost of production or transfer to Costing Profit & Loss Account.
    d) Treatment of Abnormal Idle Time: Abnormal idle time cost is debited to Costing P&L A/c separately and NOT included in overhead absorption rate computation.
    e) Treatment of Normal Idle Time: Included in overhead cost (by reducing estimated output or adding to overheads).
    f) Recalculation of overhead rate after revised estimates: New rate = Revised overheads ÷ Revised base.
  • a) Key equation: Profit = (Contribution per unit × Units sold) − Fixed Costs; at BEP, Profit = 0.
    b) P/V Ratio (Profit-Volume Ratio) = (Contribution ÷ Sales) × 100; also = (Change in Contribution ÷ Change in Sales) × 100.
    c) BEP (₹) = Fixed Cost ÷ P/V Ratio; BEP (units) = Fixed Cost ÷ Contribution per unit.
    d) Multi-product BEP requires weighted average contribution or sales-mix approach.
    e) Managerial Breakeven Analysis uses accounting profit (includes depreciation); Cash Breakeven excludes non-cash items like depreciation.
    f) Impact of tax on BEP: BEP (after-tax) = [Fixed Cost + (Required Profit × 100/(100−Tax%))] ÷ Contribution per unit.
    g) CVP graph shows total cost line intersecting total revenue line at BEP; area above BEP = profit, below = loss.
  • a) Types of budgets: Fixed (static), Flexible, Incremental, Zero-Based (ZBB), rolling/continuous,master, operational, financial, capital.
    b) Zero-Based Budgeting: Every activity starts from zero; justifies each expenditure afresh each period; no carry-forward of previous budget figures.
    c) Flexible Budget adjustment: Variable cost scales with activity level; fixed cost remains constant within relevant range.
    d) Formula for flexible budget at actual activity: Adjusted Budget = Fixed Cost + (Variable Cost per unit × Actual activity level).
    e) Budget manual defines roles, responsibilities, and procedures for budget preparation and control.
    f) Budget committees coordinate preparation; budget controllers implement; budget office maintains records.
  • a) Material Price Variance (MPV) = (Standard Price − Actual Price) × Actual Quantity Purchased/Used.
    b) Material Usage Variance (MUV) = (Standard Quantity − Actual Quantity) × Standard Price.
    c) Material Cost Variance (MCV) = MPV + MUV = (Standard Cost − Actual Cost).
    d) Material Yield Variance = (Actual Yield − Standard Yield from actual input) × Standard cost per unit of output.
    e) Material Mix Variance = Standard Price × (Actual Mix − Standard Mix for actual total input).
    f) Revised Standard Quantity = Total Actual Input × (Standard Quantity of material ÷ Total Standard Quantity).
    g) All material variances use Standard Price except usage/yield/mix which relate to quantity differences evaluated at standard price.
  • a) Labour Cost Variance = (Standard Cost − Actual Cost).
    b) Labour Rate Variance = (Standard Rate − Actual Rate) × Actual Hours.
    c) Labour Efficiency Variance = (Standard Hours − Actual Hours) × Standard Rate.
    d) Labour Idle Time Variance = Idle Hours × Standard Rate (separately disclosed if material).
    e) Labour Variance can also be analysed as: Labour Cost Variance = Labour Rate Variance + Labour Efficiency Variance.
    f) For part-time workers or mixed workforce, use revised standard hours based on actual mix proportion.
  • a) Fixed Overhead Volume Variance = (Actual Output − Budgeted Output) × Standard Fixed Overhead Rate per unit; indicates utilisation of capacity.
    b) Fixed Overhead Capacity Variance = (Actual Hours − Budgeted Hours) × Standard Fixed Overhead Rate per hour.
    c) Fixed Overhead Efficiency Variance = (Standard Hours for actual output − Actual Hours) × Standard Fixed Overhead Rate per hour.
    d) Fixed Overhead Expenditure (Spending) Variance = Budgeted Fixed Overhead − Actual Fixed Overhead.
    e) Variable Overhead Cost Variance = (Standard Cost − Actual Cost); split into Variable OH Expenditure Variance and Variable OH Efficiency Variance.
    f) Variable OH Expenditure Variance = (Standard Rate − Actual Rate) × Actual Hours.
  • a) Four steps: (1) Identify activities and assign overheads to activity cost pools; (2) Identify cost drivers for each activity; (3) Calculate activity rate = Activity cost pool ÷ Total cost driver units; (4) Assign overhead to products = Activity rate × Driver units consumed by product.
    b) ABC is superior to traditional costing when overheads are high, products are diverse, and resource consumption varies significantly across products.
    c) Cost drivers: Transaction drivers (count of events), Duration drivers (time required), Intensity drivers (resources consumed per event).
    d) Value-added activities: those that increase customer value; non-value-added activities should be minimised.
    e) ABC provides more accurate product costing for decision-making but is more complex and costly to implement.
  • a) Source of funds: Increase in long-term liabilities, issue of shares/debentures, profit before tax (adjusted for non-funding items), sale of fixed assets, long-term loans received.
    b) Use of funds: Decrease in long-term liabilities, redemption of shares/debentures, loss before tax, purchase of fixed assets, dividend paid, long-term loans repaid.
    c) Adjustments: Add back non-funding expenses (depreciation, amortisation, goodwill written off); deduct non-funding incomes.
    d) Gain on sale of asset is a funding source only to the extent of cash received; loss is added back.
    e) Provision for tax: Opening provision is not a source; closing provision is not a use. Actual tax paid is a use.
    f) Inter-company transactions are eliminated; intra-group transfers are ignored.
  • a) Operating Activities: Start with PBIT or profit before tax; adjust for non-cash items (depreciation, amortisation, provisions); adjust for gains/losses on sale of assets; changes in working capital (increase in current assets = use, decrease = source; increase in current liabilities = source, decrease = use).
    b) Investing Activities: Purchase/sale of fixed assets, investments, subsidiaries; interest received/paid may be classified here or under operating.
    c) Financing Activities: Issue/buyback of shares, issue/repayment of debentures/loans, dividend paid (not received).
    d) Cash and cash equivalents: Cash on hand + demand deposits + short-term highly liquid investments (maturity ≤ 3 months).
    e) Net decrease/increase in cash = Sum of operating + investing + financing cash flows.
    f) Reconciliation: Opening cash + net cash flow = Closing cash; cross-check with balance sheet.
  • a) Liquidity Ratios: Current Ratio = Current Assets ÷ Current Liabilities; Quick Ratio = Quick Assets ÷ Current Liabilities (Quick Assets = CA − Stock − Prepaid Expenses).
    b) Solvency/Leverage Ratios: Debt-Equity Ratio = Long-term Debt ÷ Shareholders' Funds; Proprietary Ratio = Shareholders' Funds ÷ Total Assets; Interest Coverage Ratio = PBIT ÷ Interest.
    c) Turnover Ratios: Stock Turnover = COGS ÷ Average Stock; Debtors Turnover = Credit Sales ÷ Average Debtors (in days = 365 ÷ ratio); Creditors Turnover = Credit Purchases ÷ Average Creditors; Working Capital Turnover = Sales ÷ Average Working Capital.
    d) Profitability Ratios: Gross Profit Ratio = Gross Profit ÷ Sales × 100; Net Profit Ratio = NPAT ÷ Sales × 100; ROCE = PBIT ÷ Capital Employed × 100; ROE = NPAT ÷ Shareholders' Funds × 100.
    e) DuPont Analysis: ROE = Net Profit Margin × Asset Turnover × Equity Multiplier = (NP/S) × (S/Assets) × (Assets/Equity).
  • a) Format: Direct Material Consumed + Direct Labour + Direct Expenses = Prime Cost; Prime Cost + Factory/Works Overheads = Works Cost; Works Cost + Admin Overheads = Cost of Production; Cost of Production + Selling & Distribution Overheads = Cost of Sales; Cost of Sales + Profit = Selling Price.
    b) Closing stock of finished goods adjustment: Cost of Production + Closing FG Stock − Opening FG Stock = Cost of Goods Sold.
    c) Work-in-progress adjustment: Works Cost + Closing WIP − Opening WIP = Adjusted Works Cost.
    d) Specific items: Freight inward added to purchases; carriage outward is a selling expense; interest on loans is a financial expense (not in cost sheet unless specifically required).e) Preliminary expenses amortised during the year are shown as an expense.

Important facts

  • EOQ formula: EOQ = √(2DS/H), where H is often expressed as i × C (percentage of unit cost). If H is given as a percentage, multiply by unit cost first.
  • Under the Taxation Act, Section 44AB mandates cost audit for businesses whose turnover exceeds the prescribed limit (currently ₹10 crore for businesses, ₹2 crore for professions under 44AD).
  • Cost Accounting Standards (CAS) issued by ICAI: CAS-1 (Identification & Measurement of Materials), CAS-2 (Identification & Measurement of Labour), CAS-3 (Identification & Measurement of Overheads), CAS-4 (Product Costing), CAS-8 (Capacity Estimation), CAS-10 (Valuation of Stock-in-Trade).
  • As per Ind AS / Schedule III, depreciation is charged on written down value method for most assets, but straight-line method is also permitted under Companies Act 2013.
  • In marginal costing, fixed manufacturing overheads are treated as period costs and charged entirely to the period's Profit & Loss Account; they are NOT inventoried.
  • For multi-product analysis, calculate contribution per limiting factor (scarce resource) to determine optimal production mix.
  • In standard costing, material price variance is normally computed on quantity purchased (not used) to isolate purchasing performance early.
  • Fixed overhead volume variance has three sub-variances in some ICAI frameworks: Capacity, Efficiency, and Volume — ensure you understand the relationship: Volume = Capacity + Efficiency.
  • Cash Flow Statement under Ind AS 7 requires disclosure of three categories: Operating, Investing, and Financing activities.
  • Interest coverage ratio ≥ 3 is generally considered healthy; < 1.5 signals severe debt servicing risk.
  • P/V Ratio is also called Contribution Margin Ratio; it remains constant per unit but changes in total with sales volume.
  • In flexible budgeting, fixed costs do NOT vary with activity level within the relevant range.
  • Zero-Based Budgeting was introduced in India's public sector by the 5th Five Year Plan; it requires rigorous justification of every rupee spent.
  • The Cash Flow from Operations (direct method) is an alternative presentation allowed under Ind AS 7 alongside the indirect method.
  • Activity-based management (ABM) uses ABC information for operational improvement, not just product costing.

Memory aids

  • EOQ mnemonic: 'Root of Two Deep Sources Held' → √(2 × Demand × Setup cost ÷ Holding cost).
  • Contribution mantra: 'Sales minus Variable is Contribution; Contribution minus Fixed is Profit.'
  • Variance formula shortcut: 'Price = (S − A) × Aqty'; 'Quantity = (SQ − AQ) × SP'. Remember: for price variance, use Actual Quantity; for usage variance, use Standard Price.
  • Budget hierarchy memory: 'Top-Bottom-Zero' — Top-down (fixed budget), Bottom-up (ZBB), Rolling (continuous).
  • Cash flow categories: 'O-I-F' → Operating, Investing, Financing. Operating = day-to-day; Investing = long-term assets; Financing = capital structure.
  • Ratio grouping: 'LCAP' → Liquidity, Creditors (turnover), Ability (profitability), Profitability — remember which ratios belong where.
  • Overhead variance family tree: Total Overhead Variance splits into Cost Variance (spending) and Volume Variance; Volume splits into Capacity and Efficiency for fixed OH.
  • P/V ratio trick: 'P/V is the fraction of each rupee of sale that becomes contribution — think of it as the 'contribution harvest rate'.
  • Marginal costing vs Absorption: 'Marginal leaves fixed costs outside; Absorption packs them into inventory.'
  • ABC steps: 'Assign → Drive → Rate → Charge' — assign costs to activities, identify drivers, compute rate, charge to products.

Exam tips

  • For numerical problems, always write the formula first with symbol definitions before substituting values — this secures method marks even if final answer is wrong.
  • In variance analysis questions, show detailed workings for each sub-variance; examiners award step marks separately for price, usage, yield, and mix.
  • For Cash Flow Statement, prepare a separate Working Note for every reconciliation (e.g., changes in working capital, gain/loss on sale) — unlabelled workings lose marks.
  • In marginal costing problems involving limiting factors, always compute contribution per unit of the scarce factor to determine the optimum mix.
  • For ABC questions, present a clear table: Activity → Cost Pool → Cost Driver → Total Driver Units → Activity Rate → Allocation to Products.
  • In funds flow questions, treat all balance sheet changes systematically: compare each item between two years and classify as source or use with a clear schedule.
  • Remember that ICAI expects answers in a structured format — use headings like 'Working Notes', 'Schedule', 'Statement of Variances' to organise your response.
  • When asked for 'managerial interpretation', go beyond numbers: explain WHY a variance occurred (e.g., price rise due to inflation, efficiency loss due to machine breakdown) and suggest remedial action.
  • For ratio analysis, always interpret ratios in pairs — e.g., Current Ratio alone is insufficient; pair with Quick Ratio and Trading Cycle to give a complete liquidity picture.
  • In cost sheet questions, double-check that prime cost, works cost, cost of production, and cost of sales are correctly stepped — a mistake at one level cascades downward.
  • For budgetary control questions, distinguish clearly between ZBB and flexible budgeting — ZBB rebuilds from zero; flexible budget adjusts for actual activity level.
  • Standard costing numericals often combine material and labour variances — practice integrated problems where data is interleaved.
  • Always state assumptions clearly in qualitative answers (e.g., 'Assuming no abnormal idle time is included in overheads...').
  • For ABC versus traditional costing comparison questions, highlight at least three advantages of ABC and two situations where traditional costing may still be adequate.

Common traps

  • Using Actual Quantity for material price variance numerator instead of Actual Quantity Purchased — if purchase and usage quantities differ, price variance must be on purchased quantity to reflect purchasing department's performance.
  • Forgetting to annualise holding cost: If H is given as a percentage of unit cost, multiply by unit cost first; do not use the percentage alone in the EOQ denominator.
  • Including abnormal idle time in overhead absorption rate — it must be excluded and debited separately to Costing P&L Account.
  • Computing material yield and mix variances with Actual Price instead of Standard Price — all these variances are valued at Standard Price.
  • Mixing up Labour Efficiency Variance sign convention: (SH − AH) × SR; if AH > SH, result is adverse (negative), not favourable.
  • Treating depreciation as a cash outflow in Cash Flow Statement — it is a non-cash item and must be added back to profit.
  • Counting provision for depreciation as a source of funds in Funds Flow Statement — increase in provision is a source only if it represents a genuine funding source; actually, provision for depreciation is NOT a source of funds in funds flow.
  • Using closing stock instead of average stock for turnover ratios — average of opening and closing is standard practice unless average is explicitly not possible.
  • Applying P/V Ratio to Fixed Cost instead of Sales to find BEP — BEP (₹) = Fixed Cost ÷ P/V Ratio, NOT Fixed Cost × P/V Ratio.
  • Forgetting to adjust for inter-period items (like prepaid expenses, accrued income) in Cash Flow from Operations.
  • In multi-product BEP, forgetting to weight contributions by sales mix — use weighted average contribution per unit or weighted P/V ratio.
  • Confusing Fixed Overhead Volume Variance with Expenditure Variance: Volume relates to under/over-absorption due to output difference; Expenditure relates to budget vs actual spend.
  • In ABC, assigning overheads using a single plant-wide rate defeats the purpose of ABC — always use activity-specific cost drivers.
  • Incorrect treatment of dividend received vs dividend paid: Dividend received is operating/investing (depending on policy); Dividend paid is financing activity.
  • Using Sales instead of COGS for Stock Turnover Ratio — it should be COGS/Average Stock (though some variations use Sales, ICAI typically uses COGS).
