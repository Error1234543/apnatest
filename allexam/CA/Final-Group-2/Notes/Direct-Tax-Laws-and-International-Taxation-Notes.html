1. Basic Concepts: Residential Status, Previous Year and Assessment Year

The cornerstone of direct tax administration in India rests on the interplay between residential status, previous year, and assessment year. Under Section 6 of the Income-tax Act, 1961 ('the Act'), an individual's residential status determines the scope of total income taxable in India. An individual is said to be resident in India in any previous year if they satisfy at least one of two conditions: (a) they are in India for 182 days or more during the previous year, or (b) they are in India for 60 days or more during the previous year and 365 days or more in the preceding four previous years. For Indian citizens or persons of Indian origin visiting India or deemed residents under the relaxed 182-day rule, special provisions under Sections 6(6) apply where the 60-day threshold is extended to 182 days.

For companies, a company is resident in India if its Place of Effective Management (POEM) in the previous year is in India, or if it is incorporated in India. POEM is defined under Section 6(3A) as the place where key management and commercial decisions necessary for the conduct of the business are substantively made. The CBDT has issued Notification No. 13/2020 specifying factors for determining POEM.

A firm, LLP, or other artificial judicial person is resident in India if it is created or established in India. Non-residents include foreign companies, individuals staying outside India, and entities managed outside India. The scope of total income varies dramatically: resident individuals are taxed on worldwide income, while non-residents are taxed only on income received or deemed received in India or accruing, arising, or produced in India.

Previous Year (Section 3) is the financial year immediately preceding the assessment year in which income is assessed. Assessment Year (Section 2(9)) is the twelve-month period beginning on 1st April each year. A crucial distinction: income earned in PY 2024-25 is assessed in AY 2025-26, regardless of when the return is filed.

The concept of 'total income' under Section 2(45) means the aggregate amount computed under the Act, from which deductions under Chapter VI-A (Sections 80C to 80U) and relief under Sections 90, 90A, 91 are allowed. The computation follows a strict order: first compute income under each of the five heads, then set off losses under Section 70, carry forward losses under Sections 72-76, arrive at Gross Total Income under Section 99, then deduct Chapter VI-A allowances to reach Net Taxable Income.

2. Income from Salary (Sections 15-17)

Salary income arises from an employer-employee relationship and is determined under Sections 15 to 17. Section 15 categorizes salary into four components: (a) salary due from an employer or former employer during the relevant previous year, whether paid or not; (b) salary due from them but received in a subsequent previous year; (c) any advance of salary; and (d) the annual accretion to the balance at the credit of the employee in a recognised provident fund (taxable under Section 17(5)(i)). Notably, even unpaid salary accruing during the year is taxable — cash basis does not apply here.

Salaries include wages, pension, gratuities, fees, commissions, perquisites, profits in lieu of salary (Section 17(3)), and annual accretions to recognised provident fund balances. Perquisites under Section 17(2) encompass any benefit or amenity provided by the employer free or at reduced cost. Value of perquisites is determined under Rule 3, with specific formulae for different types: rent-free accommodation (Rules 4-4A), standard formula for other benefits (Rule 3(1)), and actual value for certain specified perquisites like cars, clubs, etc.

Profits in lieu of salary under Section 17(3) cover: (a) any compensation payable at or in connection with termination of employment; (b) any payment/free share/scheme made at or in connection with termination or change of management; and (c) any sum receivable at retirement or otherwise under an insurance policy (excluding life insurance governed by Section 10(10D)).

Exempt salary components are critical for exam purposes: government employees receive full exemption for foreign exchange salaries (Section 10(7)); diplomatic/consular staff enjoy exemptions under the Diplomatic Relations Act; travel tour facilities provided by employers may be exempt under Section 10(5); home town travel is exempt for government employees on deputation; local allowance for government employees posted outside India; conclave entertainment allowance for sportspersons; uniform allowance (Section 10(14)(ii)); family pension up to ₹1,50,000 or one-third of pension, whichever is less (Section 10(10)); and commuted pension (Section 10(10A)) which enjoys varying degrees of exemption depending on employer type and service period.

Standard deduction under Section 16(ia) allows ₹50,000 from salary income. Entertainment allowance is fully deductible only for government employees (Section 16(i)). Professional tax under Section 16(iii) is deductible to the extent actually paid, capped at ₹2,500.

3. Perquisites — Valuation and Special Provisions

Perquisite valuation under Rule 3 is a high-yield area. The general rule: if the perquisite is available on payment of rent, value equals actual rent payable minus rent actually paid by the employee (Rule 3(1)(a)). If no rent is charged, value is determined as a percentage of salary under Rule 3(2)(i).

Rent-free accommodation (Rules 4, 4A, 4B): Value depends on city population and ownership. For cities above 10 lakhs population (Rules 4A), if owned by employer, value is 15% of salary; if rented, higher of actual rent paid or 15% of salary. The government has notified specific cities: Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai above 10 lakhs, and others like Ahmedabad, Bangalore, Hyderabad, Pune, etc. Population thresholds determine applicability.

Provided furniture (Rule 4): Value is 10% of actual cost of furniture per year. Government furniture is valued at 7.5%.

Employee's domestic servant or gardener provided by employer (Rule 4): Actual expenditure incurred by employer is the value, reduced by any amount paid by the employee.

Securities and shares: Where employer provides securities/shares at concessional price, perquisite value is the difference between fair market value and price paid (Section 17(2)(vi)). Fair market value is determined under Rule 3A.

Group health insurance and group pension schemes: Exempt up to limits prescribed under Rule 2(2A) — currently group mediclaim premium up to ₹15,000 and group pension contribution up to ₹7.5 lakh per annum are exempt.

Educational facility provided to employee and their children: Value is actual expenditure incurred by employer, reduced by any amount recovered from employee (Rule 4). However, education allowance for up to two children is fully exempt under Section 10(14)(vii), subject to ₹100 per month per child limit (higher rates prescribed for children with handicaps).

Special perquisite valuation rules apply for stock options (Section 17(2)(iv)), specified securities (Section 17(2)(v)), and sweat equity shares (Section 17(2)(v)), with valuation based on Fair Market Value as per Rule 3A using methods prescribed under the Companies (Share Capital and Debentures) Rules.

4. Income from House Property (Sections 22-27)

Under Section 22, annual value of property consisting of any buildings or lands appurtenant thereto, owned by the assessee, is chargeable to tax under the head 'Income from House Property', unless the property is used for own business/profession (in which case it falls under PGBP). Two essential conditions must be met: (a) the property must consist of building or land appurtenant, and (b) the building must be owned by the assessee.

Gross Annual Value (GAV) determination (Sections 23-24): For let-out property, GAV is the higher of Actual Gross Rent Received (AGRR) or Expected Gross Rent (EGR). EGR = Municipal Value or Fair Rental Value, whichever is higher — subject to a ceiling of 150% of AGRR. Standard deduction under Section 24(a) is 30% of GAV (non-negotiable, one-third flat deduction).

Interest on borrowed capital under Section 24(b): For let-out/self-occupied property, deduction is limited to ₹2,00,000 per annum if condition(s) of Section 24(b)(c) are satisfied — i.e., loan taken after 1 April 1999 for acquisition/construction/repair/reconstruction, and construction/completion within 5 years from end of FY in which loan was taken. Pre-construction interest (max 5 installments from completion year) is deductible under Section 24(b)(ii). For vacant property, interest deduction remains available.

Self-Occupied Property (SOP): GAV is zero. No standard deduction. Interest on borrowed capital up to ₹2,00,000 is deductible (same 5-year condition). However, if the property was constructed/acquired after 30 March 1999 and completed within 5 years, the full ₹2,00,000 applies; otherwise nil.

Let-out of part and self-occupied part: If a building is let out in part and occupied by owner in part, the let-out portion's GAV follows normal rules, and the self-occupied portion gets zero GAV with interest deduction.

Deemed let-out property: When an assessee owns more than one house property, only one can be treated as SOP; the rest are deemed let-out (Section 23(4)). GAV is determined based on reasonable expected rent. Interest deduction of ₹2,00,000 is available subject to conditions.

Unlettable space: Income from letting of unlettable space forming part of residential house property is charged under SAP (Section 56) if separate from main building, but if integral, may fall under HOP. Key principle: municipal taxes paid are deductible from GAV under Section 23(1)(a).

5. Income from Profits and Gains of Business or Profession (Sections 28-44D)

Under Section 28, income from business includes profit and gain from any business or profession, any pre-operational income, bonus received from customers, value of perquisites, fees for professional practice, and compensatory loss/foul play damage. Section 29 defines profession as vocation requiring skilled manual or mental work. Business includes trade, commerce, manufacture, adventure in nature of trade, and any systematic activity with object of earning income.

Deductible expenses (Sections 30-43D): Section 30 allows deduction for rent, repairs of building, insurance of building, general insurance premiums on stock, rates and taxes, electricity charges, depreciation (Section 32), and renewal of implements. Section 31 permits deduction for insurance of plant/machinery and depreciation.

Capital allowances/depreciation (Section 32): Rate of depreciation is prescribed under Rule 5 of Income-tax Rules — Buildings (structural): 10%; Plant & Machinery: various rates from 5% to 60% depending on type; Furniture & Fittings: 15%; Vehicles: 15%; Computers: 40-60%. Written Down Value method applies unless initial depreciation@ 150% for new manufacturing plant set up on or after 1 April 2005 (available once only). Specified plant under Section 32(1)(iia) attracts higher rate.

Scientific research expenditure (Section 35): Deductible in full — to scientific research association, approved university, local authority, N.T.R.O, or in-house R&D. Contribution to approved scientific research scheme gets 100% deduction. Expenditure on development of goodwill (Section 35(2AB)) is amortised over 14 years. Disputed expenditure (Section 35A) maintains block of expenditure.

Broadcast fees (Section 35ABB): Broadcast fees paid for airing advertisements are deductible over the period of broadcast. Brand endorsement fees (Section 35CCC): deductible in year of payment.

Non-deductible expenses: Personal expenses (Section 37(1) general clause requires business connection); fines/penalties (Section 27 — penalty for contravention of any law); touting commission to brokers (Section 37(2A)); sums paid on account of any matter which is an offence (Section 37(1)); personal expenses of partner (Section 40(b)(v)); interest exceeding reasonable rate to relatives (Section 40(b)(bi)).

Disallowances under Section 40: Interest paid to partners (40(b)), remuneration to partners limited to higher of book profit@ 9% or ₹500,000 (whichever is higher) times capital employed. Payment to consultants/technicians without TDS disallowed under 40(a)(ia). Dividend declared but not paid disallowed under 40(a)(i). Any sum paid or accrued for any offence disallowed.

Withholding tax (TDS) requirements under Section 194J (professional/technical services @ 10%), 194C (contractors @ 1%-2%), 194H (commission/brokerage @ 5%), 194Q (purchase of goods @ 0.1% above ₹50 lakh turnover). Non-compliance leads to disallowance and interest under Section 234B/234C.

6. Capital Gains (Sections 45-55)

Capital gains arise on transfer of capital assets (Section 2(47)). A capital asset includes any property of any kind held by an assessee, excluding stock-in-trade, personal effects, and specific financial instruments. Transfer includes sale, exchange, relinquishment, extinguishment of rights, compulsory acquisition, and conversion into moveable property.

Classification: Short-term capital assets (STCA) held ≤36 months (≤24 months for immovable property, ≤12 months for listed securities, << 12 months for listed shares/securities under Section 2(42A)). Short-term capital gains (STCG) are taxed at normal slab rates except under Section 111A (@ 15% + surcharge + cess on listed securities). Long-term capital gains (LTCG) refer to STCA exceeding the threshold period.

Computation of Capital Gains (Section 48): Full value of consideration less cost of acquisition (Section 48) less cost of improvement (Section 48) less expenditure wholly and exclusively on transfer. Indexation of cost under Section 48(2) applies for LTCG on indexed assets (immovable property, long-term debt instruments, etc.) using Cost Inflation Index (CII).

Cost of Acquisition: For inherited asset, cost to previous owner (Section 49). For gifted asset, similar provision. Section 55 prescribes cost of acquisition/depreciation/CMV as applicable. Section 55(2) provides historical cost base — fair market value as on 1.1.2018 for assets acquired before 1.2.2018, for computation of LTCG u/s 112A.

Exemptions under Sections 54-55: Section 54 — residential house property; Section 54B — agricultural land; Section 54D — land/building compulsorily acquired; Section 54EC — bonds (NHAI/REC) up to ₹50 lakh; Section 54F — residential house (full exemption requires investing entire consideration); Section 54G — plant/machinery transferred to new industrial undertaking; Section 54GA — plant in SEZ; Section 54GB — equity shares of domestic company.

STCG exemptions (limited): Section 54(5A) for REITs/InvITs, Section 47(xiv)/(xv) for conversion of mutual funds to REITs/InvITs.

LTCG taxation rates: Section 112 — 20% with indexation; Section 112A — 10% above ₹1 lakh without indexation (applicable to listed equity shares and mutual funds). Securities Transaction Tax (STT)-paid listed shares/gains have special treatment. Carry forward of unabsorbed depreciation is allowed under Section 74A. Capital loss cannot be set off against salary income.

7. Income from Other Sources and Set-off/Carry Forward of Losses

Income from Other Sources (SAP) under Section 56 is the residuary head. It includes: dividend income (taxable under Section 56(2)(i)), interest income (bank FDs, savings account), winning from lotteries/racecourses/casinos (taxable u/s 56(2)(ix) and taxed at special rate under Section 57), gifts exceeding ₹50,000 in a year from non-relatives (Section 56(2)(x)), sum received under keyman insurance policy (Section 56(2)(iii)), royalty income (Section 56(2)(iv)), any sum received without adequate consideration (Section 56(2)(vi)), and agricultural income (exempt but relevant for surcharge and slab rate purposes under Section 56(5)).

Set-off and carry forward of losses (Sections 70-76): Section 70 allows intra-head set-off — loss under one source can be set off against income under the same head. Section 71 allows inter-head set-off — loss under one head can be set off against income under another head, EXCEPT speculative loss can be set off only against speculative income, and capital loss can be set off only against capital gains.

Carry forward rules (Sections 72-76):
• Loss from business/profession (Section 72): Can be carried forward for 8 assessment years. Must be filed ITR on time. Cannot be set off against salary but can be set off against other heads. Speculative loss (Section 73) can only be set off against speculative income.
• Capital loss (Section 74): Short-term capital loss can be carried forward for 8 years; long-term capital loss for 8 years. Must be set off against capital gains only.
• Loss under SAP (Section 74A): Cannot be carried forward.
• House property loss (Section 71(1)): Can be carried forward for 8 years, set off against house property income (max ₹2,00,000 per year against other heads).
• Unabsorbed depreciation (Section 74A): Can be carried forward indefinitely along with business loss, treated as business loss. Must file return on time.
• Loss from speculative business (Section 73): Carried forward for 4 years, set off only against speculative business income.
• Loss from Specified Business (Section 36(6) — old mining concession business): 8 years.

8. Rates of Tax, Surcharge and Health & Education Cess

Tax rates are prescribed annually via Finance Act and vary by residential status and entity type. For FY 2023-24 (AY 2024-25), key rates:

Individuals/HUF/AOP/BOI:
- Resident Individual: Slab rates — up to ₹2.5L: Nil; ₹2.5L-₹5L: 5%; ₹5L-₹10L: 20%; above ₹10L: 30%. Surcharge @ 4% (income up to ₹50L), 10% (up to ₹1Cr), 15% (up to ₹2Cr), 25% (up to ₹5Cr), 37% (above ₹5Cr). Health & Education Cess @ 4% on tax + surcharge.
- Non-Resident Individual/Firm/LLP: Flat 30% (+ surcharge + cess). No basic exemption limit.
- Companies: Domestic company: 25% (if turnover ≤ ₹400Cr and total income ≤ ₹400Cr, Section 115BAA election); 30% otherwise. Foreign company: 40% (plus MAT if applicable). SEIU/EIU: 25%.
- Trusts: VTD beneficiaries' share taxed at individual rates; balance at 30%.
- AOP/BOI: Generally 30%.

Alternative Minimum Tax (AMT) under Section 115JC: Applicable to assesses claiming deduction under Chapter VI-D ( Sections 80IA to 80U). AMT rate is 18.5% (+ surcharge + cess) on book profit. Credit of AMT can be carried forward for 15 years under Section 115JEE.

Minimum Alternate Tax (MAT) for companies: Section 115JB taxes companies at 18.5% (+ surcharge + cess) on book profit. MAT credit available under Section 115JEE for 15 years.

Surcharge calculation: For individuals, HUF, AOP, BOI — surcharge is on tax liability only. For companies, surcharge is on (tax + surcharge) i.e., compounding effect. Surcharge caps are governed by Section 193A for non-residents.

Health and Education Cess: Always 4% of (tax + surcharge), compounded.

9. Advance Tax, Self-Assessment Tax, Interest under Sections 234A, 234B, 234C, 234E

Advance Tax (Section 207-211): Every person liable to pay tax during the previous year must pay advance tax when tax liability is ₹10,000 or more in that year. Advance tax is payable in instalments: (a) on or before 15th June — 15% of estimated tax; (b) on or before 15th September — 45% cumulative; (c) on or before 15th December — 75% cumulative; (d) on or before 15th March — 100% cumulative. For specified taxpayers (those with tax deducted/collected at source), a single instalment on or before 15th March for balance tax is sufficient.

Adjustment of advance tax: If estimated tax increases after original estimation, shortfall attracts interest. Under Section 215, interest @ 12% p.a. is levied on short/deferred payment of advance tax from the date each instalment was due till actual payment.

Self-Assessment Tax (SAT) (Section 234A-234C framework): Tax paid voluntarily at the time of filing return under Section 139. SAT under Section 234A attracts interest @ 1% per month or part thereof from the due date of filing return till the date of actual payment.

Interest for deferment of advance tax (Section 234B): If advance tax paid is less than 90% of assessed tax, interest @ 1% p.m. from 1st April of assessment year till date of determination of total income under Section 143(1) or 144B. This applies when advance tax + TDS < 90% of total tax liability.

Interest for deferment of each instalment (Section 234C): Applicable when advance tax is paid late in any instalment. Interest @ 1% p.m. for 3 months from the due date of that instalment.

Interest on delayed return filing (Section 234E): ₹200 per day for late filing of return (maximum total ₹5,000). Applied irrespective of tax liability.

Interest for deferment of regular assessment (Section 234D): Applicable when tax payable on regular assessment is more than tax already paid; interest from due date of return filing till date of payment.

10. Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) — Key Provisions under Chapter XVII-B

TDS is a mechanism where tax is deducted at the source of income, enabling the government to collect tax continuously throughout the year. Key TDS provisions:

Section 192: TDS on salary — employer deducts tax based on estimated annual income and applicable slab rates. Must deposit within 7 days of due date (31st May for March deductee).

Section 194: TDS on dividends (now under Section 194K for mutual funds/dividends @ 10% above ₹5,000). Section 194A: Interest income (bank FDs @ 10% above ₹40,000; post office @ 10% above ₹5,000; co-operative society @ 10% above ₹5,000).

Section 194C: Payment to contractors/sub-contractors @ 1% (individual/HUF) or 2% (others) for work contract; 1%/2% for subcontract. Higher rates for non-residents (20%/30%). Threshold: ₹30,000 (single) / ₹1,00,000 (aggregate).

Section 194H: Commission/brokerage @ 5% (above ₹15,000).

Section 194J: Fees for professional/technical services @ 10% (above ₹30,000). Includes consulting, legal, architectural, technical services.

Section 194Q: Purchase of goods @ 0.1% (above ₹50 lakh turnover) by buyer. Applicable from 1 July 2021.

Section 194N: Withdrawal from bank/post office @ 2% (above ₹20L aggregate) or 0.5% (above ₹50L with PAN/Aadhaar). Applicable from 1 July 2021.

Section 194BA: TDS on interest from NPS on retirement @ 10% (above threshold).

Section 194EE: Withdrawal from EPF @ 10% (above ₹50,000 without PAN).

Section 194N relates to PAN/Aadhaar-based deduction rates. Section 206CCB applies when PAN is not quoted — TDS at double the rate or 20%, whichever is higher.

TDS returns: Quarterly TDS statements (Form 24Q for salary, Form 26Q for non-salary, Form 27Q for non-residents, Form 26QB for property purchase). Due dates: 31st July, 31st August, 31st October, 31st May following the quarter.

Penalties for failure to deduct/deduct at lower rate: Section 271C — penalty equal to tax short-demanded; Section 271D — penalty for accepting deposits in cash above ₹20,000; Section 276B — imprisonment for willful failure to deduct TDS.

11. Tax Collected at Source (TCS) — Section 206C

TCS operates as a parallel mechanism to TDS where tax is collected by the seller/collector at the point of sale/collection. Key TCS provisions under Section 206C:

Section 206C(1): Collection on sale of goods — 0.1% on sale consideration exceeding ₹50 lakh (by seller whose turnover exceeds ₹10 crore in preceding FY). Applicable from 1 October 2020. Goods include alcoholic liquor for human consumption, timber, sugarcane, scrap, mining products, and any other goods notified.

Section 206C(1F): Sale of goods — 0.1% on receipt of consideration exceeding ₹50 lakh (from 1 July 2021).

Section 206C(1H): TCS on sale of goods by retailer — 0.1% on receipt from buyer exceeding ₹50 lakh in a FY (from 1 Oct 2020). Applicable only where seller is required to get audit under Section 44AB.

Section 206C(1F) — Sale of goods: 0.1% on receipt exceeding ₹50 lakh by the seller whose total sales/gross receipts exceed ₹10 crore in preceding FY.

Section 206C(2): Collection on withdrawal of deposits — TCS on withdrawal from current account exceeding ₹50 lakh in a FY @ 0.2% (from 1 July 2021).

Section 206C(3): LRS (Liberalised Remittance Scheme) — 5% TCS on remittances exceeding ₹7 lakh per FY for purposes other than education/medical. However, TCS @ 5% applies from 1 Oct 2020 (was exempt earlier).

Section 206C(3A): Sale/purchase of motor vehicle exceeding ₹50 lakh @ 1% (on amount exceeding ₹50 lakh).

Section 206C(3B): Foreign tour packages @ 5%.

Section 206C(4): Coal/lignite mining lease right @ 2% (abolished with effect from 1 April 2023).

Section 206C(4A): Carrying passengers through vehicle @ 5% (introduced w.e.f. 1 Oct 2022).

Defaults in TCS collection attract penalty under Section 271HA and interest under Section 206C(5) at 1% p.m. from the date of collection to date of deposit.

12. Penal Provisions and Consequences of Defaults

The Act prescribes penal consequences for various defaults:

Failure to furnish return (Section 271F): Penalty up to ₹5,000 for delay beyond due date. Under Section 271F(1), penalty ranges from ₹5,000 to ₹50,000 depending on reasonableness of cause.

Contempt of assessment order (Section 272): Penalty for concealing income, furnishing inaccurate particulars — 50% to 200% of tax sought to be evaded under Section 270A.

Mis-reporting (Section 270A): Penalty @ 50% of tax on mis-reported income. Aggravated misreporting attracts 200% penalty. Misreported income includes income on which tax has not been paid and which is falsely claimed as lost/exempt.

Willful attempt to evade tax (Section 276C): Imprisonment up to 7 years and fine for evasion; imprisonment up to 3 years and fine for attempting to evade. Section 276CC covers failure to deduct/account for TDS/TCS — imprisonment up to 7 years and fine.

Failure to furnish TDS/TCS statement (Section 273B): Penalty up to ₹20,000 with continuation penalty of ₹200/day.

Penalty for furnishing false statement (Section 277): Punishment up to 3 years imprisonment and fine.

Penalty for failing to maintain books of account (Section 274): Penalties for non-maintenance of accounts, failure to get accounts audited.

Prosecution (Chapter XXIII): Various provisions for prosecution including Section 276B (willful attempt to defraud revenue), Section 277 (false statement/declaration), Section 278 (fraudulent avoidance of tax), Section 278A (abetment of offences).

Penalty under Section 234F for late filing of return: ₹5,000 if filed after due date but before 31st December; ₹10,000 if filed after 31st December but before 31st March; ₹5,000 if return is for low-income individuals (up to ₹5 lakh with no TDS/TCS, Section 139(1A)).

Section 270AD penalty framework replaces many earlier penalties under the new dispute resolution mechanism introduced from AY 2017-18.

13. International Taxation — DTAA, POEM, Transfer Pricing, GAAR

Double Taxation Avoidance Agreements (DTAA): India has entered into DTAAs with over 90 countries. Under Section 90, provisions of DTAA override domestic provisions to the extent more beneficial to the assessee. Key features of DTAA include: (a) Definition of resident (Article 4) — tie-breaker rules for dual residents; (b) Permanent Establishment (Article 5) — fixed place of business, dependent agent PE, construction PE (>6 months in most cases); (c) Allocation of taxing rights — business profits (Article 7) taxed only in PE country; (d) Withholding tax rates on dividends (Article 10), interest (Article 11), royalties (Article 12);

Place of Effective Management (POEM): Introduced via Amendment Act 2015 (effective from 1 April 2017). Section 6(3A) provides that a company whose POEM in any previous year is in India shall be deemed resident in India. POEM refers to the place where key management and commercial decisions are substantially made. CBDT issued POEM guidelines (Notification 13/2020) listing factors: board meetings, strategic decisions, senior management operations, etc.

Transfer Pricing (Chapter X, Sections 92-92F): Applicable to International Transaction (IT) and Specified Domestic Transaction (SDT). Arm's Length Price (ALP) is the price which would be charged between independent enterprises (Section 92C). Methods: Comparable Uncontrolled Price (CUP), Resale Price Method (RPM), Cost Plus Method (CPM), Profit Split Method (PSM), and Transactional Net Margin Method (TNMM). ALP must be determined using the most suitable method.

Documentation requirements (Rule 10DA): Maintain contemporaneous documentation including international transaction details, comparables, functions/assets/risk analysis (FAR analysis), and benchmarking study. Transfer pricing report (Form 3CD part B) must be obtained from chartered accountant.

General Anti-Avoidance Rules (GAAR): Introduced w.e.f. 1 April 2017 (AY 2017-18). GAAR applies where the main purpose is to obtain tax benefit and the arrangement is an impermissible avoidance arrangement (Section 96). CAAB (Commissionerate for Avoidance of Avoidance Arrangements) under Section 144B has power to issue notice. Tax benefit includes reduction in tax liability, avoidance of withholding tax, obtaining refund, etc.

Designated Authority: Issues show-cause notice; assessee may reply within 30 days. Proceedings before tribunal. Review by Assessing Officer. Time limit: 3 years from end of FY in which order is passed (extendable by 2 years in certain cases).

14. Non-Resident Taxation and Special Provisions

Non-resident taxation involves several specialized areas:

Royalty (Section 9(1)(vii)): Any payment for use of patent, copyright, trademark, software, technical knowledge, or know-how is deemed to accrue in India if receivable by non-resident. Rate of royalty tax depends on DTAA/Section 115A.

Technical Services Fee (Section 9(1)(vii)): Similar to royalty but distinct — includes management fees, consultancy fees, technical assistance.

Specified Persons (Section 195): TDS on payments to non-residents — deductible at usual rates (or DTAA rate if lower). Form 15CA/CB required for remittances abroad.

Remittance basis of taxation: Under Section 5(2), income earned outside India by non-resident is taxable in India only when received in India (Section 5(2)(b)).

Shipping business (Section 172): Profits from shipping business of non-resident calculated at 7.5% of amounts received in India for carriage of passengers/goods. Option to compute actual profits exists.

Air transport business (Section 172A): Similar framework for non-resident airlines — 7.5% of amounts received in India, or actual profits, whichever is higher.

Life insurance business (Section 44B): Presumptive taxation @ 8% of amounts received in India or overseas, or 5% of total premium, whichever is higher. Option for actual profits.

Foreign tour operators (Section 44BBA): 5% of amount received in India or 10% of aggregate of amounts received in India and abroad, whichever is higher.

Geographical Information System (GIS) (Section 44BBB): 10% of amount received in India for GIS services.

Hotel (Section 44B): 5% of aggregate of amounts received in India and outside India.

Mining (Section 44BA): Presumptive taxation for companies engaged in mining.

SECI (Section 44ADA): Presumptive taxation for small taxpayers (not applicable to NRs).

15. Special Assessment Provisions and Returns of Income

Return of Income (Section 139): Every person whose total income exceeds the maximum amount not chargeable to tax must furnish return of income in prescribed form (ITR-1 to ITR-7) to the Assessing Officer. Due date for individuals/HUF: 31st July of AY (extended to 31st August for audit cases u/s 44AB). For companies: 31st October. Revised return (Section 139(5)) can be filed if original return is incomplete/wrong, before end of relevant AY or completion of assessment, whichever is earlier.

Search and seizure (Section 132): Authorized officer can enter premises, search, seize documents, record statements, and seal records. Block period assessment under Section 158BC computes undisclosed income from relevant previous year (prior to search) plus current year income.

Reassessment (Section 147-149): Where Assessing Officer has reason to believe income has escaped assessment, reassessment can be made within 4 years (Section 148) — extendable to 6 years for income > ₹50 lakh escaping assessment, and 10 years for income > ₹200 lakh escaping assessment. Conditional extension powers exist under Section 148A for assessment/reassessment.

Scrutiny Assessment (Section 143(2)): Return processed under Section 143(1) may be selected for scrutiny. Notice under Section 143(2) requires personal hearing/evidence submission.

Completion of Assessment (Section 143(3)): Upon scrutiny, AO passes assessment order determining total income, tax payable, refund due.

Estimated tax/self-assessment: Under Section 140A, taxpayer must pay self-assessment tax before filing return.

Revision of orders (Section 254/264): Tribunal can revise its order; Commissioner can revise orders prejudicial to revenue within specified time.

Important facts

  • Residential status determines scope of total income — resident on worldwide basis, non-resident on India-sourced income only
  • Section 6 test for individuals: 182 days in PY OR 60 days in PY + 365 days in preceding 4 PY; special rule for Indian citizens/PIOs
  • POEM test (Section 6(3A)) is decisive for companies — if POM in India, company is resident in India
  • Standard deduction on salary is fixed at ₹50,000 (Section 16(ia))
  • Rent-free accommodation perquisite valuation depends on city population (10 lakh threshold) and ownership
  • House property GAV = higher of Actual Rent or Expected Rent; Expected Rent = higher of MV or FRV, subject to 150% cap
  • Depreciation on buildings (structural) @ 10%; plant & machinery varies 5%-60%; computers @ 40%-60%
  • Capital gains: STCG for listed equity @ 15% (Section 111A) with STT; LTCG above ₹1 lakh @ 10% (without indexation) u/s 112A
  • Indexation benefit available under Section 48 for LTCG computation on non-equity assets
  • Advance tax threshold: ₹10,000 tax liability; instalments on 15th Jun/Sep/Dec/Mar
  • TDS on contractor payments u/s 194C: 1%/2%; professional services u/s 194J: 10%
  • TCS on sale of goods u/s 206C(1H): 0.1% above ₹50 lakh; on LRS u/s 206C(2): 5%
  • Section 90 DTAA override: treaty provisions prevail over domestic law if more beneficial
  • GAAR applicable from AY 2017-18; impermissible avoidance arrangements attract tax adjustment
  • Transfer pricing ALP determination uses most suitable of 5 methods under Rule 10B
  • Section 270A penalty: 50% for misreporting, 200% for aggravated misreporting
  • Mandatory TDS/TCS compliance critical — non-compliance attracts 100% penalty + interest

Formulas and relationships

Gross Annual Value (House Property) = Higher of Actual Gross Rent Received or Expected Gross Rent; Expected Rent = Higher of Municipal Value or Fair Rental Value, subject to 150% of AGRR ceiling
Net Annual Value = GAV minus Municipal Taxes paid during the year
Income from House Property = NAV minus 30% (standard deduction u/s 24(a)) minus Interest on borrowed capital (u/s 24(b)) — max ₹2,00,000 for let-out/SOP
Capital Gains = Full Value of Consideration minus Cost of Acquisition (index-adjusted if LTCG) minus Cost of Improvement minus Expenditure wholly & exclusively on transfer
Indexed Cost of Acquisition = CIP × (CII of year of transfer / CII of year of acquisition)
AMT Tax Liability = 18.5% of Book Profit adjusted for adjustments prescribed u/s 115JC
Advance Tax Instalment Pattern: 15th Jun = 15%, 15th Sep = 45%, 15th Dec = 75%, 15th Mar = 100%
TDS on Contractor u/s 194C: 1% for individual/HUF, 2% for others (higher for non-residents)
Interest u/s 234B = 1% per month on shortfall between 90% of assessed tax and advance tax + TDS
Interest u/s 234C = 1% per month for deferment of advance tax instalments
Perquisite value of provided furniture = 10% of actual cost per annum (7.5% for government furniture)
POEM determination: based on where key management and commercial decisions are substantively made — board meetings, strategic decisions

Common mistakes

  • Confusing residential status tests for individuals versus companies — companies use POEM test, individuals use day-count test
  • Treating unpaid salary as non-taxable — salary is taxable on accrual basis, not just receipt basis u/s 15
  • Forgetting that标准 deduction on house property is a flat 30% of NAV, not calculated separately for each expense
  • Applying indexation benefit to listed equity shares — no indexation benefit for equity under Section 112A
  • Incorrectly computing AGV with the 150% cap applied to both MV and FRV — cap applies only to Expected Rent calculation
  • Assuming GAAR applies to all arrangements — GAAR requires impermissible purpose as primary objective
  • Missing the 5-year condition for interest deduction on housing loan under Section 24(b) — if construction not completed within 5 years from end of FY of loan, deduction is nil
  • Treating TDS rates uniformly across all payees — non-resident rates under Section 195 differ from resident rates
  • Not checking DTAA benefits before applying domestic TDS rates on payments to non-residents — DTAA rates are usually lower
  • Confusing MAT with AMT — MAT applies to companies only, AMT applies to all entities claiming deductions under Chapter VI-D
  • Assuming speculative loss can be set off against any head — speculative loss can ONLY be set off against speculative income
  • Forgetting that revised return under Section 139(5) can be filed only before expiry of relevant AY or completion of assessment, whichever is earlier
  • Applying TCS provisions incorrectly — TCS rate on sale of goods is 0.1% only when buyer's turnover exceeds ₹10 crore
  • Ignoring the requirement of Form 15CA/CB for remittances to non-residents — failure attracts consequences
