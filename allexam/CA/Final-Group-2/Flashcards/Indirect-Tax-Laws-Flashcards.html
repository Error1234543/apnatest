CA Final Group 2 — Indirect Tax Laws (Flashcards)

CAEnglishFlashcards

Card 1

What constitutional amendment introduced GST in India?

The Constitution (101st Amendment) Act, 2016 introduced GST. It was implemented from July 1, 2017. It inserted Articles 246A, 269A, 279A, 279B, and Chapter V-A (Articles 268A–269A) into the Constitution.

Card 2

What are the four components of GST under the Indian federal structure?

GST has four components: • CGST — Central GST (Union Government share) • SGST — State GST (State Government share) • UTGST — Union Territory GST (UT share, applies to UTs without legislature) • IGST — Integrated GST (inter-state supply, collected by Centre, apportioned between Centre and State)

Card 3

Under Article 246A, who has legislative power over GST?

Article 246A grants Parliament and every State Legislature concurrent power to make laws with respect to GST. However, Parliament has exclusive power to make laws with respect to IGST (Article 246A read with Article 269A). State Legislatures have exclusive power over CGST/SGST/UTGST for intra-state supplies.

Card 4

Distinguish between intra-state supply and inter-state supply under GST.

Intra-state supply: Both the location of the supplier and the place of supply are within the same state. Tax applicable: CGST + SGST (or UTGST). Inter-state supply: The location of the supplier and the place of supply are in different states (or one is in a foreign territory/UDIAs). Tax applicable: IGST. Import of goods/services: Treated as inter-state supply.

Card 5

What is the threshold exemption limit for registration under CGST/SGST?

For supply of goods: ₹40 lakhs (₹20 lakhs for special category states). For supply of services: ₹20 lakhs (₹10 lakhs for special category states). Special Category States include: Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Nagaland, Manipur, Mizoram, Arunachal Pradesh, Tripura, Meghalaya, Sikkim, Assam, Jammu & Kashmir, Ladakh, and Puducherry. Note: These thresholds apply unless the person is an e-commerce operator or makes taxable inter-state supplies (no threshold exemption for such persons).

Card 6

Who is required to register compulsorily under GST despite below threshold?

Compulsory registration (Section 24) includes: • Persons making inter-state taxable supplies • E-commerce operators • Persons required to pay tax under reverse charge • Casual taxable persons • Non-resident taxable persons • Persons supplying through e-commerce operators who are required to collect tax at source (TCS) • Persons whose aggregate turnover exceeds threshold during the financial year • Persons required to deduct tax at source (TDS) • Input service distributors • Persons paying tax under composition scheme who opt out • Others notified by Govt.

Card 7

What is a Composition Scheme under GST? Give eligibility and rate structure.

Composition Scheme (Section 10) is a simplified compliance regime for small taxpayers. Eligibility: Aggregate turnover up to ₹1.5 crore (₹75 lakhs for special category states). Not available to: inter-state suppliers, e-commerce operators, casual taxable persons, non-residents, manufacturers of ice cream/pansala/nASC. Rates: • Manufacturer of goods: 1% (CGST 0.5% + SGST 0.5%) • Restaurant (not in hotel premises): 5% • Other taxable persons: 6% (CGST 3% + SGST 3%) Features: No input tax credit, quarterly return filing, no separate inter-state supply, no e-invoicing.

Card 8

Define 'Supply' under Section 7 of CGST Act. What is included/excluded?

Section 7 defines supply broadly and includes: (a) For or in the course of business (b) Warped or for consideration / Between related persons (even without consideration) (c) Specified activities listed in Schedule I (e.g., permanent transfer of business assets for own use) (d) Import of services by unregistered person from a non-taxable offshore person. Excluded from supply: • Activities listed in Schedule II (e.g., construction of immovable property) — treated as supply of goods or services respectively • Transactions not amounting to supply per Schedule III (e.g., alcohol for human consumption, electoral votes, services by employee to employer)

Card 9

What is the place of supply of goods under general rules (Sections 10 and 12)?

General rule (Section 12(2)): Place of supply = location where the movement of goods terminates for delivery. Specific cases: • Goods delivered to a transporter: Location of recipient • Goods installed at a site: Location where installation is completed • Goods imported into India: Place of进口 (import destination) — i.e., place of entry into India • Goods transported outside India: Destination of goods • Goods supplied to e-commerce operator: Location of recipient (if registered)

Card 10

What is the place of supply of services (Section 13)?

General rule (Section 13(2)): Location of the recipient of services. If recipient is unregistered: Location of the supplier. Specific rules: • Real estate services: Location of the real estate • Hotel/accommodation services: Location of the establishment where services are provided • Restaurant services: Location where services are actually provided • Performance at a fixed venue: Location of the venue • Training/conference: Location where services are physically performed • Passenger transport: Location where the passenger embarks

Card 11

Determine the time of supply for goods under Section 12 of CGST Act.

Time of supply of goods (Section 12): • If invoice issued within prescribed time: Date of issuance of invoice • If invoice not issued within prescribed time: Date of completion of removal of goods • If goods released before invoice: Date of release Prescribed time for invoice issuance: • Registered person: Before or on date of removal • For goods requiring registration-based delivery: Date of delivery note Input: Where multiple invoices are issued for a single removal, the time of supply is the earliest date of any invoice.

Card 12

Determine the time of supply for services under Section 13 of CGST Act.

Time of supply of services (Section 13): • If invoice issued within prescribed time (30 days from date of supply, 45 days for banking/financial Institutions): Date of issuance of invoice • If payment made before invoice: Date of receipt of payment or date of invoice, whichever is earlier • If invoice not issued within prescribed time: Date of provision of service • If payment received after invoice date: Date of receipt of payment (under Rule 33 — deemed issue on date payment received) Minimum time of supply: Date of issuance of invoice if issued within prescribed period, or date of completion of supply.

Card 13

How is the value of supply determined under Section 15 of CGST Act?

Value of supply (Section 15): • Transaction value — price actually paid or payable where supplier and recipient are not related and price is sole consideration. Included in value: • Packing, freight, insurance • Subsidies (except those conferred by govt.) • Commission, packing, forwarding charges • Any taxes other than GST • Interest/late fee for deferred payment Excluded from value: • Discount before/during supply (if recorded in invoice) • SGST/CGST/IGST/UTGST shown separately in invoice • UTGST where IGST is charged

Card 14

What adjustments can be made in the value of supply for future discounts?

Discount allowed after the supply is made may be deducted from the value of supply if: • The discount was known at the time of supply • The recipient claims input tax credit in respect of the invoice • The supplier has obtained acknowledgment from the recipient confirming that ITC has been claimed This ensures anti-profiteering and prevents double benefit.

Card 15

Explain the concept of Input Tax Credit (ITC) under Section 16 of CGST Act.

Section 16 provides that every registered person shall be entitled to take credit of input tax charged on any supply of goods or services used in the course or furtherance of business. Conditions for availing ITC (Section 16(2)): • Possession of tax invoice or debit note • Receipt of goods/services • Tax has been actually paid to govt. under Section 39 • Supplier has furnished GST return and tax charged thereon is paid • No breakdown of tax in invoice ITC cannot be claimed on: • Goods/services used for exempt supplies • Personal use • Goods lost/stolen/theft • Block credits u/s 17(5)

Card 16

What are the blocked credits under Section 17(5) of CGST Act?

Section 17(5) blocks ITC on: • Motor vehicles (except when used for transportation of goods, passenger carrying, driving training, or used for further taxable supply of such vehicles) • Food, beverages, eating purposes, beauty treatment, health services, cosmetic surgery, life insurance, health insurance • Construction of immovable property other than plant/factory • Goods/services received for free (e.g., samples, gift) • Works contract services for construction of immovable property (except when used for further supply of works contract service) • Membership of health club, gym, sports club • Travel benefits to employees (leave travel concession, etc.) • Goods/ services used partly for exempt supply

Card 17

How is ITC distributed for mixed use (taxable and exempt supplies)?

Section 17(2): ITC on inputs/service used partly for taxable and partly for exempt supply must be apportioned. Method: • ITC attributable to taxable supply = Total ITC × (Turnover from taxable + exempt + nil-rated supplies excluding exempt)/Total turnover • Balance ITC is not available (clawed back). Exception: ITC on capital goods used only for exempt supply is 100% clawed back. ITC on input used exclusively for taxable supply is fully available.

Card 18

What is the reverse charge mechanism (RCM) under GST?

Under reverse charge mechanism (Section 9(3)), the liability to pay tax is on the recipient instead of the supplier. Applicable when: • Supplier is unregistered person • Notification notifies specific supplies to attract RCM (goods or services) Recipient can claim ITC only if he is registered and the goods/services are used for business purpose. Recipient must pay tax within 60 days from date of receipt of goods/services (extendable by 30 days).

Card 19

When does e-invoicing apply under GST? Describe the key requirements.

E-invoicing applies to: • Aggregate turnover exceeding ₹500 crore (currently reduced to ₹100 crore as per recent notifications — verify latest government notification at exam time) • B2B supplies of goods or services. Requirements: • Invoice must be generated on IRP (Invoice Registration Portal) and assigned a 14-digit IRN (Invoice Reference Number) • QR code mandatory on invoice • Invoice must comply with Rule 46 and Invoice Rules, 2019 • Self-certified invoice copy uploaded to portal (not necessarily through API) • Benefits: Auto-population in GSTR-1, GSTN Portal will generate e-way bill parts A automatically in certain cases.

Card 20

What are the rules for generation of E-Way Bill under GST?

E-Way Bill required when value of consignment exceeds ₹50,000 (for intra-state) or ₹50,000 (for inter-state). Rules: • Part A: Required for movement of goods — contains description, value, GSTIN of sender/receiver, HSN, transport document number • Part B: Vehicle details — vehicle number, distance Valid period: • Up to 500 km = 1 day • Additional 1 day for every additional 500 km (or part thereof) Generated online on GST Portal (ewaybill gst.gov.in). Not required for exempt goods, certain specified goods, or where transported by non-motorized conveyance.

Card 21

What is the procedure for refund under GST (Section 16)?

Refund situations: • Export of goods/services without payment of tax (zero-rated supply) — bond/LUT u/s 16 • Export with payment of tax — refund of input tax credit including IGST paid • Accumulated credit due to inverted duty structure — refund of ITC on input/input services • Supplies to SEZ/DTA — refund of unutilized ITC • Reject, remanufacture, return of goods • Overpayment of tax (error/duress) Procedure: • File application RFD-01 within 2 years from relevant date • Acknowledgment through RFD-02 • Verification by proper officer — order passed through RFD-03 • Interest payable on delayed refund at rate prescribed (currently 6% per annum)

Card 22

What is an Advance Ruling in GST? Who can apply and what is the scope?

Advance Ruling (Section 28) allows a registered person to obtain a ruling on: • Classification of any goods or services • Determination of value of taxable supply • Eligibility for ITC • Determination of liability to pay tax on any supply • Whether supply is taxable or not • Determination of place of supply Applicant: Either supplier or recipient (both must be in same state) or a registered person from a different state applying jointly. Authority: AAR (GST) for central matters; State AAR for state matters. Ruling binding on applicant and the concerned tax authority.

Card 23

What are the provisions regarding job work under GST?

Job work (Section 143): Treatment of goods sent to a job worker for processing. Key provisions: • Principal can send inputs/capital goods to job worker without payment of tax • Job worker can issue bill of supply instead of invoice • Goods must be returned within 1 year (inputs) or 3 years (capital goods) from date of sending • Time of supply to job worker: Date of removal or date of receipt by principal, whichever is earlier • Extension of time by 1 year possible with approval of proper officer • Tax liability arises at time of return or after expiry of period (if not returned)

Card 24

What are the differences between CGST Act, SGST Act and IGST Act?

CGST Act: Governs intra-state supply of goods and services. Tax collected by Central Government. Rate split between Centre and State equally. SGST Act: Governs intra-state supply. Tax collected by respective State Government. IGST Act: Governs inter-state supply and import/export. Tax collected by Central Government only, then apportioned between Centre and State equally. All three Acts are co-extensive and mirror each other in structure but differ in administrative authority and destination of tax revenue.

Card 25

What is the appeal process under GST? Discuss tiers and timelines.

Appeal hierarchy under GST: 1. First Appeal (Section 112): To Appellate Authority — filed within 3 months from date of order (extendable by 1 month on sufficient cause) 2. Second Appeal (Section 117): To Appellate Tribunal (GST) — filed within 45 days from date of order 3. Further Appeal (Section 118): To High Court — only on substantial question of law, within 60 days Jurisdiction: • CGST/SGST orders appeal to Central or State Appellate Authority (as per tax component) • IGST orders appeal to Central Appellate Authority • Joint appeals: Both CGST and SGST portions go to respective authorities

Card 26

What are the provisions regarding audit under GST?

Three types of audits: 1. Annual Audit (Section 35): Every registered person with turnover exceeding ₹2 crore must get accounts audited and furnish Form GSTR-9 along with audited annual statement in Form GSTR-9C. 2. Special Audit (Section 46): Proper officer may direct registered person to get accounts audited by CA/CMA appointed by proper officer if he considers it necessary due to complexity or interest of revenue. 3. Audit by proper officer (Section 65): Proper officer may authorize any officer to conduct inspection based on suspicion of suppression of turnover or any fraud. Time limits: Annual audit within 31st December of next FY; Special audit within 90 days (extendable to 180 days).

Card 27

What is the treatment of unregistered person receiving goods/services from registered person?

An unregistered person receiving goods/services from a registered person: • Cannot claim ITC (Section 17(5) implies ITC available only to registered persons) • Supplier must issue bill of supply instead of tax invoice • Recipient is liable to pay tax under RCM only if notified under Section 9(4) (now withdrawn) • For goods: Supplier is liable to pay tax

Card 28

How is GST levied on import of goods and services?

Import of goods: Treated as inter-state supply u/s 5(1) of IGST Act. IGST levied on imported goods in addition to customs duty. Import of services: Treated as inter-state supply u/s 5(1) of IGST Act. IGST levied u/s 3(7) read with Section 3(6) of IGST Act. Customs valuation: Customs value as per Customs Act + applicable IGST = total tax payable. Exemptions: Notifications may exempt certain imports from IGST.

Card 29

What are the key differences between GST and earlier indirect tax regime in India?

Pre-GST regime had multiple overlapping taxes: • Central Excise/Duty (manufacture) • Service Tax (services) • VAT/Sales Tax (intra-state sale) • Entry Tax/Octroi (entry into local area) • CST (inter-state sale) • Luxury Tax, Entertainment Tax, etc. Key issues addressed by GST: • Cascading effect (tax on tax) eliminated through ITC mechanism • Unified national market created • Simplified compliance through fewer returns • Input tax credit cascading eliminated • Digital compliance (e-invoicing, e-way bill)

Card 30

What is the effect of 'deemed supply' under Schedule I of CGST Act?

Schedule I specifies transactions deemed to be supply even without consideration: • Permanent transfer/disposal of business assets where ITC has been claimed • Supply between related persons or distinct persons (as defined) — even without consideration • Supply of goods between head offices and branch offices in different states • Supply by principal to branched office or vice versa • Provision of goods or services free of cost by one branch to another branch in same state where ITC claimed

Card 31

What is the role of E-commerce Operator under GST?

E-commerce operator means any person who owns, operates or manages digital/ electronic facility for facilitating supply of goods or services (Section 7(32)). Obligations: • Collect Tax at Source (TCS) u/s 52 — 1% CGST + 1% SGST (2% IGST) on net taxable supplies • Deposit TCS by 10th of following month • Furnish statement of collection in FORM GST PMT-10 by 10th of following month • Provide information to registered taxpayer on demand • Pay GST on supply facilitated through platform where supplier is unregistered (as per Rule 31(3))

Card 32

What is the difference between tax evasion and misutilization of ITC under GST?

Tax Evasion (Section 132): Willful suppression of facts, furnishing false information, or deliberately evading tax payment. Penalties range from 10% to 100% of tax evaded + prosecution. Misutilization of ITC (Section 132(1)(c)): Using ITC availed in contravention of provisions — e.g., ITC on blocked credits, ITC on personal use, ITC without valid invoice. Treated as tax evasion if intentional. Penalties are stricter for tax evasion (compounding not allowed in most cases) while misutilization may be compounded in certain circumstances with interest and penalty of 50% or 100% depending on circumstances.

Card 33

What is the procedure for composition dealer filing returns?

Composition dealer (Section 10) files: • GSTR-4 (Annual Return) — on or before 30th May of following financial year • GSTR-4A (Auto-generated statement) • Payment of tax quarterly (GSTR-4) — by 18th of month succeeding the quarter • No separate GSTR-1/GSTR-3B required • No ITC claimed by composition dealer • Cannot issue tax invoice — can only issue bill of supply • No inter-state supply allowed • No e-invoicing requirement

Card 34

What are the provisions regarding cancellation of GST registration?

Cancellation modes (Section 29): 1. Suo moto cancellation: By registered person when business ceases, insolvent, or permanently discontinues business. 2. Authority-initiated cancellation: By proper officer where registration obtained by fraud, non-filing of returns for 6 consecutive months, or other grounds. 3. On surrender: Filed in FORM GST REG-16. Effects of cancellation: • Output tax liability till date of cancellation • ITC reversal on closing stock of inputs/capital goods (within 6 months from date of cancellation) • Final tax payment through FORM GST DRC-03 • No refund of unutilized ITC (except in case of closure of business)

Card 35

What is the impact of supply of goods on approval basis under GST?

Approval basis supply: When goods are supplied conditionally with approval for use in manufacturing subsequent goods. Treatment: • Time of supply: Date of removal of goods for further supply or date when conditions are fulfilled — whichever is later • Value: Transaction value (price paid) — no additional tax if goods are further processed and supplied with tax • ITC: Available on inputs used for manufacturing approved goods • If goods not used as approved, tax payable on original value + interest

Card 36

How is IGST apportioned between Centre and State?

IGST collected by Central Government is apportioned between Centre and State in manner prescribed by Parliament (Article 269). • For inter-state supply where destination is a State: IGST apportioned equally — 50% to Centre, 50% to destination State • For import of goods: Entire IGST credited to Centre • For export of goods/services: IGST refunded to exporter, balance (if any) credited to Centre The Goods and Services Tax (Compensation to States) Act, 2017 provides for compensation to states for loss of revenue for 5 years post-GST implementation (extended through 2022, further extended).

Card 37

What is the concept of 'supply against advance payment' under GST?

Section 31(1) read with Rule 32: Time of supply when advance received. Goods: Date of issue of invoice OR date of receipt of advance — whichever is earlier. Services: Date of issue of invoice OR date of receipt of advance — whichever is earlier. Invoice must be issued within prescribed time (30 days for services) after receipt of advance. If invoice not issued within prescribed time, time of supply is date of completion of supply.

Card 38

What is the difference between 'distinct person' and 'related person' under GST?

Distinct person (Section 25): Two or more businesses registered under same GSTIN but having separate establishments in different states or same state — treated as distinct persons for ITC, inter-state supply purposes. Related person (Section 28): Persons who are associated enterprises, or one is individual and other is relative, or one is director of other, or both are partners, or one controls other, or both controlled by same person. Implication: Supply between distinct/related persons is taxable even without consideration (Schedule I), unlike supply to unrelated persons without consideration which is not taxable (Schedule III).

Card 39

What are the key features of GSTN (Goods and Services Tax Network)?

GSTN is a non-profit company (Section 8, Companies Act) providing IT infrastructure for GST implementation. Functions: • Registration portal (single window) • Return filing and processing • Invoice registration for e-invoicing • E-way bill generation • Refund processing • Matching of input tax credit (IRP matching) • Portal for communication between taxpayers and department • Data analytics for risk profiling GSTN does not have statutory powers — it is an IT facilitator. The assessment, audit, and enforcement powers remain with the proper officer under CGST/SGST/IGST Acts.
