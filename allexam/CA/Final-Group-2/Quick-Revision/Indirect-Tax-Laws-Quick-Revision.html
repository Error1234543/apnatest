CA Final Group 2 — Indirect Tax Laws: Quick Revision

CAEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Goods: All materials, substances, and articles, and includes implicit/exclusive supplies like money, securities, etc. as specified under Section 2(52) of CGST Act.
  • Service: Any activity except exclusionary items; includes activities in relation to goods such as transport, warehousing, processing, etc. as per Section 2(102).
  • Supply: Includes all forms of supply of goods or services or both, made for consideration, import of services for consideration (whether or not for consideration), and activities specified as supply even without consideration under Schedule II. (Section 7)
  • Taxable Person: A person who is in registered or required to be registered under Section 22-24.
  • Business: Includes trade, commerce, manufacture, profession, attachment, occupation, or undertaking of any kind, whether organized or not. (Section 2(13))
  • Input Tax:GST paid on input goods/services received by a registered person.
  • Output Tax: GST charged on outward supplies made by a registered person.
  • Place of Supply: Determines whether supply is intra-state or inter-state. Key rules are provided under Section 10-12 of IGST Act.
  • Time of Supply: Determines when tax liability arises — for goods (Section 12), services (Section 13), and continuous supply of services (Section 14).
  • Valuation of Taxable Supply: General valuation rule under Section 15 — transaction value including price paid between supplier and recipient, adjusted for adductions and deductions specified therein.

Key concepts

  • Composite vs Mixed Supplies: ['Composite Supply (Section 2(30)): A supply made by a taxable person consisting of two or more supplies which are naturally bundled and supplied in conjunction with each other in the ordinary course of business, one of which is a principal supply.', 'Example: Mobile phone + carrying case + charger — phone is principal supply.', 'Mixed Supply (Section 2(74)): Two or more individual supplies made in conjunction together for a single price, where none constitutes a principal supply.', 'Example: Hotel package with accommodation + breakfast + laundry — taxed at highest rate among components.', 'Important: Composite supplies take the GST rate of principal supply; mixed supplies take the highest rate among individual supplies.']
  • GST Registration — Composition Scheme: ['Normal registration: Mandatory under Section 22 if aggregate turnover exceeds threshold (₹40 lakhs general; ₹20 lakhs special category states; ₹10 lakhs for goods from special category states).', 'Composition Scheme (Section 10): Available to suppliers of goods (not services); turnover limit up to ₹1.5 crore; CGST@0.5%, SGST@0.5%, IGST@1% for intra/inter-state respectively; eligible for credit but cannot collect tax from buyers separately; quarterly returns only.', 'Special category states (Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, etc.) have lower thresholds — note current limits carefully.', 'E-commerce operators must register irrespective of turnover (Section 24(v)).', 'CAS/COID (Common Account Number / Centralized Assessment) is relevant for large taxpayers.']
  • Goods and Services Tax — Input Tax Credit (ITC): ['Section 16: Every registered person shall be entitled to the credit of input tax charged on any supply of goods or services or both to him, used or intended to be used in the course or furtherance of business.', 'Conditions: (a) taxpayer has received goods/services; (b) tax invoice produced; (c) GST paid is actually deposited to government; (d) return filed under Section 39.', 'Blocked Credits (Section 17(5)): No ITC on motor vehicles except when used for transport of passengers/goods, manufacturing, or furtherance of business; food/beverages; health/fitness membership; cosmetic/plastic surgery; housing loan interest; goods for personal use; works contract services for construction of immovable property; goods consumed in making exempt supplies; reverse charge applicable on specified goods/services.', 'Pro-rata restriction (Section 17(2)): When ITC is claimed on mixed/multiple use — proportionate ITC on capital goods/services used for taxable as well as exempt supplies.', 'Annual reversal (Section 16(3)): ITC availed exceeds actual entitlement — difference must be paid with interest.']
  • Place of Supply Rules: ['Goods (Section 10 of IGST Act): Location of goods at time of movement/termination of movement = place of supply. For un-moved goods, location of goods at time of delivery. If multiple deliveries, last location before delivery to recipient.', 'Services — Recipient is registered person: Location of recipient = POS. If unregistered: Location of supply of services = location of supplier. Exceptions include hotel/accommodation, event management, entry ticket services where POS = location of event/premises.', 'Real estate services: Location of immovable property = POS.', 'Transport services: POS = last point of departure or destination depending on service type.', 'Key application: Wrong POS determination leads to wrong nature (CGST+SGST vs IGST) and incorrect ITC claimability.']
  • Time of Supply (TOS): ['Goods (Section 12): Earlier of — date of issue of invoice or last date invoice should have been issued, OR date of receipt of payment (whichever is earlier). In case of reverse charge, TOS is earlier of — date of receipt of goods or date of payment, or date immediately after 60 days from invoice date.', 'Services (Section 13): Normal case: Earlier of — date of issue of invoice (within 30 days of supply) or date of receipt of payment. Provisional invoice rule applies if invoice not issued within time limit.', 'Reverse charge on services: Same rules apply but from perspective of recipient.', 'Continuous supply of services (CSS): For regular supply, TOS is date of invoice or date of receipt of payment — whichever is earlier. For periodic supply, TOS is date on which each recurring obligation is due. Advance received: TOS is date of receipt unless invoice issued within prescribed period.', 'Interest/penalty for delay in payment is part of TOS at time of payment or provision of invoice.']
  • Valuation of Supply (Section 15): ['Transaction Value: Price actually paid or payable when supplier and recipient are not related and price is sole consideration. Must include: any expenses incurred by supplier, commissions, packing charges, interest/late fee, subsidy excluded from price.', 'Deductions allowed: Internal discount (if invoiced), post-supply discount supported by agreement, subsidies from central/state governments not covered by notification.', "Where price is not sole consideration: Determined per Section 15(2). Related party transactions: Section 16 applies — arm's length price.", 'Valuation of intra-group services: As per Rule 28 — cost plus 5% where no precedent exists, or based on self-declaration or comparable market practice.', 'Customs valuation: Determine assessable value per Customs Valuation (Determination of Price of Imported Goods) Rules, 2007 — transaction value method first, then alternative methods sequentially.']
  • Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM): ['Under Section 9(3), RCM applies when notified categories are received from unregistered supplier or specified service providers regardless of GST registration.', 'List of RCM-covered services (relevant notifications): Goods transport agency services (GTAs) where recipient is business entity (not unregistered); legal services; sponsorship; director appointment; recruitment of personnel; security services; renting of motor cab/vehicle; taxi/transport services.', 'RCMTCS (Section 52): TCS provisions apply to e-commerce operators.', 'Time of supply under RCM follows the same rules — but calculation of tax falls on recipient.', 'ITC on RCM payments is available subject to normal conditions in Section 16.']
  • GST Appeals and Adjudication: ['Adjudicating authority: Commissioner designated under Section 107.', 'First Appeal: Authority — Appellate Authority (AA), appointed by appropriate Government. Filed within 3 months from order date (extendable by 1 month for sufficient cause). Fee: ₹25,000/₹10,000.', 'Second Appeal: To National Appellate Authority (NAA) or State Appellate Authority — filed within 90 days from order date.', 'Supreme Court: Further appeal lies only on substantial question of law; leave is necessary.', 'Reopening of proceedings: Section 108 allows reopening within 3 years from order date under certain conditions.', 'Stay applications may be filed before AA/NAA but stay is discretionary.']
  • Composition Scheme Details and Limitations: ['Eligible: Only suppliers of goods (not services). Manufacturers can opt if aggregate turnover ≤ ₹1.5 crore (for special category states). IT/service providers cannot join.', 'Tax rates: CGST 0.5% + SGST 0.5% = 1% total on standard goods; IGST 1% on inter-state; certain notified goods may attract higher composite rates (e.g., 1%+1%=2%).', 'Restrictions: Cannot collect tax separately; no ITC on inputs/capital goods; cannot make inter-state supplies; cannot supply through e-commerce operator unless TCS applicable.', 'Returns: GSTR-4 filed annually with GSTR-3B quarterly summary.', 'Revocation: If turnover exceeds limit, scheme lapses and normal provisions apply from start of FY.']
  • Customs Duty — Basic Structure: ['Basic Customs Duty (BCD): levied u/s 12 of Customs Act 1962; rate depends on tariff classification under ITC(HS).', 'Social Welfare Surcharge (SWS): currently 10% of BCD (replaced earlier countervailing duty in many cases).', 'Integrated GST (IGST on imports): Charged u/s 3 of IGST Act — same as IGST on domestic supply; collected at customs port.', 'Additional Customs Duty (AD) / Countervailing Duty (CVD): formerly equivalent to excise duty + VAT; now largely subsumed into IGST on imports.', 'Anti-dumping duty, safeguard duty, and countervailing duty are extra duties under Sections 9, 9A, 9B of Customs Act.', 'Valuation: Customs Valuation Rules 2007 — transaction value first, then sequential fallback methods (transaction value of identical/similar goods, deductive method, computed value, reasonable means).']
  • SEZ, DEPB & Special Economic Zone Provisions: ["SEZ units are treated as 'deemed exports' for customs purposes — supply from SEZ to domestic tariff area attracts BCD + IGST.", "Drawback schemes: Customs Duty Drawback (Section 16 of Customs Act) allows refund of duties paid on inputs used in export goods; Merchant Exporters' Drawback available.", 'Export promotion capital goods (EPCG) scheme: Import capital goods at concessional BCD subject to fulfilment of export obligation.', 'Duty exemption notifications for exports, SEZ units, EOUs must be referenced precisely — these are frequently asked in CA Final.']
  • GST Anti-Profiteering (Section 171): ['If benefit of reduction in GST rate or increase in ITC is not passed on to recipient, adjudicating authority can direct refund to recipients with interest.', 'GST Council constitutes anti-profiteering authority; consumer/complaint mechanism exists.', 'Burden of proof is on the registered person to demonstrate that benefit has been passed on.', 'Penalty: Up to ₹50,000 for first offence; higher for subsequent offences; refund ordered with interest @12% p.a.']

Important facts

  • GST commenced from 1 July 2017; replaced CENVAT, VAT, Service Tax, Excise Duty, Octroi, Entry Tax, Luxury Tax, Entertainment Tax (except cinema), and several state levies.
  • CGST Act 2017 governs central component; IGST Act 2017 governs inter-state supply; UTGST Act 2017 for union territories; respective SGST Acts for states.
  • GST Council (Article 279A) — Chairperson: Union Finance Minister; members include Union Minister of State for Finance + State Ministers of Finance/Commerce.
  • Council decisions require 3/4 majority of members present and voting.
  • GST Portal URL: gst.gov.in; e-Invoicing portal: einvoice.gst.gov.in; IRP: Invoice Registration Portal.
  • E-way bill threshold: ₹50,000 for consignment value; valid for different distances as per Rule 138 — first 100 km = 1 day, then additional day per 200 km (or part thereof) for road; per 20 km for air/water.
  • HSN classification: Mandatory for turnover > ₹5 Cr (6-digit); 4-digit for ₹2-5 Cr; 2-digit for < ₹2 Cr (as per current notifications).
  • Advance Ruling Authority (ARA) — State and Central components dealt with separately.
  • Section 74 (fraud with intent to evade tax): Show cause notice within 3 years from due date of annual return; penalty equal to tax evaded. Section 73 (non-fraudulent): SCN within 3 years; penalty 10% of tax.

Memory aids

  • GST REGISTRATION THRESHOLD: 40 (general)/20 (special category)/10 (specific goods from special category) — mnemonic: 'Fourty Twenty Ten, Special Places Need Attention'
  • ITC BLOCKED MEMO (Section 17(5)): MVCF — Motor Vehicles, Food, Cosmetic, Fitness/Housing/Personal services — Remember: 'My Car Focused On Care'
  • TIME OF SUPPLY mnemonic: TOS-GS = 'Time Of Supply — Goods first, Services next'. For both: Invoice OR Payment — whichever EARLIER.
  • PLACE OF SUPPLY: 'Goods go where they MOVE, Services follow the RECIPIENT (when registered) or SUPPLIER (when unregistered)'. Real estate = LOCATION of property.
  • COMPOSITE vs MIXED: 'One principal = Composite; No principal = Mixed'. Think: Principal is leader; Mixed is crowd.
  • RCM SERVICES: Legal, GTA, Security, Director, Recruitment, Sponsorship — mnemonic: 'LGS DR' — Lawyer Gets Security, Directors Recruited
  • CUSTOMS VALUATION ORDER: Transaction → Identical → Similar → Deductive → Computed → Reasonable. Memory: 'The Incredible Detective Computes Reasonably'

Exam tips

  • In CA Final Indirect Tax, questions are problem-based. Always identify first: (a) nature of supply (goods/services/CSS), (b) nature of parties (related/unrelated, registered/unregistered), (c) place and time of supply, then (d) valuation.
  • For ITC questions, always check Section 17(5) blocked credits FIRST before computing net output tax. Examiners specifically test knowledge of blocked items.
  • Practice HSN-wise classification problems — CA Final asks to identify correct tariff heading and applicable duty rate.
  • For GST valuation, remember the hierarchy: (1) transaction value, (2) Section 15 adjustments, (3) valuation rules for related parties, (4) rule 28 for intra-group services.
  • For appeals, memorize timelines: 3 months to AA, 90 days to NAA, leave for SC. Practice numerical problems involving stay applications and interest calculations.
  • Customs duty computation is usually a multi-step problem. Set up clearly: (i) assessable value, (ii) BCD, (iii) SWS, (iv) IGST, (v) total duty liability. Show each step — partial marks are awarded.
  • Know the latest CBIC notifications and Circulars on ITC reversals, e-invoicing, TCS/TDS provisions, as these form recent addition to the syllabus.
  • For SEZ/export-related problems, always verify whether IGST is payable or not, and whether it is refunded via LUT or bonded warehouse facility.
  • In answer writing, cite section numbers and notification numbers wherever possible — this signals precision expected at CA Final level.
  • Revise GST Council meeting outcomes, especially recent rate changes and threshold modifications, as current affairs-linked GST questions appear frequently.

Common traps

  • Trap: Assuming composition scheme is available to service providers. CORRECT: Only goods suppliers can opt for composition. Service providers CANNOT join.
  • Trap: Computing ITC on blocked credits (Section 17(5)) as available. Always verify whether capital goods, works contract, food, vehicles, etc. fall under Section 17(5) before claiming ITC.
  • Trap: Applying general time of supply rule instead of advance payment rule. When advance is received, TOS is date of receipt — check whether invoice issued within prescribed time limit.
  • Trap: Using place of supply rule for services when goods movement is involved. For goods, POS = location at time of termination of movement; misapplying service POS rule causes wrong CGST/SGST vs IGST determination.
  • Trap: Confusing Section 73 (non-fraud) vs Section 74 (fraud) provisions. Section 73: 10% penalty; Section 74: 100% penalty. The distinction turns on whether there is suppression, forgery, willful misstatement, or contravention to evade tax.
  • Trap: Applying mixed supply rule when it is actually a composite supply. Always identify whether there is a principal supply. If yes → composite; if no → mixed.
  • Trap: Forgetting that e-commerce operators are liable to collect TCS (section 52) separately from GST. TCS is 1% each of CGST/SGST or 1% IGST on net taxable supplies.
  • Trap: Treating SEZ supply as export without IGST. Supply to SEZ may attract IGST; IGST refund process is separate from export with zero rate.
