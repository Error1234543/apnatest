Definitions

  • Income: Under Section 2(24) r.w.s. 4–9 of the IT Act, 1961, income means any sum received in money or money's worth, includable in total income, whether under the heads of income or not. It includes surplus from capital transactions only if chargeable to tax as capital gains.
  • Total Income: The total amount of income computed under the Act from all sources, after allowing exemptions under Chapter VI-A (ss. 80C–80U) and set-off/carried forward of losses as per ss. 70–80.
  • Assessable Income: Gross total income after deduction of exemptions (if any) but before Chapter VI-A deductions. It is the income upon which tax rate is applied.
  • Per Year (Section 2(18))
  • Year of Assessment (YA): The period starting from 1st April to 31st March next immediately following the previous year, in which income is assessed and tax is charged.
  • Previous Year (PY): The financial year immediately preceding the YA in which income is assessed. Every assessee must have a PY; for a new business/person, the first PY may differ.
  • Person (Section 2(31)): Includes an individual, HUF, company, firm, AOP/BOI, local authority, artificial juridical person (AJP), and any body of individuals or persons.
  • Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR): An individual who satisfies basic residential conditions but fails at least one of the two additional conditions (182 days in 9 out of 10 PYs or 730 days in 7 out of 10 PYs). RNOR is taxed on Indian-sourced income and foreign income received in India.
  • Place of Effective Management (POEM): The place where key management and commercial decisions necessary for the conduct of the business are in substance made. POEM determines residential status of a company (s. 6(3)).
  • Specified Person (Section 2(13A)): A person specified under clause (b) to (ggg) of s. 2(13A), including relatives, firms/trusts in which the transferor has substantial interest, parties to whom a transfer or adjustment is made, and certain other persons. Transfer pricing provisions apply to transactions with specified persons.
  • Advance Ruling (APB/GNR): The Authority for Advance Rulings (AAR) or GST Appellate Authority (GAAR) gives binding ruling on questions relating to liability to tax, status, or eligibility for benefits, sought in advance by an assessee.
  • Transfer Pricing (TP): The pricing of international or specified domestic transactions between associated enterprises. The arm’s length price (ALP) must be determined per Section 92C and rules prescribed thereunder.

Important facts

  • Basic exemption limit (FY 2024-25, old regime): Individual < 60 yrs — ₹2.5 lakh; 60–80 yrs — ₹3 lakh; > 80 yrs — ₹5 lakh. New regime (s. 115BAC): ₹3 lakh for all individuals. — High — frequently tested in numerical problems.
  • Surcharge: 10% (income ₹50L–₹1Cr), 15% (₹1Cr–₹2Cr), 25% (₹2Cr–₹5Cr), 37% (> ₹5Cr). Health & Education Cess @ 4%. — High — surcharge calculation is a recurring problem type.
  • Threshold for audit u/s 44AB: Business turnover > ₹1 crore (₹10 crore if 95% receipts via digital mode); Profession profession income > ₹10 lakh or turnover > ₹50 lakh. — High — threshold-based questions appear regularly.
  • Depreciation rates (WDV method): Building (non-residential) 10%, Plant & Machinery 15%, Furniture 10%, Computer 40%, Motor car (cost ≤ ₹10 lakh) 15%. — Medium — rates change; verify current notification.
  • Clubbing provisions (ss. 60–64): Income of spouse/daughter (unmarried) is clubbed if transferred without adequate consideration. Karta’s income from HUF not clubbed. — High — conceptual and application-based.
  • Speculation business loss: Carry forward only if return filed on time. Set off only against speculation income. — Medium — commonly confused with normal business loss.
  • Section 194Q: TDS on purchase of goods @ 0.1% if buyer’s turnover > ₹10 crore in preceding PY and purchase exceeds ₹50 lakh in current PY. — Medium — recent addition, important for current year.
  • Section 115BBE: Income from undisclosed investments/foreign income taxed @ 30% + surcharge + cess — no deduction allowed. — Medium — black money provision.

Memory aids

  • MNEMONIC FOR SECTION 80C ITEMS: 'PP FIE LNS ULD' — PF, PPF, ESIC, Insurance, Tuition Fee, ELSS, NSC, ULIP, 5-Year FD, Loan principal (housing).
  • ORDER OF SET-OFF OF LOSSES: 1) Speculation loss against speculation income only. 2) Short-term capital loss against any capital gain. 3) Long-term capital loss against LTCG only. 4) Business loss against any head except salary. 5) HP loss against any head up to ₹2L.
  • RESIDENTIAL STATUS CHEAT SHEET: Individual — 182 days OR (60 days + 365 days). Company — POEM India OR incorporated India. AOP/Firm/HUF — Control & Management India.
  • TP METHODS: CUP (Comparable Uncontrolled Price), Resale Price, Cost Plus, Profit Split, TNMM (Transactional Net Margin Method). Remember: 'CRPT' for main methods.
  • PE TYPES: Fixed Place PE, Construction PE (>6 months), Service PE (>183 days in 12 months). Remember: 'FCS' for types.

Exam tips

  • Tip 1: Always cite the relevant section number in your answer. ICAI evaluators look for precise statutory references.
  • Tip 2: For numerical problems, show step-by-step computation: Gross Total Income → Deductions → Total Income → Tax → Surcharge → Cess → Tax Liability.
  • Tip 3: In international taxation questions, first determine residential status, then examine DTAA provisions, and finally consider GAAR if applicable.
  • Tip 4: Memorize key thresholds (audit, TDS, TCS) as they are frequently tested in MCQs and case studies.
  • Tip 5: For deduction questions, remember the order: exemption → deduction u/s 10 → deduction u/s 56–59 → set-off → carry forward → Chapter VI-A deductions.
  • Tip 6: Practice calculating tax liability under both old and new regimes to identify which is more beneficial for different scenarios.
  • Tip 7: In TP questions, ensure you determine ALP correctly and mention the documentation requirements (Form 3CEB).
  • Tip 8: For penalty questions, distinguish between 270A (under-reporting) and 271AAC (concealment) penalties.

Common traps

  • Trap 1: Confusing resident and non-resident tax treatment. Remember: NRIs are taxed only on India-sourced income, while residents are taxed on worldwide income.
  • Trap 2: Applying DTAA benefit without verifying beneficial entitlement. The assessee must be the beneficial owner, not a conduit.
  • Trap 3: Forgetting that DDT is abolished. Dividends are now taxable in hands of shareholder, not distributed by company.
  • Trap 4: Not considering the option between old and new tax regime. New regime (s. 115BAC) has lower rates but fewer deductions.
  • Trap 5: Misapplying indexation benefit. Indexation is available for LTCG on immovable property and debt instruments only up to AY 2023-24. From AY 2024-25, indexation is not available for immovable property.
  • Trap 6: Overlooking the condition for carry forward of losses — timely filing of return is mandatory.
  • Trap 7: Incorrect calculation of ALP. Must use prescribed method and maintain contemporaneous documentation.
  • Trap 8: Not distinguishing between GAAR and treaty override. GAAR applies when treaty shopping is abusive, not merely because treaty rate is higher.
