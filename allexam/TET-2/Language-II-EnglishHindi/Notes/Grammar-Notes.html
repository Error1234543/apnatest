1. Parts of Speech (શબ્દપ્રકાર)

ઈંગ્લીશમાં સાત પ્રમુખ શબ્દપ્રકારો છે, જેમ કે નામ (Noun), સર્વનામ (Pronoun), સંજ્ઞા (Adjective), ક્રિયાપદ (Verb), ક્રિયાવાચક (Adverb), પૂર્વસર્ગ (Preposition) અને સંયોજક (Conjunction). દરેકનો પોતાનો વિધેય અને ઓળખાણ ચિન્હો છે.

નામ (Noun): વ્યક્તિ, સ્થળ, વસ્તુ અથવા ભાવની ઉપાધિ દર્શાવે છે. તેના મુખ્ય પ્રકારો:
- સામાન્ય નામ (Common Noun): boy, city, river
- વિશેષ નામ (Proper Noun): Ram, Ahmedabad, Ganga
- ભાવવાચક નામ (Abstract Noun): happiness, courage
- દ્રવ્યવાચક નામ (Material Noun): gold, wood, iron
- સમૂહવાચક નામ (Collective Noun): team, army, flock

સર્વનામ (Pronoun): નામને બદલે આવે છે — I, you, he, she, it, we, they, one, someone, each, either, neither, who, which, that, this, myself, anyone, everybody. તેના પ્રકાર: વ્યક્તિવાચક (Personal: I/he), પરાવૃત્ત (Reflexive: myself/yourself), પરસ્પર (Reciprocal: each other), અનિશ્ચિત (Indefinite: someone/everybody), સંબંધક (Relative: who/which), પૂછપરછ (Interrogative: who/what).

સંજ્ઞા (Adjective): નામ અથવા સર્વનામને વિશેષણ આપે છે. પ્રકાર: ગુણવાચક (Descriptive: tall), સંખ્યાવાચક (Numerical: three), સૂચક (Demonstrative: this/those), પૂછપરછ (Interrogative: which?), સંબંધક (Relative: whose), અનિશ્ચિત (Indefinite: some/many). સંજ્ઞાના ત્રણ સ્તર: ધન (Positive: good), તુલનાત્મક (Comparative: better), સર્વતોભાવ (Superlative: best).

ક્રિયાપદ (Verb): વાક્યનું મુખ્ય અંગ. કાર્યરત (Finite: She sings) અને અકાર્યરત (Non-finite: to sing, singing, sung). અકાર્યરત ત્રણ પ્રકારના: અનિશ્ચિત (Infinitive: to go), સર્વનામિક (Gerund: swimming), વિશેષણ (Participle: running/ran).

ક્રિયાવાચક (Adverb): ક્રિયાપદ, સંજ્ઞા અથવા બીજા ક્રિયાવાચકને વિશેષણ આપે છે — adverb of time (yesterday), place (here), manner (quickly), frequency (always), degree (very).

પૂર્વસર્ગ (Preposition): નામ/સર્વનામ સાથે સંબંધ દર્શાવે છે — in, on, at, by, with, about, between, among, through.

સંયોજક (Conjunction): શબ્દો/વાક્યો જોડે છે — co-ordinating (and, but, or, so, yet, for, nor), sub-ordinating (because, although, if, when, since, while), correlative (either...or, neither...nor, not only...but also).

2. Tenses (કાળ)

ત્રણ મુખ્ય કાળ: વર્તમાન (Present), ભૂતકાળ (Past), ભવિષ્ય (Future). દરેકમાં ચાર રૂપો હોય છે — Simple, Continuous, Perfect, Perfect Continuous. કુલ 12 કાળ રચાય છે.

PRESENT TENSE:
- Simple Present: S + V1( s/es ) + O. Use: habit, universal truth, scheduled events. Example: She walks to school. The sun rises in the east.
- Present Continuous: S + am/is/are + V1+ing + O. Use: happening now. Example: He is reading a book.
- Present Perfect: S + has/have + V3 + O. Use: completed action with present result. Example: I have finished my homework.
- Present Perfect Continuous: S + has/have been + V1+ing + O. Use: started in past, still continuing. Example: She has been studying since morning.

PAST TENSE:
- Simple Past: S + V2 + O. Example: They visited Delhi last year.
- Past Continuous: S + was/were + V1+ing + O. Example: I was sleeping at 8 pm.
- Past Perfect: S + had + V3 + O. Use: action completed before another past action. Example: She had left before I arrived.
- Past Perfect Continuous: S + had been + V1+ing + O. Example: They had been waiting for two hours.

FUTURE TENSE:
- Simple Future: S + will/shall + V1 + O. Example: We will go tomorrow.
- Future Continuous: S + will be + V1+ing + O. Example: I will be teaching at 10 am.
- Future Perfect: S + will have + V3 + O. Example: By next year, he will have graduated.
- Future Perfect Continuous: S + will have been + V1+ing + O. Example: By December, she will have been working here for five years.

મહત્વની નોંધ: Stative verbs (know, believe, belong, seem, like, love, hate, understand, possess) સામાન્ય રીતે continuous formમાં વాપરતા નથી. ઉદા: 'I know him' નહીં 'I am knowing him'.

SIGNAL WORDS:
- Simple Present: always, usually, often, sometimes, never, every day
- Present Continuous: now, at present, look!, listen!
- Present Perfect: already, yet, just, ever, never, so far
- Present Perfect Continuous: since, for, all day
- Simple Past: yesterday, last week, ago, in 1990
- Past Continuous: while, when, at that time
- Past Perfect: before, after, by the time
- Future: tomorrow, next year, soon, by next week

3. Voice (કારક — Active/Passive)

Active Voice: કર્તા ક્રિયા કરે છે. Passive Voice: કર્તા પર ક્રિયા થાય છે. Passive માં Object → Subject બને છે, Main Verb → be + V3 થાય છે.

રચના નિયમ: Passive = Subject (previous Object) + appropriate form of 'be' + V3 + by + Agent (previous Subject)

કાળ પ્રમાણે be નું રૂપ:
- Simple Present: is/am/are + V3 → The ball is kicked by him.
- Present Continuous: is/am/are being + V3 → The letter is being written by her.
- Present Perfect: has/have been + V3 → The work has been done by them.
- Simple Past: was/were + V3 → The book was read by me.
- Past Continuous: was/were being + V3 → The house was being painted.
- Past Perfect: had been + V3 → The message had been sent.
- Simple Future: will be + V3 → The game will be played.
- Future Perfect: will have been + V3 → The bridge will have been built.
- Modals: can/may/must + be + V3 → The task must be completed.

મહત્વના નિયમો:
1. Intransitive verbs (sleep, die, happen, come, go) ની passive ન હોય.
2. Imperative sentences: Let + Object + be + V3 — Let the work be done.
3. Interrogative:疑问 + is/are/was/were + object + V3? — Was the letter sent?
4. 'Help' ની passive માં by દૂર કરી શકાય: He is helped (by me).
5. Double object વાળા વાક્યમાં કોઈ પણ object subject બની શકે:
- He gave me a pen. → I was given a pen / A pen was given to me.
6. 'Let' વાળા વાક્ય: Let them go. → They be let go.
7. Special cases: I see him. → He is seen by me. / Say it again. → Let it be said again.

4. Direct and Indirect Speech (આગેહાત અને પરોક્ષ વાણી)

Direct Speech: કથન અક્ષરતઃ લખાય. Indirect/Narrated Speech: કથન ભાવતઃ રજૂ થાય.

મુખ્ય ફેરફારો:

QUOTATION MARKS: "" અથવા '' દૂર કરી અને કનેક્ટિવ using 'that' (affirmative), 'if/whether' (yes/no question), interrogative word (wh-question).

TENSE CHANGE (_reporting verb past માં હોય તો):
- Simple Present → Simple Past: He says, "I play." → He said that he played.
- Present Continuous → Past Continuous: "I am reading." → He said that he was reading.
- Present Perfect → Past Perfect: "I have finished." → He said that he had finished.
- Simple Past → Past Perfect: "I came." → He said that he had come.
- Past Continuous → Past Perfect Continuous: "I was waiting." → He said that he had been waiting.
- Past Perfect → Past Perfect (no change)
- Simple Future → Conditional (would): "I will go." → He said that he would go.
- would/could/might/should → no change

Reporting verb present/past માં: Reporting verb present હોય તો tense બદલાતું નથી.

PRONOUN CHANGE:
- First person (I/we) → subjectના અનુસાર
- Second person (you) → objectના અનુસાર
- Third person → unchanged
- ઉદા: She said to me, "I am busy." → She told me that she was busy.

TYPE OF SENTENCE BASED TRANSFORMATION:
- Statement: using 'that'
- Question (Yes/No): using 'if/whether'
- Question (Wh-): wh-word જાળવી રાખીએ
- Imperative: to + V1 / not to + V1 — He said, "Go." → He told him to go.
- Exclamatory: what/a/oh દૂર કરીએ — She exclaimed, "What a beautiful flower!" → She exclaimed that it was a very beautiful flower.

OTHER CHANGES:
- This → that, these → those, here → there, now → then, today → that day, yesterday → the previous day, tomorrow → the next day, ago → earlier
- પૂછપરછ વાક્યમાં word order normal (Subject-Verb) થાય: He asked where I was going.

5. Clauses and Sentence Types (વાક્યભાગ અને વાક્ય પ્રકાર)

Clause: Subject + Predicate ધરાવતો અર્થપૂર્ણ સમૂહ. Main (Independent) Clause સ્વતંત્ર રહે છે. Subordinate (Dependent) Clause main clause પર આધારિત.

CLAUSE TYPE:
- Noun Clause: noun ની જેમ વર્તે. Example: What he said is true. / I know that he is honest.
- Adjective (Relative) Clause: noun ને વર્ણવે. Example: The boy who won the prize is my brother. / She is the girl whom I met.
- Adverb Clause: time, reason, condition, purpose, contrast દર્શાવે. Example: When he came, I left. / Although he is poor, he is happy.

SUBORDINATE CLAUSE PARTICLES:
- Time: when, while, as, before, after, until, till, since, as soon as
- Reason/Cause: because, since, as
- Purpose: that, lest, so that
- Condition: if, unless, provided, even if
- Contrast/Concession: although, though, even though, while, whereas
- Place: where, wherever
- Manner: as, as if, as though

SENTENCE TYPES:
- Simple Sentence: One independent clause. Example: She sings.
- Compound Sentence: Two or more independent clauses joined by co-ordinating conjunction (FANBOYS: for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so) અથવા semicolon.
- Complex Sentence: One independent + one or more dependent clauses.
- Compound-Complex: Two+ independent + one+ dependent.

Relative Pronouns: who (subject, person), whom (object, person), which (thing/animal), that (person/thing, defining), whose (possession).
- Defining Relative Clause: essential info, no commas — The man who called yesterday is my uncle.
- Non-defining Relative Clause: extra info, commas — My brother, who lives in London, is a doctor.

6. Subject-Verb Agreement (વિષય-ક્રિયા સંમતિ)

જ્યારે Subject Single હોય ત્યારે Verb Single, Subject Plural હોય ત્યારે Verb Plural.

મુખ્ય નિયમો:

1. બે Subject 'and' વડે જોડાયેલા → Plural: Ram and Shyam are coming.
Exception: When both refer to same person/thing → Singular: The poet and singer is here. / Bread and butter is my breakfast.

2. Each/every/any + Singular Noun → Singular: Each student has a book. Every boy and girl is present.

3. Either/or, Neither/nor, Not only/but also → Verb matches nearest Subject (Proximity Rule): Neither the teacher nor the students were present. / Not only he but also his friends were invited.

4. Collective Noun (team, committee, jury, crowd) → Singular when group एकका act, Plural when members分开 act:
- The team is winning. / The team are arguing among themselves.

5. Amount/number words (hundreds, thousands, millions, decades, pounds, miles) as subject → Singular if measured quantity: Five hundred rupees is enough. / Ten miles is a long distance.

6. The + Adjective (representing group) → Plural: The rich are blessed. / The poor need help.

7. Each of + Plural Noun/Pronoun → Singular: Each of the boys has a pen.

8. There is/There are → Depends on following noun: There is a book on the table. / There are books on the table.

9. Nouns appearing plural but singular meaning: News, physics, ethics, mathematics, economics, carriage, meerschaum → Singular verb: The news is good. Physics is interesting.

10. 'As well as', 'along with', 'together with', 'accompanied by' → Verb agrees with first subject: The captain, along with the players, is coming.

11. One of the + Plural Noun + Relative Clause → Singular verb: She is one of the girls who studies hard. (Studies — relative refers to 'girls') vs She is the only one of the girls who studies hard. (Studies — refers to 'one')

12. Gerund/Infinitive as subject → Singular: Swimming is good exercise. To err is human.

7. Prepositions (પૂર્વસર્ગ)

Preposition એ noun/pronoun સાથે સંબંધ દર્શાવે છે. Common prepositions: in, on, at, by, with, from, to, for, of, about, between, among, through, during, before, after, above, below, under, over, inside, outside, against, across, around, within, without.

મહત્વપૂર્ણ Preposition Usage Rules:

1. IN: inside enclosed space, months/years/seasons, languages, direction: in the room, in 2024, in Gujarati, in the morning.
2. ON: surface, day/date, transport (public), topic: on the table, on Monday, on the bus, on the subject.
3. AT: precise point/time, institution: at 5 pm, at the door, at school, at night.
4. BY: means, deadline, agent (passive): by bus, by tomorrow, by Ram.
5. WITH: accompaniment, instrument: with my friend, with a pen.
6. FROM: origin, separation: from Delhi, from 1990.
7. TO: direction, receiver: to Mumbai, give to me.
8. FOR: purpose, duration: for you, for two hours.
9. OF: possession, material: book of Ram, made of wood.
10. ABOUT/OF: regarding — talk about/of.
11. BETWEEN: two definite entities; AMONG: more than two.
12. SINCE: point of time; FOR: period of time.

Fixed Prepositional Phrases (મહત્વના):
- Accuse of, Agree with/to/upon, Afraid of, Anger at, Apply for, Believe in, Belong to, Borrow from, Capsule of, Complain about, Confess to, Depend on, Different from, Envious of, Fault with, Gift to, Good at, Interested in, Judge by, Kind to, Listen to, Look at/for/to, Lucky with, Married to, Pleased with, Responsible for, Respect for, Search for, Similar to, Superior/Inferior to, Thank for, Used to, Witness to.

Common Errors:
- He is good in Maths. (Wrong) → He is good at Maths. (Correct)
- I am waiting for you. (Correct) NOT 'waiting to you'
- She is married with a doctor. (Wrong) → She is married to a doctor.
- Difference between... and... (Correct) NOT 'Difference between... and of'

8. Conjunctions, Determiners, Modal Verbs (સંયોજક, નિર્ણાયક, સહાયક ક્રિયાપદ)

CONJUNCTIONS (સંયોજક):

Co-ordinating (FANBOYS):
- And: addition — She sings and dances.
- But: contrast — He is rich but unhappy.
- Or: alternative — Tea or coffee?
- So: result — He studied hard so he passed.
- Yet: unexpected contrast — She is young yet wise.
- For: reason (formal) — He is tired, for he worked all day.
- Nor: negative addition — He neither eats nor sleeps.

Sub-ordinating:
- Reason: because, since, as — I stayed home because it rained.
- Time: when, while, as, before, after, until — Wait until I return.
- Condition: if, unless, provided — If it rains, we will cancel.
- Concession: although, though, even though — Although he is sick, he came.
- Purpose: that, so that, lest — He spoke softly lest he wake the baby.

Correlative Conjunctions:
- Either...or — Either you come or I go.
- Neither...nor — Neither Ram nor Shyam is here.
- Not only...but also — Not only does he sing but also he dances.
- Whether...or — I don't know whether he will come or not.
- Both...and — Both Ram and Shyam are here.
- Scarcely...when / No sooner...than (inversion required):
- No sooner did he enter than the phone rang.
- Scarcely had she left when it started raining.

DETERMINERS (નિર્ણાયક):
- Article: a/an (indefinite), the (definite). Zero article with generic plurals/mass nouns.
- Demonstrative: this, that, these, those.
- Possessive: my, your, his, her, its, our, their.
- Quantifiers: some, any, many, much, few, little, several, all, both, each, every, either, neither, one, another, other, the other.
- Interrogative: which, what, whose.

Common quantifier rules:
- Many + countable plural; Much + uncountable: Many books, much water.
- Few (negative) vs A few (positive): Few people came. / I have a few friends.
- Little (negative) vs A little (positive): Little hope remains. / I have a little time.
- Less (uncountable) vs Fewer (countable): Less time, fewer errors.

MODAL AUXILIARIES (મોડલ્સ):
can/could, may/might, shall/should, will/would, must, ought to, need, dare, used to.
- Can: ability, permission (informal)
- Could: past ability, polite request
- May: formal permission, possibility
- Might: remote possibility
- Shall: future (first person), suggestion
- Should: advice, duty
- Will/Would: future, habitual past, polite request
- Must: obligation, strong necessity
- Ought to: moral duty
- Need: necessity (mostly negative/interrogative)
- Used to: past habit

9. Common Error Detection (ભાષાગત ભૂલ શોધ)

TET-2 માં 'Error Detection' પ્રકારના પ્રશ્નો આવે છે. નીચેનાં common error patterns યાદ રાખો:

1. Preposition Errors:
- He is fond in music. → fond of music.
- She is superior to me. (Correct — no 'than' after superior/inferior/prior/posterior)
- He has no knowledge about his work. → of his work.
- I am agree with you. → I agree with you. (Agree is verb, not adjective)

2. Tense Errors:
- He has gone to Delhi last week. → He went to Delhi last week. (Specific past time → Simple Past)
- I have known him since ten years. → for ten years. (Duration → for)
- He is knowing the answer. → He knows the answer. (Stative verb)

3. Subject-Verb Agreement Errors:
- Each of the boys have books. → has books.
- The number of students are increasing. → is increasing. (Number = specific count)
vs 'A number of students are increasing.' (A number = many)
- One of the girls who are singing is my sister. → who is singing (when 'the only one')

4. Article Errors:
- He is an honest man. (H is silent in honest — vowel sound)
- He is the King of England. (Unique entities take 'the')
- Gold is a metal. (Generic substance — zero article: Gold is a metal)

5. Redundancy/Synthesis Errors:
- Free gift (redundant — gift is inherently free)
- End result (redundant — result is inherently end)
- Merge together (redundant)
- Repeat again (redundant)
- Past history (redundant)
- Unexpected surprise (redundant)

6. Wrong Word Choice:
- His brother is a doctor at hospital. → at the hospital / in hospital (British)
- He suffers with malaria. → from malaria.
- He is waiting for the bus. (Not 'waiting to the bus')
- The police is coming. → The police are coming. (Police = plural)
- The media is/are → both accepted; are more traditional.

7. Comparative-Superlative Errors:
- He is the taller of the two. → correct (of two → comparative)
- He is the most intelligent boy in the class. → correct
- He is more stronger. → wrong (double comparison)

8. Wrong Preposition after Adjective:
- Interested on → interested in
- Angry with (person) / Angry at (thing)
- Afraid of (not 'from')
- Busy with (work) / Busy in (doing)
- Familiar with (thing) / Familiar to (person)

10. Pedagogy of Teaching Grammar (વ્યાકરણ શિક્ષણની પદ્ધતિ)

TET-2 માં Grammar ની શિક્ષણ પદ્ધતિ પર પણ પ્રશ્નો આવે છે. મુખ્ય ખ્યાલો:

1. Grammar teaching's purpose: Not merely rule memorization — but developing accurate and effective language use. Grammar supports communication, comprehension, and writing skills.

2. Traditional Methods (often criticized):
- Deductive Method: Rule first → examples → practice. Teacher-centered. Rote learning. TET exams generally favor inductive approach.
- Inductive (Discovery) Method: Examples first → students discover rule → practice. Learner-centered. Promotes thinking and retention. Recommended by NCF 2005.

3. Natural Approach:
- Grammar is acquired naturally through meaningful communication.
- Focus on input (listening/reading) rather than explicit rule teaching.
- Error correction is delayed; fluency prioritized initially.

4. Communicative Language Teaching (CLT) in Grammar:
- Grammar taught through authentic communicative activities.
- Task-based exercises: information gap, role-play, debate, discussion.
- Function-based: teaching 'how to apologize', 'how to request' rather than just tenses in isolation.

5. Integrated Skill Approach:
- Grammar should never be taught in isolation.
- Integrate with Reading, Writing, Listening, Speaking.
- Example: Teach past tense through a reading passage, then writing activity, then speaking discussion.

6. NCF 2005 Position:
- Language is a living, dynamic system — not a set of rigid rules.
- Grammar should emerge from language use, not be imposed mechanically.
- Children should construct grammatical knowledge through interaction.
- Avoid testing grammar through isolated transformation drills alone.

7. Assessment of Grammar:
- Formative assessment: observation, portfolio, peer feedback.
- Summative: MCQs, error detection, sentence transformation, fill-in-the-blanks with context.
- Authentic assessment: writing tasks, spoken presentations where grammar is evaluated in context.

8. Common Pedagogical Mistakes:
- Teaching grammar as dead rules without context.
- Over-emphasis on transformation exercises (active-passive, direct-indirect) without communication.
- Ignoring mother-tongue influence in error analysis.
- Not addressing L1-L2 interference appropriately.
- Testing only mechanical skills, not communicative competence.

Important facts

  • Seven parts of speech: Noun, Pronoun, Adjective, Verb, Adverb, Preposition, Conjunction.
  • 12 Tenses total: 3 (Present/Past/Future) × 4 aspects (Simple/Continuous/Perfect/Perfect Continuous).
  • Passive Voice formula: Object + be (matching tense) + V3 + by + Subject.
  • Intransitive verbs cannot be converted to Passive Voice.
  • Direct to Indirect: reporting verb 'said' changes tense; 'says' keeps original tense.
  • No sooner...than and Scarcely...when require inversion (auxiliary before subject).
  • Subject-Verb Agreement: 'Each/Every/One of' takes singular verb.
  • Correlative conjunctions follow Proximity Rule: Neither the teacher nor the students were present.
  • Between = two; Among = three or more.
  • Since = point of time; For = period of time.
  • Police, Cattle, People, Media → plural verb usage.
  • News, Physics, Mathematics, Ethics → singular verb.
  • Fixed prepositions must be memorized: Good at, Proud of, Afraid of, Responsible for, Married to.
  • Redundant pairs to avoid: free gift, end result, repeat again, past history, merge together.
  • Inductive method is preferred over deductive method per NCF 2005 for grammar teaching.
  • Grammar must be taught integrated with four language skills (reading, writing, listening, speaking).
  • Noun Clauses act as subjects/objects; Relative Clauses modify nouns; Adverb Clauses modify verbs.
  • Gerund and Infinitive as subjects always take singular verb.
  • The number of = singular; A number of = plural.
  • When a collective noun acts as a single unit → singular verb; when members act individually → plural verb.

Formulas and relationships

Active → Passive: Object + be (tense-appropriate) + V3 + by + Subject
Present Simple Passive: is/am/are + V3
Present Continuous Passive: is/am/are being + V3
Present Perfect Passive: has/have been + V3
Past Simple Passive: was/were + V3
Past Continuous Passive: was/were being + V3
Past Perfect Passive: had been + V3
Future Simple Passive: will be + V3
Future Perfect Passive: will have been + V3
Modal Passive: can/must/should + be + V3
Imperative Passive: Let + Object + be + V3
Double Object Passive: Subject1 was given Object1 by Agent / Object1 was given to Subject1
Direct → Indirect (Statement): said that / told that
Direct → Indirect (Yes/No Q): asked if / whether
Direct → Indirect (Wh- Q): asked + wh-word + subject + verb (normal order)
Direct → Indirect (Imperative): told/asked + to + V1 / not to + V1
No sooner + had + Subject + V3 + than + clause
Scarcely + had + Subject + V3 + when + clause
Either/or, Neither/nor, Not only/but also → verb agrees with NEAREST subject (Proximity Rule)
Each/Every/One of + plural noun → singular verb
The number of + plural noun → singular verb; A number of + plural noun → plural verb
Collective noun as single unit → singular; as individuals → plural
This → that; These → those; Here → there; Now → then; Today → that day; Yesterday → the previous day; Tomorrow → the next day
Since → point in time; For → duration of time
Between → two items; Among → three or more items

Common mistakes

  • Using passive voice with intransitive verbs (e.g., 'He was died' is wrong — die is intransitive).
  • Writing 'he is knowing' instead of 'he knows' — stative verbs resist continuous forms.
  • Mixing since and for: 'I have lived here since 5 years' is wrong → use 'for'.
  • Writing 'he is good in maths' instead of 'good at maths'.
  • Writing 'she is married with a doctor' instead of 'married to a doctor'.
  • Double comparatives/superlatives: 'more better', 'most best' — both incorrect.
  • Using 'difference between X and Y of' — correct: 'difference between X and Y'.
  • Applying 'than' after words like superior, inferior, prior, posterior — these take 'to'.
  • Using plural verb with 'each of the boys' — should be singular ('has').
  • Confusing 'fewer' and 'less': use 'fewer' for countable, 'less' for uncountable.
  • Writing 'the police is coming' — police is plural; use 'are'.
  • Using restrictive articles unnecessarily: 'the milk is white' (generic) vs 'the milk in the glass'.
  • Missing inversion in 'no sooner...than' and 'scarcely...when' structures.
  • Using continuous tense with modal verbs (can being, should having) — modals are followed by base form only.
  • Adding redundant words: 'free gift', 'repeat again', 'end result', 'past history'.
  • Agreeing verb with wrong subject in compound sentences joined by 'and' referring to one entity (bread and butter IS my breakfast).
  • Forgetting that gerunds and infinitives as subjects take singular verbs.
  • Using 'between' for more than two items — use 'among'.
  • Incorrect tense sequence in reported speech when reporting verb is in present tense — tense should NOT change.
  • Writing 'I am agreeing with you' — agree is a stative verb; use 'I agree with you'.
