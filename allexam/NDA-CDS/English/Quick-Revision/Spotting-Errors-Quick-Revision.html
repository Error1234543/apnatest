Spotting Errors — Quick Revision (NDA & CDS English)

NDA & CDSEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Spotting Error: The task of identifying the grammatically incorrect part in a sentence divided into three segments plus an option 'No Error'.
  • Segment/Part A/B/C: Each sentence is split into three read­able parts labelled A, B, C; each part may carry a single possible fault.
  • No Error: Chosen when the sentence is grammatically correct across all three segments.
  • Subject-Verb Agreement: The rule that the finite verb must match its subject in person and number, regardless of intervening phrases.
  • Tense Consistency: The requirement that tenses remain logically compatible across clauses, especially in complex and compound sentences.
  • Parallelism: The principle that elements joined by coordinating conjunctions or presented in series must share the same grammatical form.
  • Absolute Phrase / Elliptical Clause: A reduced participial or adjective phrase that lacks an explicit subject or auxiliary but must still attach correctly to the main clause.
  • Correlative Conjunction Pair: Paired conjunctions such as either-or, neither-nor, not-only-but-also that govern agreement and balance.
  • Inversion: Reversal of normal subject-auxiliary order used after negative/adverbial fronting or in conditional omission of 'if'.
  • Factive vs Non-factive Verb: Factive verbs (know, realize, regret) presuppose the truth of their complement; non-factive verbs do not.

Important facts

  • NDA & CDS English Spotting Errors tests pure grammar correctness — spelling, punctuation, and vocabulary-meaning questions fall under other English sections.
  • Each question presents a sentence divided into three parts A, B, C; the error, if any, lies in exactly one part.
  • Common highest-frequency error areas: subject-verb agreement, preposition collocation, parallelism, tense inconsistency, and confusable word pairs.
  • 'One of the + plural noun + singular verb' is a guaranteed recurring pattern.
  • 'There is/are' must agree with the notional subject that follows.
  • Correlative pairs always dictate proximity agreement: nearer subject controls the verb.
  • Inverted conditionals omit 'if' and require auxiliary-fronting: Had, Were, Should.
  • Negative fronting triggers partial inversion only when the negative element is an adverbial, not a subject.
  • Superlatives with 'of the two' are wrong; use comparative: 'the better of the two.'
  • 'Like' is a preposition taking a noun/pronoun; 'as' is a conjunction taking a clause.

Memory aids

  • SAVEN: Subject-Agreement, Tense-Consistency, Verbs-prepositions, End-structure (parallelism/fragments), Noun-determiner-pronoun accuracy.
  • RAINY: Redundancy, Articles, Infinitive/gerund alignment, Noun modifiers, Prepositions.
  • COPPS: Conjunctions, Order/agreement, Pronouns, Parallelism, Prepositions/Sentence boundaries.
  • FANBOYS parallelism rule: elements joined by for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so must match grammatically.
  • The 3-L rule for comparisons: Less-lower for two, Least-lowest for three-plus; never 'more better'.
  • Raise-rise rhyme aid: You raise something (transitive); the sun rises (intransitive).
  • Lie-lay-lain-laid chain: Lie-lay-lain (recline/set); Lay-laid-laid (put/place). Remember: 'Chickens lay eggs, people lie down.'
  • Affect Effect mnemonic: Affect is Action verb; Effect is End result noun.
  • Despite/In spite of + noun/gerund; Although/Even though + subject-verb clause.
  • So-such placement trick: So + adj; Such + a/an + adj + singular noun; Such + adj + plural/uncountable noun.

Exam tips

  • Time management: spend roughly 40–50 seconds per spotting-error question in NDA English paper.
  • Read the entire sentence before looking at options; partial reading misses misplaced modifier or tense-shift errors.
  • Underline the subject and circle the finite verb in every question to catch agreement errors instantly.
  • Eliminate obviously correct segments first; the error is always in one segment only.
  • If a choice feels stylistically odd but grammatically sound, do not mark it — NDA/CDS tests correctness, not taste.
  • Watch for trap options that swap a minor error for a bigger one; choose the segment containing the first error you detect.
  • Practice with past papers under timed conditions; repeated exposure builds pattern recognition for high-frequency error types.
  • Mark doubtful questions and revisit them after completing the section if time permits — initial intuition is often reliable in grammar.
  • For NDA specifically, emphasis is on standard written British-influenced English; avoid colloquial shortcuts in your reasoning.
  • Carry a personal error log: note recurring mistake types and review it weekly before the exam.

Common traps

  • Intervening phrases fooling agreement: 'The captain, together with his players, are...' → 'is'.
  • Headline-style brevity overriding grammar in options; read full sentence structure.
  • Assuming 'No Error' because sentence sounds acceptable — idiomatic acceptability does not equal grammatical correctness.
  • Missing that omission in formal writing where 'that' introduces a restrictive clause.
  • Using 'who' where 'whom' is required after preposition: 'to who(m) it concerns.'
  • Double positive or double negative errors: 'hardly ever seen anyone' is correct; 'hardly ever seen no one' is wrong.
  • Confusing 'fewer' (countable) with 'less' (uncountable): 'fewer items', 'less water'.
  • Using 'different than' instead of 'different from' in formal written English tested by UPSC.
  • Appositive agreement mistakes: 'My friend, the author of books, are...' → 'is'.
  • Treating 'each' inside a plural subject as plural: 'Each of the boys have...' → 'has'.
