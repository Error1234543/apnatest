Definitions

  • Spotting Error: Identifying the part of a sentence that contains a grammatical, syntactical, or idiomatic mistake.
  • Sentence Improvement: Replacing an underlined or bolded portion with the most appropriate alternative.
  • Voice (Active/Passive): Active voice = subject performs the action; Passive voice = subject receives the action.
  • Narration (Direct/Indirect Speech): Direct speech repeats exact words; Indirect speech reports them without quotation marks.
  • Subject-Verb Agreement: A singular subject takes a singular verb; a plural subject takes a plural verb.
  • Tense Consistency: The tense of the verb must remain consistent unless the time frame genuinely shifts.
  • Preposition: A word governing a noun/pronoun to show relation (time, place, direction, etc.).
  • Conjunction: A word connecting clauses or sentences (co-ordinating: and, but, or; subordinating: because, although, if).
  • Article: Definite article 'the' refers to a specific noun; indefinite articles 'a/an' refer to non-specific singular countable nouns.
  • Participle: A verb form used as an adjective (present participle ends in -ing; past participle typically ends in -ed/en).
  • Gerund: A verb ending in -ing functioning as a noun.
  • Infinitive: The base form of a verb preceded by 'to' (to + verb), often functioning as a noun, adjective, or adverb.

(plural verb because subject is 'boys')."]
  • ["Present Indefinite: habitual actions, general truths — 'She walks to school.'", "Present Continuous: action happening now — 'He is reading a book.'", "Present Perfect: action completed recently or with present relevance — 'I have finished my homework.'", "Present Perfect Continuous: action started in past and continuing — 'She has been working since morning.'", "Past Indefinite: completed action at a definite past time — 'I went there yesterday.'", "Past Continuous: ongoing past action — 'I was reading when he called.'", "Past Perfect: action completed before another past action — 'She had left before I arrived.'", "Past Perfect Continuous: ongoing past action up to another past point — 'They had been waiting for two hours when the bus came.'", 'Future forms: will/shall + base verb for simple future; will be + -ing for continuous; will have + past participle for perfect.', 'Sequence rule: main clause in past + subordinate clause in past perfect (if earlier); if simultaneous, both in past continuous.', 'Historical present: past events described in present tense for dramatic effect.', "Universal truths always use present tense even if the main clause is past: 'He said that water boils at 100°C.'", 'Never mix tenses unnecessarily within a single sentence.']
