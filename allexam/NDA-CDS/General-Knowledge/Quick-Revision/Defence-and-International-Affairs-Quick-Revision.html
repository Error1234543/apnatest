Quick Revision: Defence and International Affairs — NDA & CDS

NDA & CDSEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Integrated Theatre Command (ITC): A joint command of the Indian Armed Forces that brings together elements of the Army, Navy, and Air Force under a single commander for a specific geographical theatre, aimed at improving interoperability and operational efficiency. India is moving toward establishing ITCs; as of recent years, the first ITC (Southern Theatre) has been announced.
  • Strategic Forces Command (SFC): An integrated tri-service command of the Indian Armed Forces responsible for the operational management of India's nuclear forces. It operates under the Nuclear Command Authority (NCA).
  • Nuclear Command Authority (NCA): The apex body for deployment and control of India's nuclear weapons, headed by the Prime Minister as the Civilian Control Authority and chaired by a Nuclear Crisis Management Panel. The Political Council gives directives, while the Executive Council (chaired by the National Security Advisor) executes them.
  • Missile Technology Control Regime (MTCR): An informal multinational regime established in 1987 to prevent proliferation of missiles and drone technology capable of delivering weapons of mass destruction. India joined MTCR in 2016 as a full member.
  • Cut-off Point System: India's declared policy of No First Use (NFU) means India will not be the first to use nuclear weapons but reserves the right to retaliate with nuclear weapons if attacked by an adversary using nuclear, biological, or chemical weapons.
  • Standoff Missile (SSM): A long-range missile launched from a safe distance beyond the enemy's air defences. Examples include BrahMos (supersonic cruise missile), Nirbhay (subsonic cruise missile), and Agni series (ballistic missiles).
  • Defence Diplomacy: The use of military-to-military engagements, joint exercises, defence trade, and capacity-building assistance by one country to foster goodwill, build partnerships, and advance strategic interests internationally.
  • Act East Policy: India's foreign policy initiative launched in 2014 that expanded upon the earlier Look East Policy, deepening strategic, economic, and defence ties with Southeast Asian nations and participating in regional security architectures like ASEAN.
  • Neighbourhood First Policy: India's foreign policy approach prioritising strong bilateral relations with its immediate neighbours through diplomatic engagement, development cooperation, and connectivity projects.
  • Quadrilateral Security Dialogue (Quad): An informal strategic dialogue between India, Japan, Australia, and the United States focused on ensuring a free, open, and inclusive Indo-Pacific region, covering areas including maritime security, pandemic response, critical technology, and climate change.

Key concepts

  • The Indian Armed Forces consist of three regular services and several auxiliary organisations: (1) Indian Army — largest service, organised into regional commands (Northern, Southern, Eastern, Western, Central, South Western, Training, Mechanical Equipment, Strategic Forces); handles land warfare and internal security. (2) Indian Navy — operates across Western and Eastern Naval Commands (headquartered at Mumbai and Visakhapatnam respectively), also manages the Naval Air Command and Maritime Operations Command; responsible for sea control and maritime security. (3) Indian Air Force — organised into Air Commands (Western, Eastern, Central, Southern, Maintenance), Head Quarter Air Headquarters and Air Defence; primary roles are air supremacy, strategic airlift, and aerial reconnaissance. (4) Integrated Defence Staff (IDS) — the apex body for jointness among the three services, headed by the Chief of Integrated Defence Staff. India is in the process of creating Unified Theatre Commands to replace traditional single-service commands for more effective joint operations.
  • India's internal security and border management rely heavily on paramilitary forces: Central Armed Police Forces (CAPF) include Border Security Force (BSF — Pakistan and Bangladesh borders), Sashastra Seema Bal (SSB — Nepal and Bhutan borders), Indo-Tibetan Border Police (ITBP — China/ LAC border), Assam Rifles (Myanmar border, also anti-insurgency in NE), Coast Guard (maritime security), Central Reserve Police Force (CRPF — internal security, also serves as central reserve), Indo Tibetan Police Border Force, National Security Guard (NSG — counter-terrorism, known as 'Black Cats'), and Special Boundary Police. Each force has a specific border or role assignment. These forces fall under the Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA) except Coast Guard which comes under the Ministry of Defence.
  • India's nuclear programme began under Homi Jehangir Bhabha. The Pokhran-I test in 1974 (codenamed Smiling Buddha) was India's first nuclear test conducted at Pokhran, Rajasthan. Pokhran-II tests in 1998 (codenamed Shakti) included five nuclear detonations under Prime Minister Atal Bihari Vajpayee. India maintains a credible minimum deterrent and follows a No First Use (NFU) doctrine, though this is under periodic review. Key missiles: Prithvi (short-range ballistic, ~150–250 km), Agni-I to Agni-V (intermediate to intercontinental range, Agni-V can reach ~5000+ km), Agni-Prime (new generation), BrahMos (supersonic cruise missile co-developed with Russia, exported to Philippines, Vietnam, etc.), Nag (anti-tank guided missile), Pinaka (multi-barrel rocket launcher), K-series ballistic missiles (nuclear-capable submarines like INS Arihant). The Agni-V is India's first Inter Continental Ballistic Missile (ICBM). India is not a signatory to the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty (NPT) or Comprehensive Test Ban Treaty (CTBT).
  • India has made significant strides in defence indigenisation through the 'Atmanirbhar Bharat' initiative. Key PSU defence manufacturing bodies include Defense Research and Development Organisation (DRDO), Hindustan Aeronautics Limited (HAL), Ordnance Factory Board (OFB) now demerged into seven unit PSUs under Defence PSUs under the Ministry of Defence, Bharat Dynamics Limited (BDL), Mazagon Dock Shipbuilders, Garden Reach Shipbuilders and Engineers (GRSE), and Goa Shipyard Limited. The Defence India Startup Challenge (DISC) promotes defence start-ups. India exports defence equipment to over 85 countries. Important indigenous platforms include Tejas (LCA by HAL), Arjun Tank, P-8I maritime patrol aircraft, INS Vikrant (indigenous aircraft carrier), Arighat-class SSBNs, and various DRDO missiles. The negative import list and positive indigenisation list are updated annually to boost domestic production.
  • India conducts numerous bilateral and multilateral military exercises: (1) Mitra Shakti — with France (air force), (2) Yudh Abhyas — with USA, (3) Shangri-La Dialogue participation, (4) Varuna — with France (naval), (5) Malabar — with USA and Japan (naval, now includes Singapore and Australia as observers/participants), (6) Indra — with Russia (army and air force), (7) Ajay Eagle — with Thailand (air force), (8) Garuda Shakti — with Indonesia (army), (9) Sahayata — with Sri Lanka, (10) Maitri — with Thailand (army), (11) Deep Straits — with Singapore (naval), (12) Sineex — with Tanzania (navy), (13) Al Nisaar — with UAE, (14) Red Star — with Russia (naval), (15) Exercise Desert Strike — with UAE. These exercises enhance interoperability, share best practices, and strengthen strategic partnerships.
  • United Nations (UN) — India is a founding member, permanent member of UN Security Council (advocating for reform), contributes significantly to UN Peacekeeping missions (one of the top troop contributors historically). Regional organisations: SAARC (South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation — headquarters Kathmandu, currently suspended), BIMSTEC (Bay of Bengal Initiative for Multi-Sectoral Technical and Economic Cooperation — Bangladesh, India, Myanmar, Thailand, Sri Lanka, Nepal, Bhutan), ASEAN Plus mechanisms, Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (SCO — India joined in 2017, focuses on counter-terrorism, regional stability), IBSA (India, Brazil, South Africa dialogue forum), Commonwealth of Nations. India is a founding member of the Non-Aligned Movement (NAM), advocate of nuclear disarmament, and a champion of the Global South. India has applied for permanent membership in UNSC, G4 nation along with Japan, Germany, and Brazil.
  • India maintains strategic partnerships globally: Russia — long-standing defence partner, supplies arms (S-400, Su-30MKI, aircraft carriers), key energy partner; USA — major defence伙伴 through INDIA-PACIFIC strategy, COMCASA, LEMOA, BECA agreements signed, growing strategic convergence; France — key defence partner (Rafale, Scorpene submarines, Mirage 2000, BrahMos integration), space cooperation; Israel — defence technology partner (Barak 8, Heron drones, Arrow system integration, military hardware); China — complex relationship with ongoing border disputes along LAC (Line of Actual Control in Ladakh sector, Galwan Valley clash 2020); Middle East — strategic energy ties with Gulf nations (UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar), defence cooperation agreements; Africa — through Indo-Africa dialogues, capacity building, medical assistance.
  • India shares borders with Pakistan ( western border ~3323 km with LoC), China (~3488 km along LAC disputed), Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh (~4096 km, one of world's most heavily guarded borders), Myanmar, and Afghanistan (via Siachen region). Key disputed areas: Aksai Chin (claimed by India, controlled by China since 1962 war), Doklam (disputed tri-junction between India-China-Bhutan, 2017 stand-off), Galwan Valley (2020 clash), Demchok, Depsang Plains. Siachen Glacier — world's highest battlefield, India controls the glacier since 1984 Operation Meghdoot. Indus Water Treaty (1960, brokered by World Bank) governs water sharing with Pakistan. The Sir Creek dispute is with Pakistan in the Rann of Kutch region. India also has maritime boundaries in the Indian Ocean Region (IOR) which it considers its strategic sphere.
  • Key treaties and regimes: Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty (NPT) — India is not a signatory (joined as de facto nuclear weapon state through US-India civil nuclear deal 2008). Comprehensive Test Ban Treaty (CTBT) — India signed in 1996 but has not ratified (conducted tests in 1974 and 1998). Chemical Weapons Convention (CWC) — India is a member, destroyed chemical stockpiles. Biological Weapons Convention (BWC) — India is a party. Fissile Material Cut-off Treaty (FMCT) — negotiations ongoing at Conference on Disarmament in Geneva. Missile Technology Control Regime (MTCR) — India joined in 2016. Zonal Nuclear Weapons Free Zones — India supports establishment but insists on universal, non-discriminatory approach. Treaty on the Prohibition of Nuclear Weapons (TPNW) — India did not sign, citing it does not address nuclear-weapon states. India is also part of the Australia Group (chemical/biological weapons non-proliferation) and Wassenaar Arrangement (conventional arms export control).
  • Key milestones: 1947 — Independence, partition, first Indo-Pak war over Kashmir. 1962 — Sino-Indian War, defeat led to major defence restructuring. 1965 — Second Indo-Pak War. 1971 — Liberation of Bangladesh war, creation of Bangladesh. 1974 — Pokhran-I (Smiling Buddha). 1984 — Operation Meghdoot (Siachen). 1987 — IPKF deployment in Sri Lanka (ended 1990). 1998 — Pokhran-II (Shakti), nuclear tests. 1999 — Kargil War. 2001-02 — Parliament attack crisis and military mobilisation with Pakistan. 2008 — Mumbai 26/11 terrorist attacks. 2016 — Surgical strikes across LoC post Uri attack. 2019 — Balakot airstrike after Pulwama attack. 2020 — Galwan Valley clash with China. 2023 — India assumed G20 presidency with emphasis on Global South. India has received numerous gallantry awards including Param Vir Chakra (highest wartime gallantry) — 21 awarded (15 in 1947 war, 3 in 1965, 1 in 1971, 2 in Kargil). Major generals who received PVC: Major Sobha Singh, Major Shankar Lal Sharma, Lance Naik Hendrick, Flying Officer Nirmal Jit Singh Sekhon, etc.

Important facts

  • Paramount Command of Indian Armed Forces is the President of India (Constitutional Commander-in-Chief), while operational command vests with the respective Service Chiefs.
  • Chief of Defence Staff (CDS) — first held by General Bipin Rawat (appointed Dec 2019); acts as permanent chair of Chiefs of Staff Committee, oversees joint training, procurement, and theatre commands.
  • Total strength approximately 1.45 million active personnel (Army ~1.2 million, Air Force ~140,000, Navy ~70,000) making it one of the world's largest standing militaries.
  • INS Vikrant is India's first indigenously built aircraft carrier, commissioned in September 2022 at Cochin Shipyard Limited, Kochi.
  • INS Arihant is India's first nuclear-powered submarine (SSBN), commissioned in 2016 at VISakhapatnam; part of the Nuclear Triad (land, sea, air nuclear delivery capability).
  • BrahMos Aerospace is a joint venture between India's DRDO and Russia's NPO Mashinostroyeniya, headquartered in New Delhi; BrahMos is named after Brahmaputra and Moscow rivers.
  • Agni-V has a range exceeding 5,000 km, classified as an Inter-Continental Ballistic Missile (ICBM) by India.
  • Indus Waters Treaty was signed on 19 September 1960 between India and Pakistan, brokered by the World Bank; it allocates eastern rivers (Sutlej, Beas, Ravi) to India and western rivers (Indus, Jhelum, Chenab) to Pakistan.
  • Line of Actual Control (LAC) is the de facto border between India and China in the Himalayan region, established after the 1962 war; total length approximately 3,488 km.
  • India is the world's largest importer of arms according to SIPRI data (2017-2021), with USA, France, and Russia as major suppliers.
  • The Ministry of Defence comprises four departments: Department of Military Affairs (DMA), Department of Defence Production (DOP), Department of Defence Research & Development (DDR&D), and Department of Defence Finance (DDF).
  • Operation Parakram (2001-02) was India's massive military mobilisation along the India-Pakistan border following the December 2001 Parliament attack.
  • National Defence Academy (NDA) is located in Pune, Maharashtra; it is a tri-services academy training officers for Army, Navy, and Air Force entry.
  • National Defence Academy cadets undergo training at the Combined Defence Services (CDS) entrance examination route; NDA exam is conducted by UPSC twice yearly.
  • Service Selection Board (SSB) is the final screening process for NDA/CDS candidates; centres are located in Allahabad (now Prayagraj), Bhopal, Dehradun, Bangalore, and Chandigarh.
  • India's nuclear doctrine was formally approved in 2003, emphasizing credible minimum deterrence, no-first-use, and massive retaliation as the second-strike response.
  • The Kargil War took place from May to July 1999; Major Sundar Singh (PVC posthumously) and Lieutenant Manoj Kumar Pandey (MVC) were notable heroes.
  • Operation Vijay (1999) was the code name for Indian military operations during the Kargil War; Operation Safed Sagar was the air component.
  • The Andaman and Nicobar Command (ANC) is India's only tri-service theatre command, headquartered at Port Blair; it is strategically positioned to control the Strait of Malacca.
  • Samudra Shakti is a naval exercise with Indonesia; Milind Soman served as the first director-general of the Andaman & Nicobar Command (2001-2004).
  • India's defence budget for FY 2024-25 was approximately ₹6.24 lakh crore, with allocation for capital acquisition increasing significantly.
  • The Defence Acquisition Procedure (DAP) 2020 streamlined the procurement process with a focus on indigenisation, buy (Indian-IDDM category), and make categories.
  • India has signed defence cooperation agreements with over 50 countries; key defence trade partners include USA, Russia, France, Israel, and UK.
  • The National Security Adviser (NSA) heads the National Security Secretariat and coordinates intelligence agencies including RAW (Research and Analysis Wing), IB (Intelligence Bureau), and NIA (National Investigation Agency).
  • RAW was formed in 1968 after the 1965 war, replacing the Research Wing of Intelligence Bureau (IB); its first chief was Rameshwar Yashwant Rao Yaduvanshi (Y.R. Sharma).
  • The concept of 'Cold Start' doctrine (also called 'Proactive Strategy') was developed post-2001 Parliament attack; it aims at rapid mobilisation and limited conventional strikes against Pakistan without crossing nuclear threshold. However, India has never officially confirmed this doctrine.
  • The National Military Committee (NMC) of India advises the government on military matters; the CDS chairs this committee.
  • India's military zones/commands: 17 operational commands, 1 Strategic Forces Command, and 1 Integrated Defence Staff; each Army command is headed by a Lieutenant General.
  • The concept of 'One Belt One Road' (BRI) by China is viewed with suspicion by India as it includes the China-Pakistan Economic Corridor (CPEC) passing through Pakistan-occupied Kashmir.
  • India's contributions to UN Peacekeeping: India has contributed to over 49 UN missions; Indian peacekeepers have received numerous UN medals; current deployments include MONUSCO (DRC), MINUSCA (CAR), UNIFIL (Lebanon), etc.

Memory aids

  • Mnemonic for Indian Armed Forces Commands (Army): Remember 'NEWECASTLE' — Northern, Eastern, Western, Central, Southern, South Western, Training, Mechanical Equipment, Logistic, Army Aviation. Alternatively, group them: Northern + Eastern face China/Pakistan threats; Western faces Pakistan; Southern faces Indian Ocean; Central is reserve/strategic;
  • For missile ranges: Think 'Agnes' for Agni — A (Agni-I short), Gn (Agni-II medium), Ni (Agni-III intermediate), V (Agni-V long/ICBM). Remember: 'Agni-V is the tallest, reaches five thousand plus' — over 5000 km range.
  • Nuclear tests mnemonic: 'SB-S' — Smiling Buddha (1974) then Shakti (1998). Two tests, two names, five years apart from start to full capability: 1974 first, 1998 declared.
  • Quad members mnemonic: 'JAIQ' or 'JAiQ' — Japan, Australia, India, Qatar? No — remember the map: USA is west of Japan, Australia south of Indonesia, India south of Tibet. Four corners of Indo-Pacific: USA, Japan, India, Australia.
  • Border forces mnemonic: 'BIBS' — BSF (border), ITBP (Tibet/China), SSB (Sikkim/Bhutan/Nepal), ASR (Assam/Rifles — Myanmar). Each gets a letter: B=BSF, I=ITBP, B=SSB, S=Sashastra.
  • Exercises by partner: 'RUS' — Russia (Indra, Red Star), USA (Yudh Abhyas), UAE (Al Nisaar). Another grouping: 'FRI' — France (Mitra Shakti, Varuna), Israel (various), UK (various). Grouping by type: Naval (Varuna, Malabar, Deep Sea), Air (Mitra Shakti, Ajay Eagle, Golden Eagle), Army (Yudh Abhyas, Garuda Shakti, Desert Strike).
  • Think of 'NCA' as 'Nobody Can Attack' — Nuclear Command Authority with Civilian Control (PM) at top. Two tiers: Political Council (policy) and Executive Council (implementation). Remember: 'First you decide politically, then execute operationally.'
  • Dr DO's — 'Doctor Drishti Driv'D Druth D'rin'De' — think: Defence Production, DRDO, DAF, DAP. Four Ds of Defence Modernisation: Doctrine, Organisation, Training, Equipment, plus Materiel (the 'Five Ds'). Remember: 'DOTEM' — Doctrine, Organisation, Training, Equipment, Materiel. India's military modernisation framework follows the 'Five Ds'.
  • Think of 'SIMPL' for Sino-Indian border issues: Siachen, Indo-China main dispute, Mt. Everest base (Doklam), Political question of Tibet/Aksai Chin, LAC. SIMPL — remember it as 'simple problems are complex'!
  • For gallantry awards: PARAM = 'Pare (above)aram (strength)' — Param Vir Chakra is above all other bravery awards. Maha Vir Chakra (MVC) is next, then Vir Chakra (VrC), then Ashok Chakra ( peacetime gallantry). Remember: 'PMAV' — Param, Maha, Ashok, Vir.

Exam tips

  • NDA/CDS frequently ask about the exact year and codename of nuclear tests — memorise: Pokhran-I = 1974, Smiling Buddha; Pokhran-II = 1998, Shakti. Also know the number of tests: 1 in 1974, 5 in 1998.
  • Be prepared for questions on recent defence acquisitions and indigenisation — focus on what India has recently purchased (e.g., Rafale, S-400) versus what it has built indigenously (Tejas, INS Vikrant, BrahMos).
  • For questions on international organisations, focus on India's role and membership status rather than detailed history of the organisation itself.
  • Memorise the exact names of key military exercises with their partner countries — this is a very high-yield topic for NDA/CDS. Practice matching questions.
  • Pay attention to current affairs related to defence — recent deals, exercises, appointments, and geopolitical developments are frequently asked. Stay updated on quarterly defence budgets and major procurement approvals.
  • Understand the difference between various missile types: Ballistic (Agni, Prithvi), Cruise (BrahMos, Nirbhay), Anti-tank (Nag), and Surface-to-Air (Akash, Barak-8). Know the range and propulsion type where applicable.
  • Focus on India's strategic location in the Indian Ocean Region (IOR) — the concept of 'String of Pearls' by China, India's 'Necklace of Diamonds' strategy, and chokepoints like Strait of Hormuz, Strait of Malacca, and Bab-el-Mandeb are important.
  • Practice statement-based questions on defence doctrine — especially those involving assertions about nuclear doctrine, no-first-use policy, and credible minimum deterrence.
  • Remember key dates: 26 January 1950 (Republic Day, first republic), 15 August 1947 (Independence), 23 March 1974 (first nuclear test announcement), 11-13 May 1998 (Pokhran-II tests), 3 October 1999 (Kargil War end).
  • For geography-related defence questions, focus on strategic passes (Bolan Pass, Khyber Pass, Nathu La, Cho La), vital sea lanes, and international borders. Know the exact locations of key military establishments (Chandipur test range, DRDO HQ, etc.).
  • Read recent Union Budget allocations for defence carefully — understand the split between revenue expenditure and capital expenditure, and the significance of the increase in defence modernisation outlays.
  • When answering questions on international relations, link geography with strategy — e.g., why India focuses on the Indian Ocean Region, the significance of the Andaman & Nicobar Islands, and the strategic importance of Chabahar Port (Iran) and Gwadar Port (Pakistan- China).

Common traps

  • Confusion: NDA exam is conducted by UPSC but NDA itself is a training academy in Pune run by the Ministry of Defence. Do not confuse the entrance exam with the institution.
  • Trap: INS Vikrant (IAC-1) is an aircraft carrier; INS Vikramaditya (modified Kiev-class) is also an aircraft carrier. Both are operational but Vikrant is indigenous while Vikramaditya was acquired from Russia.
  • Common mistake: Confusing Agni series missiles — Agni-I is shortest range (~700 km), Agni-II (~2000 km), Agni-III (~3500 km), Agni-V (>5000 km). Do not mix up the range order.
  • Trap: BrahMos is supersonic cruise missile, NOT a ballistic missile. Do not confuse it with Agni series which are ballistic missiles.
  • Common error: Thinking India is a signatory to NPT or CTBT — India is NOT a signatory to either treaty. This is frequently asked in NDA/CDS.
  • Trap: Misidentifying the head of Intelligence Bureau (IB) vs Research and Analysis Wing (RAW). IB is domestic intelligence; RAW is external intelligence. Both report to the Cabinet Secretariat.
  • Confusion point: The Chief of Defence Staff (CDS) is NOT the Chief of Army Staff (COAS). CDS is a separate appointment created in 2019; first CDS was Gen. Bipin Rawat who was also COAS before his death in 2021.
  • Trap: Confusing the Andaman & Nicobar Command (tri-service) with regular theatre commands. ANC is currently the only permanent tri-service command; unified theatre commands are still being implemented.
  • Common error: Thinking the Indus Waters Treaty is between India and China — it is between India and Pakistan, brokered by the World Bank.
  • Trap: Confusing Operation Meghdoot (1984, Siachen) with Operation Vijay (1999, Kargil) and Operation Parakram (2001-02, mobilisation). Each operation has a different objective and timeframe.
  • Confusion: NDA cadets are trained at the National Defence Academy in Pune, but the SSB interviews happen at multiple centres (Allahabad, Bhopal, Dehradun, Bangalore, Chandigarh). Do not confuse the training location with testing locations.
  • Trap: Thinking India has a formal 'No First Use' policy without any exceptions — India's NFU policy has caveats and is subject to periodic review by the government.
  • Common mistake: Forgetting that the President of India is the Constitutional Commander-in-Chief but actual operational control rests with the respective Service Chiefs and the Ministry of Defence.
  • Trap: Confusing the scope of SCO (Shanghai Cooperation Organisation) — it focuses on counter-terrorism, separatism, and extremism ('Three Evils'), not just economic cooperation.
