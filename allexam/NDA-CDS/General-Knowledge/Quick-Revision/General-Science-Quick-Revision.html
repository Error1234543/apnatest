Definitions

  • Newton's First Law of Motion: An object at rest stays at rest and an object in motion stays in uniform motion in a straight line unless acted upon by an external unbalanced force.
  • Newton's Second Law of Motion: The rate of change of momentum of an object is proportional to the applied unbalanced force in the direction of force; expressed as F = ma.
  • Newton's Third Law of Motion: For every action there is an equal and opposite reaction; forces always occur in pairs.
  • Density: Mass per unit volume, expressed as ρ = m/V, SI unit kg/m³.
  • Pressure: Force per unit area, P = F/A; in fluids, P = hρg where h is depth, ρ is density, g is gravitational acceleration.
  • Work: Product of force and displacement in the direction of force, W = F·s·cosθ, measured in joules (J).
  • Power: Rate of doing work, P = W/t, measured in watts (W).
  • Kinetic Energy: Energy possessed by a body due to its motion, KE = ½mv².
  • Potential Energy: Stored energy due to position, PE = mgh for gravitational case.
  • Ohm's Law: Current through a conductor is directly proportional to potential difference across it, V = IR, provided temperature remains constant.
  • Resistance: Opposition to current flow, R = ρL/A where ρ is resistivity, L is length, A is cross-sectional area.
  • refractive index: Ratio of speed of light in vacuum to speed in medium, n = c/v.
  • Focal Length: Distance between pole/center of lens or mirror and its principal focus.
  • pH: Measure of acidity or alkalinity of a solution, pH = -log[H⁺]; scale ranges from 0 to 14.
  • Mole: Amount of substance containing Avogadro's number (6.022 × 10²³) of entities.
  • Cell: Fundamental structural and functional unit of all living organisms.
  • DNA: Deoxyribonucleic acid; double-helix molecule carrying genetic information.
  • Osmosis: Movement of water molecules from a region of higher water concentration to lower water concentration through a semipermeable membrane.
  • Photosynthesis: Process by which green plants convert light energy into chemical energy using CO₂ and water to produce glucose and oxygen: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂.

Key concepts

  • Mechanics — Laws of Motion: Inertia is resistance to change in motion; greater mass means greater inertia. Momentum p = mv is conserved in isolated systems. Rocket propulsion and recoil of guns are classic applications of Newton's third law. Friction opposes relative motion and is essential for walking, braking, and gripping.
  • Mechanics — Gravitation: Universal law F = Gm₁m₂/r² explains planetary motion. Acceleration due to gravity g varies with altitude (decreases) and depth (decreases toward zero at Earth's center). Weight = mg; mass remains constant everywhere while weight changes on different planets.
  • Mechanics — Work, Energy and Power: Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed. In pendulum motion, KE and PE interchange continuously; total mechanical energy remains constant (ignoring friction). Efficiency = output/input × 100%. 1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J.
  • Mechanics — Pressure and Buoyancy: Pascal's law states pressure applied at any point in enclosed fluid transmits equally in all directions — basis of hydraulic lifts. Archimedes' principle: buoyant force equals weight of fluid displaced. Objects float when buoyant force equals weight; density comparison determines whether object sinks or floats.
  • Sound: Sound requires a material medium; cannot travel in vacuum. Speed of sound is maximum in solids, less in liquids, minimum in gases. Frequency determines pitch, amplitude determines loudness.超声 (ultrasound) frequency > 20,000 Hz; infrasonic < 20 Hz. SONAR uses ultrasound for underwater detection.
  • Light — Reflection and Refraction: Angle of incidence equals angle of reflection. Mirrors: concave mirrors form real inverted images (used in headlights, torches); convex mirrors form virtual erect diminished images (used as rear-view mirrors). Lenses: convex lenses converge light (used in magnifying glasses, cameras); concave lenses diverge light (used in myopia correction).
  • Light — Dispersion and Scattering: White light splits into seven colors through a prism (VIBGYOR — Violet indigo blue green yellow orange red). Blue sky due to Rayleigh scattering (shorter wavelengths scatter more). Red sunrise/sunset because light travels longer path through atmosphere, leaving mostly red/orange wavelengths.
  • Electricity: Series circuit — same current throughout, voltage divides. Parallel circuit — same voltage across branches, current divides. Power dissipated in resistor P = VI = I²R = V²/R. Household wiring uses parallel connection. Fuse wire melts when excessive current flows, breaking circuit — protects appliances.
  • Magnetism and Electromagnetic Induction: Magnetic field lines never intersect; denser lines mean stronger field. Right-hand thumb rule gives direction of magnetic field around current-carrying conductor. Fleming's left-hand rule gives direction of force on current-carrying conductor in magnetic field. Faraday's law: changing magnetic flux induces EMF — basis of generators and transformers.
  • Periodic Table: Elements arranged by increasing atomic number. Groups are vertical columns (similar valence electron configuration, similar properties). Periods are horizontal rows. Metals on left, non-metals on right, metalloids in between. Atomic size decreases across period (left to right) and increases down a group. Ionization energy increases across period and decreases down a group.
  • Chemical Reactions: Combination, decomposition, displacement, double displacement, oxidation-reduction are major types. Oxidation = gain of oxygen / loss of hydrogen / loss of electrons. Reduction = loss of oxygen / gain of hydrogen / gain of electrons. Corrosion is slow oxidation of metals (rusting of iron). Neutralization: acid + base → salt + water.
  • Acids, Bases and Salts: Strong acids completely ionize in water (HCl, H₂SO₄, HNO₃); weak acids partially ionize (CH₃COOH, H₂CO₃). Strong bases completely dissociate (NaOH, KOH); weak bases partially (NH₄OH). pH scale: acidic < 7, neutral = 7, basic > 7. Indicators: litmus (red in acid, blue in base), phenolphthalein (colorless in acid, pink in base), methyl orange (red in acid, yellow in base).
  • Metals and Non-Metals: Metals are shiny, malleable, ductile, good conductors of heat and electricity. Reactivity series orders metals by ease of losing electrons: K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > Zn > Fe > Pb > H > Cu > Ag > Au. Metals above hydrogen displace hydrogen from dilute acids. Sodium stored in kerosene due to high reactivity with air and water.
  • Carbon Compounds: Carbon shows catenation (self-linking) and tetravalency. Organic compounds: alkanes (CnH2n+2), alkenes (CnH2n), alkynes (CnH2n-2). Functional groups determine chemical properties — -OH (alcohol), -CHO (aldehyde), -COOH (carboxylic acid), -CO- (ketone). Soaps are sodium/potassium salts of fatty acids; detergents are ammonium/sulfonate salts effective in hard water.
  • Cell Structure: Prokaryotic cells lack a true nucleus and membrane-bound organelles (bacteria). Eukaryotic cells have a nuclear membrane and organelles. Cell membrane is selectively permeable. Plant cells have cell wall, large vacuole, chloroplasts; animal cells lack these. Mitochondria is power house of cell (ATP production). Ribosomes synthesize proteins.
  • Tissues and Organs: Meristematic tissue in plants has actively dividing cells (apical, lateral, intercalary). Permanent tissue: simple (parenchyma, collenchyma, sclerenchyma) and complex (xylem transports water, phloem transports food). In humans: epithelial (covering/protection), connective (support/transport), muscle (movement), nervous (control).
  • Human Physiology: Digestive system breaks food into absorbable units — mouth (salivary amylase), stomach (HCl + pepsin), small intestine (pancreatic juice + bile + intestinal juice). Liver produces bile (emulsifies fats). Heart has four chambers; right side pumps deoxygenated blood to lungs, left side pumps oxygenated blood to body. Blood groups: A, B, AB, O based on antigens.
  • Respiration and Circulation: Breathing involves inhalation (diaphragm contracts, moves down) and exhalation (diaphragm relaxes, moves up). Gas exchange occurs in alveoli of lungs. Hemoglobin carries oxygen in RBCs. Urinary system filters blood in kidneys (nephrons are functional units). Hormones are chemical messengers secreted by endocrine glands.
  • Nervous System: Neuron is functional unit — cell body, dendrites (receive signals), axon (transmits signals). Synapse is junction between two neurons. Reflex arc is involuntary rapid response path. CNS = brain + spinal cord; PNS connects CNS to body parts. Brain: cerebrum (thinking), cerebellum (balance), medulla (vital functions).
  • Endocrine System: Pituitary gland — master gland (growth hormone, TSH, ACTH). Thyroid — thyroxine (regulates metabolism); deficiency causes goiter. Pancreas — insulin (lowers blood sugar) and glucagon (raises blood sugar). Adrenal — adrenaline (fight-or-flight response). Sex hormones: testosterone (testes), estrogen and progesterone (ovaries).
  • Reproduction: Sexual reproduction involves fusion of male and female gametes. In humans, fertilization occurs in fallopian tube; embryo implants in uterus. Asexual reproduction: binary fission (amoeba, paramoecium), budding (hydra, yeast), spore formation (fungi, mosses), regeneration (planaria), vegetative propagation (plants).
  • Ecology and Environment: Ecosystem = biotic community + abiotic environment. Food chain: producer → primary consumer → secondary consumer → tertiary consumer. Energy flow is unidirectional (sun → producers → consumers). Biomagnification: accumulation of non-biodegradable toxins (like DDT) increases at higher trophic levels. Ozone layer (O₃) in stratosphere protects from UV radiation.
  • Environmental Issues: Deforestation causes soil erosion, loss of biodiversity, climate change. Pollution: air (SO₂, NOₓ, CO, particulate matter), water (sewage, industrial waste, pesticides), soil (pesticides, heavy metals). Greenhouse effect: CO₂, CH₄, CFCs trap infrared radiation causing global warming. Acid rain: SO₂ and NOₓ react with water vapor forming sulfuric and nitric acids.
  • Human Diseases — Communicable: Caused by pathogens — bacteria (TB, cholera, typhoid), viruses (dengue, chickenpox, COVID-19), protozoa (malaria, sleeping sickness), worms (filariasis, roundworm). Transmission modes: contact, air, water, vector-borne (mosquito, flea, tick). Immunity: innate (present at birth) vs acquired (vaccination, natural infection).
  • Human Diseases — Non-Communicable: Caused by lifestyle, genetics, deficiency. Diabetes mellitus: insulin deficiency or insulin resistance. Hypertension: chronically high blood pressure. Cancer: uncontrolled cell division. Deficiency diseases: scurvy (vitamin C), rickets (vitamin D), night blindness (vitamin A), beriberi (vitamin B₁), anemia (iron), goiter (iodine).
  • Nutrition: Macronutrients — carbohydrates (energy), proteins (growth/repair), fats (energy storage/insulation). Micronutrients — vitamins (organic, required in trace amounts) and minerals (inorganic). Vitamins: A (vision), B-complex (metabolism), C (immunity/collagen), D (calcium absorption/bones), E (antioxidant), K (blood clotting). Balanced diet contains all nutrients in correct proportion.
  • Plant Physiology: Transpiration is loss of water vapor from plant surfaces (mainly leaves). It creates pull for water ascent and cools plant. Photosynthesis occurs in chloroplasts containing chlorophyll. Stomata regulate gas exchange and transpiration. Tropisms: phototropism (toward light), geotropism (toward gravity), chemotropism (toward chemical), thigmotropism (touch).
  • Modern Physics Basics: Photoelectric effect — light ejects electrons from metal surface (Einstein explained using photon concept). Nuclear fission: heavy nucleus splits into lighter nuclei releasing energy (nuclear reactors, atomic bomb). Nuclear fusion: light nuclei combine to form heavier nucleus releasing enormous energy (sun, hydrogen bomb). X-rays discovered by Röntgen; used in medicine.
  • Semiconductors and Electronics: Intrinsic semiconductors (silicon, germanium) have conductivity between conductors and insulators. Doping with pentavalent impurity creates n-type (extra electrons). Doping with trivalent impurity creates p-type (holes). p-n junction diode allows current in one direction. Transistor = amplifier or switch; basis of modern electronics.
  • Important Scientific Discoveries and Scientists: Newton — laws of motion, gravitation, optics. Einstein — relativity, photoelectric effect. Curie — radioactivity. Rutherford — nuclear model of atom. Bohr — atomic model. Watt — steam engine improvement, unit of power. Tesla — AC current. Fleming — electric motor/generator rules. Pasteur — germ theory. Jenner — smallpox vaccine.
  • Everyday Science Applications: Baking soda (NaHCO₃) used in baking and fire extinguishers. Bleaching powder (CaOCl₂) used for disinfection and whitening. Plaster of Paris (CaSO₄·½H₂O) used in casts and molds. Washing soda (Na₂CO₃·10H₂O) used in cleaning. Gun met al = Cu + Sn + Zn. Bronze = Cu + Sn. Brass = Cu + Zn. Steel = Fe + C + small amounts of other metals.
  • Units and Measurements: Standard SI units — length (meter), mass (kilogram), time (second), electric current (ampere), temperature (kelvin), luminous intensity (candela), amount of substance (mole). Common conversions: 1 km = 1000 m, 1 hour = 3600 s, 1 atm = 101325 Pa = 760 mm Hg. One horsepower = 746 W. 1 astronomical unit ≈ 1.5 × 10¹¹ m.

Important facts

  • Speed of light in vacuum = 3 × 10⁸ m/s. Speed of sound in air ≈ 340 m/s at room temperature.
  • Solar constant = 1.36 kW/m². 1 calorie = 4.184 joules.
  • Earth's rotation causes day-night cycle; revolution around Sun causes seasons (tilt of axis = 23.5°).
  • Number of chromosomes in human somatic cells = 46 (23 pairs); gametes have 23 chromosomes.
  • Blood volume in adult human ≈ 5 liters. Heart beats ≈ 72 times per minute. Normal BP = 120/80 mm Hg.
  • Liver is largest internal organ; pancreas is both endocrine and exocrine gland. Kidney has ~1 million nephrons.
  • Human body temperature normal = 98.6°F or 37°C. Heart temperature is slightly higher (~38°C).
  • Ear drum (tympanic membrane) vibrates in response to sound waves. Cochlea converts vibrations to nerve impulses.
  • Vitamin D is synthesized in skin on exposure to sunlight. Vitamin B₁₂ is found only in animal products.
  • pH of human blood = 7.35–7.45 (slightly alkaline). pH of gastric juice ≈ 1.5–3.5. pH of pure water = 7.
  • Ozone layer is located in stratosphere at about 15–35 km altitude. Montreal Protocol (1987) aimed to protect ozone layer.
  • The seven colors of visible spectrum have wavelengths: violet (~400 nm) to red (~700 nm).
  • 1 Ampere = 1 Coulomb/second. 1 Volt = 1 Joule/Coulomb. 1 Ohm = 1 Volt/Ampere.
  • Avogadro's number = 6.022 × 10²³. Molar volume of gas at STP = 22.4 liters.
  • Atomic number of Iron (Fe) = 26. Atomic number of Gold (Au) = 79. Atomic number of Uranium (U) = 92.
  • Most abundant gas in Earth's atmosphere: nitrogen (78%), oxygen (21%), argon (0.93%), CO₂ (~0.04%).
  • Hematite (Fe₂O₃) is most important iron ore. Bauxite is main ore of aluminum. Galena (PbS) is lead ore.
  • DNA has antiparallel double helix structure discovered by Watson and Crick (1953). RNA is single-stranded.
  • Insulin was discovered by Banting and Best (1921). Penicillin discovered by Alexander Fleming (1928).
  • Malaria is caused by Plasmodium (protozoan) transmitted by female Anopheles mosquito. Dengue transmitted by Aedes aegypti.
  • Lungs contain approximately 300-500 million alveoli providing vast surface area for gas exchange (~70 m² total).
  • Hemoglobin contains iron; each hemoglobin molecule carries 4 oxygen molecules. Carboxyhemoglobin (from CO) prevents oxygen transport.
  • Red blood cells live approximately 120 days; produced in bone marrow. White blood cells fight infection. Platelets help clotting.
  • Human genome has approximately 3 billion DNA base pairs; contains about 20,000-25,000 genes.
  • Biosphere includes all ecosystems; extends from deepest ocean trenches (~11 km) to highest mountains (~8.8 km).
  • Chandrayaan-3 (2023) was India's successful lunar landing mission; landed near Moon's south pole.
  • ISRO's Mangalyaan (Mars Orbiter Mission, 2013) made India first Asian country to reach Mars orbit on first attempt.
  • Tejas is India's light combat aircraft. Agni and Prithvi are indigenous missile systems. INS Vikrant is India's first indigenously built aircraft carrier.
  • Sputnik-1 (1957) was first artificial satellite. Yuri Gagarin (1961) was first human in space. Neil Armstrong (1969) first walked on Moon.
  • CROSSLINGUAGIC NOTE: 'Germ theory' was established by Louis Pasteur; 'Antiseptic surgery' by Joseph Lister; 'Cell theory' by Schleiden, Schwann, and Virchow.
  • Sound cannot travel in vacuum — demonstrated by bell jar experiment. Light can travel through vacuum — electromagnetic wave.
  • Diamond is hardest natural substance (carbon). Graphite is softest form of carbon (used as lubricant). Both are allotropes of carbon.
  • CNG (Compressed Natural Gas) is cleaner fuel — burns completely with minimal pollutants. Hybrid vehicles use both petrol/diesel and electric motor.
  • Fiber optics works on principle of total internal reflection. Used in endoscopy and high-speed internet transmission.
  • LASER = Light Amplification by Stimulated Emission of Radiation. Properties: monochromatic, coherent, highly directional. Uses: surgery, barcodes, communications, military.
  • NANOTECHNOLOGY: Manipulation of matter at atomic/molecular scale (1-100 nanometers). Carbon nanotubes, graphene are notable examples.
  • SUPERCONDUCTORS: Materials with zero electrical resistance below critical temperature. YBCO (yttrium barium copper oxide) is high-temperature superconductor.
  • BLACK HOLE: Region of spacetime where gravity is so strong nothing escapes. Boundary is event horizon. First image captured by Event Horizon Telescope (2019).
  • NEUTRINO: Nearly massless neutral particle that interacts very weakly with matter. Trillions pass through your body every second from the Sun.

Memory aids

  • VIBGYOR for visible spectrum colors: Violet, Indigo, Blue, Green, Yellow, Orange, Red — remember 'Very Interesting Birds Generally Open Red feathers'.
  • Reactivity series: 'Please Stop Calling Me A Careless Zebra Instead, Let Us Know How Copper Saves Gold' — K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al, Zn, Fe, Pb, H, Cu, Ag, Au.
  • Metals above hydrogen displace hydrogen from acids — only K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al, Zn, Fe, Pb do this. Cu, Ag, Au do not.
  • Planets in order from Sun: 'My Very Educated Mother Just Served Us Noodles' — Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune.
  • Parts of brain: Cerebrum (thinking/large), Cerebellum (balance/small), Medulla (vital functions/base) — remember 'Can Cats Mend?'
  • Human heart chambers: Right Atrium → Right Ventricle → Lungs → Left Atrium → Left Ventricle. Remember blood goes 'Up then Down then Across then Down'.
  • Vitamins mnemonic: 'A for Eyes, K for Clotting, B for Brain, C for Colds, D for Bones'. Fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K) need fat for absorption.
  • Photoelectric effect formula: E = hν = hc/λ. Planck's constant h = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ Js. Energy of photon depends on frequency, not intensity.
  • Mirror formula: 1/f = 1/v + 1/u. Lens formula: 1/f = 1/v - 1/u. Sign convention: distances measured from pole/optic center; above principal axis positive.
  • Ohm's law triangles: V triangle divided into I and R (V = IR). P triangle: P = VI, also P = I²R and P = V²/R. Remember 'VIR' for voltage-current-resistance.
  • Series-parallel circuits: Series — same current, voltage divides. Parallel — same voltage, current divides. Household wiring is parallel so each appliance gets full voltage.
  • Cell organelles memory: 'Mitochondria = power house', 'Ribosomes = protein factories', 'Golgi body = packaging unit', 'Lysosomes = suicide bags', 'Vacuole = storage'.
  • Food chain mnemonic: 'Plants feed Deer, Deer feeds Lion' — producer → herbivore → carnivore. Always starts with producer (plant) and energy from sun.
  • Periodic trends: Size decreases left to right across period (more protons pull electrons closer). Size increases top to bottom in group (more shells).
  • Soaps vs detergents: Soaps are sodium/potassium salts of fatty acids (natural, biodegradable, fail in hard water). Detergents are sulfonates (synthetic, work in hard water).
  • Naming organic compounds: Meth- (1C), Eth- (2C), Prop- (3C), But- (4C). Suffix -ane (single bond), -ene (double bond), -yne (triple bond). Prefix -ol (alcohol), -al (aldehyde), -oic acid (carboxylic acid).
  • Island of stability for neutron-rich nuclei: Elements with magic numbers of protons/neutrons (2, 8, 20, 28, 50, 82, 126) are more stable.
  • Conservation laws: Mass-energy, momentum, charge, angular momentum always conserved in isolated systems. These are absolute laws in classical and modern physics.
  • Half-life concept: Time for half of radioactive atoms to decay. After n half-lives, remaining fraction = (1/2)ⁿ. After 3 half-lives, 1/8th remains.
  • Nuclear symbols: Atomic number Z (protons), mass number A (protons + neutrons). Neutrons = A - Z. Isotopes: same Z, different A. Isobars: same A, different Z.

Exam tips

  • NDA/CDS General Science questions are typically from Classes 8-10 NCERT level — focus on conceptual clarity rather than rote memorization of advanced topics.
  • For Physics, practice numerical problems on speed, density, Ohm's law, mirror/lens, and electricity — these appear frequently with direct formula application.
  • For Chemistry, focus on everyday chemistry (acids-bases, metals-nonmetals, carbon compounds) and environmental chemistry rather than detailed reaction mechanisms.
  • For Biology, emphasis is on human physiology (digestive, circulatory, respiratory, excretory systems), diseases (communicable/non-communicable), and basic cell biology.
  • In NDA GAT Section B, General Science questions carry equal weight per question — manage time wisely and avoid spending too long on any single difficult question.
  • Negative marking applies in both NDA and CDS — attempt only questions you are confident about. Blind guessing can cost more than it gains.
  • Previous years show recurring patterns: light (reflection/refraction), electricity (Ohm's law, series-parallel), human physiology (heart, blood, digestion), and environmental science (pollution, ozone, greenhouse effect).
  • Focus on understanding graphs and diagrams — questions often ask to interpret a labeled diagram (human eye, ear, heart, plant cell, animal cell, nuclear reaction).
  • Don't ignore computer fundamentals and space science — ISRO achievements (Chandrayaan, Mangalyaan) and basic computer knowledge (input/output devices, memory units, networking) appear periodically.
  • Current affairs related to science (new discoveries, awards, space missions, health alerts) should be reviewed monthly, especially in the 6 months before the exam.
  • Practice identifying the odd one out and matching questions — these formats test breadth of knowledge across multiple sub-topics simultaneously.
  • For numerical problems, keep formulas handy but understand when to apply them. Unit conversion errors are the most common mistake — always check units match.
  • Remember: NDA/GK Science questions test APPLICATION, not just recall. A question may give a real-life scenario and ask which principle applies (e.g., why does a ship float? Archimedes' principle).
  • The Defence services value knowledge of defense-related science (radar, sonar, missile technology, optics in targeting, communications) — give extra attention to these applications.
  • Quick revision strategy: Create flashcards for one-word answers (names of scientists, discoveries, units, diseases, vitamins) and review daily in the final weeks before exam.

Common traps

  • Confusing mass with weight: Mass is constant everywhere; weight = mg changes with location. On Moon, weight is 1/6th but mass is same.
  • Mixing up series and parallel: In series, current is same through all resistors (voltage divides). In parallel, voltage is same across all (current divides).
  • Forgetting sign convention in mirror/lens formula: Object distance (u) is usually taken as negative. Focal length is positive for convex mirror/concave lens, negative for concave mirror/convex lens.
  • Assuming velocity of light changes in different media while frequency remains constant: When light enters denser medium, speed decreases, wavelength decreases, but frequency stays the same.
  • Confusing conductor with semiconductor behavior: Conductors decrease in resistance with temperature increase. Semiconductors increase in resistance with temperature decrease (opposite trend).
  • Thinking pH = 7 always means neutral: pH = 7 is neutral ONLY at 25°C. At other temperatures, neutral pH differs (e.g., at body temperature ~37°C, neutral pH ≈ 6.8).
  • Believing photosynthesis only happens in daylight: Photosynthesis requires light, but dark reactions (Calvin cycle) can proceed briefly after light is removed using stored ATP/NADPH.
  • Confusing arteries with veins: Arteries carry blood AWAY from heart (usually oxygenated except pulmonary artery). Veins carry blood TO heart (usually deoxygenated except pulmonary vein).
  • Assuming all isotopes are radioactive: Most isotopes are stable. Only unstable radioisotopes decay. Common stable isotopes: Carbon-12, Oxygen-16, Hydrogen-1.
  • Thinking sound travels faster in denser media because of density alone: Actually sound travels faster in solids because of elasticity, not just density. The elastic property dominates over density effect.
  • Mixing up oestrogen and progesterone functions: Oestrogen develops female secondary sexual characteristics. Progesterone maintains pregnancy and prepares uterus for implantation.
  • Confusing myopia with hypermetropia correction: Myopia (near-sightedness) corrected by concave lens (diverging). Hypermetropia (far-sightedness) corrected by convex lens (converging).
  • Thinking fusion happens in nuclear reactors: Nuclear reactors use FISSION (splitting heavy nuclei). Fusion (combining light nuclei) powers the Sun and is being researched for practical energy.
  • Assuming all acids turn blue litmus red: Only acids do this. Bases turn red litmus blue. Some substances (like distilled water) don't change litmus color — they are neutral.
  • Forgetting that weightlessness in orbit is due to free fall, not absence of gravity: Astronauts experience microgravity because both they and the spacecraft are in continuous free fall toward Earth.
  • Thinking renewable energy sources produce zero emissions: Solar/wind are clean during operation but manufacturing and disposal have environmental costs. Biofuels produce CO₂ when burned.
  • Confusing greenhouse gases: CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, CFCs, and water vapor are greenhouse gases. N₂ and O₂ (major atmospheric components) are NOT greenhouse gases.
  • Assuming deficiency diseases only affect developing countries: Vitamin D deficiency (rickets/osteomalacia) is common even in developed countries due to indoor lifestyles and sunscreen use.
  • Thinking all mutations are harmful: Most mutations are neutral or beneficial. Only some cause disease. Mutation is the source of all genetic variation and evolution.
  • Confusing X-rays and gamma rays origin: X-rays come from electron transitions outside nucleus. Gamma rays come from nuclear transitions. Both are electromagnetic radiation; gamma rays have higher frequency/energy.
