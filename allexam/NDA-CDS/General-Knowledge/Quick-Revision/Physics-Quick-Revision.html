NDA & CDS – General Knowledge: Physics Quick Revision

NDA & CDSEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Displacement: Vector quantity representing the shortest straight-line change in position from initial to final point, with direction. Measured in metres (m).
  • Speed vs Velocity: Speed is scalar (distance/time); velocity is vector (displacement/time). Average speed ≥ magnitude of average velocity. Instantaneous velocity magnitude equals instantaneous speed.
  • Acceleration: Rate of change of velocity with time, a = dv/dt. Uniform acceleration means equal changes in velocity in equal time intervals (e.g., free fall g = 9.8 m/s² downward).
  • Inertia: Inherent property of a body to resist change in its state of rest or uniform straight-line motion. Quantified by mass; greater mass → greater inertia.
  • Momentum: Product of mass and velocity, p = mv (kg·m/s). A vector quantity; conserved in isolated systems during collisions.
  • Work: Scalar product of force and displacement, W = F·s·cosθ. Zero when force ⊥ displacement (e.g., centripetal force does no work in uniform circular motion). Measured in joules (J).
  • Power: Rate of doing work or transferring energy, P = W/t. Measured in watts (W = J/s). Average power over interval; instantaneous power P = F·v.
  • Gravitational Field Strength (g): Force per unit mass at a point, g = GM/R². Varies with altitude and depth; zero at Earth's centre; independent of test mass.
  • Potential Energy: Energy stored due to position/configuration. Gravitational: PE = mgh (near surface) or −GMm/r (universal). Elastic: PE = ½kx².
  • Kinetic Energy: Energy of motion, KE = ½mv². For rotational motion, KE_rot = ½Iω². Related to momentum: KE = p²/2m.
  • Temperature (Kelvin): SI unit of thermodynamic temperature; 0 K is absolute zero. Relation: K = °C + 273.15. Temperature determines direction of heat flow.
  • Specific Heat Capacity: Heat required to raise 1 kg of substance by 1 K, Q = mcΔT. Unit: J/(kg·K). Water has high specific heat (4200 J/(kg·K)).
  • Latent Heat: Heat absorbed/released during phase change at constant temperature. Q = mL, where L is latent heat (fusion/vaporisation). No temperature change during phase transition.
  • Refractive Index: n = c/v = sin i / sin r (Snell's law). Ratio of speed of light in vacuum to speed in medium. Always ≥ 1. Determines bending of light at interface.
  • Focal Length (Mirror/Lens): Distance from pole/optical centre to principal focus. For spherical mirror: f = R/2. Sign conventions apply (Cartesian sign convention).
  • Electric Current: Rate of flow of charge, I = Q/t. SI unit: ampere (A). Direction: conventional current flows positive → negative; electron flow is opposite.
  • Resistance: Opposition to current flow, R = V/I (Ohm's law). Depends on material (resistivity ρ), length l, area A: R = ρl/A. Unit: ohm (Ω).
  • Electromagnetic Induction: Production of induced emf when magnetic flux through a coil changes. Faraday's law: ε = −dΦ/dt. Lenz's law gives direction (opposes cause).
  • Radioactivity: Spontaneous emission of radiation from unstable nuclei. Three types: α (He nucleus, low penetration), β (electron/positron), γ (EM photon, highest penetration).
  • Half-life: Time taken for half the radioactive nuclei in a sample to decay. t₁/₂ = 0.693/λ, where λ is decay constant. Independent of external conditions.
  • Critical Angle: Angle of incidence in denser medium for which angle of refraction in rarer medium is 90°. sin C = 1/n (when rarer medium is air). Enables total internal reflection.
  • Photon: Quantum/packet of electromagnetic energy. E = hν = hc/λ. Zero rest mass; travels at c in vacuum. Explains photoelectric effect and Compton scattering.
  • Superconductor: Material with zero electrical resistance below a critical temperature Tc. Perfect diamagnetism (Meissner effect). Used in MRI, particle accelerators.
  • Sonar: Sound Navigation and Ranging. Uses reflection of ultrasonic waves in water. Distance = (speed of sound in water × time)/2.

Key concepts

  • Newton's First Law: A body remains at rest or uniform straight-line motion unless acted upon by unbalanced external force — this defines inertia. Newton's Second Law: Force equals rate of change of momentum; for constant mass, F = ma. This is the foundational equation linking dynamics to kinematics. Newton's Third Law: Action and reaction are equal, opposite, and act on different bodies — they never cancel each other because they act on separate objects.

    Impulse = change in momentum = F×Δt; useful in collision problems. Recoil velocity derived from conservation of momentum. Friction opposes relative motion; static friction is self-adjusting up to limiting value μ_s N; kinetic friction is roughly constant at μ_k N. Banking of roads provides horizontal component of normal reaction to supply centripetal force without friction dependence.
  • For uniformly accelerated motion along a straight line, three core equations apply:
    • v = u + at
    • s = ut + ½at²
    • v² = u² + 2as

    These hold ONLY when acceleration is constant (e.g., free fall ignoring air resistance, blocks sliding down smooth inclines).

    Key derivations: Average velocity = (u+v)/2; displacement = average velocity × time. For vertical projection: time of ascent = time of descent = u/g; total flight time = 2u/g; maximum height H = u²/2g. Projectile range R = u²sin2θ/g; maximum range at θ = 45°; R(θ) = R(90°−θ) for complementary angles.

    Velocity-time graph slope = acceleration; area under curve = displacement. Position-time graph slope = velocity.
  • Uniform circular motion: velocity magnitude constant but direction changes continuously toward centre → centripetal acceleration a_c = v²/r = ω²r. Centripetal force F_c = mv²/r directed towards centre (provided by tension, friction, gravity as situation demands). No centrifugal 'force' in inertial frames — it is a pseudo-force in rotating frames.

    Universal Gravitation: F = GMm/r²; g = GM/R² at Earth's surface. Variation of g:
    • With altitude h: g_h = g(1 − 2h/R) for h << R
    • With depth d: g_d = g(1 − d/R) (zero at Earth's centre)
    • Latitude variation: g equator < g poles (Earth's oblateness + rotation)

    Orbital velocity: v_o = √(gR) ≈ 7.9 km/s for near-surface orbit. Escape velocity: v_e = √(2gR) ≈ 11.2 km/s = √2 · v_o. Geostationary satellite: period = 24 h, orbit in equatorial plane at ~36,000 km altitude.
  • Work-Energy Theorem: Net work done on a body = change in its kinetic energy, W_net = ΔKE. This theorem holds regardless of whether forces are conservative or non-conservative.

    Conservative forces (gravity, electrostatic, elastic spring): work is path-independent; define potential energy; mechanical energy conserved if only conservative forces act.
    Non-conservative forces (friction, air drag): dissipate mechanical energy as heat; W_nc = ΔME.

    Elastic potential energy in spring: U = ½kx² where k is spring constant, x is extension/compression. In collisions: elastic → both KE and momentum conserved; inelastic → only momentum conserved; perfectly inelastic → bodies stick together, max KE loss.

    Power: P_avg = W/t; P_inst = F·v. For electric circuits P = VI = I²R = V²/R.
  • SHM occurs when restoring force ∝ negative displacement: F = −kx, giving a = −ω²x where ω = √(k/m). Key features:
    • Displacement: x = A cos(ωt + φ)
    • Velocity: v = ±ω√(A² − x²); maximum at mean position v_max = ωA
    • Acceleration: a = −ω²x; maximum at extremes a_max = ω²A
    • Period T = 2π√(m/k) for spring-mass system; T = 2π√(l/g) for simple pendulum (independent of mass and amplitude for small oscillations)

    Energy in SHM: KE = ½mω²(A² − x²), PE = ½mω²x², Total E = ½mω²A² (constant). At mean position KE max, PE min; at extremes KE=0, PE max.
  • Zeroth Law: If A is in thermal equilibrium with B, and B with C, then A is in thermal equilibrium with C — basis for temperature measurement.

    First Law: ΔQ = ΔU + ΔW (energy conservation). For gas: heat supplied = increase in internal energy + work done by gas. Special cases:
    • Isothermal (T const): ΔU = 0, Q = W
    • Adiabatic (Q = 0): ΔU = −W, PV^γ = const
    • Isobaric (P const): W = PΔV
    • Isochoric (V const): W = 0, Q = ΔU

    Second Law (two statements — equivalent):
    Kelvin-Planck: No engine can convert全部 heat from a reservoir entirely into work.
    Clausius: Heat cannot spontaneously flow cold → hot without external work.

    Heat engines: η = W/Q_H = 1 − Q_C/Q_H. Carnot engine (ideal): η = 1 − T_C/T_H (temperatures in Kelvin). Efficiency always < 1.

    Entropy: measure of disorder/randomness; total entropy of isolated system never decreases (ΔS ≥ 0).
  • Sound is longitudinal mechanical wave requiring medium. Speed in medium depends on elasticity and density: v = √(B/ρ) for fluids, v = √(Y/ρ) for solids.

    Speed of sound in air: v = 331 + 0.6T m/s (T in °C). Increases with humidity, decreases with molecular weight. Does NOT depend on pressure.

    Reflection and refraction of sound follow same laws as light. Echo: minimum distance for distinct echo ≈ 17 m (human ear persistence ~0.1 s).

    Doppler Effect: Apparent frequency shift due to relative motion between source and observer.
    f' = f(v ± v_o)/(v ∓ v_s). Approaching → higher frequency; receding → lower frequency. Applications: radar, medical ultrasound, astronomy (redshift).

    SONAR uses ultrasonic pulses in water. Medical USG, lithotripsy use focused ultrasound.

    Noise pollution threshold: sustained exposure >80 dB causes hearing damage. Damping reduces amplitude of vibrations.
  • Reflection (plane mirror): image virtual, erect, same size, laterally inverted, distance behind mirror = object distance in front.
    Spherical mirrors: 1/f = 1/v + 1/u (sign convention: distances measured from pole; real-is-positive convention). Magnification m = −v/u. Concave mirror: converging (real focus); convex: diverging (virtual focus).

    Refraction: Snell's Law n₁sinθ₁ = n₂sinθ₂. Ray bends towards normal entering denser medium, away from rarer. Apparent depth: d' = d/n (object in denser appears raised).

    Total Internal Reflection (TIR): occurs when light travels dense→rare and angle of incidence > critical angle. Conditions: (i) light in denser medium, (ii) i > C. Applications: optical fibres, mirage, diamond sparkle, prism binoculars.

    Thin lens formula: 1/f = 1/v − 1/u (Cartesian sign convention). Lens maker's equation: 1/f = (n−1)(1/R₁ − 1/R₂). Power of lens P = 1/f (metres) in dioptres (D).
  • Human eye: cornea provides most refraction; iris controls pupil size; crystalline lens changes shape (accommodation) via ciliary muscles for near/far vision. Near point (closest distinct vision) ≈ 25 cm for normal eye; far point = ∞. Persistence of vision ≈ 1/16 s basis for film/TV.

    Defects of vision:
    Myopia (short-sighted): far point nearer than ∞; corrected by concave lens.
    Hypermetropia (long-sighted): near point farther than 25 cm; corrected by convex lens.
    Presbyopia: age-related loss of accommodation; bifocal lens.
    Astigmatism: unequal curvature of cornea; cylindrical lens.

    Microscope: two convex lenses (objective short focal length, eyepiece longer). Magnification M = (v_o/u_o)(1 + D/f_e) for final image at near point.
    Telescope: objective large focal length, eyepiece small focal length. Angular magnification M = f_o/f_e (normal adjustment, image at infinity). Refracting vs reflecting (Newtonian) telescopes — reflectors avoid chromatic aberration and can be made much larger.
  • Ohm's Law: V = IR for ohmic conductors at constant temperature. Non-ohmic devices (filament lamp, diode) do not obey linear V-I relation.

    Resistor combinations: Series R = R₁ + R₂ + …; Parallel 1/R = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + …

    Electrical energy: E = VIt = I²Rt = V²t/R (joules). 1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J (commercial billing unit).

    Heating effect: Joule's law Q = I²Rt. Fuse wire: high resistivity, low melting point, placed in series.

    Magnetic effect of current: Moving charge produces magnetic field. Right-hand thumb rule, Fleming's left-hand rule (force on conductor), right-hand grip rule (field around wire).

    Force on moving charge: F = qvBsinθ; on current-carrying conductor: F = BIl sinθ. Maximum at θ = 90°.

    Electromagnetic induction: Changing magnetic flux through coil induces emf (Faraday). Lenz's law: induced current opposes change causing it (energy conservation basis). AC generator: rotating coil in magnetic field produces sinusoidal emf. Transformer steps voltage up/down; works only with AC; efficiency < 100%.
  • Atomic models: Thomson (plum pudding) → Rutherford (nuclear model, α-scattering experiment proved dense nucleus) → Bohr (quantised orbits, E_n = −13.6/n² eV for hydrogen). Bohr explained hydrogen spectrum; lines: Lyman (UV, n→1), Balmer (visible, n→2), Paschen (IR, n→3).

    Radioactivity: Natural phenomenon, unaffected by chemical/physical conditions. α-particle: ⁴₂He nucleus, +2e charge, low penetration (paper stops), high ionising power. β-particle: high-speed electron/positron, moderate penetration (aluminium), moderate ionisation. γ-ray: EM radiation, no mass/charge, highest penetration (thick lead/concrete needed), lowest ionisation.

    Nuclear reactions: Fission (heavy nucleus splits into lighter, releases energy — used in reactors/bombs); Fusion (light nuclei combine at extreme T — powers Sun/stars, H-bomb). Mass defect Δm converted to energy: E = Δmc².

    Binding energy per nucleon peaks at ⁵⁶Fe (~8.8 MeV) — most stable nucleus. Curve explains why fission releases energy (heavy → mid) and fusion releases energy (light → mid).
  • Conductors: valence band overlaps conduction band (metals). Insulators: large band gap (>5 eV). Semiconductors: small band gap (~1 eV for Si, ~0.7 eV for Ge) — conductivity increases with temperature (opposite of metals).

    Intrinsic semiconductor: pure crystal, equal electrons and holes. Extrinsic: doped.
    N-type: pentavalent dopant (P, As) → excess electrons (majority carriers).
    P-type: trivalent dopant (B, Al) → excess holes (majority carriers).

    p-n junction diode: forward bias (p connected +ve) → current flows after ~0.7 V (Si) threshold; reverse bias → negligible current until breakdown.

    Transistor (BJT): three regions — emitter, base, collector. NPN or PNP. Acts as amplifier (small base current controls large collector current, β = I_c/I_b) or switch.

    Logic gates: AND (Y = A·B), OR (Y = A+B), NOT (Y = Ā); NAND and NOR are universal gates (can build any gate from them). Truth tables essential.
  • Order by increasing frequency/decreasing wavelength: Radio waves < Microwaves < Infrared < Visible < Ultraviolet < X-rays < Gamma rays.

    Radio waves: broadcasting, communication. Microwaves: radar, microwave ovens (water resonance), satellite communication.
    Infrared: heat therapy, remote controls, night vision, greenhouse effect.
    Visible: 400–700 nm; violet highest freq, red longest wavelength. Rainbow, dispersion by prism (VIBGYOR).
    Ultraviolet: causes vitamin D synthesis, sterilization, fluorescence; ozone layer absorption critical.
    X-rays: high penetration, medical imaging, crystallography; discovered by Röntgen. Hard X-rays more penetrating than soft.
    Gamma rays: highest energy, nuclear transitions, cancer treatment (radiotherapy), sterilization.

    All EM waves travel at c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s in vacuum; no medium needed; transverse waves; carry momentum p = E/c = h/λ.
  • SI base units: metre (m), kilogram (kg), second (s), ampere (A), kelvin (K), mole (mol), candela (cd). All other units derived from these.

    Common derived units: newton N = kg·m/s²; pascal Pa = N/m²; joule J = N·m; watt W = J/s; coulomb C = A·s; volt V = J/C; ohm Ω = V/A; weber Wb = V·s; tesla T = Wb/m².

    Dimensional formulas (high-yield):
    • Work/Energy/Heat: [ML²T⁻²]
    • Power: [ML²T⁻³]
    • Pressure/Stress: [ML⁻¹T⁻²]
    • Momentum: [MLT⁻¹]
    • Force: [MLT⁻²]
    • Gravitational constant G: [M⁻¹L³T⁻²]
    • Planck's constant h: [ML²T⁻¹]
    • Surface tension: [MT⁻²]
    • Viscosity: [ML⁻¹T⁻¹]

    Dimensional analysis checks equation correctness, derives relations, converts units. Cannot determine dimensionless constants or handle equations with additive terms of different dimensions.

Important facts

  • Speed of light in vacuum: c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s (exact). Refractive index n = c/v, so v in medium = c/n. — Foundational constant appearing in nearly every modern physics and optics question.
  • Escape velocity from Earth: v_e ≈ 11.2 km/s. Orbital velocity (near surface): v_o ≈ 7.9 km/s. Relation: v_e = √2 · v_o. — Direct MCQ in NDA/CDS; know the numerical values and the √2 relationship.
  • Acceleration due to gravity at Earth's surface: g ≈ 9.8 m/s² (often 10 m/s² in MCQ options). g decreases with altitude and depth; maximum at poles, minimum at equator. — Standard GK fact; depth-altitude variations frequently tested.
  • Planck's constant h = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s. Einstein's photoelectric equation: hν = φ₀ + KE_max, where φ₀ = hν₀ (work function). — Photoelectric effect is important for modern physics section.
  • De Broglie wavelength: λ = h/p = h/mv. Applies to all matter; significant only for microscopic particles (electrons, protons). — Wave-particle duality concept frequently asked.
  • Nuclear fusion powers the Sun: 4¹H → ⁴He + 2e⁺ + 2ν + energy (~26.7 MeV). Temperature at solar core ≈ 15 million K. — Current affairs link (astrophysics) and conceptual link to NDA physics.
  • Resistance temperature dependence: R = R₀(1 + αΔT). For metals, α > 0 (resistance increases with T). For semiconductors, α < 0 (resistance decreases with T). — Distinguishes metallic vs semiconductor behaviour — common MCQ trap.
  • Transformer relation: V_s/V_p = N_s/N_p = I_p/I_s (ideal). Step-up: V_s > V_p, I_s < I_p. Step-down: vice versa. Losses: copper, iron, eddy current, hysteresis. — AC circuits and power transmission topic; very common in NDA/CDS.
  • Total internal reflection critical angle condition: light must travel from denser to rarer medium. C = sin⁻¹(n_rare/n_dense). For glass-air: C ≈ 42°. For water-air: C ≈ 49°. — Direct MCQ on critical angle values and TIR conditions.
  • Sound speed in steel ≈ 5000 m/s, in water ≈ 1500 m/s, in air ≈ 340 m/s (at 20°C). Sound cannot travel in vacuum; light can. — Comparison-based MCQ: which medium transmits sound fastest? Why astronauts use radio in space?
  • Nuclear reactor components: fuel (uranium-235/plutonium-239), moderator (graphite/heavy water slows neutrons), control rods (cadmium/boron absorb neutrons), coolant (water/liquid sodium), shielding (concrete/lead). — Defence/GK relevance; nuclear energy questions in NDA/CDS GK.
  • Laser: Light Amplification by Stimulated Emission of Radiation. Properties: coherent, monochromatic, highly directional, high intensity. Applications: surgery, holography, fibre communication, welding, barcode scanners. — Frequently asked definition/application question.
  • Fusion vs Fission comparison:
    • Fusion: light nuclei combine, huge energy, fuel abundant, no long-lived waste, requires extreme T, not yet commercial.
    • Fission: heavy nucleus splits, less energy per reaction, fuel有限, radioactive waste, commercial reactors exist. — Comparison-type MCQ likely in NDA/CDS.
  • Kirchhoff's laws: Junction rule (ΣI_in = ΣI_out, based on charge conservation). Loop rule (ΣV = 0 around closed loop, based on energy conservation). — Circuit analysis fundamental; may appear in calculation-based MCQs.
  • Lenz's law consequence: induced current direction is such that it opposes the change in magnetic flux that produced it. Energy conservation basis — without opposition, perpetual motion would occur. — Conceptual MCQ on direction of induced current.
  • Radioactive decay law: N = N₀e^(−λt). Activity A = λN = A₀e^(−λt). Relationship: t₁/₂ = ln2/λ ≈ 0.693/λ. — Half-life calculations often feature in numerical MCQs.

Memory aids

  • VITE: v = u + at; S = ut + ½at²; v² = u² + 2as; Equations only for CONSTANT acceleration. — Kinematics
  • Right-hand thumb rule (field around wire): thumb = current, curled fingers = magnetic field direction. Fleming's Left Hand (motor): Thumb=Force, Forefinger=Field, Centre finger=Current. — Magnetism
  • Ohm's triangle: V = IR. Power triangle: P = VI. Energy triangle: E = VIt = I²Rt = V²t/R. — Electricity
  • Lens/mirror sign convention (Cartesian): incident light direction = +ve. Object distance u negative (left of pole). Focal length: concave mirror/negative lens = −f; convex mirror/positive lens = +f. Image distance v: +ve = right (real), −ve = left (virtual). — Optics
  • SHM mnemonics: 'a is against x' (a = −ω²x). 'Energy sloshes between KE and PE' — at extremes KE=0, PE=max; at centre KE=max, PE=0. T pendulum is AMPLITUDE-independent (for small angles only!). — Oscillations
  • EM spectrum memory phrase: 'Really Mindful Indians Very Unusually eXamine Gamma rays' (Radio, Microwave, Infrared, Visible, UV, X-ray, Gamma). Increasing energy, decreasing wavelength. — Modern Physics
  • Photoelectric effect facts: (1) Threshold frequency ν₀ exists — below it, NO emission regardless of intensity. (2) KE_max depends on frequency, NOT intensity. (3) Number of photoelectrons ∝ intensity. Einstein's equation resolved the classical wave paradox. — Modern Physics
  • α β γ penetration order: Paper stops α, aluminium stops β, thick lead/concrete stops γ. Ionising power inverse: α strongest ioniser, γ weakest. Charge: α (+2e), β (−e/+e), γ (0). — Nuclear Physics

Exam tips

  • NDA/CDS Physics GK questions test CONCEPTS, not heavy calculations. Focus on understanding 'why' — e.g., why sky is blue (Rayleigh scattering), why stars twinkle (atmospheric refraction), why sun appears red at sunrise (scattering of blue). — Apply to all current-affairs-linked physics questions, especially defence-tech applications.
  • Memorise key numerical values: c = 3×10⁸ m/s, g = 9.8 m/s², escape velocity = 11.2 km/s, orbital velocity = 7.9 km/s, speed of sound in air ≈ 340 m/s, refractive index of water ≈ 1.33, glass ≈ 1.5. These frequently appear as options. — Eliminate wrong options using known magnitudes without full calculation.
  • Defence-application linkage: Every physics topic has a defence relevance — radar (EM waves), sonar (sound), night vision (IR), laser (coherent light), nuclear weapons (fission/fusion), communication (radio/ microwave), stealth (RAM coating absorbs radar). Review these links. — Questions often ask 'which phenomenon is used in X defence application?' — match concept to application.
  • Dimensions and units questions: Practice dimensional analysis for 5–6 key quantities (G, h, c, e, k_e). Know which pair has same dimensions: [Work and Energy], [Torque and Energy], [Impulse and Momentum]. — Dimensional matching eliminates options quickly in time-constrained exams.
  • Optics sign convention: Most NDA/CDS questions use the 'new Cartesian' convention. Learn: object always on left (u negative), real image on right (v positive for lenses, negative for mirrors), virtual on left. Practice with 3 standard cases each for convex/concave lens and mirror. — Avoid sign errors in lens/mirror formula questions. Draw ray diagrams to verify answer intuition.
  • Modern Physics priority order for NDA/CDS: (1) Photoelectric effect (Einstein's equation, stopping potential, threshold frequency) → (2) Radioactivity (α/β/γ properties, half-life) → (3) Nuclear reactions (fission/fusion differences, binding energy curve) → (4) Bohr model (hydrogen spectrum series names). Modern Physics carries disproportionate weight. — Allocate study time accordingly — this section is high-yield with relatively less syllabus.
  • Elimination strategy: Many Physics GK questions have clearly wrong options based on orders of magnitude or sign. E.g., if asked for orbital velocity and options include 11.2 km/s (escape velocity) and 7.9 km/s, knowing the √2 relation immediately helps. — Use approximate values and relationships rather than precise calculation to save time.
  • Common NDA/CDS question patterns: Assertion-Reason type frequently appears. Typical trap: Assertion true but reason false, or reason true but doesn't explain assertion. Always verify BOTH statements independently before checking the link. — Practice assertion-reason questions specifically — this format is distinctive to UPSC exams.

Common traps

  • Confusing distance and displacement: distance is scalar (total path length); displacement is vector (shortest change in position). In one complete circle, displacement = 0 but distance = 2πr. Average speed ≠ average velocity magnitude generally. — Read the question word carefully — 'distance covered' vs 'displacement'. For round trips, displacement can be zero while distance is non-zero.
  • Thinking centrifugal force is a real force in inertial frame: In an inertial frame, only centripetal (inward) force exists. Centrifugal 'force' appears only in rotating (non-inertial) reference frames as a pseudo-force. — In NDA/CDS questions, always assume inertial frame unless specified. The force keeping a car turning is friction directed inward (centripetal), not outward.
  • Weightlessness means no gravity: Astronauts in orbit experience weightlessness because they are in free fall — gravity IS acting (providing centripetal acceleration), but there's no normal reaction force from floor. — g at ISS altitude (~400 km) is still ~8.7 m/s², about 90% of surface value. Weightlessness is due to continuous free-fall, not absence of gravity.
  • Potential energy is always positive: Gravitational PE (universal form) is negative: U = −GMm/r. Only near-surface PE (mgh) is taken positive with reference at surface. Elastic PE (½kx²) is always positive. — Sign of PE depends on reference point chosen. In orbital mechanics, total energy E = −GMm/2r is negative for bound orbits.
  • Specific heat and heat capacity are same: Specific heat capacity is per unit mass (J/kg·K); heat capacity is for the whole object (J/K). C = mc where c = specific heat capacity. — Check units. Large bodies have large heat capacity even with same specific heat. Water has high specific heat → resists temperature change.
  • Convex mirror forms only virtual images: Convex mirrors ALWAYS produce virtual, erect, diminished images regardless of object position. Never form real images. Concave mirrors can form real OR virtual depending on object position. — Convex mirror: v always positive (behind mirror) → virtual. Used as rear-view mirrors (wide field of view, diminished image gives sense of distance). Concave mirror: real image when object beyond F.
  • Increasing intensity increases photoelectron energy: In photoelectric effect, intensity affects NUMBER of electrons emitted, not their maximum kinetic energy. KE_max depends solely on frequency (via hν − φ₀). — Higher intensity → more photons → more electrons ejected, but each electron's energy still limited by individual photon energy hν. This was the key evidence against classical wave theory.
  • Radioactive half-life can be altered: Half-life is a nuclear property — unaffected by temperature, pressure, chemical state, or physical conditions. Only nuclear reactions can change it. — Half-life is intrinsic to the isotope. A sample of ¹⁴C will always decay with t₁/₂ ≈ 5730 years whether in wood, CO₂, or living tissue.
  • Transformer works with DC: Transformers require changing magnetic flux, which only AC provides. DC produces constant flux → no induced emf in secondary (except during switching on/off transients). — Transformers ONLY work with AC. For DC voltage conversion, electronic inverters/converters are used instead.
  • Resistance depends only on material: R = ρl/A shows resistance depends on resistivity (material), length, AND cross-sectional area. Doubling length doubles R; doubling diameter quarters R (area ∝ d²). — For given material and temperature, R ∝ l/A. Wire stretched to double length → R becomes 4R (volume constant, area halves). Common numerical trap.
  • Sound travels faster in denser media always: Sound speed v = √(B/ρ). While solids are denser than gases, their bulk modulus B is MUCH larger, so v solid > v liquid > v gas. But between two solids/liquids, higher density alone doesn't guarantee slower speed — elasticity matters more. — Order: steel (~5000 m/s) > water (~1500 m/s) > air (~340 m/s). Remember: sound needs medium, light doesn't.
  • Fusion and fission are same energy source: Both release energy from mass conversion (E = Δmc²), but fusion combines light nuclei (H→He in stars) while fission splits heavy nuclei (U→Ba+Kr in reactors). Fusion yields more energy per nucleon but requires extreme temperatures. — Fusion: Sun, H-bomb, experimental tokamaks. Fission: nuclear reactors, A-bombs. Different mechanisms, same mass-energy principle.
