Quick Revision: Geography of India and the World — NDA & CDS

NDA & CDSEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Geography is the systematic study of the Earth's physical features, climate, resources, and human activity.
  • Drainage pattern: The arrangement of rivers and their tributaries in a particular area, reflecting the underlying topography and geology.
  • Interfluvial: The area between two adjacent river systems.
  • Alluvium: Soil deposited by rivers, rich in nutrients, dominant in the Indo-Gangetic plains.
  • Laterite: Iron-rich soil formed in hot and wet tropical areas through intense leaching.
  • Black soil (Regur): Volcanic origin, high in lime, iron, magnesia; retains moisture well; ideal for cotton.
  • Alluvial soil: Most widespread soil in India, classified as Khadar (new alluvium) and Bhangar (older alluvium).
  • Pedocal/Podzol: Soils developed under cool, moist climates with accumulation of silica and aluminium oxides.
  • Red soil: Rich in iron oxide, low in nitrogen and humus; found in southern peninsula.
  • Arid soil: Sandy, saline, low water-holding capacity; found in Rajasthan and western India.
  • Peat soil: Organic-rich, found in marshy areas like Kerala and Tamil Nadu.

Key concepts

  • Physiographic Divisions of India: India is divided into five major physiographic units: (1) The Northern Mountains (Himalayas) — young fold mountains, three ranges: Himadri (Greater), Himachal (Lesser), Shiwaliks (Outer). Height average 6,000 m, highest peak K2 (Godwin Austen, 8,611 m) in Pakistan-occupied Kashmir; in India, Kanchenjunga (8,598 m) is highest. (2) Northern Plains — formed by Indus-Ganga-Brahmaputra system, ~2,400 km long, 240–320 km wide, alluvial deposits. Divided into Bhabar (porous, streams disappear), Terai (marshy, waterlogged), Bhangar (older alluvium), Khadar (new alluvium). (3) Peninsular Plateau — oldest landmass, composed of igneous and metamorphic rocks. Includes Malwa, Chota Nagpur, Marathwada, Rayalsima, Coorg, Nilgiri regions. Contains the Dharwar rock system (oldest), Deccan Traps (volcanic basalt). (4) Coastal Plains — Western Coastal Plain (Konkan, Karwar to Kannur; Kannur's backwaters; Lakshadweep islands are coral origins); Eastern Coastal Plain (Coromandel, Magdalena Bay, Chilika Lake, Mahanadi delta). (5) Islands — Lakshadweep (coral, Arabian Sea), Andaman and Nicobar (tectonic, Bay of Bengal). Western Ghats (Sahyadri): continuous, height 1,000–1,600 m, highest Anamudi (2,695 m). Eastern Ghats: discontinuous, lowest at Nilgiris where they meet Western Ghats.
  • River Systems of India: India's rivers are classified into Himalayan and Peninsular. Himalayan rivers are perennial (snowmelt-fed): Indus system (originating near Mansarovar, flows through Ladakh, confluences: Zaskar-Satyaji, Chenab from Chandra-Bhaga; 5 jhelum, 5 chenab, 5 ravi, 5 beas, 5 satluj — the 'Five Rivers of Punjab'); Ganga system (Bhagirathi + Alaknanda at Devprayag, Bhagirathi is longer headstream but Alaknanda is higher volume — Gangotri glacier source; Yamuna joins at Allahabad, Gomti, Ghaghara, Gandak, Kosi 'sorrow of Bihar', Son join); Brahmaputra (Yarlung Tsangpo in Tibet, enters India through Arunachal as Dihang, joins Lohit and Subansiri, becomes Brahmaputra in Assam, Teesta joins, Sangu in Bangladesh; final confluence with Ganga-Bhagirathi as Meghna in delta). Peninsular rivers: Godavari (longest peninsular river, 1,465 km, originates Trimbakeshwar, flows into Bay of Bengal, Lakshman Ganga tributary), Krishna (4th longest peninsular, 1,300 km, originates Mahabaleshwar), Kaveri (1st long peninsular river fully within India, 765 km, originates Brahmagiri, delta in Tamil Nadu), Narmada (flows west through rift valley, forms Dhaman-Dholar falls, Gulf of Khambhat; Tapti parallels it, Mahi also west-flowing). East-flowing peninsular rivers: Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri feed the Coromandel coast. West-flowing: Narmada, Tapti. Peninsular rivers are rain-fed, seasonal, non-perennial. Key difference: Himalayan rivers carry high silt load, erode valleys (V-shaped); peninsular rivers flow over stable crust, wider valleys, less erosion.
  • Climate of India: India's climate is classified as 'monsoon type' (Koeppen). The monsoon mechanism: differential heating creates low pressure over Tibetan plateau and high pressure over Indian Ocean; southwest monsoon blows from Sea to land (June–September). Jet streams: Subtropical westerly jet stream south of Himalayas pushes monsoon in; Tropical easterly jet stream overpeninsula in July-August triggers post-monsoon rainfall in TN. Retreating monsoon (Oct–Nov): NE winds, TN receives most rainfall from retreating SW monsoon (Coromandel coast). Rainfall distribution: Western ghats (windward side) receive >250 cm; Assam (Garo-Khasi hills orographic) >400 cm; Rajasthan (rain shadow of W.G.) <50 cm. Temperature: highest in Thar desert (45°C+); least in Ladakh. Seasonal classification: (1) Cold weather (Dec-Feb, NW rainfall), (2) Hot weather (Mar-May, loo winds, pre-monsoon showers in Kerala/Karnataka), (3) Southwest monsoon (Jun-Sep), (4) Retreating monsoon (Oct-Nov). Cyclones: Bay of Bengal cyclones Oct-Nov (more frequent); Arabian Sea fewer. El Niño weakens monsoon (drought), La Nina strengthens. Indian Meteorological Department (IMD) classifies monsoon as 'normal' (96-104% of Long Period Average of 89 cm), 'above normal' (>104%), 'below normal' (<96%). Famine code and drought classification used by Govt.
  • Soils of India: Classification by distribution: (1) Alluvial — 43% of area, Indo-Gangetic plain + coastal deltas, Khadar (fertile, new) vs Bhangar (older, calcareous kankar nodules). (2) Black/Regur — 15%, Deccan trap region (MH, MP, Gujarat), high clay content, crack when dry (self-ploughing), ideal for cotton — called 'black cotton soil'. (3) Red — 20%, eastern and southern peninsula, iron oxide gives red colour, low fertility, needs manure. (4) Laterite — 8%, high rainfall areas of W.Ghats, NE, TN, Kerala — leached soils, brick-like when dried, used in construction. (5) Arid — 11%, Rajasthan, sandy, saline, low organic matter. (6) Forest/mountain — Himalayan regions, varied by altitude. (7) Peat — marshy areas, organic-rich, alkaline. Soil erosion causes: deforestation, overgrazing, improper irrigation (waterlogging-salinisation in Punjab-Haryana), terracing absent in hilly areas. Control measures: contour ploughing, terracing, shelter belts, afforestation, check dams. Soil degradation is serious in Punjab-Haryana due to over-irrigation causing salinity and alkalinity.
  • Natural Vegetation and Forests: Classification by rainfall: (1) Tropical evergreen — >250 cm rainfall, Western Ghats, NE India, Assam; dense canopy, no leaf shedding, rosewood, ebony, mahogany; hard to exploit. (2) Tropical deciduous (monsoon forests) — 100-250 cm, deodar, sal, teak, neem — most economically important; moist deciduous (higher rain) vs dry deciduous. (3) Thorn and scrub — <75 cm, Rajasthan, arid; acacia, cassia, xerophytic. (4) Tidal (Mangrove) — Ganga-Brahmaputra delta, Sundarbans, Sunderbans = 'Sundari' trees, royal Bengal tiger habitat. (5) Mountain/Alpine — Himalayan altitude-based: subtropical pine (Chir Pine ~1,000 m), oaks and chestnuts (~1,500-2,000 m), deodar and fir (~2,000-3,000 m), juniper and rhododendron (~3,000 m), alpine grassland above 3,500 m, snowline above 4,500-5,000 m.Forest coverage: ~24.6% of India's geographical area (ISFR 2021). Important forest types by IUCN classification in India include subtropical, tropical, montane. Projects: Project Tiger (1973), Project Elephant (1992), Project Crocodile (1975). Biosphere reserves: Nilgiri, Nanda Devi, Gulf of Mannar, Sundarbans, Pachmarhi, Great Nicobar, Similipal, Manas, Gangotri, Agasthyamala, Khangchendzonga. National Parks >100; Wildlife Sanctuaries >500.
  • Agriculture in India: Types: Subsistence (small holdings, low input), Commercial (cash crops for market), Plantation (tea, coffee, rubber — labour-intensive). Major crops: (1) Food crops — Rice (Kharif, lowland, >100 cm rain, W.B., Punjab, AP, UP, TN, Kerala; two varieties: Aus, Aman, Boro; India is 2nd largest producer after China); Wheat (Rabi, cool growing season, bright sun at ripening, >75 cm, NW India — PB, HP, UP, GN; Green Revolution centres: Ludhiana, Karnal, Muzaffarnagar); Millets (Ragi, Jowar, Bajra — dryland crops, low rainfall, Rajasthan, Karnataka, MH). (2) Cash crops — Sugarcane (UP 1st, PB, MH, TN, AP; needs 21-27°C, 75 cm); Cotton (black soil, 21-30°C, frost-free 210 days; PB, MH, GJ, TN); Jute (Ganga-Brahmaputra delta, W.B. dominates, 'Golden Fibre'); Tea (Assam, Darjeeling, Nilgiri, Dooars, Terai — 20-30°C, frost-free, well-drained slopes); Coffee (Karnataka—Coorg, Chikmagalur, Nilgiri — Arabica & Robusta). Seasons: Kharif (Jun-Oct, rice, millets, cotton, jute, sugarcane); Rabi (Oct-Mar, wheat, barley, mustard, gram); Zaid (Mar-Jun, short-duration crops like cucumber, melon). Agricultural regions: Indo-Gangetic (wheat-rice), Peninsula (ragi-jowar-cotton-coffee), North-West (wheat-cotton-sugarcane), NE (rice-tea), Central (millets-cotton). Issues: Fragmented holdings, low yield, water scarcity, MSP problems, lack of tech adoption, farmer suicides.
  • Mineral and Energy Resources: Minerals: (1) Iron ore — Gondwana belt (Dhanbad-Jharkhand, Keonjhar-Odia, Bellary-Karnataka); Hematite (best quality), magnetite (strongest magnetic); India is major exporter. (2) Coal — Gondwana age (Damodar valley: Jharia, Raniganj, Bokaro; Godavari valley; Neyveli-TN); Coking coal for steel, non-coking for thermal; India has large reserves but imports premium coking coal. (3) Manganese — essential for steel, Odisha, Maharashtra, MP. (4) Bauxite — lateritic origin, Odisha (largest), Guinea equivalent, Gujarat (Kandla). (5) Copper — Singhbhum-Jharkhand, Palana-Rajasthan. (6) Gold — Kolar-Karnataka, Hutti, Narwapur. (7) Limestone — cement, Chota Nagpur, MP. (8) Petroleum — Mumbai High (offshore, largest producer),迪吉 (Assam, oldest), Cambay (Gujarat); ONGC is operator. (9) Natural gas — KG basin (Reliance), KG-D6 field. Energy: Thermal (coal-based, ~70% electricity), Nuclear (Tata, Bhabha Atomic Research Centre — Tarapur, Kudankulam, Kaiga, Rawatbhata), Renewable — Solar (Gujarat, Rajasthan, Tamil Nadu — largest parks), Wind (Tamil Nadu, Maharashtra, Gujarat), Hydro (Bhakra-Nangal, Hirakud, Nagarjunsagar, Sardar Sarovar, Koyna, Tehri). Power grid: Northern, Southern, Eastern, Western, North-Eastern. Rural electrification through ISGD scheme.
  • Population and Demographics: Census 2011 data (latest official): Total 1,210.87 million. Sex ratio: 943 females per 1000 males. Literacy: 74.04% (male 82.14%, female 65.46%). decadal growth rate: 17.64%. Density: 382 per sq km. States by population: UP most (199.8M), Bihar 2nd, Maharashtra 3rd. Smallest: Sikkim. Density highest: West Bengal (1,029/sq km), Bihar (1,102/sq km). SC population ~16.6%, ST ~8.6%. Urban population ~31.2%. Major migration: rural-to-urban, inter-state migration to Maharashtra, Delhi, Punjab, Haryana. Migration drivers: employment, marriage, education. Census 2021 delayed; provisional estimates suggest growth decelerating. Demographic dividend: India's working-age population (15-59 years) ~67% — opportunity if educated and employed. Challenges: Female sex ratio improving slowly, child sex ratio still concern, ageing population in some states, urbanisation pressure, slums, sanitation issues. National Population Policy 2000 targets: stabilisation by 2045, universal immunisation, TFR replacement level. Family welfare schemes: JSY, ANM, ASHA workers.
  • World Geography — Physical Features: Continents by area: Asia (44.58M km²), Africa, North America, South America, Antarctica, Europe, Australia/Oceania. Highest peak: Everest (8,849 m, Nepal-China border). Longest river: Nile (6,650 km) — debate with Amazon (6,400 km by some measurements); Amazon is largest by discharge. Largest desert: Sahara (9.2M km²), largest cold desert: Antarctic. Largest island: Greenland (2.16M km²), largest peninsula: Arabian. Deepest ocean trench: Mariana Trench (11,034 m, Pacific). Major mountain ranges: Alps (Europe, Matterhorn 4,478 m), Andes (longest, Aconcagua 6,961 m), Rockies, Himalayas, Urals (continent divide Europe-Asia), Apennines, Carpathians, Caucasus. Great lakes of N.America: Superior (largest freshwater), Huron, Michigan, Erie, Ontario. Important deserts: Kalahari (Botswana), Namib (Namibia), Thar (India-Pakistan), Gobi (China-Mongolia), Atacama (Chile — driest), Simpson (Australia). Important straits: Strait of Gibraltar (Atlantic-Mediterranean), Hormuz (Persian Gulf), Malacca (Indian-Pacific), Bosphorus/Dardanelles (Black-Sea), Dover (English Channel), Magellan (Tierra del Fuego).
  • World Geography — Climate and Ocean: Climate zones: Tropical (Af, Am, Aw), Dry (BWh, BWk, BSh, BSk), Temperate (Csa, Csb, Cfa, Cfb, Cfc), Continental (Dfa, Dfb, Dwc, Dwd), Polar (ET, EF). Mediterranean climate (Cs): hot dry summers, mild wet winters — California, Mediterranean basin, Cape Town, Perth, central Chile. Ocean currents affect climate: warm currents on east coasts of continents, cold on west coasts (mid-latitudes). Gulf Stream warms NW Europe; Labrador current chills SE Canada; Agulhas (warm, E.Africa), Benguela (cold, W.South Africa); Kuroshio (warm, Japan), Oyashio (cold). Tides: Spring tides (full moon/new moon, greatest range), Neap tides (quarter moons). Ocean belts: coral reefs (30°N-S, warm shallow water) — Great Barrier Reef (Australia); mangroves (tidal zones). Monsoons globally: Indian, African, Australian, North American (Sonoran). ENSO affects global climate: El Niño warms eastern Pacific, La Nina cools.
  • Important Locations and Maps: International date line (IDLoosely follows 180° meridian). Prime Meridian (0°) passes through Greenwich, London. Tropic of Cancer (23½°N) passes through: India (8 states — MH, CG, JH, WB, RP, UP, DL, MP, TL, BR); Myanmar, Bangladesh, China, Saudi Arabia, UAE, Oman, India, Mexico, Bahamas, Cuba, Haiti, Dominican Republic, Somalia. Tropic of Capricorn (23½°S): Chile, Argentina, Paraguay, Brazil, Namibia, Botswana, South Africa, Mozambique, Zimbabwe, Australia. Equator (0°): passes through 13 countries.   • Important Questions — NDA/CDS Specific: High-yield facts: (1) India's total geographical area: 3.287M km² (6th largest in world). Important facts

  • India's total geographical area: 3,287,263 km² (6th largest globally after Russia, Canada, China, USA, Brazil)
  • India's coastline: 7,516.6 km (mainland 5,422.6 km + island territories 2,094 km)
  • Latitudinal extent: 8°4′N to 37°6′N; longitudinal extent: 68°7′E to 97°25′E
  • K2 (Godwin Austen) is highest peak in the world (8,611 m) but in PoK; Kanchenjunga (8,598 m) is highest in India
  • Gangotri glacier is source of Bhagirathi; 'Gangotri' is sacred; Yamunotri source of Yamuna
  • Mawsynram (Meghalaya) receives the highest annual rainfall in India (~11,872 mm)
  • Narmada and Tapti are the only major peninsular rivers flowing west through rift valleys
  • Lakshadweep islands are of coral origin; Andaman and Nicobar are tectonic/volcanic
  • Indira Point (6°45′N) is the southernmost point of Indian territory (Great Nicobar)
  • Palk Strait separates India from Sri Lanka; Gulf of Mannar is between Palk Strait and Tamil Nadu coast
  • India's Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ): 2.02 million km² — 4th largest in the world
  • Great Andamanese, Onge, Jarawa, Sentinelese are the four indigenous tribes of Andaman
  • Nikhil Bhavan (Delhi) hosts the Parliament of India; Rashtrapati Bhavan is the President's residence
  • Sunderbans (W.B.) is the largest mangrove forest in the world and UNESCO World Heritage Site
  • Kaziranga National Park (Assam) is home to two-thirds of the world's one-horned rhinoceroses
  • Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve is the first Biosphere Reserve in India (1986)
  • India has 103 National Parks, 568 Wildlife Sanctuaries, and 29 Biosphere Reserves (as of recent data)
  • Silvassa (Dadra and Nagar Haveli) and Daman and Diu are UTs (merged in 2020 as Dadra-Nagar-Haveli and Daman and Diu)
  • Ladakh became a UT (Oct 31, 2019); J&K became a UT (same date)
  • Jammu and Kashmir reorganisation split: J&K UT + Ladakh UT (each with legislature and without respectively)

Memory aids

  • Rivers mnemonic — 'I Come Back Once More': Indus, Chenab, Ravi, Beas, Satluj, Brahmaputra — the 'Five Rivers' of Punjab plus Brahmaputra; think of them as five brothers (Panca) who become one family with Ganga and Brahmaputra at Prayagraj
  • Mountain ranges of the world (highest peaks): Everest (Asia/China-Nepal), Aconcagua (Andes/Argentina), Denali (North America/USA), Kilimanjaro (Africa/Tanzania), Elbrus (Europe/Russia), Vinson Massif (Antarctica), Kosciuszko (Australia)
  • Crops by season: Kharif = 'Khuraf' = rainy — Rice, Cotton, Jute, Millets, Sugarcane (think 'RCJMS'); Rabi = winter — Wheat, Barley, Mustard, Gram (think 'WBMG'); Zaid = summer — Watermelon, Cucumber, Melon, Leafy vegetables
  • Mineral belts mnemonic: 'Iron-Jharkhand-Odisha-Karnataka'; 'Coal-Jharia-Raniganj-Bokaro'; 'Manganese-Odisha-Maharashtra'; 'Bauxite-Odisha (largest producer)'; 'Petroleum-Mumbai High (offshore, largest)'
  • States through which Tropic of Cancer passes: 'Goa-Maharashtra-Chhattisgarh-Jharkhand-West Bengal-Rajasthan-Uttar Pradesh-Mizoram' — remember 'GM-CJWW-RUM'
  • Peninsular rivers draining west: NT (Narmada, Tapti) — remember 'NT stands apart'; East-flowing: MGCK (Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri) — remember 'MGCK pours into Bay'
  • Climate zones mnemonic for Koeppen: 'Aw' = tropical savanna (most of India); 'BWh' = hot desert; 'Cwa' = humid subtropical with dry winter (North India); 'EF' = ice cap (Antarctica, high Himalaya)
  • World trade routes (maritime): (1) Suez route (Europe-Asia via Mediterranean-Red Sea-Suez Canal-Persian Gulf/Arabian Sea); (2) Cape route (Cape of Good Hope); (3) Panama route (Americas)
  • Time zones: IST = UTC+5:30; GMT = UTC+0; JST (Japan) = UTC+9; EST (US East) = UTC-5; PST (US West) = UTC-8
  • Island groups: Lakshadweep (coral, Arabian Sea, 36 islands) vs Andaman-Nicobar (tectonic, Bay of Bengal, 572 islands); Nicobar group is closer to Sumatra

Exam tips

  • For NDA, focus on地图-based questions: locate places on blank map (rivers, hills, ports, national parks, important towns, earthquake zones). Practice labeling these from memory.
  • CDS GK geography section frequently asks about physical geography — mountains, rivers, climates, soils, vegetation — and their distribution. Use mnemonics and comparative tables for quick recall.
  • Don't confuse similar-sounding places: Kanara/Konkan, Nilgiri/Nallamala, Shevroy/Sathyamangalam, Khasi-Garo-Jaintia (Meghalaya hills), Palni-Wynad-Anaimalai (TN hill ranges).
  • For river questions, know: source, mouth, direction of flow, tributaries (left/right bank), basin area, major dams on the river. Indus: 5 main tributaries; Ganga: 4 major (Yamuna, Gomti, Ghaghara, Gandak); Brahmaputra: Lohit, Subansiri, Manas, Teesta.
  • Mineral questions — know which mineral is associated with which state (e.g., iron ore → Jharkhand-Odisha-Karnataka; coal → Jharia-Raniganj-Bokaro; bauxite → Odisha (largest); petroleum → Mumbai High-Assam).
  • Monsoon mechanism is highly tested. Know: onset dates (Kerala 1st June), withdrawal (NW India by end Nov), retreating monsoon (TN rainfall), Western Disturbances (winter rain in NW), Magonization concept.
  • Soil types — match soil to state and crop: Black soil→Cotton→MH, MP, GJ; Red→Ragi, groundnut→TN, AP, Karnataka; Laterite→Tea→Kerala, TN; Alluvial→Rice-Wheat→Punjab, UP, WB; Arid→Grapes, bajra→Rajasthan.
  • Important rivers — know which flow through rift valley (Narmada, Tapti), which form deltas (Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri, Mahanadi), which are tidal (Hooghly, Mahanadi), which are perennial vs seasonal.
  • Current affairs link — geography-related current affairs often appear: tsunamis, earthquakes (memorise seismic zones of India: Zone V = Himalayan, Zone IV = Peninsular shield + parts of NW), cyclones, floods, landslides.
  • Practice map work daily: mark all rivers, hills, passes, ports, industries, national parks, biosphere reserves, earthquake zones on a blank India map. This alone can fetch 15-20 marks in NDA/CDS geography section.

Common traps

  • Confusing Kanchenjunga as India's highest peak — K2 is higher but in PoK; Kanchenjunga is highest IN India proper (Sikkim-Nepal border)
  • Thinking all west-flowing rivers of Peninsular India form deltas — Narmada and Tapti form estuaries; Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri form deltas
  • Mixing up Khadar vs Bhangar — Khadar is NEWER/alluvial, more fertile; Bhangar is OLDER, has kankar nodules
  • Assuming all Himalayan rivers are perennial — some are not; the major ones (Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra) are, but smaller ones may be seasonal
  • Thinking Lakshadweep islands are volcanic — they are CORAL; Andaman-Nicobar are tectonic/volcanic
  • Confusing port locations — e.g., Kandla is in Gulf of Kutch (Gujarat), not Gulf of Mannar; Paradeep is in Odisha, not Tamil Nadu
  • Mixing up crop seasons — Kharif (monsoon: rice, cotton, jute); Rabi (winter: wheat, gram, mustard); Zaid (summer short-duration:瓜果类)
  • Assuming all states have same climate — India has vast climatic diversity: arid west, humid NE, temperate north, tropical south
  • Confusing latitude lines — Tropic of Cancer passes through 8 Indian states; Tropic of Capricorn does NOT pass through India
  • Thinking Brahmaputra originates in India — it originates as Yarlung Tsangpo in TIBET; enters India through Arunachal Pradesh
  • Mixing up river origins — Ganga: Gangotri Glacier; Yamuna: Yamunotri Glacier; Narmada: Amarkantak (MP); Tapi: near Multan in Satpura; Godavari: Trimbakeshwar (Maharashtra)
  • Confusing biosphere reserves — Sundarbans (WB), Nilgiri (TN-Kerala-Karnataka), Nanda Devi (UT), Gulf of Mannar (TN), Pachmarhi (MP), Great Nicobar, etc.
