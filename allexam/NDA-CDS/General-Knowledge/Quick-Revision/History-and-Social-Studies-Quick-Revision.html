History and Social Studies — Quick Revision for NDA & CDS

NDA & CDSEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Civilization: A complex human society typically characterized by urban development, social stratification, central government, and cultural/technological advancement.
  • Indus Valley Civilization (IVC): Bronze Age civilization (c. 3300–1300 BCE) of South Asia; mature phase c. 2600–1900 BCE; known for planned cities, drainage systems, standardized weights, and undeciphered script.
  • Vedic Period: Era of composition of the four Vedas (Rigveda, Samaveda, Yajurveda, Atharvaveda), divided into Early Vedic (c. 1500–1000 BCE, Rigvedic) and Later Vedic (c. 1000–600 BCE) phases.
  • Mahajanapadas: Sixteen (16) major political entities/kingdoms that emerged in the Indo-Gangetic plain around 6th century BCE; included Magadha, Kosala, Vaishali, Avanti, etc.
  • Maurya Empire: First pan-Indian empire (c. 322–185 BCE); founded by Chandragupta Maurya; reached peak under Ashoka (c. 268–232 BCE); notable for centralized administration and Ashokan edicts.
  • Gupta Empire: 'Golden Age' of ancient India (c. 320–550 CE); patronized arts, sciences, mathematics (Aryabhata, Varahamihira), and literature (Kalidasa).
  • Bhakti Movement: Medieval devotional movement (7th century onwards) emphasizing personal devotion to a deity, transcending caste barriers; had Southern (Alvar/Nayanar) and Northern phases.
  • Sufi Movement: Mystic Islamic spiritual movement within Islam; prominent Silsilas in India included Chishti and Suhrawardi; emphasized love, tolerance, and devotion.
  • Feudalism: Decentralized socio-political system where land was granted by sovereigns to vassals in exchange for military/service obligations; prominent in medieval India under the Iqta system.
  • Iqta System: Land-revenue assignment system of the Delhi Sultanate where holders (Iqtadars/Muqti) collected revenue in lieu of salary and maintained troops.
  • Mansabdari System: Military-civil ranking system introduced by Akbar where officers (Mansabdars) held ranks (Zat and Sawar) determining salary and troop-maintenance duty.
  • Pritzocracy: Government by priests or religious officials; referred to in ancient Indian context as the dominance of Brahmanas in ritual and administrative affairs during certain periods.
  • Janmashtami, Diwali, Holi, Navratri, Durga Puja: Major Hindu festivals with historical-religious significance; NDA/CDS may ask about associated deities, regions, or customs.
  • Reign of Ashoka: Maurayan emperor who embraced Buddhism after Kalinga War (c. 261 BCE); promoted Dhamma, non-violence, and installed pillars/edicts across the subcontinent.

Key concepts

  • Major cities: Harappa (Punjab, discovered by Daya Ram Sahni, 1921) and Mohenjo-Daro (Sindh, discovered by R.D. Banerji, 1922); other sites include Lothal (Gujarat — dockyard, earliest known waterfront), Kalibangan (Rajasthan — ploughed field), Dholavira (Gujarat — water management system), Rakhigarhi (Haryana — largest IVC site). Urban features: grid-pattern streets, baked brick houses, advanced drainage, granaries, Great Bath (Mohenjo-Daro), Great Granary (Harappa). Economy: agriculture (wheat, barley, sesame), trade with Mesopotamia, craft specialization (beads, shell-work, metallurgy). Society: likely egalitarian with little evidence of kings; undeciphered script (symbols on seals); standardised weights (binary and decimal); cattle reared; domesticated horses are NOT confirmed. Decline c. 1900 BCE — theories include气候变化, river shifting, Aryan invasion (largely rejected today). Notable artifacts: Dancing Girl (bronze), Priest-King sculpture, Pashupati seal, Unicorn seal.
  • Early Vedic Period (c. 1500–1000 BCE, Rigvedic): Kings were elected/chief-based (Rajan); sabha and samiti were assemblies; society was pastoral; Rigveda composed; varna system was fluid (based on occupation); women had education and participated in rituals; Panchajanya was chief deity (indras); Later Vedic Period (c. 1000–600 BCE): Yajurveda, Samaveda, Atharvaveda composed; iron use spread; kingdoms emerged (Kosala, Magadha, Vajji, Gandhara); varna became rigid; Rajas performed ashvamedha, rajsuya, agnistoma sacrifices; Brahmanas gained prominence; Upanishads composed (philosophical texts exploring Brahman and Atman); Sati, child marriage, early caste restrictions began. Important figures: Yajnavalkya, Gargi, Maitreyi (women philosophers).
  • Buddhism: Founded by Siddhartha Gautama (the Buddha, c. 563–483 BCE, Lumbini, Nepal); attained enlightenment at Bodh Gaya; first sermon at Sarnath (Four Noble Truths, Eightfold Path); ordained disciples formed Sangha; parinirvana at Kushinagar; First Buddhist Council at Rajgir (under Ajatashatru, led by Mahakasyapa) just after Buddha's death; Second Council at Vaishali (about sectarian分歧); Third Council at Pataliputra (under Ashoka, led by Moggaliputta Tissa) — decided canon, sent missionaries; Fourth Council under Kanishka (Kashmir/Pushkavati, led by Vasumitra and Asvaghosha) — split into Hinayana and Mahayana. Tripitakas: Vinaya Pitaka (monastic rules), Sutta Pitaka (discourses), Abhidhamma Pitaka (philosophy). Key terms: Nirvana, Karma, Samsara, Ahimsa. Jainism: Founded by Mahavira (24th Tirthankara, c. 599–527 BCE, Vaishali); 23rd was Parshvanatha; First Council at Pataliputra (held by Sumati); Ten Precepts; ultimate goal: liberation through asceticism; Svetambara (white-clad) vs Digambara (sky-clad) sects; principle of Ahimsa paramount; Anekantavada (multiple viewpoints).
  • Chandragupta Maurya (c. 322–298 BCE): Overthrew Nandas with Chanakya/Kautilya's help; defeated Seleucus Nicator (305 BCE); treaty gave him territory up to Hindu Kush; abdicating as Jain ascetic at Shravanabelagola (inscribed by Hemachandra); Arthashastra (Kautilya) and Mudrarakshasa (Vishakhadatta) are key texts. Bindusara (c. 298–272 BCE): called 'Amitraghata' by Greeks; extended empire to south; Greek envoy Megasthenes wrote Indika (describing Pataliputra, administration, society). Ashoka (c. 268–232 BCE): fought Kalinga War (261 BCE); after converting to Buddhism, propagated Dhamma through rock and pillar edicts; Major Rock Edicts at Girnar, Sopra, Shahbazgarhi, Mansehra; Dhamma Mahamattas appointed; missions sent to Sri Lanka, Central Asia, Greece; Stupas at Sanchi and Amaravati erected. Post-Mauryas: Sungas (Pushyamitra Sunga founded after assassinating last Maurya), Kanvas, Indo-Greeks (Menander/Sikandar was prominent king, patronized Pushpadas), Shakas (Western Kshatrapas), Parthians, Kushanas (Kanishka — Golden Age of Gupta predecessor era; Council at Kashmir; Gandhara and Mathura schools of art flourished).
  • Gupta Empire (c. 320–550 CE): Samudragupta (c. 335–375 CE) called 'Napoleon of India' by V.A. Smith; accomplished through Politeia (military campaigns, described in Allahabad Pillar inscription by Harishena); Chandragupta II (c. 375–415 CE) also known as Vikramaditya; defeated Sakas; hosted Fa-Hien (Chinese pilgrim) during his reign; Kumaragupta and Skandagupta (repelled Huna invasions) later declined. Administration: decentralized with provinces (Bhuktis), districts (Vishayas), villages; local self-government strong. Science & Mathematics: Aryabhata ( zero, decimal system, earth rotates on axis, computed pi ≈ 3.1416), Varahamihira (Brihat-Samhita — astronomy, weather), Brahmagupta (later, zero as number, algebra). Literature: Kalidasa (Shakuntala, Meghaduta, Raghuvamsha, Kumarsambhava); Vishakhadatta (Mudrarakshasa); Gunadhya (Brihat-Katha). Art: Ajanta caves (paintings, Buddha Jataka tales), Ellora (rock-cut temples), Nagarjuni caves; Gupta architecture — Dashavatara Temple at Deogarh, Bhitargaon temple. Decay factors: Huna invasions, feudal fragmentation, weak successors.
  • Five Dynasties: (1) Slave/Dynasty (1206–1290): Qutub-ud-din Aibak (built Qutub Minar, started construction; died playing polo at Lahore; Iltutmish completed Qutub Minar); Iltutmish (real consolidator, introduced Iqta system, silver Tanka, copper Paisa); Balban (Theory of Blood-Zabita, crushed nobility, Diwan-i-Arz reformed army). (2) Khilji (1290–1320): Alauddin Khilji — market reforms (fixed prices, Sarai, Diwan-i-Riyasat), military reforms (numbered horses, Dag-bagh system), expanded empire southwards (Malwa, Gujarat, Deccan — Ala-Alla Pier, Alai Darwaza at Qutub complex). (3) Tughlaq (1320–1414): Muhammad bin Tughlaq — controversial transfers of capital (Delhi to Daulatabad), Token currency failure, Jizya on Muslims; Firoz Shah Tughlaq — built canals, hospitals (Dar-ul-Shifa), abolished Jizya for converted Hindus. (4) Sayyid (1414–1451). (5) Lodi (1451–1526): Bahlul Lodi founded; Ibrahim Lodi defeated at First Battle of Panipat (1526) by Babur. Revenue: Iqta system; revenue collection by wazir; land measured (gaj-dharna, bumbani).
  • Babur (r. 1526–1530): Defeated Ibrahim Lodi at Panipat (1526) using gunpowder and Turkish tulugma tactic; defeated Rana Sanga at Khanwa (1527); wrote autobiography 'Tuzuk-i-Baburi' (Persian); Zeb-un-Nisa (daughter) was poet. Humayun (r. 1530–1540, 1555–1556): Lost to Sher Shah Suri; regained throne with Persian aid; built Din-i-Ilahi precursor ideas; Akbar adopted him. Sher Shah Suri (r. 1540–1545): Founded Sur Dynasty; introduced Rupee; reformed land revenue (Measurement by bamboo rods — bandobot); built Grand Trunk Road (Srimgarh to Palakkad stretch); Died at Kalpi from gunpowder explosion. Akbar (r. 1556–1605): Defeated Hemu at Second Battle of Panipat (1556, guided by Bairam Khan); abolished Jizya (1562, restored by Jahangir); Din-i-Ilahi (1582, 'Religion of God' — syncretic, not widely followed); Iqta/Mansabdari system; Sulk-i-Kul (tolerance); Navratnas (nine gems including Tansen, Birbal, Faizi, Abdul Rahim); Mariam-uz-Zamani (Jodha Bai); Built Fatehpur Sikri ( Buland Darwaza, Jama Masjid, Panch Mahal). Jahangir (r. 1605–1627): Nur-Jahan dominant; painting flourished (Ustad Mansur — bird/nature painter); visited Kashmir; executed Guru Arjun Dev (1606). Shah Jahan (r. 1628–1658): Built Taj Mahal (Mumtaz Mahal's tomb), Red Fort, Jama Masjid, Sheesh Mahal (Lahore); Peak of Mughal architecture. Aurangzeb (r. 1658–1707): Re-imposed Jizya on non-Muslims (1679); destroyed Kashi Vishwanath temple; annexed Bijapur and Golconda; Deccan campaigns exhausted treasury; succeeded by weak rulers leading to decline.
  • Causes of 1857: Political (Doctrine of Lapse by Dalhousie — annexed Satara, Jaitpur, Sambalpur, Udaipur, Nagpur, Jhansi, Awadh); Social-Religious (Sati abolition 1829, Widow Remarriage Act 1856, spread of Christianity); Economic (deindustrialization, heavy taxes, land revenue systems); Military (sepoys unhappy with overseas service, pay disparity, Enfield rifle cartridges greased with cow/pig fat — immediate trigger). Leaders: Bahadur Shah Zafar (figurehead emperor), Nana Sahib (Kanpur), Rani Lakshmibai (Jhansi), Tantia Tope, Kunwar Singh (Bihar), Begum Hazrat Mahal (Awadh). Results: Government of India Act 1858 transferred power from East India Company to Crown; Secretary of State for India created; Viceroy title replaced Governor-General; Queen's Proclamation (1859); princely states recognized; army reorganized. Early National Congress: Founded 1885 by A.O. Hume (Retired ICS officer); Moderates (Badruddin Tyabji, W.C. Bonnerjee, Dadabhai Naoroji, G.K. Gokhale, Pherozeshah Mehta) — methods: petitions, prayers, protests; 'Drain of Wealth' theory by Naoroji; Extremists (Bal Gangadhar Tilak, Lala Lajpat Rai, Bipin Chandra Pal — Lal-Bal-Pal) — methods: Swadeshi, Boycott, National Education, Tilak's 'Swaraj is my birthright'); Swadeshi Movement (1905) against Curzon's Partition of Bengal (1905, annulled 1911); Simon Commission (1927 — all-white, 'Simon Go Back').
  • Gandhi's return to India (1915); First mass movement: Champaran Satyagraha (1917, first in India — indigo plantation oppression); Kheda (1918, peasants); Ahmedabad Mill Strike (1918); Rowlatt Act (1919 — detention without trial); Jallianwala Bagh Massacre (April 13, 1919, General Dyer ordered firing, hundreds killed); Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–22, withdrawn after Chauri-Chaura incident Feb 1922); Khilafat Movement (1919–24, Ali brothers); Swaraj Party formed (1923, Chitta Ranjan Bose & Motilal Nehru); Civil Disobedience Movement (1930–34) launched with Dandi March (March 12 – April 6, 1930, 390 km, Salt Satyagraha); Gandhi-Irwin Pact (1931); Round Table Conferences (1930–32); Poona Pact (1932, Ambedkar vs Gandhi on separate electorate); Quit India Movement (1942, 'Do or Die', led after August 8 resolution at Bombay AICC); Indian National Army (INA) formed by Subhas Chandra Bose (1943, Azad Hind Fauj); Forward Bloc (1939, Bose); Socialist Party (1934); Muslim League: founded 1906 (Dhaka, Nawab Salimullah); Lucknow Pact (1916, Congress-League unity under Patel); Lahore Resolution (1940, demand for Pakistan); Cabinet Mission (1946); Mountbatten Plan/Indian Independence Act 1947 (partition on Aug 15, 1947); Radcliffe Line determined borders.
  • Raja Ram Mohan Roy (1772–1833): Founder of Brahmo Samaj (1828); campaigned against Sati (abolished 1829); promoted English education; 'Father of Indian Renaissance'; supported Widow Remarriage Act. Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar (1820–1891): Championed widow remarriage (passed 1856); promoted women's education; wrote Bengali primers. Jyotiba Phule (1827–1890): Founded Satyashodhak Samaj (1873); worked for lower castes and women's education; 'Gulamgiri' book; ran first school for girls in Pune. Savitribai Phule: First female teacher in India; worked alongside Jyotiba; opened schools for girls and widows. Swami Vivekananda (1863–1902): Disciple of Ramakrishna Paramahansa; represented India at Parliament of Religions, Chicago (1893); founded Ramakrishna Mission (1897); promoted Hindu revivalism and service. Raja Ram Mohan Roy, Dayanand Saraswati, and others. Arya Samaj (1875): Founded by Swami Dayanand Saraswati; 'Back to the Vedas'; opposed idol worship, caste system; promoted Sanskrit and women's education; Dayanand founded 'Samudaya Parishad'. Ramakrishna Mission: Service-oriented, spiritual. Singh Sabha Movement (1873): Sikh reform in Punjab. Congress socialist: Jayaprakash Narayan, Subhas Chandra Bose, M.N. Roy.
  • French Revolution (1789): Storming of Bastille July 14, 1789; Declaration of Rights of Man and Citizen; Reign of Terror under Robespierre; Napoleon Bonaparte rose to power; Napoleonic Wars (1803–1815); Battle of Waterloo (1815, final defeat). Russian Revolution (1917): February Revolution overthrew Tsar Nicholas II; October Revolution led by Lenin and Bolsheviks; USSR established 1922. American Revolution (1775–1783): Thirteen colonies declared independence 1776; Declaration of Independence by Thomas Jefferson; George Washington as Commander-in-Chief; Treaty of Paris 1783. World War I (1914–1918): Assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand (June 28, 1914, Sarajevo); Allied Powers (Britain, France, Russia, Italy, USA) vs Central Powers (Germany, Austria-Hungary, Ottoman, Bulgaria); ended Nov 11, 1918; Treaty of Versailles (1919). World War II (1939–1945): Germany invaded Poland Sept 1, 1939; Holocaust; Pearl Harbor attack Dec 7, 1941; D-Day June 6, 1944; Atom bombs on Hiroshima (Aug 6) and Nagasaki (Aug 9, 1945); UN formed Oct 1945. Key thinkers: Karl Marx (Communist Manifesto 1848), Adam Smith (Wealth of Nations), John Locke (social contract).
  • Temple styles: Nagara (North Indian — shikhara/curvilinear tower; examples: Khajuraho Group (UNESCO, Chandela dynasty, 10th–11th century), Kandariya Mahadeva Temple; Modhera Sun Temple (Gujarat)); Dravida (South Indian — gopuram/towered gateway, vimana/pyramidal tower; examples: Brihadeeswarar Temple at Thanjavur (Rajaraja Chola I, 1010 CE — UNESCO), Meenakshi Temple Madurai, Shore Temple Mahabalipuram); Vesara (hybrid — Chalukyan style; examples: Badami cave temples, Pattadakal). Cave Temples: Ajanta (Maharashtra, 1st century BCE–5th century CE, Buddhist paintings, frescoes, UNESCO); Ellora (Maharashtra, 6th–10th century, 34 caves — Hindu, Buddhist, Jain; Kailash Temple, Cave 16 — UNESCO); Elephanta (Maharashtra, 5th–6th century, Shaiva sculptures). Sculpture schools: Gandhara (Greco-Buddhist art, Taxila region, 1st century CE); Mathura (Indian Buddhist/Hindu art, red sandstone); Amravati (Andhra, white marble). UNESCO World Heritage Sites in India relevant to history: Ajanta-Ellora, Khajuraho Group, Group of Monuments at Hampi, Red Fort Complex (Delhi), Champa Monument (Varanasi), etc.
  • Major battles of Indian history: Battle of Plassey (1757, Clive vs Siraj-ud-Daulah — British gain foothold); Battle of Buxar (1764, Mir Qasim vs East India Company — Diwani rights); First Anglo-Maratha War (1775–82); Second Anglo-Maratha War (1803–05, Arthur Wellesley vs Scindia-Holkar); Third Anglo-Maratha War (1817–18) — Maratha supremacy broken; Anglo-Sikh Wars (1845–46, 1848–49) — Punjab annexed; Anglo-Afghan Wars (three wars: 1839–42, 1856–63, 1878–80); Anglo-Burmese Wars. Indian Armed Forces: Army (oldest, 25 Jan 1950 as Republic day; but origins trace to 1600s EIC soldiers); Navy (oldest naval force in Asia, modern Indian Navy from British Eastern Fleet); Air Force (1 Oct 1932 as RAF auxiliary, 8 March 1942 as IAF). Major weapons: Akash missile (SAM), Agni (ballistic), BrahMos (cruise, joint Russia-India). Nuclear test: Pokhran-I (1974, Smiling Buddha), Pokhran-II (1998, Operation Shakti, six nuclear devices). Defense academies: Indian Military Academy (Dehradun), National Defence Academy (Pune), Officers Training Academy (Chennai/America).

Important facts

  • Harappa discovered by Daya Ram Sahni (1921); Mohenjo-Daro by R.D. Banerji (1922); Lothal by S.R. Rao (1955).
  • First Buddhist Council: Rajgir, under Ajatashatru; Second: Vaishali; Third: Pataliputra (Ashoka); Fourth: Kashmir (Kanishka).
  • Ashoka's Kalinga War: 261 BCE; Rock Edict XIII describes the war's horror and conversion to non-violence.
  • Mahaparinirvana of Buddha: 483 BCE (traditional date); Kushinagar.
  • Birth of Mahavira: 599 BCE (traditional), Vaishali; Mahaparinirvana: 527 BCE.
  • Akbar born: 1542 at Amarkot (Sindh); ruled 1556–1605.
  • Shivaji crowned as Chhatrapati: 1674 at Raigad; died 1680.
  • Battle of Panipat I (1526): Babur vs Ibrahim Lodi (Mughal origin).
  • Battle of Panipat II (1556): Akbar vs Hemu (Mughal restoration).
  • Battle of Plassey (1757): Robert Clive vs Siraj-ud-Daulah.
  • Battle of Buxar (1764): East India Company vs combined forces of Mir Qasim, Shuja-ud-Daula, Shah Alam II.
  • First War of Independence (1857) — alternative names: Sepoy Mutiny, First Indian Uprising.
  • Lord Dalhousie's Doctrine of Lapse applied to 6+ states including Jhansi, Nagpur, Satara, Sambalpur, Udaipur, Jaitpur.
  • Dandi March: March 12 – April 6, 1930; distance: 390 km from Sabarmati Ashram to Dandi.
  • Quit India Movement launched: August 8, 1942 at Bombay AICC session.
  • Indian National Congress founded: 1885; first president: W.C. Bonnerjee; second: Dadabhai Naoroji ('Grand Old Man of India').
  • Subhas Chandra Bose formed Forward Bloc: 1939; INA formed: 1943 in Singapore; died: 18 Aug 1945, Taipei.
  • Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev, Rajguru hanged: 23 March 1931 at Lahore Central Jail.
  • Dadabhai Naoroji wrote 'Poverty and Un-British Rule in India' (Drain of Wealth theory).
  • Sardar Patel united 562 princely states into Indian Union (1947–49).
  • Mahatma Gandhi returned to India from South Africa: 1915; gave first Satyagraha: Champaran, 1917.
  • First Governor-General of independent India: Lord Mountbatten (1947–48); first Indian: C. Rajagopalachari (1948–50).
  • Indian Constitution adopted: 26 Nov 1949; came into force: 26 Jan 1950.
  • Constituent Assembly had 389 members initially (post-partition: 299).
  • Rajendra Prasad was President of Constituent Assembly; Dr. B.R. Ambedkar was Chairman of Drafting Committee.
  • Qutub Minar height: 72.5 m; built by Qutub-ud-din Aibak (started) and Iltutmish (completed).
  • Taj Mahal: built 1632–1653 by Shah Jahan; architect Ustad Ahmad Lahori; UNESCO site since 1983.
  • Red Fort Delhi: built by Shah Jahan (1638–48); main gate Lahori Gate, Diwan-i-Aam, Diwan-i-Khas.
  • Humayun's Tomb (Delhi): built by Hamida Banu Begum (Humayun's wife); first garden-tomb in India; UNESCO 1993.
  • Great Stupa at Sanchi: originally built by Ashoka (3rd century BCE); rediscovered by Cuninghame (1818); carvings added later.
  • Ajanta Caves: 30 caves; 29 Buddhist; paintings depict Jataka stories; UNESCO 1983.
  • Ellora Caves: 34 caves (12 Buddhist, 17 Hindu, 5 Jain); Kailash Temple (Cave 16) is monolithic;
  • Khajuraho Group: 85 temples originally; 25 survive; built by Chandela dynasty (950–1050 CE); UNESCO 1986.
  • Hampi: Capital of Vijayanagara Empire (1336–1565); UNESCO 1986; Virupaksha Temple, Vitthala Temple (stone chariot), Royal Enclosure.
  • Maritime trade: Romans traded with Cheras, Cholas, Pandyas; pepper, cinnamon, precious stones exchanged for gold.
  • Ashokan edicts: Major Rock Edicts (14), Major Pillar Edicts (7), Minor Edicts scattered; scripts: Brahmi, Kharosthi, Greek, Aramaic.
  • Kanishka: Patron of Gandhara art; Council at Kashmir; sent missionaries to China.
  • Chandragupta II defeated Western Kshatrapas; controlled western sea routes; Fa-Hien visited during his reign.
  • Samudragupta's Allahabad Pillar inscription (Prayag Prashasti) by Harishena lists 12 kings he conquered.
  • Gupta period: Calendar reforms, decimal system, astronomy, surgery (Sushruta tradition continued).
  • First railway in India: Bombay to Thane (1853); telegraph introduced 1850s; postal system reformed by Lord Dalhousie (1854).
  • Hunter Commission (1882) on education; Fletcher Commission (1883) on civil services.
  • Indian Councils Act 1861: Introduced legislative councils; portfolio system (except law member).
  • Morley-Minto Reforms (1909): Separate electorates for Muslims; Indian Councils Act 1909.
  • Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms (1919): Dyarchy in provinces; Government of India Act 1919.
  • Government of India Act 1935: Federal scheme, provincial autonomy, All-India Federation proposed.
  • Cripps Mission (1942): Offered Dominion status post-WWII; rejected by Congress and Muslim League.
  • Wavell Plan (1945): Expand Viceroy's Executive Council; failed due to Congress-Muslim League differences.
  • Boleto Act (1947): Indian Independence Act — partition and dominion status.
  • Cabinet Mission Plan (1946): Proposed grouping of provinces; three-tier federation.
  • Partition of Bengal (1905): Annulled 1911 at Delhi Durbar; revived 1947 as lasting partition.
  • Jallianwala Bagh: Amritsar, Punjab; General Reginald Dyer; thousands killed (official: 379 dead, 1137 injured).
  • Chauri-Chaura: Gorakhpur, Uttar Pradesh; 22 Feb 1922; violence led Gandhi to withdraw Non-Cooperation.
  • Lahore Resolution (1940): Passed on 23 March; demand for 'Independent States' for Muslims; now called Pakistan Resolution.
  • Netaji Subhas Bose escaped house arrest: 1941 (via Afghanistan to USSR then Germany); reached Singapore 1943.
  • INA trials: Red Fort trials (1945–46) of three INA officers — Shah Nawaz Khan, Prem Sahgal, Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon.
  • Navjote Singh KahanSingh was first Chief of Army Staff (1948–49) of independent India.
  • Param Vir Chakra: India's highest military decoration; first recipient: Major Somnath Sharma (1947 Indo-Pak War).

Memory aids

  • IVC Sites mnemonic: HAR-MOH-LOH-KAL-RAK = Harappa, Mohenjo-Daro, Lothal, Kalibangan, Rakhigarhi. Remember Lothal for dockyard, Kalibangan for ploughed field, Rakhigarhi as largest.
  • Vedic Literature sequence: Rigveda → Yajurveda → Samaveda → Atharvaveda. 'RYSA' (Why So? Rig Yajur Sam Atharva). Upanishads = end of Vedas (Vedanta).
  • Buddhist Councils: 'Really Smart Monk Always' = Rajgir, Vaishali, Pataliputra, Kashmir (1st–4th).
  • Mughal Emperors timeline: Babur → Humayun → Akbar → Jahangir → Shah Jahan → Aurangzeb. Mnemonic: 'Baba Har AKhbar Jaisi Shah' (Baba had an old man who looked like a king).
  • British Battles (chronology): Plassey (1757) → Buxar (1764) → Anglo-Maratha Wars (1775–82, 1803–05, 1817–18) → Anglo-Sikh Wars (1845–46, 1848–49).
  • Congress Presidents 1885–1929: Bonnerjee → Naoroji → Wedderburn →anthony → Madan Mohan Malaviya → Besant → Nagpur (1920, Gandhi's first) → Lucknow (1916, Unity session) → Calcutta (1928, Poorna Swaraj) → Lahore (1929, Nehru, Purna Swaraj) → Karachi (1931, Gandhi, Resolutions) → Faizpur (1936, Nehru) → Lahore (1940, Jinnah's resolution).
  • Freedom fighters chronology hint: 1857 revolt → 1885 Congress → 1905 Swadeshi → 1915 Gandhi returns → 1919 Jallianwala Bagh → 1920 Non-Cooperation → 1930 Dandi March → 1942 Quit India → 1947 Independence.
  • Key dates to memorize in clusters: 1757, 1764 (British rise), 1857 (revolt), 1885 (Congress), 1905 (Bengal partition), 1919 (Jallianwala Bagh + Rowlatt), 1920–22 (Non-Cooperation), 1930 (Dandi), 1942 (Quit India), 1947 (Independence).
  • Ashoka's Edicts locations mnemonic: 'Rock on mountain, pillar in cities' — Major Rock Edicts at Girnar (Gujarat), Sopra (Delhi), Shahbazgarhi (PK), Mansehra (PK), Junagadh (Gujarat). Pillar edicts at Allahabad (Prayag), Meerut, Delhi.
  • Temple Styles: Nagara = North (shikhara tower), Dravida = South (gopuram gate), Vesara = Central (hybrid). Remember: North has curved towers like mountains (shikhara = peak), South has pyramid gates (gopuram = entrance).
  • UNESCO Sites quick check: Ajanta, Ellora, Khajuraho, Hampi, Taj Mahal, Qutub Minar, Red Fort, Sanchi Stupa, Group of Monuments at Mahabalipuram (seven pagodas), Champa (Varanasi), Fatehpur Sikri, Humayun's Tomb, Rani-ki-Vav.

Exam tips

  • For NDA/CDS History section: Focus on dates, names, places, and cause-effect relationships. MCQs frequently ask 'which battle happened in which year?' or 'who founded which movement?' Practice matching pairs.
  • Use elimination technique: When options contain names you vaguely recognize, eliminate the obviously wrong ones (wrong time period, wrong region, wrong religion). For example, if a question asks about Mughal architecture, eliminate anything mentioning Qutub Minar or Ajanta Caves.
  • Pay attention to qualifiers: Words like 'first', 'only', 'never', 'always' in options often signal incorrect statements. In NDA/CDS, absolute claims are frequently wrong unless they are well-known facts (e.g., 'First Battle of Panipat was in 1526').
  • Combine history with geography: Many questions link historical events to locations — e.g., 'Where was the Jallianwala Bagh massacre?' (Amritsar, not Delhi/Lucknow). Use geographical knowledge to anchor historical facts.
  • Focus on 'high-yield' topics: 1857 revolt, Gandhian movements, Mughal administration, Indian freedom struggle chronology, and Important cultural heritage sites appear repeatedly. Prioritize these over obscure facts.
  • Learn to identify 'incorrectly matched' pairs: NDA/CDS frequently asks 'Which of the following is NOT correctly matched?' — practice identifying mismatches between person-event, date-place, or concept-follower.
  • For Medieval history: Remember that 'Sufi' and 'Bhakti' movements ran parallel and shared themes (devotion, tolerance, rejection of caste). Questions often ask about similarities between them.
  • For World History: French Revolution (1789), Russian Revolution (1917), WWI (1914–18), WWII (1939–45), Cold War basics — these are frequent. Know key dates, leaders, and outcomes concisely.
  • Quick revision strategy: Make flashcards for every emperor/dynasty with key achievements and dates. Create a timeline wall-chart from 1500 BCE to 1947 CE. Revise the same topic 3–4 times across different days for retention.
  • Don't over-generalize about 'decline': Each empire's decline had specific causes — e.g., Mughal decline was NOT just Aurangzeb's religious policy but also noble factionalism, fiscal crisis, and Maratha rise. Understand multi-causal analysis.

Common traps

  • Confusing IVC sites: Lothal = dockyard (not Harappa/Mohenjo-Daro); Kalibangan = ploughed field; Dholavira = water reservoirs; Rakhigarhi = largest. Do not assume every major site has the same feature.
  • Ashoka's religion: He did NOT found Buddhism; he became a Buddhist CONVERT after the Kalinga War. Do not say he 'created' or 'started' Buddhism — Mahavira and Buddha founded it.
  • Din-i-Ilahi vs Islam: Din-i-Ilahi was Akbar's syncretic moral code, NOT a new religion with its own places of worship or priesthood. It was limited to court circle.
  • Gandhi's role: He was NOT in jail during 1942 when Quit India started — he was arrested on Aug 9. The movement continued leaderlessly with underground communication.
  • Battle confusion: Do not mix up First (1526, Babur vs Ibrahim Lodi) and Second (1556, Akbar vs Hemu) Battles of Panipat. Third Panipat (1761) was Ahmad Shah Abdali vs Marathas.
  • Constitution facts: Constituent Assembly adopted Constitution on 26 November 1949; came into effect 26 January 1950. Do not interchange these two dates. Republic Day ≠ Independence Day.
  • Drain of Wealth theory: Dadabhai Naoroji propounded it, NOT R.C. Dutt (who wrote 'Economic History of India' and supported the theory but was not the originator).
  • Caste origins: Varna system originated in Rigveda (Purusha Sukta, 10th Mandala); Jati (birth-based subdivision) developed later. Do not conflate Varna (4-fold, fluid early on) with Jati (thousands, rigid).
  • British policy mistakes: Doctrine of Lapse was Dalhousie's, NOT Curzon's. Curzon is associated with Partition of Bengal (1905). Do not confuse their achievements.
  • Gandhi's first Satyagraha: Champaran (1917), NOT Kheda (1918) or Ahmedabad (1918). Champaran was his first experiment in India.
  • Mughal art schools: Gandhara art is GRECO-BUDDHIST (not purely Greek or purely Buddhist); it originated in the region of Gandhara (modern Pakistan/Afghanistan border). Mathura school is INDIAN Buddhist art. Don't confuse them.
  • Sepoy Mutiny vs Freedom Struggle: The 1857 revolt was NOT organized nationally; it was regional with different leaders. Calling it 'First War of Independence' is one viewpoint (V.D. Savarkar's term); NDA/CDS may expect balanced recognition.
  • INA formation: INA was formed by Subhas Bose in 1943 in SINGAPORE (not in India proper) with Japanese support. The original nucleus was Indian prisoners of war captured by Japan in 1942.
  • Partition dates: Bangladesh was formed in 1971 (formerly East Pakistan); not 1947. 1947 partition was only India-Pakistan (West + East Pakistan).
  • Qutub Minar construction: Started by Qutub-ud-din Aibak (red sandstone, 5 stories) but COMPLETED by Iltutmish (added 3 more stories with marble/stone). Do not credit only one.
