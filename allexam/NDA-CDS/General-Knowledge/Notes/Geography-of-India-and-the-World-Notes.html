1. Physical Divisions of India

India is divided into five major physiographic units, each with distinct characteristics that frequently appear in NDA/CDS questions.

(a) The Himalayan Mountains: A young fold mountain system running ~2,400 km from NW to SE. Divided into three parallel ranges:
- Shiwalik Range (outermost, 900–1,100 m): composed of unconsolidated sediments; prone to landslides.
- Lesser Himalaya / Himachal (2,700–4,500 m): includes valleys like Kangra, Kullu, Nainital; contains the Pir Panjal, Mahabharat, and Dhauladhar ranges.
- Great Himalaya / Himadri (6,000 m avg.): permanently snow-bound; highest peaks — Everest (8,848 m, Nepal border), K2/Godwin Austen (8,611 m, PoK), Kanchenjunga (8,586 m, Sikkim — highest in India), Nanda Devi (7,816 m, Uttarakhand), Kamet (7,756 m).

(b) Northern Plains: Formed by alluvial deposition by the Indus, Ganga, and Brahmaputra river systems. Extends ~2,400 km long, 240–320 km wide. Divided into:
- Bhabar: porous gravelly belt at foothills where streams disappear.
- Terai: marshy, swampy region south of bhabar; once teeming with wildlife, now largely reclaimed.
- Bhangar: older alluvium forming upland, contains calcareous deposits (kankar).
- Khadar: newer, fertile alluvium in floodplains; renewed annually.

(c) Peninsular Plateau: Oldest landmass,一块 of ancient crystalline igneous and metamorphic rocks. Divided into:
- Deccan Traps (NW): large volcanic basaltic province formed ~65 Ma.
- Central Highlands: east of Deccan traps; includes Malwa plateau, Chambal valley.
- Meghalaya Plateau (Shillong plateau): separated from main block by Garo-Rajmahal gap.
- Aravalli Hills: one of the world's oldest folded ranges; runs SW–NE across Rajasthan; peak Guru Shikhar (1,722 m, Mt Abu).
- Western Ghats (Sahyadri): continuous chain, avg. 1,200–1,500 m; higher in south (Anamudi 2,695 m — highest in India); passes include Thal, Bhor, Palghat (lowest pass ~60 m).
- Eastern Ghats: discontinuous, lower (avg. 600 m); intersected by rivers; highest peak Janapada Lava (1,690 m) or Araku Valley area.

(d) Coastal Plains: Western coastal plain narrow, sandwiched between Western Ghats and Arabian Sea; includes Konkan (Mumbai–Goa), Kanara, Malabar coasts. Backwaters (kayals) in Kerala; deltas minimal. Eastern coastal plain broader; includes Northern Circars and Coromandel coast; major deltas: Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri (Kolleru lake near Krishna delta).

(e) Islands: Andaman & Nicobar (Bay of Bengal) — tectonic origin, volcanic Barren Island (only active volcano in India), Snow Mount (846 m highest in A&N). Lakshadweep (Arabian Sea) — coral atolls; originally called Laccadive Islands; Minicoy is largest; Amindivi group = northern, Laccadive = central, Maldives = southern.

2. Major River Systems and Drainage

India's rivers are classified into Himalayan (perennial, snowmelt-fed) and Peninsular (rain-fed, non-perennial) systems.

Himalayan River Systems:
- Indus System (70,400 km² basin in India): Originates near Mansarovar (Tibet); enters India via Ladakh; flows NW through Punjab; critical tributaries from right: Zaskar, Suru, Shyok, Nubra, Shigar, Gilgit; from left: Ledtsu, Bunabs, Spiti, Ravi, Beas, Satluj (largest tributary); crosses Pakistan near Uch; empties into Arabian Sea near Karachi.
- Ganga System (861,452 km² total): Originates Gangotri glacier (Bhagirathi); joins Alaknanda at Devprayag; emerges from hills at Rishikesh (Bhaironghat); flows through Upper, Middle, Lower Ganga plains; key left-bank tributaries: Yamuna (largest, originates Yamunotri), Gomti, Ghaghara (Karnali, longest left-bank tributary), Gandak, Kosi ("sorrow of Bihar", originates from Tibetan source Sunkoshi); key right-bank: Son (largest right-bank, originates Amarkantak); reaches sea at Sunderbans delta (Sundri island — name origin).
- Brahmaputra System: Originates Chemayungdung glacier (Tibet) as Tsangpo; enters India via Zamar gap in Arunachal as Dihang/Siang; receives tributaries Teesta, Manas, Torsa, Sankosh, Dinajpur, Burhi Dihing, Dilao from Assam valley; joins Ganga at Salmara (Bangladesh) as Meghna; massive delta shared with Ganga.

Peninsular River Systems:
- west-flowing: Narmada (flows through rift valley, estuary not delta; originates Amarkantak; falls: Dhuandhar, Baba Harishchandgarh (Navapur); islands: Marble Rocks at Umbargam); Tapi (originates Velhundi range near Betul; flows through rift parallel to Narmada but south of it); Mahi, Sabarmati, Luni (inner drain, terminates in Rann of Kutch — seasonal).
- East-flowing: Godavari (longest peninsular river, 1,465 km; originates Trimbakeshwar; flows through Maharashtra, Telangana, AP; delta: Rajahmundry – "confluence island"); Krishna (1,400 km; originates Mahabaleshwar; 7th largest in India; Tungabhadra, Koyna, Bhima, Musi are tributaries); Kaveri (800 km; originates Talakaveri, BR Hills; flows through KS, PL, TN; delta at Srirangapatna/Tiruchirapalli; tributaries: Kabini, Hemavati, Bhavani, Amravati); Mahanadi (858 km; originates Nasik area; delta at Cuttack; Hooghly distributary origin);

Important fact: The line of division between east-flowing and west-flowing peninsular rivers runs roughly through the junction of Aravallis and Vindhyas, with slight deviation near Mumbai.

Lake-related: Chilika (Odisha – largest brackish water lagoon, Ramsar site, migratory birds), Wular (J&K – largest freshwater lake in India, formed by glacial action), Pulicat (TN/KA border – second largest brackish lagoon), Sambhar (Rajasthan – largest inland salt lake), Loktak (Manipur – floating lake, has keenan), Dal (Kashmir – floating gardens), Manasarovar (Tibet – sacred lake).

3. Climate and Monsoon

India's climate is classified under Köppen's scheme. The dominant feature is the monsoon circulation — seasonal reversal of wind direction driven by differential heating of land and sea and the shifting of ITZ (Inter-Tropical Convergence Zone).

Seasons (IMD classification):
- Cold Weather (Dec–Feb): Northern plains cool; shivering winters in north; frost in Punjab/Haryana/UP; cyclonic disturbances (Western disturbances) from Mediterranean bring rain to NW India; Tamil Nadu gets winter rains from Northeast trade winds.
- Hot Weather (Mar–May): Heat builds;loo/western disturbances end; mango showers (KL/Karanataka — pre-monsoon rains aiding mango harvest); calm conditions in NE; western India hottest zone.
- Monsoon Approach (June): Southwest monsoon hits Kerala ~1 June (retreating from SE coast); Bay of Bengal branch arrives Assam first; Rajasthan receives rain ~10 June; whole country covered by ~15 July.
- Monsoon Withdrawal (Sept–Nov): Retreating SW monsoon; Tamil Nadu coast receives NE monsoon rains (seasonal reversal); October heat prevalent in plains.

Monsoon mechanisms:
- SW Monsoon: Two branches — Arabian Sea branch (hits Western Ghats — orographic rainfall; Maharashtra, Karnataka, Kerala receive heavy rain; rain shadow in Deccan plateau); Bay of Bengal branch (hits NE India — Assam gets highest rainfall; cycles through Ganga plain; Mawsynram — 11,872 mm, highest in world; Cherrapunji ~11,777 mm). Factors affecting monsoon: El Niño (weakens — causes deficit rainfall), Indian Ocean Dipole (positive phase strengthens, negative weakens), Madden-Julian Oscillation.
- Northeast Monsoon (Oct–Dec): Prevails over SE peninsula after SW retreat; accounts for 50–60% of TN rainfall; also called retreating monsoon.

Rainfall distribution:
- >250 cm: Western GH slopes, Assam, coastal Odisha.
- 100–250 cm: Most of Ganga plain, parts of Peninsula.
- <50 cm: Western Rajasthan, Ladakh, trans-Himalayan Lahaul-Spiti.
- Rain shadow areas: Deccan plateau interior, parts of Rayalseema, north Karnataka.

Jet streams: Tropical easterly jet (summer, over peninsular India — guides cyclones), Sub-tropical westerly jet (winter — brings western disturbances). Absence of subtropical westerly jet over northern India in summer is linked to monsoon onset.

Cyclones: Bay of Bengal more active than Arabian Sea due to warmer SST; cyclones form pre/post monsoon; recent: Amphan (2020), Fani (2019), Gurjar (2022). Mechanism involves warm SST (>26°C), Coriolis force, low vertical wind shear.

4. Soils of India

Soil classification in India is based on ICAR (Indian Council of Agricultural Research) taxonomy. Six major soil groups are relevant:

(a) Alluvial Soil (~43% of area): Most widespread. Composed of sediments deposited by rivers. Rich in potash, poor in phosphorus and nitrogen. Two types: Khadar (newer, more fertile, finer texture) and Bhangar (older, contains kankar nodules of CaCO₃). Ideal for cereal crops, sugarcane, pulses.

(b) Black Soil (Regur, ~15%): Deccan trap origin (basalt weathering). High in Fe, Mg, Ca, alumina; low in P, N, humus. Expansive when wet (self-aeration/cracks). Best suited for cotton (Black Cotton Soil), also soybean, sugarcane, wheat. Found in Maharashtra, MP, Gujarat, parts of KA and TN.

(c) Red Soil (~27%): Formation from crystalline igneous rocks under high temperature/low rainfall. Red colour from Fe diffusion in crystalline rocks; yellow where moisture present. Low in N, humus, P; rich in K. Needs irrigation and fertilizers. Found in parts of TN, Karnataka, Odisha, Jharkhand, Maharashtra (eastern). Laterite forms from intense leaching under high rainfall — high in Fe/Al oxides, low in nutrients. Used for brick-making. Found in high-rainfall areas: Western GH slopes, NE states, parts of Odisha, TN.

(d) Arid Soil (~11%): Found in W Rajasthan, parts of Gujarat, Haryana, UP. Sandy texture, saline (high NaCl/KCl), dry. Lacks humus and water-holding capacity. Irrigation needed for cultivation. Natron accumulation common.

(e) Forest Soil: Found in hilly/mountainous regions (Himalayas, Western/Eastern Ghats). Variable texture (sand to clay), rich in humus in lower valleys, acidic and low in humus in upper hills. Used for tea, coffee, spices, pulses, fruits.

(f) Peaty/Marshy Soil: Heavy, black, organic-rich, humid. Found in NW Kerala, parts of GBPUR, deltaic regions. Poor in N, K; high organic matter; highly acidic.

Key matching fact often asked: Regur = black, Alluvial = khadar/bhangar, Laterite = laterisation/leaching, Arid = saline/natron.

Soil conservation methods: Contour ploughing, terracing, strip cropping, shelterbelts, mulching, check dams, afforestation. Soil degradation causes: deforestation, overgrazing, improper irrigation (salinisation — biggest in NW India canal command areas, waterlogging in Ganga-Yamuna doab).

5. Natural Vegetation and Wildlife

India's vegetation is classified by duration of growing season and rainfall. About 24.6% of land area is under forest cover (ISFR 2021). The National Green Hydrogen Mission targets 5 MMT/year green hydrogen by 2030 — related conceptually to eco-sustainability.

Types of natural vegetation (classified by annual rainfall):

(a) Tropical Evergreen Forests (>200 cm rainfall, temp 15–30°C): Dense, multi-layered, no definite felling season. Trees: rosewood, ebony, mahogany, rubber, cinchona. Found in: W Ghats slopes, NE states (Assam, Meghalaya), Odisha parts, Andaman & Nicobar. Wild life: elephants, monkeys, deer, one-horned rhino (Assam), birdlife diverse.

(b) Tropical Deciduous Forests (100–200 cm rainfall): Most widespread. Two sub-types:
- Moist deciduous: teak, sal, shisham, aks, mahua, amaltas. Found in: wetter parts of Peninsula, foothills of Himalayas, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, MP.
- Dry deciduous: tendu, palas, bel, semul, sandalwood. Found in: rainier parts of Ganga plain, plateau edges.
Teak is most important commercial species.

(c) Tropical Thorn and Scrub (<70 cm rainfall): Xerophytic — deep roots, small leaves, waxy coating. Species: cactus, date palm, neem, khejri, babul. Found in: Rajasthan, Gujarat, MP, Haryana, PN, Karnataka, TN interiors.

(d) Tropical Semi-evergreen and Moist Thorn: Transitional zones in fragmented areas.

(e) Montane (Temperate) Forests:
- Temperate evergreen (W Himalaya, 1,000–2,000 m): pine, deodar, silver fir, spruce, cedar.
- Temperate deciduous (2,000–3,000 m): oak, chestnut, birch (kail, bhur).
- Alpine (above 3,000 m): juniper, rhododendron, shrubs; meadows in summer. Above treeline = alpine pasture (bugyals).

(f) Mangrove Forests: Tidal coastal forests, salt-tolerant. Sundari (Heritiera fomes) gives Sundarbans its name. Found in Ganga-Brahmaputra delta, Mahanadi, Kaveri deltas. Wildlife: Bengal tiger, crocodiles, sea turtles. Roots: pneumatophores (breathing roots).

National Parks (frequently asked): Sundarbans (WB — deltaic, tiger), Kaziranga (Assam — one-horned rhino), Keoladeo Ghana (Bharatpur, RB — bird sanctuary, deer), Manas (Assam — tiger, rhino, wild dog), Corbett (UK — first national park, 1936, tiger), Ranthambore (RB — tiger), Hemis (Ladakh — snow leopard), Valley of Flowers (UK — alpine flora), Nanda Devi (UK — biosphere reserve, alpines), Gir (GJ — Asiatic lion only remaining wild population), Periyar (KL — tiger, elephant), Bhitarkanika (OB — saltwater crocodile, Ramsar), Kanha (MP — tiger, barasingha), Dudhwa (UP — tiger, barasingha), Bandipur (KA — Project Tiger), Nagarhole (KA), Sundarbans (WB). Biosphere Reserves: Nilgiri, Nanda Devi, Sundarbans, Gulf of Mannar, Pachmarhi, Simlipal, Nokrek, Kangchenjunga, Agasthyamalam, Manipur, Great Nicobar — 18 total (as of recent updates).

Wildlife Protection Act: 1972. Schedule I animals get maximum protection (tiger, rhino, elephant, etc.). CITES: international trade in endangered species.

6. Agriculture and Cropping Patterns

Agriculture remains the largest employer in India. ~52% of workforce engaged. Contribution to GDP declining (~18%).

Types of farming:
- Subsistence farming: small landholdings, traditional methods, family labour. Divided into primitive subsistence (slash-and-burn/jhumming in NE hills — declining due to govt. ban) and intensive subsistence (high density, manual labour, multiple cropping).
- Commercial farming: plantation (tea, coffee, rubber, sugarcane — single crop, capital-intensive, export-oriented). Also includes dairy, poultry (White Revolution — Operation Flood).
- Modern/intensive farming with HYVs, fertilisers, irrigation — post-Green Revolution (1960s, NW India: Punjab, Haryana, western UP).

Major crops:
- Food crops: Rice (kharif, requires 100–200 cm rain, temp 20–35°C, standing water) — major producers: WB, UP, AP, TN, TS, PB, Odisha. Wheat (rabi, 10–15°C germination, 50–75 cm rain) — PB, UP, HR, RB, MP. Millets (jowar, bajra, ragi — hardy, dryland) — MP, MA, KA, RJ, BR, upland areas. Pulses (protein source, N-fixing, rotate with cereals) — MP (largest producer), RAJ, MH, UP, WB. Legumes like pigeon pea (arhar/tur), gram (chana), lentil (masoor).
- Cash crops: Sugarcane (Uttar Pradesh top producer, then MA, KA, TN, RJ; requires 21–27°C, 75–100 cm rain, 10–12 month maturation) — India is world's largest producer. Cotton (kharif, black soil, 21–30°C, 50–100 cm rain, frost-free 210+ days) — GJ, MH, GUJ (Saurashtra), TN, AP. Tea (assam, darjeeling, nilgiris, dooars, sikkim; 15–30°C, 150–250 cm rain, well-drained slopes) — WB, Assam, TN, KL, NL (Sikkim). Coffee (Arabica ~50%, Robusta ~50%; 15–28°C, 120–200 cm rain) — KA (Mysore, Coorg, Chikmagalur), TN (Nilgiris), PL. Rubber (Kerala top, then TN, Kerala, NL) — requires 20–33°C, 150–250 cm, well-distributed. Jute ("Golden Fibre") — WB top, also AS, BP, OD; requires hot climate, standing water during growth, well-drained fertile soil for retting. Tobacco — AP (Guntur), MA, GUJ, OR. Horticulture — India is world's largest producer of fruits (mango, banana, papaya, citrus) and vegetables (potato, onion, tomato). Spices — Kerala, TN, WL, Assam.

Irrigation: Canal irrigation (NW India, UP, PB, HR), tank irrigation (peninsular — DL, TN, KA — traditional, rain-fed), wells/tube wells (UP, PB, HR, WB — groundwater), drip/sprinkler (GJ, RAJ, ML — water-saving).

Government schemes: PM-KISAN (income support ₹6,000/yr), NMSMA (mission for sustainable agriculture), RKVY, Paramparagat Krishi Vikas Yojana (organic), Soil Health Card Scheme, Kisan Drone initiative, Agri-Infrastructure Fund.

Green Revolution: NW India, 1960s; wheat yield jumped from 10 to 30 MT/ha; Norman Borlaug; led to Punjab, Haryana, WUP transformation; also Green Revolution in rice in TN, AP. Localised — less success in eastern/eastern regions due to agro-climatic constraints.

7. Mineral and Energy Resources

Minerals are grouped as metallic, non-metallic, and energy minerals.

Ferrous minerals:
- Iron ore: India is 4th largest producer. Types: Hematite (best quality, >60% Fe), Magnetite (highest grade, magnetic), Limonite (40–60% Fe), Siderite (low grade). Major belts: Chota Nagpur plateau (Odisha-Jharkhand — Bailadila, Noamundi, Barbil, Gua, Kendujhar, Singhbhum), Durg-Bastar-Chandrapur (Chhattisgarh), Bellary-Chitradurga-Kumbaralta (Karnataka), Salem-Dindigul (TN), Sundargarh-Keonjhar (Odisha).
- Manganese: used in steel-making, ferro-manganese. Major: MP, MH, OR, KA, TN, Jharkhand.
- Bauxite (aluminium ore): Laterite-derived. Major: Odisha (largest producer — Koraput, Kalahandi, Bolangir), Jharkhand, MA, GUJ, TN, Chhattisgarh. ALCOA, HINDALCO, NALCO, BALCO are major PSUs.

Non-ferrous minerals:
- Copper: Balaghat (MP) — major open-cast mine; Hindustan Copper Ltd (Jharkhand — Khetri, Malanjkhand); also Rajasthan (Dariba, Raivasan).
- Lead/Zinc: Rajasthan ( Rampura-Aga — largest lead-zinc deposit, Zawar), UP (Lachhpur), Nepal (export via). Galena (PbS), Sphalerite (ZnS).
- Gold: Karnataka (Kolar, Hutti — largest producer), Jharkhand (Rammin, Mohark), Andhra Pradesh.
- Tin (cassiterite): Only in Khondalite belt of TN.
- Uranium: Jaduguda (Jharkhand), Narora (UP). Used in nuclear energy.
- Thorium: Monazite sands — Kerala (Travancore), TN coasts. India has large thorium reserves for 3-stage nuclear programme.

Non-metallic:
- Mica: Bihar (Koderma — "mica capital"), Jharkhand, Andhra Pradesh, Rajasthan. Sheet mica, scrap mica.
- Limestone: Used in cement. Major: MP, KA, RJ, AP, OR, TG.
- Gypsum: RJ (Bhinmal), GJ.
- Phosphate: Rajasthan (Jaisalmer), MP, TN.
- Coal: Classified by carbon content — Anthracite (>86%), Bituminous (68–86%), Sub-bituminous, Lignite (lowest, brown coal — TN, Gujarat, PG). Major reserves: Jharkhand (Jharia — oldest and largest, Gondwana), Odisha (Mahanadi basin), Chhattisgarh (Mahanadi, Son valley), MP (Son valley), Assam (Rangpo), TN (Neyveli lignite), CG (Hasdaypur). Coal India Ltd (CIL) is world's largest coal producer company.
- Petroleum/Natural gas: Oil fields — Mumbai High (KG basin, offshore, largest), Digboi (AS — oldest, 1901), Naharkatia, Moran, Jhum Tili, Guwahati (AS); Mangala, Baghnapur, Barmer (RJ — Rajasthan state oilfield), KG basin (AP), Cambay (GJ). ONGC, OIL, IOC, HPCL, BPCL, RIL (Reliance) are key players. Natural gas: KG basin, Assam, Mumbai High, Rajasthan. Pipelines: Hazira-Jagdishpur-Vijaipur (HVJ).
- Nuclear power: Tarapur (MH — first, 1969, US-supplied), Rawatbhata (RJ), Kakrapaar (GJ), Kudankulam (TN — Russian VVER), Narora (UP), Kaiga (KA), Kota (RJ), Haripur (WB under construction).
- Renewable energy: Solar — Gujarat (Bhadla Solar Park largest in India, 2.2 GW), Rajasthan (Khavda), Tamil Nadu, MP. Wind — Tamil Nadu (Muppandal wind farm), Gujarat, MA, KA. Bioenergy — bagasse, biogas. Hydro — Bhakra Nangal, Tehri, Hirakud, Sardar Sarovar, Nagarjunsagar, Tungabhadra, Koyna.

Strategic minerals: Rare earths, lithium (recently discovered in RE — Renuka, Karnataka; also J&K).

8. Population and Demography

India is the most populous country in the world (2023 estimate ~1.43 billion, crossing China). Census data: 2011 census is latest official; 2021 census was delayed.

Demographic indicators (2011 Census):
- Population: 1,210,854,977. Growth rate (decadal 2001–2011): 17.7% (declining trend).
- Sex ratio: 940 females per 1,000 males (improved from 933 in 2001). Child sex ratio (0–6 yrs): 914 (concerning decline from 927 in 2001).
- Literacy: 74.04% (male 82.14%, female 65.46%). Kerala highest (93.91%), Bihar lowest (63.82%).
- Density: 382 persons/km² (UP 829, Bihar 1,102, WB 1,029, AR 76 — lowest among large states).
- Urbanization: 31.16% urban. Megacities: Mumbai, Delhi, Kolkata, Bangalore, Chennai, Hyderabad.

States by population (top): UP (~200M), MH (~112M), BR (~104M), WB (~91M), MP (~72M). Least populated: Sikkim, Arunachal Pradesh, Goa.

Migration: Internal migration (rural-to-urban, rural-to-rural for agricultural work). International migration patterns: Gulf countries (UAE, Saudi, Qatar), USA, UK, Canada. Remittances significant contributor to economy.

Age structure: Young population — median age ~28 years. Demographic dividend window estimated 2005–2055. Dependency ratio: ~50%. Working-age population (15–59) ~67%.

Health indicators: IMR (infant mortality rate) ~27 (2021 Est), MMR (maternal mortality ratio) ~97 per lakh live births. NFHS-5 (2019–21): TFR (Total Fertility Rate) 2.0 — replacement level.

Population policies: National Population Policy 2000; target achieving stable population by 2047. Family welfare programmes, health missions (NHM — National Health Mission), Ayushman Bharat.

Tribal population (~8.6% of total): Concentrated in central India (MP, CG, Jharkhand, Odisha), NE states, Andaman. Largest tribal groups: Gond, Bhil, Santhal, Oraon, Munda, Bhuiya. Scheduled Tribes enjoy constitutional safeguards (Article 244, 5th/6th Schedule).

Census: Conducted every 10 years since 1881 (first complete census under British — Sir Robert Bruce Porter; real census began 1872 under Lord Mayo, first comprehensive 1881 by William Wilson Hunter). Registrar General and Census Commissioner of India is headquartered in New Delhi.

9. Industry and Economic Geography of India

Industrial location depends on raw material, power, transport, market, labour, and policy incentives.

Major industrial regions:
- Mumbai-Pune region: Cotton textiles (historical — "Manchester of India"), petrochemical (Panvel, Jafrabad), pharmaceuticals (Pune), IT (Pune, Mumbai), engineering. Key cities: Mumbai, Pune, Nashik, Thane.
- Kolkata-Varanasi region: Jute (world's largest jute-producing region — Hooghly basin), textiles, engineering, heavy chemicals. Declining due to jute competition, political issues. Also Durgapur (steel), Bokaro (steel), Rourkela (steel — Odisha), Berhampur (paper).
- Chennai-Coimbatore region: Automobiles (Chennai = "Detroit of Asia"), textiles (Coimbatore = "Manchester of South India"), engineering, leather, IT.
- Bangalore-Mysore region: IT hub (Electronic City, Whitefield), aerospace (HAL, DRDO), electronics, textile, silk.
- Delhi-NCR: Diverse — pharmaceuticals, textiles, software, food processing, automobiles (Manesar, Faridabad).
- Gujarat: Petrochemicals (Jamnagar — Reliance, world's largest refinery complex), diamonds (Surat — 90% of world's diamond cutting/polishing), textiles, chemicals, ports (Kandla, Mundra).
- Rajkot-Ahmedabad corridor: Engineering, ceramics, textiles.

Iron and Steel:
- First plant: Tata Steel (Jamshedpur, Jharkhand — 1907, formerly IISCO).
- Other major plants: Rourkela (Odisha — German collaboration, 1959), Bhilai (Chhattisgarh — Soviet, 1959), Durgapur (WB — 1959), Bokaro (Jharkhand — Soviet, 1965), Vijayanagar (KA), Visakhapatnam (AP), Salem (TN), Hirakud (Odisha), Anuppur (MP), Burnpur (WB).
- SAIL (Steel Authority of India Ltd) is main public sector.

Cotton Textile: Mumbai, Pune, Ahmedabad, Surat, Coimbatore, Erode, Ludhiana, Kanpur. Shift to synthetic fibres, powerlooms.

Silk Textile: Varanasi (Brocade, Banarasi), Mysore (silk), Assam (Muga, Eri), West Bengal (Dhakai). Sericulture.

Sugar: UP (largest), Maharashtra (second), BR, TN, Haryana, PB. Seasonal industry — crushing season Oct–Mar.

Petroleum Refining: Mumbai High, Jamnagar (GJ), Guwahati (AS), Vadodara (GJ), Paradip (OP), Kochi (KL), Panipat (HR), Bongaigaon (AS), Mathura (UP).

Pharmaceuticals: Hyderabad (Genome Valley), Mumbai, Ahmedabad, Delhi, Bangalore. India = "Pharmacy of the world" — largest generic drug producer.

IT/Software: Bangalore, Hyderabad, Pune, Noida/Gurgaon, Chennai, Mumbai, Kochi (InfoPark), Surat (software tech park). SEZs and STPIs.

Automobile: Chennai, Pune, Delhi-NCR, Hyderabad, Mumbai, Lucknow, Indore. Maruti, Hyundai, TATA, Bajaj, Mahindra, Ashok Leyland.

Port sectors: Major ports (12) — Kandla, Mormugao, Mumbai, JNPT, Mangalore, Kochi, Tuticorin, Chennai, Kamarajar, Visakhapatnam, Paradip, Haldia/DHamra. Mini ports numerous. Container ports: JNPT (largest), Mundra (private, largest), Sagar (private), Chennai.

Major canals: Indira Gandhi Canal (originates Harike barrage, Punjab — feeds Thar desert — 649 km), Upper Ganga Canal (Hardwar), Buckingham Canal (TN-AP coastal). Irrigation commands: Sardar Sarovar (Narmada), Bhakra Nangal (Satluj), Hirakud (Mahanadi), Nagarjuna Sagar (Krishna), Tungabhadra, Krishnaraja Sagara (Kaveri).

10. World Geography — Key Concepts and Facts

Continents and Oceans:
- Seven continents by area: Asia (44.6M km² — largest, most populous), Africa (30.4M), N. America (24.7M), S. America (17.8M), Antarctica (14.2M — coldest, driest, highest avg. elevation), Europe (10.2M), Australia/Oceania (8.6M).
- Four oceans: Pacific (largest, deepest — Mariana Trench 11,034 m), Atlantic (second), Indian (third — entirely in E hemisphere), Arctic (smallest, shallowest, mostly ice-covered).
- Three great circulation systems: Gulf Stream (Atlantic, warms W Europe), Kuroshio (Pacific, warms Japan), Antarctic Circumpolar Current.

Physical Geography Highlights:
- Longest rivers: Nile (6,650 km, Africa), Amazon (6,400 km — largest discharge, S. America), Yangtze (6,300 km, China), Mississippi-Missouri (6,275 km).
- Largest deserts: Sahara (N. Africa — 9.2M km²), Arabian, Thar (India/Pakistan), Gobi (China/Mongolia), Kalahari (S. Africa), Simpson (Australia), Atacama (Chile — driest), Antarctica (cold desert — largest overall).
- Highest mountains: Himalaya (Everest 8,848m), Karakoram (K2 8,611m), Andes (Aconcagua 6,961m — highest outside Asia), Rockies (Mount Elbert 4,401m), Alps (Mont Blanc 4,808m), Ural (Mount Narodnaya 1,895m), Scandinavian.
- Major plateaus: Tibetan ("Roof of the World" — 4,500 m avg.), Deccan, Iranian, Anatolian, Colorado, Columbia, Ethiopian, South African (Highveld), Australian.
- Largest lakes: Caspian Sea (saltwater, world's largest enclosed body of water — 371,000 km²), Victoria (Africa — second largest freshwater), Huron, Michigan, Superior (North America), Baikal (deepest, 1,642 m — Russia), Titicaca (highest navigable, S. America).
- Longest mountain ranges: Andes (7,000 km — S. America), Mid-ocean ridges (global — 65,000 km), Rocky Mountains (4,800 km), Alps-Himalaya chain.
- Deltas: Nile (bird's foot), Ganga-Brahmaputra (Sundarbans — largest), Mekong, Niger, Yangtze (Chang Jiang), Mississippi, Indus, Kaveri.

Climate Zones (Köppen):
- A: Tropical wet (rainforest — Congo, Amazon, W. India).
- B: Dry/arid (Sahara, Arabian, Thar, Kalahari, Australian interior).
- C: Temperate (Mediterranean — CA, Chile, S. Africa, S. Australia, W. Europe; humid subtropical — E. USA, E. China, S. Korea, S. India, S. Africa).
- D: Continental (Russia, Canada, N. China, N. USA — warm summer vs cold summer variants).
- E: Polar (Antarctica, Arctic, Tundra, Ice cap).
- H: Highland (mountain climates).

Monsoon regions: Indian monsoon, East Asian monsoon (China, Japan, Korea), Australian monsoon (NW Australia), West African monsoon, North American monsoon (SW USA/Mexico).

Plate tectonics: India colliding with Eurasia (~50 Ma) created Himalayas. Ring of Fire (Pacific margin) — 75% of world's volcanoes, 90% of earthquakes. Mid-oceanic ridges — divergent boundaries. Subduction zones — convergent. Transform faults — San Andreas.

Strategic waterways: Suez Canal (Egypt — connects Med to Red Sea, shortcut Europe-Asia), Panama Canal (links Atlantic-Pacific), Strait of Hormuz (oil chokepoint), Strait of Malacca ( busiest shipping lane), Bab-el-Mandeb, Bosporus/Dardanelles, English Channel, Dover Strait, Gibraltar, Bass Strait, Torres Strait.

International Date Line: Runs along ~180° meridian; deviates to avoid land masses. West side ahead, east side behind. Samoa, Kiribati recently shifted date line side.

Time zones: India uses IST (UTC+5:30) — based on 82.5°E (Mirzapur near Allahabad). Countries like Nepal (UTC+5:45), Sri Lanka (UTC+5:30), Iran (UTC+3:30), Afghanistan (UTC+4:30) use half-hour offsets.

11. Indian Geography — Environment, Disasters and Maps

Environmental geography is a major NDA/CDS topic area.

Natural hazards:
- Earthquakes: India lies in seismic zones V (highest risk — NE India, Andaman, Kashmir, Kutch, parts of NW India) and IV (Delhi, Mumbai, parts of UP, Bihar). Bhuj (2001, M7.7), Chamoli (1999), Sikkim (2011, M6.9), Nepal (2015 — affected J&K, HP, Uttarakhand), Latur (1993, Maharashtra — intraplate), Kashmir (2005). IMD operates National Seismological Network. BIS seismic zoning map is reference.
- Landslides: Occur in Himalayan foothills (UT, HP, SL, SK), Western Ghats (MH, KA, KL, TN), Eastern Ghats (OR, JS), NE states. Controlled by slope, rainfall, vegetation, geology. Mitigation: afforestation, terracing, drainage control.
- Floods: Annual event — Bihar, WB, Assam, UP, Haryana, PB, MP. Causes: heavy rainfall, snowmelt, siltation, deforestation, encroachment of floodplains, barrages. Brahmaputra, Ganga, Teesta, Kosi, Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna.
- Cyclones: Bay of Bengal more active. Pre-monsoon (May–Jun) and post-monsoon (Oct–Nov) peaks. IMD issues warnings via NRSC (Hyderabad). Affected states: Odisha, AP, WB, TN, GJ.
- Droughts: Rajasthan, Gujarat, MP, MA, KA, TN (rainfall failure). IMD classifies drought: moderate (26–50% deficiency), severe (51–90%), extreme (>90%).
- Tsunami: Dec 26, 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami (Sumatra 9.1–9.3M) — affected TN, Kerala, AP, Andaman & Nicobar (most severely). National Tsunami Warning Centre at INCOIS, Hyderabad.

Environmental issues:
- Air pollution: Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai, Ahmedabad top polluted cities (AQI). Sources: vehicular emissions, construction dust, industrial, biomass burning. National Clean Air Programme (2019). AQI categories: Good (0–50), Satisfactory (51–100), Moderate (101–200), Poor (201–300), Very Poor (301–400), Severe (401+).
- Water pollution: Ganga (Namami Gange Programme), Yamuna, Buckingham Canal. Sources: industrial effluents, sewage, agricultural runoff.
- Deforestation: ~1–2% annual loss historically; now slowing. Causes: logging, mining, infrastructure, agriculture. Impacts: soil erosion, loss of biodiversity, climate change.
- Desertification: Rajasthan, Gujarat, parts of MP and MAR. ICAR has Desert Ecology Research Institute (Jodhpur).
- Ozone depletion: Montreal Protocol (1987) phased out CFCs. Ozone hole over Antarctica monitored by NASA/NOAA.
- Climate change: IPCC reports (latest AR6, 2021–23). India's commitments: 500 GW renewable energy by 2030, net-zero by 2070, National Action Plan on Climate Change (NAPCC — 8 missions).

Protected area statistics: ~104 National Parks, ~566 Wildlife Sanctuaries, ~27 Biosphere Reserves (as of ISFR 2021). Total protected area ~5.68% of land area. tiger reserves: 56 (Project Tiger, 1973 — NDMA headquarters in Delhi; National Disaster Management Authority headed by PM).

Ramsar sites (wetlands of international importance): India has 70+ Ramsar sites (as of 2023). Include: Sundarbans, Chilika, Wular, Loktak, Keoladeo, Pulicat, Kuttanad, Vembanad, Nal sarovar, Sambhar, Dahanu-Gopalganj, Bakhreshwar Wetland.

Important passes: Nathu La (Sikkim-China border, reopened 2006), Bomdila (AR), Shipki La (HP-China), Lipulekh (UK- China — disputed), Karakoram Pass (PoK-China), Zoji La (J&K), Rohtang Pass (HP), Palghat (KL-TN), Thalakottai (not a pass, confusion), Nathu La (historically Silk Road). Zojila connects Kashmir valley to Ladakh.

Important facts

  • Highest peak in India (claimed): K2/Godwin Austen 8,611 m (in PoK, administered by Pak); Kanchenjunga 8,586 m is highest fully within India.
  • Longest river in India: Ganga (2,525 km). Longest peninsular river: Godavari (1,465 km). Longest west-flowing river: Narmada (1,312 km). Longest east-flowing peninsular river: Godavari.
  • Mawsynram (Meghalaya) receives the highest annual rainfall in the world (~11,872 mm). Cherrapunji is second.
  • India has 75,166 km land boundary and 7,516.6 km coastline (mainland). Islands add ~2,307 km coastline (A&N + Lakshadweep).
  • Tamil Nadu has the longest coastline among mainland states (≈1,076 km). Gujarat has the longest overall (including islands).
  • Sundarbans delta is the largest delta in the world. Ganga-Brahmaputra delta spans India and Bangladesh.
  • Guru Shikhar (1,722 m) is the highest peak of the Aravalli range. Anamudi (2,695 m) is the highest peak of the Western Ghats and South India.
  • Palk Strait separates India (TN) from Sri Lanka. Pamban Bridge (Rameswaram-Islamtelev) is the second-longest sea bridge in India.
  • Indira Gandhi Canal (649 km) originates at Harike barrage (PB) and irrigates Thar Desert. It is the longest canal in India.
  • Bharatpur Bird Sanctuary (Keoladeo) is a Ramsar site and UNESCO World Heritage Site. Known for Siberian cranes (now rare).
  • Loktak Lake (Manipur) is known for phumdis (floating biomass). Keenan island is here.
  • Sambhar Lake (Rajasthan) is India's largest inland salt lake. Major salt production centre.
  • Wular Lake (J&K) is a large freshwater lake formed by glacial activity. One of Asia's largest.
  • Diu and Diu Island were Portuguese territory until 1961 (liberation). Dadra and Nagar Haveli merged with Daman & Diu in 2020 to form UT of DNH-DD.
  • Andaman & Nicobar Islands: 572 islands (approx.), 38 inhabited. Straddles equator. Only active volcano: Barren Island.
  • Lakshadweep: 36 islands, 10 inhabited. Originally called Laccadive, Minicoy, and Amindivi islands. Renamed Lakshadweep in 1973.
  • India's standard meridian: 82°30'E passing through Mirzapur (near Allahabad/Prayagraj). Basis for IST (UTC+5:30).
  • India extends from 8°4′N (Indira Point, Big Andaman — southernmost point, submerged partially in 2004 tsunami) to 37°6′N (near China border in J&K). Latitudinal extent ≈28°32'. Longitudinal extent ≈30°44'.
  • Indira Point (6°45′N) is the southernmost point of India. It was named after Indira Gandhi and submerged partially after 2004 tsunami.
  • Line of Actual Control (LAC) separates Indian and Chinese administered territories in the Himalayan region. Eastern sector (Arunachal/NEFA), Central sector (UP/UK), Western sector (PoK/Ladakh).
  • Line of Control (LoC) separates Indian-administered J&K from Pakistani-administered PoK. Ceasefire line upgraded to LoC in 1972 Simla Agreement.
  • Sir Creek is a 90 km tidal bay in Rann of Kutch — disputed between India and Pakistan.
  • Kalapani, Lipulekh, Limpiyadhura — disputed territory with China in Uttarakhand. India issued new map in 2020 incorporating these areas.
  • Kachin, Kachin state border issue with Myanmar — Tuting, Kibithu areas occupied by China (Arunachal Pradesh). Claimed by India as part of Arunachal Pradesh.
  • Siachen Glacier (63 km long, 4,300–6,000 m elevation) — world's highest battlefield. Indo-Pak ceasefire line (Ceasefire Line) established 2003.
  • Nathu La pass (4,310 m) connects Sikkim to Tibet. Reopened in 2006 for trade. Historical Silk Road route.
  • Zoji La (3,524 m) connects Srinagar to Leh via NH-1. Opened in summer; closed in winter due to snow.
  • Rohtang Pass (3,978 m) connects Kullu Valley to Lahaul-Spiti. Strategic road link. Named after rohtang (local name for wild rose).
  • Palghat Gap (60 m) is the lowest pass in Western Ghats, connecting Kerala and Tamil Nadu. Major rail and highway corridor.
  • Thal Ghat (74 km tunnel under construction) and Bhor Ghat (old route) connect Mumbai to Pune via Western Ghats.
  • India shares borders with 7 countries: Pakistan (W), China (N-NE), Nepal (N), Bhutan (NE), Bangladesh (E), Myanmar (E), Afghanistan (thin corridor via Siachen — disputed). Sea borders with Sri Lanka, Maldives, Indonesia, Thailand, Vietnam, Malaysia.
  • Palk Bay separates India (TN) from Sri Lanka. Only 30 km wide at narrowest point. Gulf of Mannar marine biosphere reserve (UNESCO) lies between.
  • Gulf of Kutch and Gulf of Khambhat are important indentations on west coast. Gulf of Khambhat has one of world's highest tidal ranges (~9 m).
  • Luni River is the only major river in Rajasthan that flows into the Rann of Kutch (endorheic basin). It does not reach the sea.
  • Sabarmati River flows through Ahmedabad. Ashram (Gandhi's satyagraha base) located on its bank.
  • Tapi and Narmada flow through rift valleys. Narmada flows west; Tapi flows west but parallel and south of Narmada.
  • Damodar River (Jharkhand/WB) was called 'Sorrow of Bengal' due to devastating floods. Damodar Valley Corporation (DVC) — first multipurpose river valley project (1948).
  • Godavari basin is called Dakshin Ganga. Largest peninsular river by basin area (312,812 km²).
  • Mahi River rises in MN (Madhya Pradesh), flows through RJ and GJ. An ethyl chloride plant at Mahi Dam is a notable fact sometimes asked.
  • Brahmaputra is known as Tsangpo in Tibet, Dihang in Arunachal, Jamuna in Bangladesh.
  • Yamuna is the longest tributary of Ganga. Originates Yamunotri glacier. Joins Ganga at Prayagraj (Sangam). Key cities: Delhi, Agra, Mathura.
  • Ganges basin covers 5 states: UK, HR, UP, BR, WB. Population ≈400 million in basin.
  • Son River (MP/BR) is the largest right-bank tributary of Ganga. Originates Amarkantak.
  • Chambal River (MP/RJ) is known for ravines (badlands). Gandhisagar, Jawahar Sagar, Rana Pratap Sagar projects on it.
  • Bhagirathi and Alaknanda converge at Devprayag to form Ganga. Five Confluences (Panch Prayag): Vishnu Prayag (Vishnugang+Tons), Nanda Prayag (Nandakini+Alaknanda), Karnaprayag (Pindar+Alaknanda), Rudra Prayag (Mandakini+Alaknanda), Devprayag (Bhagirathi+Alaknanda).
  • Kosi River originates from Tibetan sources (Sunkoshi, Tam Kosi, Likhu Khola). Known as 'Sorrow of Bihar'. Has changed course dramatically over centuries.
  • Teesta River (Sikkim/WB) — teesta barrage controversy with Bangladesh. Joins Brahmaputra in Bangladesh.
  • Indus system in India: only 3% of total basin is in India (rest in Pakistan). Critical for irrigation and hydropower.
  • Bhutan has 7 hydroelectric projects with India. Run-of-the-river without diversion storage capability mentioned in some treaties.
  • Neelum-Jhelum Hydroelectric Plant (Pakistan-administered Kashmir) — supplied power under Indus Waters Treaty (1960, mediated by World Bank). India got Eastern rivers (Satluj, Beas, Ravi); Pakistan got Western rivers (Indus, Jhelum, Chenab).
  • Sir Creek dispute: 90 km tidal marsh in Rann of Kutch between India and Pakistan. India claims中线; Pak claims west channel as border.
  • Nine Degree Channel separates Big Andaman from Little Andaman. Eight Degree Channel (10°N) separates Lakshadweep from Minicoy.
  • Ten Degree Channel (10°N) separates Andaman from Nicobar group.
  • Car Nicobar is the northernmost island of Nicobar group. Chowra island has a WWII Japanese fort.
  • Great Nicobar island has Indira Point — southernmost tip of India. Named after former PM Indira Gandhi.
  • Kavaratti is the capital of Lakshadweep UT. Agatti has international-standard airport.
  • Silvassa is the capital of DNH. Daman is the capital of former DD (now merged). Kavaratti is the capital of Lakshadweep.
  • Port Blair is the capital of A&N. It houses Cellular Jail (Kala Pani) — independent movement prison. Ross Island (Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose Island) and Viper Island are nearby.
  • Sagar Island (WB) is at the mouth of Hooghly river. Contains ancient temple. Ferry connectivity from mainland.
  • Havelock Island (Swaraj Dweep) and Neil Island (Shaheed Dweep) are popular tourist destinations in A&N. Radhanagar Beach (Havelock) ranked among best in Asia.
  • Minicoy island (Lakshadweep) is culturally distinct — Mahl language spoken. Closest to Maldives.
  • Bangaram, Pitti, Kadamat, Amini, Androth, Chethlath, Kalpeni, Agatti, Vallakkal, Suheli, Zavari, Kitlil, Kadmat — Lakshadweep islands.
  • Katchatheevu island — disputed with Sri Lanka. Given to Sri Lanka by INDIRA Gandhi in 70s. Fishing rights compromised.
  • India's continental shelf extent: 20 nautical miles (standard); extended to 350 nm in parts of Bay of Bengal and Arabian Sea approved by UN (2009, 2014). Critical for seabed mining (polymetallic nodules, cobalt crusts).
  • Deep Ocean Mission (₹4,000 cr, 2020–25) aims to explore deep-sea minerals, bio-prospecting, ocean mining. Monazite beach (KL) has thorium-bearing sands.
  • National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA) headquartered in New Delhi. Headed by Prime Minister. National Institute of Disaster Management (NIDM) also in Delhi.
  • National Institute of Hydrology (NIH) in Roorkee (UK). Works on hydrology, water resources.
  • India Meteorological Department (IMD) — headquarters New Delhi. Director-General is head. Established 1875. Responsible for weather forecasting, seismology, climatology.
  • National Centre for Medium Range Weather Forecasting (NCMRWF) in Mathura (UP). Issues 7-day forecasts.
  • India's only operational meteorological satellite series: INSAT/Kalpana-1/MetSat. GAGAN (GPS Aided Geo Augmented Navigation) — PRASANTH satellite-based augmentation.
  • WMO (World Meteorological Organization) headquarters: Geneva, Switzerland. India is a member state.
  • IPCC (Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change) — established 1988 by WMO and UNEP. HQ Geneva. Awards Nobel Peace Prize (2007). Latest report: AR6 (2021–23).
  • Kyoto Protocol (1997) — entered into force 2005. Canada withdrew (2011). Second commitment period 2013–2020. Paris Agreement (2015) replaced Kyoto framework.
  • Paris Agreement: 196 parties (as of 2023). Aim: limit warming to well below 2°C, pursue 1.5°C. NDCs (Nationally Determined Contributions) submitted every 5 years.
  • Montreal Protocol (1987) — phase out ODS (ozone depleting substances). CFCs, halons. Universal ratification (all UN members). Success story for environmental diplomacy.
  • Basel Convention (1989) — controls transboundary movements of hazardous wastes. India is a party. Plastic waste import ban (2020) relates to Basel amendments.
  • CITES (Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species) — Washington Convention 1973. 184 parties. Regulates trade in象牙, rhino horn, exotic pets, timber.
  • Ramsar Convention (1971) — wetlands of international importance. 172 parties, 2,400+ sites worldwide. India: 70+ sites (as of 2023). Convention HQ: Gland, Switzerland.
  • UNFCCC (United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change) — Rio Earth Summit 1992.HQ Geneva. Parent treaty for Kyoto, Paris.
  • CBD (Convention on Biological Diversity) — Rio 1992. 196 parties. Aichi Targets (2011–2020) replaced by Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework (2022): 30x30 target (protect 30% land and ocean by 2030).
  • UNCCD (UN Convention to Combat Desertification) — Paris 1994. HQ Bonn. India is a major proponent. CUDP (Clean Up Drylands Programme) initiated.
  • Stockholm Convention (2001) — persistent organic pollutants (POPs). HQ Geneva. India banned DDT, PCBs, dioxins.
  • Rotterdam Convention (1998) — prior informed consent for hazardous chemicals in international trade. HQ Geneva.
  • Helsinki Convention (1992) — Baltic Marine Environment Protection Commission. Not directly relevant to India but part of general environment awareness.
  • Convention on Migratory Species (Bonn Convention, 1979) — protects migratory animals. India hosts many migratory birds (Arctic terns, bar-headed geese).
  • CITES Appendix I: strictly prohibited trade (tigers, elephants, rhinos). Appendix II: regulated trade. India is a party since 1976.
