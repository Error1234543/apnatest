Fundamental Trigonometric Ratios and Their Definitions

Trigonometry deals with the relationship between angles and sides of triangles. The six fundamental trigonometric ratios are defined for an acute angle θ in a right-angled triangle:

• Sine (sin θ) = Perpendicular/Hypotenuse
• Cosine (cos θ) = Base/Hypotenuse
• Tangent (tan θ) = Perpendicular/Base = sin θ/cos θ
• Cosecant (cosec θ) = Hypotenuse/Perpendicular = 1/sin θ
• Secant (sec θ) = Hypotenuse/Base = 1/cos θ
• Cotangent (cot θ) = Base/Perpendicular = 1/tan θ

These ratios are only defined when the denominator is non-zero. For example, tan θ and sec θ are undefined at θ = 90° and 270°, while cot θ and cosec θ are undefined at θ = 0° and 180°.

The reciprocal relationships form the foundation: sin θ × cosec θ = 1, cos θ × sec θ = 1, and tan θ × cot θ = 1. These are frequently used in simplification problems.

For any angle θ measured from the positive x-axis in standard position, if point P(x, y) lies on the terminal side at distance r = √(x² + y²) from origin, then:
sin θ = y/r, cos θ = x/r, tan θ = y/x, and their reciprocals accordingly.

The sign of trigonometric ratios varies by quadrant:
- Quadrant I (0° to 90°): All positive
- Quadrant II (90° to 180°): sin and cosec positive only
- Quadrant III (180° to 270°): tan and cot positive only
- Quadrant IV (270° to 360°): cos and sec positive only

A mnemonic 'All Students Take Coffee' helps remember this: All (I), Sine (II), Tan (III), Cos (IV).

Trigonometric Identities and Their Derivations

Fundamental identities are derived from the Pythagorean theorem and are essential tools:

1. sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 (Primary identity)
Proof: In right triangle with hypotenuse r, perpendicular p, base b: sin θ = p/r, cos θ = b/r
sin²θ + cos²θ = p²/r² + b²/r² = (p² + b²)/r² = r²/r² = 1

2. 1 + tan²θ = sec²θ
Proof: Divide sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 by cos²θ
sin²θ/cos²θ + cos²θ/cos²θ = 1/cos²θ
tan²θ + 1 = sec²θ

3. 1 + cot²θ = cosec²θ
Proof: Divide sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 by sin²θ
sin²θ/sin²θ + cos²θ/sin²θ = 1/sin²θ
1 + cot²θ = cosec²θ

From these three primary identities, numerous derived identities can be obtained:

• sin²θ = 1 - cos²θ = (1-cos θ)(1+cos θ)
• cos²θ = 1 - sin²θ = (1-sin θ)(1+sin θ)
• tan²θ = sec²θ - 1
• cot²θ = cosec²θ - 1
• sin θ = ±√(1-cos²θ)
• cos θ = ±√(1-sin²θ)

Important product and sum-to-product identities:
• sin(A+B) = sin A cos B + cos A sin B
• sin(A-B) = sin A cos B - cos A sin B
• cos(A+B) = cos A cos B - sin A sin B
• cos(A-B) = cos A cos B + sin A sin B

Double angle formulas:
• sin 2θ = 2 sin θ cos θ
• cos 2θ = cos²θ - sin²θ = 2cos²θ - 1 = 1 - 2sin²θ
• tan 2θ = 2tan θ/(1-tan²θ)

Half angle formulas:
• sin θ/2 = ±√((1-cos θ)/2)
• cos θ/2 = ±√((1+cos θ)/2)
• tan θ/2 = sin θ/(1+cos θ) = (1-cos θ)/sin θ

General Solutions of Trigonometric Equations

A trigonometric equation is satisfied by infinitely many angles. The general solution gives all possible values.

For sin θ = sin α:
θ = nπ + (-1)ⁿα, where n ∈ Z
This means θ = α, π-α, 2π+α, 3π-α, etc.

For cos θ = cos α:
θ = 2nπ ± α, where n ∈ Z
This means θ = α, -α, 2π+α, 2π-α, etc.

For tan θ = tan α:
θ = nπ + α, where n ∈ Z
This means θ = α, π+α, 2π+α, etc.

Here α is called the principal value, usually taken in the interval [-π/2, π/2] for tan and [-π, π] for sin and cos.

Common equation types and their solutions:

Equation: sin θ = 0 → Solution: θ = nπ
Equation: cos θ = 0 → Solution: θ = (2n+1)π/2
Equation: tan θ = 0 → Solution: θ = nπ

For equations of form a sin θ + b cos θ = c:
Write as R sin(θ+α) = c where R = √(a²+b²) and tan α = b/a
This has solutions only if |c| ≤ R

For quadratic trigonometric equations like 2sin²θ - 3sin θ + 1 = 0:
Solve as quadratic in sin θ, then find θ using standard formulas

Multiple angle equations:
• sin 2θ = sin α → 2θ = nπ + (-1)ⁿα → θ = nπ/2 + (-1)ⁿα/2
• cos 2θ = cos α → 2θ = 2nπ ± α → θ = nπ ± α/2

Examination tip: When asked for solutions in a specific interval [0, 2π], substitute successive integer values of n until all solutions within the interval are found.

Inverse Trigonometric Functions

Since trigonometric functions are periodic, they are not one-to-one over their entire domain. Inverse trigonometric functions are defined by restricting domains to make them invertible.

Principal value branches:

• sin⁻¹x: Domain [-1,1], Range [-π/2, π/2]
Also written as arcsin x
• cos⁻¹x: Domain [-1,1], Range [0, π]
Also written as arccos x
• tan⁻¹x: Domain (-∞,∞), Range (-π/2, π/2)
Also written as arctan x
• cosec⁻¹x: Domain (-∞,-1]∪[1,∞), Range [-π/2,π/2]excluding 0
• sec⁻¹x: Domain (-∞,-1]∪[1,∞), Range [0,π]excluding π/2
• cot⁻¹x: Domain (-∞,∞), Range (0,π)

Key properties and formulas:

Reciprocal identities:
• sin⁻¹(1/x) = cosec⁻¹x for |x| ≥ 1
• cos⁻¹(1/x) = sec⁻¹x for |x| ≥ 1
• tan⁻¹(1/x) = cot⁻¹x for x > 0

Negative argument identities:
• sin⁻¹(-x) = -sin⁻¹x
• cos⁻¹(-x) = π - cos⁻¹x
• tan⁻¹(-x) = -tan⁻¹x

Sum formulas (valid within domain):
• sin⁻¹x + sin⁻¹y = sin⁻¹[x√(1-y²) + y√(1-x²)] if x²+y² ≤ 1
• cos⁻¹x + cos⁻¹y = cos⁻¹[xy - √(1-x²)√(1-y²)]
• tan⁻¹x + tan⁻¹y = tan⁻¹[(x+y)/(1-xy)] if xy < 1
• tan⁻¹x - tan⁻¹y = tan⁻¹[(x-y)/(1+xy)] if xy > -1

Important standard values:
• sin⁻¹(1/2) = π/6 or 30°
• cos⁻¹(1/2) = π/3 or 60°
• tan⁻¹(1) = π/4 or 45°
• sin⁻¹(√3/2) = π/3 or 60°
• cos⁻¹(√3/2) = π/6 or 30°
• tan⁻¹(√3) = π/3 or 60°

Properties of Triangles - Sine, Cosine, and Area Rules

For any triangle ABC with sides a, b, c opposite to angles A, B, C respectively:

SINE RULE:
a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C = 2R
where R is the circumradius of the triangle.

Uses: Finding unknown sides or angles when two angles and a side are known (AAS), or two sides and a non-included angle (SSA).

COSINE RULE:
c² = a² + b² - 2ab cos C
a² = b² + c² - 2bc cos A
b² = a² + c² - 2ac cos B

Uses: Finding the third side when two sides and included angle are known (SAS), or finding angles when all three sides are known (SSS).

TANGENT RULE (Napier's Analogy):
(tan((A-B)/2))/(tan((A+B)/2)) = (a-b)/(a+b)
(tan((B-C)/2))/(tan((B+C)/2)) = (b-c)/(b+c)
(tan((C-A)/2))/(tan((C+A)/2)) = (c-a)/(c+a)

AREA FORMULAS:
1. Area = ½bc sin A = ½ca sin B = ½ab sin C
2. Area = √[s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)] where s = (a+b+c)/2 (Heron's formula)
3. Area = abc/4R where R is circumradius
4. Area = rs where r is inradius
5. Area = 2R² sin A sin B sin C

PROJECTION FORMULA:
a = b cos C + c cos B
b = c cos A + a cos C
c = a cos B + b cos A

TRIANGLE IDENTITY:
In any triangle ABC: tan A + tan B + tan C = tan A · tan B · tan C
(Valid when none of angles is 90°)

HALF-ANGLE FORMULAS:
sin(A/2) = √[(s-b)(s-c)/bc]
cos(A/2) = √[s(s-a)/bc]
tan(A/2) = √[(s-b)(s-c)/s(s-a)]
where s = (a+b+c)/2

Heights and Distances - Applications

Heights and distances problems involve finding unknown heights or distances using trigonometric ratios. Key terms:

• Angle of elevation: Angle formed by line of sight above horizontal when looking up at an object
• Angle of depression: Angle formed by line of sight below horizontal when looking down at an object
• The angle of elevation equals the angle of depression when measured from corresponding positions

Basic setup: If a pole of height h casts a shadow of length d, then tan θ = h/d where θ is the angle of elevation of the sun.

Standard problem types:

1. Single pole with changing angle:
If a tower of height h subtends angles α and β at two points on ground at distances x and x+d from the foot:
h = d·tan α·tan β/(tan β - tan α)

2. Two poles with given separation:
If two poles of heights a and b are separated by distance d, and lines joining tops to opposite feet intersect at height h:
h = ab/(a+b)

3. Tower on building:
If a tower of height h stands on a building of height H, and angles of elevation of top and bottom from a point are α and β:
H = h·tan β/(tan α - tan β)

4. Cloud and river problems:
If angle of elevation of cloud from height h above lake is α and angle of depression of its reflection is β:
Distance of cloud = h(sin α + sin β)/(sin β - sin α) · cosec α

5. Wind effect problems:
If a pole leans at angle θ to vertical and subtends angle α at a point on ground, use sine rule in the triangle formed.

IMPORTANT NOTE: Always draw clear diagrams. Label all known angles, heights, and distances. Use the appropriate ratio based on which sides are known relative to the angle.

Graphs of Trigonometric Functions

Understanding graphs helps visualize behavior and solve equations graphically.

Y = SIN X:
• Period: 2π
• Amplitude: 1
• Range: [-1, 1]
• Passes through origin
• Odd function: sin(-x) = -sin x
• Key points: (0,0), (π/2,1), (π,0), (3π/2,-1), (2π,0)

Y = COS X:
• Period: 2π
• Amplitude: 1
• Range: [-1, 1]
• Y-intercept at (0,1)
• Even function: cos(-x) = cos x
• Key points: (0,1), (π/2,0), (π,-1), (3π/2,0), (2π,1)
• Cosine graph is sine graph shifted left by π/2

Y = TAN X:
• Period: π
• Range: (-∞, ∞)
• Vertical asymptotes at x = π/2 + nπ
• Odd function
• Passes through origin
• Increases from -∞ to ∞ in each period

GRAPH TRANSFORMATIONS:
• y = A sin(Bx + C) + D:
- Amplitude = |A|
- Period = 2π/|B|
- Phase shift = -C/B
- Vertical shift = D

• y = A cos(Bx + C) + D: Similar transformations

• y = A tan(Bx + C) + D:
- Period = π/|B|
- Phase shift = -C/B
- Vertical asymptotes at Bx + C = π/2 + nπ

COMMON GRAPH IDENTIFICATION QUESTIONS:
• Identify amplitude, period, phase shift from equation
• Sketch graph given key parameters
• Find equation from graph characteristics

Special Angles and Standard Values

Memorizing standard values for common angles is essential for quick computation:

Angle (degrees): 0° | 30° | 45° | 60° | 90° | 120° | 135° | 150° | 180°

sin values: 0 | 1/2 | 1/√2 | √3/2 | 1 | √3/2 | 1/√2 | 1/2 | 0
cos values: 1 | √3/2 | 1/√2 | 1/2 | 0 | -1/2 | -1/√2 | -√3/2 | -1
tan values: 0 | 1/√3 | 1 | √3 | undef | -√3 | -1 | -1/√3 | 0
cosec: undef | 2 | √2 | 2/√3 | 1 | 2/√3 | √2 | 2 | undef
sec: 1 | 2/√3 | √2 | 2 | undef | -2 | -√2 | -2/√3 | -1
cot: undef | √3 | 1 | 1/√3 | 0 | -1/√3 | -1 | -√3 | undef

MEMORIZATION TECHNIQUE:
For sin 0°, sin 30°, sin 45°, sin 60°, sin 90°:
√0/2, √1/2, √2/2, √3/2, √4/2 = 0, 1/2, 1/√2, √3/2, 1

For cos, reverse the sin sequence:
1, √3/2, 1/√2, 1/2, 0

For tan, divide sin by cos:
0, 1/√3, 1, √3, undefined

SUPPLEMENTARY ANGLE RELATIONSHIPS:
sin(180°-θ) = sin θ
cos(180°-θ) = -cos θ
tan(180°-θ) = -tan θ

COMPLEMENTARY ANGLE RELATIONSHIPS:
sin(90°-θ) = cos θ
cos(90°-θ) = sin θ
tan(90°-θ) = cot θ

These relationships help convert any angle to an acute angle reference.

Common Mistakes and Exam Strategies

FREQUENT ERRORS TO AVOID:

1. Sign errors in different quadrants:
Always determine the correct sign based on the quadrant before computing values.

2. Confusing inverse function with reciprocal:
sin⁻¹x ≠ 1/sin x. The former is the inverse function, the latter is cosec x.

3. Applying identities without checking conditions:
For example, tan⁻¹x + tan⁻¹y = tan⁻¹[(x+y)/(1-xy)] only when xy < 1.

4. Forgetting general solution format:
Always include 'nπ' or '2nπ' as appropriate, not just principal values.

5. Using wrong formula for area:
Remember Area = ½bc sin A requires the included angle, not any arbitrary angle.

6. Misapplying sine rule in ambiguous case:
When two sides and non-included angle are given (SSA), there may be 0, 1, or 2 solutions.

7. Ignoring domain restrictions:
sin⁻¹x and cos⁻¹x require |x| ≤ 1; tan⁻¹x accepts all real x.

EXAM STRATEGIES:

• For numerical problems: Always check if answer lies in expected range
• For identity proofs: Start from more complex side and simplify to match simpler side
• For equations: Find general solution first, then apply given constraints
• For triangle problems: Draw diagram, label everything, choose right tool
• For time management: Memorize standard values and practice shortcut methods

TIME-SAVING SHORTCUTS:
• For sin²θ + sin²θ·tan²θ = sec²θ (just recognize as 1+tan²θ pattern)
• For product-to-sum: sin A cos B = ½[sin(A+B) + sin(A-B)]
• For checking answers: Substitute special angles like 30°, 45°, 60° when possible

Important facts

  • sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 is the fundamental Pythagorean identity
  • General solution of sin θ = sin α is θ = nπ + (-1)ⁿα where n ∈ Z
  • General solution of cos θ = cos α is θ = 2nπ ± α where n ∈ Z
  • General solution of tan θ = tan α is θ = nπ + α where n ∈ Z
  • Sine rule: a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C = 2R where R is circumradius
  • Cosine rule: c² = a² + b² - 2ab cos C
  • Area of triangle = ½bc sin A = ½ca sin B = ½ab sin C
  • Heron's formula: Area = √[s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)] where s = (a+b+c)/2
  • sin⁻¹x has range [-π/2, π/2] and domain [-1, 1]
  • cos⁻¹x has range [0, π] and domain [-1, 1]
  • tan⁻¹x has range (-π/2, π/2) and domain all real numbers
  • Period of sin x and cos x is 2π; period of tan x is π
  • In any triangle: tan A + tan B + tan C = tan A · tan B · tan C
  • Projection formula: a = b cos C + c cos B
  • Half-angle: tan(A/2) = √[(s-b)(s-c)/s(s-a)]

Common mistakes

  • Confusing sin⁻¹x (inverse sine) with 1/sin x (reciprocal sine) - these are completely different functions
  • Forgetting that general solutions must include the integer parameter n (nπ or 2nπ as appropriate)
  • Applying tan⁻¹x + tan⁻¹y = tan⁻¹[(x+y)/(1-xy)] without checking if xy < 1 condition is satisfied
  • Using sine rule when cosine rule is needed (or vice versa) - remember sine rule needs angle-side pairs
  • Ignoring quadrant signs when computing trigonometric values for obtuse angles (90° to 180°)
  • Forgetting that tan θ and sec θ are undefined at θ = 90° and 270°, while cot θ and cosec θ are undefined at θ = 0° and 180°
  • Mixing up complementary angle relationships: sin(90°-θ) = cos θ, not sin θ
  • Forgetting the ½ factor in area formula: Area = ½bc sin A (not bc sin A)
  • Assuming ambiguous case (SSA) always gives one solution - it can give 0, 1, or 2 solutions
  • Not converting degrees to radians when working with calculus-based trigonometry problems
  • Applying double angle formula incorrectly: cos 2θ has three equivalent forms, choosing wrong one for the problem
  • Forgetting to check domain restrictions for inverse trigonometric functions (|x| ≤ 1 for sin⁻¹ and cos⁻¹)
