Trigonometry — Quick Revision for NDA & CDS

NDA & CDSEnglishQuick Revision

Definitions

  • Trigonometric Ratios (Basic): For an acute angle θ in a right triangle with perpendicular (opp), base (adj) and hypotenuse (hyp): sin θ = opp/hyp, cos θ = adj/hyp, tan θ = opp/adj; their reciprocals are cosec θ = hyp/opp, sec θ = hyp/adj, cot θ = adj/opp.
  • Trigonometric Identities: Equations involving trigonometric ratios of a variable angle that are true for ALL values of the angle for which both sides are defined. Primary: sin²θ + cos²θ = 1, 1 + tan²θ = sec²θ, 1 + cot²θ = cosec²θ.
  • Quadrant Sign Rules (ASTC): In Quadrant I (0–90°): All positive. QII (90–180°): sin positive only. QIII (180–270°): tan positive only. QIV (270–360°): cos positive only. Use this to determine the sign of any trig ratio for obtuse, reflex or negative angles.
  • Inverse Trigonometric Functions: Functions that return the angle given a trig ratio value. Principal branches: sin⁻¹x ∈ [–π/2, π/2], cos⁻¹x ∈ [0, π], tan⁻¹x ∈ (–π/2, π/2), cosec⁻¹x ∈ [–π/2, π/2] – {0}, sec⁻¹x ∈ [0, π] – {π/2}, cot⁻¹x ∈ (0, π). Domain of sin⁻¹ and cos⁻¹ is [–1, 1]; range of tan⁻¹ is ℝ.
  • General Solution of Trigonometric Equations: The set of ALL angles satisfying an equation, expressed using integer n. Key forms: sin θ = sin α ⇒ θ = nπ + (–1)ⁿα; cos θ = cos α ⇒ θ = 2nπ ± α; tan θ = tan α ⇒ θ = nπ + α, where n ∈ ℤ and α is any particular solution.
  • Law of Sines: In any triangle ABC with sides a, b, c opposite angles A, B, C: a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C = 2R, where R is the circumradius.
  • Law of Cosines: a² = b² + c² – 2bc cos A; b² = a² + c² – 2ac cos B; c² = a² + b² – 2ab cos C. Used to find a side when two sides and the included angle are known, or an angle when three sides are known.
  • Area of a Triangle (Trigonometric Form): Area = ½ bc sin A = ½ ca sin B = ½ ab sin C = √[s(s–a)(s–b)(s–c)] (Heron's formula), where s = (a+b+c)/2 is the semi-perimeter.

Key concepts

  • All Standard Angle Values: Memorise these exactly:
    • sin 0° = 0, sin 30° = 1/2, sin 45° = 1/√2, sin 60° = √3/2, sin 90° = 1
    • cos 0° = 1, cos 30° = √3/2, cos 45° = 1/√2, cos 60° = 1/2, cos 90° = 0
    • tan 0° = 0, tan 30° = 1/√3, tan 45° = 1, tan 60° = √3, tan 90° = undefined
    • cosec 30° = 2, sec 45° = √2, cot 60° = 1/√3
    • sin 18° = (√5–1)/4, cos 18° = √(10+2√5)/4, sin 36° = √(10–2√5)/4, cos 36° = (√5+1)/4 — occasional NDA questions.
    Quick pattern: sin θ values for 0°,30°,45°,60°,90° follow √0/2, √1/2, √2/2, √3/2, √4/2.
  • Compound Angle Formulae (Sum/Difference): sin(A±B) = sin A cos B ± cos A sin B
    cos(A±B) = cos A cos B ∓ sin A sin B
    tan(A±B) = (tan A ± tan B)/(1 ∓ tan A tan B)

    Key derived results:
    • sin 75° = sin(45+30) = (√6+√2)/4
    • cos 75° = cos(45+30) = (√6–√2)/4
    • sin 15° = (√6–√2)/4, cos 15° = (√6+√2)/4
    • sin 105° = sin 75°, cos 105° = –cos 75°
    • sin 120° = sin 60° = √3/2, cos 120° = –1/2
    • sin 135° = 1/√2, cos 135° = –1/√2
    • sin 150° = 1/2, cos 150° = –√3/2
  • Double, Triple and Multiple Angle Formulae: Double angle:
    • sin 2A = 2 sin A cos A = 2tan A/(1+tan²A)
    • cos 2A = cos²A – sin²A = 2cos²A – 1 = 1 – 2sin²A
    • tan 2A = 2tan A/(1 – tan²A)

    Triple angle:
    • sin 3A = 3sin A – 4sin³A
    • cos 3A = 4cos³A – 3cos A
    • tan 3A = (3tan A – tan³A)/(1 – 3tan²A)

    Half angle:
    • sin(A/2) = ±√[(1 – cos A)/2]
    • cos(A/2) = ±√[(1 + cos A)/2]
    • tan(A/2) = sin A/(1+cos A) = (1–cos A)/sin A

    Power-reduction: sin²A = (1–cos2A)/2, cos²A = (1+cos2A)/2
  • Product-to-Sum and Sum-to-Product Formulae: Product → Sum:
    • 2sin A cos B = sin(A+B) + sin(A–B)
    • 2cos A sin B = sin(A+B) – sin(A–B)
    • 2cos A cos B = cos(A+B) + cos(A–B)
    • 2sin A sin B = cos(A–B) – cos(A+B)

    Sum → Product:
    • sin C + sin D = 2 sin[(C+D)/2] cos[(C–D)/2]
    • sin C – sin D = 2 cos[(C+D)/2] sin[(C–D)/2]
    • cos C + cos D = 2 cos[(C+D)/2] cos[(C–D)/2]
    • cos C – cos D = –2 sin[(C+D)/2] sin[(C–D)/2]
    These are essential for simplifying expressions and solving equations.
  • Standard Trigonometric Results and Proofs: Important identities frequently asked:
    • sin²θ – cos²θ = –cos 2θ; sin⁴θ – cos⁴θ = –cos 2θ
    • (1+sin θ)/(1–sin θ) = (sec θ + tan θ)²; (1–sin θ)/(1+sin θ) = (sec θ – tan θ)²
    • If tan θ = p/q then sin θ = p/√(p²+q²), cos θ = q/√(p²+q²)
    • a sin θ + b cos θ = R sin(θ+α), where R = √(a²+b²) and tan α = b/a
    • Maximum value of a sin θ + b cos θ + c = c + √(a²+b²); minimum = c – √(a²+b²)
    • sin A + sin B + sin C = 4 cos(A/2) cos(B/2) cos(C/2) for triangle angles
    • cos A + cos B + cos C = 1 + 4 sin(A/2) sin(B/2) sin(C/2)
    • tan A + tan B + tan C = tan A tan B tan C (A+B+C = π)
  • Properties of Triangles — Projection, Napier's and Half-Angle Formulae: Projection formulae:
    • a = b cos C + c cos B
    • b = c cos A + a cos C
    • c = a cos B + b cos A

    Half-angle formulae (using s = (a+b+c)/2):
    • sin(A/2) = √[(s–b)(s–c)/bc]
    • cos(A/2) = √[s(s–a)/bc]
    • tan(A/2) = √[(s–b)(s–c)/s(s–a)]

    Napier's Analogy (Tangent Rule):
    • (b–c)/(b+c) = tan[(B–C)/2]/tan[(B+C)/2]
    • (c–a)/(c+a) = tan[(C–A)/2]/tan[(C+A)/2]
    • (a–b)/(a+b) = tan[(A–B)/2]/tan[(A+B)/2]

    Mollweide's Formulae:
    • (a+b)/c = cos[(A–B)/2]/sin(C/2)
    • (a–b)/c = sin[(A–B)/2]/cos(C/2)
  • Inverse Trigonometric Function Identities: Essential identity pairs (valid within domain):
    • sin⁻¹x + cos⁻¹x = π/2 for x ∈ [–1, 1]
    • tan⁻¹x + cot⁻¹x = π/2 for all x ∈ ℝ
    • sec⁻¹x + cosec⁻¹x = π/2 for |x| ≥ 1
    • tan⁻¹x + tan⁻¹y = tan⁻¹[(x+y)/(1–xy)] if xy < 1
    • tan⁻¹x – tan⁻¹y = tan⁻¹[(x–y)/(1+xy)] if xy > –1
    • 2tan⁻¹x = sin⁻¹[2x/(1+x²)] = cos⁻¹[(1–x²)/(1+x²)] = tan⁻¹[2x/(1–x²)]
    • tan⁻¹x + tan⁻¹y + tan⁻¹z = tan⁻¹[(x+y+z–xyz)/(1–xy–yz–zx)] if xy+yz+zx < 1
    Graph: sin⁻¹x is odd, increasing on [–1,1]. cos⁻¹x is decreasing on [–1,1].
  • Solving Trigonometric Equations — Pattern and Approach: Strategy framework:
    1. Reduce to single trig function using identities → quadratic in sin θ or cos θ often appears.
    2. Factorise: (2sin θ – 1)(sin θ + 1) = 0 gives sin θ = 1/2 or sin θ = –1.
    3. For sin θ = k, general sol: θ = nπ + (–1)ⁿ sin⁻¹k.
    4. For cos θ = k, general sol: θ = 2nπ ± cos⁻¹k.
    5. For tan θ = k, general sol: θ = nπ + tan⁻¹k.
    6. Equations like a cos θ + b sin θ = c: require |c| ≤ √(a²+b²) for real solutions.
    7. Conditional equations with constraints (e.g., 0 ≤ θ ≤ 2π): find principal values then check bounds.
    8. Equations involving sin 2θ, cos 3θ etc.: reduce to algebraic form first.
  • Height and Distance — Standard Cases: Case 1 — Single observation:
    • Object height h, observer at horizontal distance d from foot.
    • If angle of elevation = θ: h = d tan θ.
    • If angle of depression from top = θ: same relation, h = d tan θ.

    Case 2 — Two stations on same side:
    • From two points at distances x and (x+d) from object base, angles α and β (α > β).
    • h = d · tan α · tan β / (tan α – tan β)

    Case 3 — Two stations on opposite sides:
    • Angles α and β from either side, distance between stations = d.
    • h = d · tan α · tan β / (tan α + tan β)

    Case 4 — Moving observer:
    • Angle changes from α to β after moving distance d toward/away.
    • h = d · tan α · tan β / |tan α ∓ tan β| (use – for approaching, + for moving away).

    Note: angle of elevation measured upward from horizontal; angle of depression measured downward.
  • Range, Period and Graph Features: Period and range of standard functions:
    • sin θ, cos θ: period 2π, range [–1, 1]
    • tan θ, cot θ: period π, range ℝ (undefined at odd multiples of π/2 for tan)
    • sec θ, cosec θ: period 2π, range (–∞, –1] ∪ [1, ∞)

    Transformations:
    • y = a sin(bx + c) + d: amplitude = |a|, period = 2π/|b|, phase shift = –c/b, vertical shift = d
    • Max of a sin θ + b cos θ + c = c + √(a²+b²)
    • Min of a sin θ + b cos θ + c = c – √(a²+b²)
    • Number of solutions of sin θ = k in [0, 2π]: 0 if |k|>1; 1 if |k|=1; 2 if |k|<1 (k≠0). For k=0: 2 solutions (0, π).

Important facts

  • sin 18° = (√5 – 1)/4 and cos 36° = (√5 + 1)/4 — remember as linked pair.
  • If A + B + C = π (triangle angles): tan A + tan B + tan C = tan A · tan B · tan C. This is a favourite NDA shortcut.
  • sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 is the master identity — derive all others from it.
  • For the equation a cos θ + b sin θ = c to have real solutions, the necessary and sufficient condition is a² + b² ≥ c².
  • tan 3θ = tan θ ⇒ 3θ = nπ + θ ⇒ θ = nπ/2. So roots are 0, π/2, π, ... but tan is undefined at π/2 — check domain.
  • When converting sum to product, remember: 'sum of sines uses sine×cosine, difference of sines uses cosine×sine.'
  • cot⁻¹(–x) = π – cot⁻¹x for x > 0; sin⁻¹(–x) = –sin⁻¹x; cos⁻¹(–x) = π – cos⁻¹x.
  • In competitive exams, questions like sin⁻¹(3/5) + cos⁻¹(12/13) require finding individual angles via right triangles: sin⁻¹(3/5) = tan⁻¹(3/4), cos⁻¹(12/13) = tan⁻¹(5/12); then use tan addition.
  • For NDA specifically, practice: proving identities under constraint A+B+C = π; solving sin 2θ · sin θ = sin 5θ · sin 3θ type equations; and using substitution t = tan(θ/2) for rational trig expressions.
  • sin(nπ + (–1)ⁿα) always equals sin α; cos(2nπ ± α) always equals cos α — these patterns eliminate sign errors in general solutions.

Memory aids

  • SOH-CAH-TOA — Sin = Opp/Hyp, Cos = Adj/Hyp, Tan = Opp/Adj.
  • Shahaj (Sin Haath) — All Students Take Calculus: All (Q1), Sin (Q2), Tan (Q3), Cos (Q4) are positive respectively.
  • The star diagram: draw a right triangle, label sides, place ratios around — memorise by redrawing from memory until automatic.
  • Pascal's triangle pattern for sin: √0/2, √1/2, √2/2, √3/2, √4/2 for 0°, 30°, 45°, 60°, 90°.
  • Reciprocal pairs shortcut: Sin↔Cosec, Cos↔Sec, Tan↔Cot — write them as pairs to never confuse.
  • Identity family tree: start from sin²+cos²=1, divide by cos² to get 1+tan²=sec², divide by sin² to get 1+cot²=cosec².
  • Angle names mnemonic for compound: "Silly Snakes Infiltrate Common Areas" for sin(A±B)=sinA cosB ± cosA sinB.
  • Product to sum: keep the outer function, split the inner — 'Two sins fight' gives cosine difference; 'Two coses hug' gives cosine sum.

Exam tips

  • NDA Maths has 120 questions in 2.5 hours — trigonometry typically contributes 8–12 questions. Speed with standard angle values and identities saves crucial time.
  • When asked to prove an identity, start from the more complex side and simplify — never cross-multiply across the equality sign in a formal proof (acceptable in MCQ verification though).
  • For MCQs, substitute convenient values (like θ = 30° or 45°) into both sides of an identity question to quickly eliminate wrong options.
  • In equation-solving MCQs, check each option by direct substitution rather than solving from scratch — much faster under time pressure.
  • Remember: if a question gives sin θ + cos θ = √2, then sin θ · cos θ = 1/2 and sin⁴θ + cos⁴θ = 1/2 — such linked results appear repeatedly.
  • For inverse trig equations like tan⁻¹x + tan⁻¹(1/x) = π/2 (x > 0) — a common standalone result tested directly.
  • Height and distance questions in NDA are mostly direct formula applications — memorise the two-station formulas and practise 5–6 variants per case.
  • In CDS Elementary Mathematics, expect one or two questions on general solution and one on triangle properties — these are high-yield and quick to solve once you recognise the pattern.
  • Time management tip: spend max 90 seconds on any trigonometry MCQ. If stuck, mark and move — these questions are designed to be solved fast if you know the shortcuts.

Common traps

  • Sign errors in quadrant II/III/IV — always draw the ASTC chart before evaluating sin 150°, cos 210°, tan 300° etc.
  • Forgetting that tan⁻¹x + tan⁻¹y formula requires checking xy < 1; if xy > 1, add or subtract π depending on signs of x and y.
  • Writing sin⁻¹(2) = undefined vs treating it as a valid expression — domain of sin⁻¹ is [–1, 1] strictly.
  • Confusing general solution of sin θ = sin α with cos θ = cos α — one has (–1)ⁿ, the other has ± without alternating power.
  • In height-distance problems with two stations, mixing up the '+' and '–' in the denominator based on whether stations are on same or opposite sides.
  • Assuming a cos θ + b sin θ = c always has solutions — must verify c² ≤ a² + b² first.
  • Not checking for extraneous solutions when squaring both sides of a trig equation (e.g.,√(1+sin θ) = 1+sin θ case).
  • Applying half-angle formulas without determining the correct sign based on the quadrant of A/2.
  • In triangle problems with A+B+C = π, forgetting that sin((A+B)/2) = cos(C/2) — this substitution is frequently needed.
  • Mixing up degrees and radians in calculator or when general solutions involve π.
